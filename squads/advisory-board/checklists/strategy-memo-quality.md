# Strategy Memo Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures every strategy memo articulates a coherent theory of winning, makes explicit tradeoffs visible, and provides the board with sufficient context to evaluate strategic direction. A strategy memo is not a plan; it is a set of choices about where to play and how to win. This checklist prevents vague aspirations from masquerading as strategy.

## When to Use
- Before presenting any strategic recommendation to the board or leadership team.
- During annual and mid-year strategic planning cycles.
- When evaluating a proposed pivot, market entry, or major product bet.
- When a competitor action or market shift demands a strategic response.

## Gate Criteria

### Strategic Clarity
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The memo states a clear winning aspiration that is specific enough to be falsifiable.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Where-to-play choices are explicit: target segments, geographies, channels, and categories.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: How-to-win choices are articulated: what capabilities and value propositions create competitive advantage.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The strategy is differentiated from competitors; a side-by-side comparison demonstrates this.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: What the company will NOT do is stated as clearly as what it will do.

### Tradeoff Transparency
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: At least three explicit tradeoffs are identified and the reasoning for each choice is documented.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Resource allocation implications are quantified (people, capital, time).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Opportunity costs of the chosen strategy are acknowledged.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The memo explains what must be true for this strategy to succeed (critical assumptions).

### Evidence and Analysis
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Market sizing is based on bottom-up analysis, not top-down TAM hand-waving.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Competitive landscape includes at least direct competitors, adjacent threats, and substitute products.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer evidence (interviews, data, surveys) supports the strategic thesis.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Historical precedents or analogies are cited where relevant and their limitations acknowledged.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The memo includes a sensitivity analysis on the two or three assumptions most likely to be wrong.

### Execution Bridge
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The strategy translates into three to five measurable objectives for the next 12 months.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Capability gaps required to execute are identified with plans to close them.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Organizational alignment requirements are specified (team structure, incentives, processes).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A 90-day action plan with named owners is included.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Kill criteria are defined: what evidence would cause us to abandon or revise this strategy.

### Coherence Check
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The strategy is consistent with the company's stated mission and values.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The strategy is internally consistent (choices reinforce each other, not contradict).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The strategy accounts for current commitments and in-flight initiatives.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The strategy has been stress-tested against at least two alternative future scenarios.

## Red Flags
- The memo describes activities and projects rather than choices and tradeoffs.
- Market size is expressed only as TAM with no pathway to capture.
- No competitor is named; the strategy exists in a vacuum.
- The strategy requires the company to be great at everything simultaneously.
- The memo conflates strategy with goals or with a list of initiatives.
- No one can articulate what the company is choosing NOT to do.

## Escalation Triggers
- The strategy contradicts board-approved capital allocation without acknowledgment.
- Two or more leadership team members cannot articulate the strategy consistently.
- The strategy depends on a single assumption that has not been validated.
- Market data used is more than six months old in a fast-moving market.
- The strategy memo has been revised more than three times without converging on a clear direction.
