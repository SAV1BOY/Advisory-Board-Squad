# Naval Wealth vs Status Check Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the organization and its leaders are pursuing wealth creation (positive-sum value generation) rather than status games (zero-sum competition for rank and recognition). Naval distinguishes between wealth (assets that earn while you sleep) and status (relative ranking that requires someone else to lose). Status-seeking behavior in organizations creates politics, undermines collaboration, and diverts energy from value creation.

## When to Use
- When organizational politics are impeding performance.
- During compensation and title discussions to assess underlying motivations.
- When evaluating whether strategic choices create value or merely signal status.
- When team dynamics suggest competition for recognition over collaboration.
- During culture reviews to assess whether the organization rewards creation or politics.

## Gate Criteria

### Value Creation Orientation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Strategic decisions are evaluated on value creation potential, not on how they position the company against perceived rivals.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Internal metrics focus on absolute progress (revenue growth, customer satisfaction), not relative ranking.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Press and marketing are used to communicate value to customers, not to signal status to competitors.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Partnership decisions are made based on mutual value creation, not competitive dynamics.

### Internal Culture Assessment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Recognition and rewards are tied to value created, not to internal political positioning.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Title inflation is avoided; titles reflect scope and accountability, not status.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Cross-team collaboration is rewarded, not seen as diluting individual credit.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders share credit for successes and take ownership of failures.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Hiring decisions prioritize candidates who create value over those who compete for status.

### Decision Quality
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decisions are not made to impress external parties (investors, media) at the expense of genuine value.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Vanity metrics (press mentions, awards, conference invitations) are not confused with business health metrics.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Resource allocation is based on expected return, not on which leader lobbies most effectively.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization resists "keeping up with the Joneses" behavior (matching competitor moves for prestige, not value).

### Long-Term Wealth Building
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization invests in assets that compound (technology, brand, relationships) over status symbols (fancy offices, unnecessary perks).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Salary and equity structures incentivize long-term wealth creation, not short-term status enhancement.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization measures and communicates long-term value creation metrics, not just vanity milestones.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decisions to forgo status in favor of value creation are celebrated, not questioned.

## Red Flags
- Leaders spend more time managing their personal brand than managing their teams.
- Decisions are made to generate press coverage rather than business value.
- Internal politics determine resource allocation more than strategic merit.
- Title negotiations consume disproportionate leadership time.
- The company acquires other companies for prestige rather than for clear strategic value.
- Employee satisfaction is driven more by relative perks than by meaningful work.

## Escalation Triggers
- A major strategic decision is demonstrably motivated by status rather than value creation.
- Internal political dynamics cause the loss of a high-value employee.
- Resources are allocated to a prestige project while a value-creating project is starved.
- Customer value declines while the company continues to invest in brand positioning.
- The board identifies a pattern of status-seeking behavior that is impeding performance.
