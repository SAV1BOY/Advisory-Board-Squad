# Thiel Contrarian Question Checklist

## Purpose
Applies Peter Thiel's foundational question: "What important truth do few people agree with you on?" This checklist forces the organization to identify and articulate its contrarian beliefs, test whether they are genuinely contrarian (not just consensus dressed as contrarian), and assess whether those beliefs translate into strategic differentiation. Companies that only hold consensus views build consensus businesses, which means competition and thin margins.

## When to Use
- During annual strategic planning to identify differentiated positioning.
- When evaluating new ventures, products, or market entries.
- When the company's strategy feels generic or similar to competitors.
- When assessing whether the organization has genuine intellectual courage.

## Gate Criteria

### Contrarian Belief Identification
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization can articulate at least three beliefs about its market, customers, or technology that most competitors would disagree with.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each contrarian belief is specific enough to be falsifiable with evidence.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The beliefs are genuinely contrarian, not recycled conventional wisdom phrased differently.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The beliefs are connected to strategic action, not just intellectual exercise.

### Evidence and Reasoning
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each contrarian belief is supported by evidence or reasoning, not just intuition.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The analysis explains why the consensus view exists and why it is wrong.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team has sought out the strongest arguments against its contrarian beliefs.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The timeframe for the contrarian belief to be validated or invalidated is specified.

### Strategic Translation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Contrarian beliefs inform product, hiring, and capital allocation decisions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization is making bets that would not make sense if the consensus view were correct.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Competitive differentiation is traceable to contrarian beliefs.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization is willing to be patient while the market catches up to its view.

### Intellectual Courage
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leadership can defend the contrarian position publicly without hedging.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization hires people who strengthen contrarian positions, not just people who agree with consensus.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team revisits contrarian beliefs regularly and is willing to abandon them if evidence changes.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: There is no social penalty within the organization for holding or challenging contrarian views.

## Red Flags
- The company's strategy would be approved by every competitor's board.
- "Contrarian" beliefs are actually widely held but not publicly stated.
- The organization claims to be different but cannot articulate how.
- Contrarian positions are held out of stubbornness, not evidence-based reasoning.
- The company abandons contrarian positions at the first sign of market resistance.
- No one in the organization is willing to articulate a view that would make them unpopular.

## Escalation Triggers
- The market validates a contrarian position held by a competitor that the organization dismissed.
- The organization's strategy is indistinguishable from its top three competitors.
- Contrarian beliefs are held privately by leadership but not acted upon in strategy.
- The organization loses key talent who cite intellectual conformity as a reason for leaving.
- A contrarian belief is proving wrong but the organization is not adjusting.
