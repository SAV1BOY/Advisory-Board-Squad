# Fundraising Plan Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures that fundraising plans are grounded in realistic assumptions, aligned with strategic needs, and structured to maximize long-term value while minimizing unnecessary dilution or unfavorable terms. A fundraising plan should answer why, how much, when, and from whom with specificity and honesty.

## When to Use
- Before initiating any external capital raise (equity, debt, convertible instruments).
- When revising fundraising targets or timeline during an active raise.
- During board discussions about capital needs and runway.
- When evaluating term sheets or investment proposals.

## Gate Criteria

### Needs Justification
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The amount to raise is derived from a bottoms-up financial model, not a round number.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Use of proceeds is specified at the initiative level with expected returns.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The raise amount includes a buffer for delays and downside scenarios (minimum 20% contingency).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The plan justifies why external capital is needed versus internal cash generation or cost reduction.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The target runway post-raise is defined (minimum 18 months for venture, longer for growth).

### Timing and Market
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Market conditions for the fundraise are assessed (investor sentiment, comparable valuations, sector trends).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The timeline accounts for realistic fundraising duration (typically 3-6 months for venture rounds).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The plan identifies a deadline by which the raise must close to avoid operational risk.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Competitive fundraising dynamics are considered (peers raising simultaneously).

### Investor Strategy
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Target investors are identified with rationale for strategic fit beyond capital.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Lead investor criteria are defined (check size, domain expertise, value-add capabilities).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The plan includes a pipeline of at least 3x the number of investors needed for the round.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Existing investor participation expectations are documented.

### Terms and Dilution
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Maximum acceptable dilution is defined and approved by the board.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Key term sheet provisions are identified as must-haves, nice-to-haves, and deal-breakers.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Anti-dilution, liquidation preference, and governance terms are modeled for impact on founders and employees.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The fully diluted cap table post-raise is modeled and shared with the board.

### Contingency Planning
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A Plan B exists if the fundraise takes longer than expected or fails.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Cost-reduction scenarios are modeled to extend runway if fundraising is delayed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Bridge financing options are identified with terms pre-negotiated where possible.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The trigger point to activate Plan B is defined in terms of time or milestones.

## Red Flags
- The fundraising amount is based on what the market will bear rather than what the business needs.
- No use of proceeds breakdown exists beyond general categories.
- The company is fundraising with less than three months of runway remaining.
- The plan assumes a single investor will fund the entire round with no backup options.
- Existing investors have declined to participate without a satisfactory explanation.
- The valuation expectation is based on narrative rather than financial fundamentals.

## Escalation Triggers
- The fundraise has been active for more than six months without a lead investor.
- Runway drops below six months with no signed term sheet.
- Term sheet terms materially differ from board-approved parameters.
- A down round becomes likely, requiring board discussion on dilution implications.
- A prospective investor demands governance provisions that would limit board authority.
