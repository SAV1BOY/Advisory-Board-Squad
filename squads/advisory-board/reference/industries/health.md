# Health / Healthtech Industry Reference

## Business Model Characteristics
- **Revenue models:** SaaS to providers/payers, per-member-per-month (PMPM), outcomes-based, device sales + recurring
- **Long sales cycles:** Healthcare organizations are risk-averse. Enterprise sales to hospitals/insurers take 6-18 months.
- **Regulatory burden:** HIPAA (US), LGPD (Brazil), GDPR (EU), FDA approval (for devices/diagnostics). Non-compliance is existential.
- **Payer complexity:** In most markets, the user (patient) is not the buyer (insurer/employer/government).

## Key Segments
- **Digital health / telemedicine:** Virtual care delivery. Accelerated by COVID; now facing engagement challenges.
- **Health IT (EHR/EMR):** Core infrastructure for healthcare providers. Dominated by incumbents (Epic, Cerner). Hard to disrupt.
- **Diagnostics / biotech:** Requires deep science, clinical validation, and regulatory approval. Long timelines, high risk, high reward.
- **Wellness / consumer health:** DTC health products and services. Lower regulatory burden but also lower moats.
- **Value-based care infrastructure:** Tools and services that support the shift from fee-for-service to outcomes-based payment.

## Growth Challenges
- Clinical validation is required for adoption. Evidence-based claims take time and money to produce.
- Integration with existing health IT infrastructure (HL7, FHIR) is complex and slow.
- Clinician adoption requires demonstrating workflow efficiency, not just clinical benefit.
- Reimbursement pathways determine willingness to pay more than clinical value does.

## Board Considerations
- Regulatory and compliance expertise on the board is essential, not optional
- Evaluate whether the company's value proposition addresses all three stakeholders: patients, providers, and payers
- Clinical evidence is the moat. Companies with strong clinical evidence outperform those with better technology but weaker evidence.
- Monitor reimbursement landscape changes as they can fundamentally alter the business model
