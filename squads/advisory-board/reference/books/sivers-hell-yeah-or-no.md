# Hell Yeah or No — Derek Sivers

## Summary

A collection of short essays on decision-making, priorities, and the art of saying no. Sivers' central thesis: when you say yes to everything, you spread yourself thin and accomplish nothing meaningful. The "Hell Yeah or No" filter forces clarity—if a new commitment doesn't excite you deeply, decline it to preserve space for the things that do. The book applies this philosophy to business decisions, relationships, goals, and daily life. It is a manual for intentional living in an age of infinite options and constant distraction.

## Key Principles

- **If it's not "Hell Yeah," it's "No"** — The filter works precisely because opportunities are abundant, not scarce. Saying no to good opportunities preserves capacity for great ones.
- **Busy is a decision** — Being busy is not a badge of honor. It usually means you haven't made hard choices about what matters most.
- **Actions reveal priorities** — Ignore what people (including yourself) say their priorities are. Watch where they spend their time, money, and energy. That's the truth.
- **Unlearning is as important as learning** — Old beliefs and habits take up space. Regularly audit and discard what's no longer serving you.
- **No "yes" is forever** — Commitments should be revisited regularly. What was a "Hell Yeah" two years ago may no longer be one.
- **Subtract to add** — The most powerful improvements often come from removing things (products, features, commitments, people) rather than adding them.

## Frameworks Extracted

1. **The Hell Yeah Filter** — For every new opportunity: (1) Does it excite you deeply? (2) Does it align with your current priorities? (3) Do you have the capacity to do it well? All three must be yes. Otherwise, decline.
2. **The Priority Audit** — List everything you're committed to. Rate each on excitement (1-10) and alignment (1-10). Anything below 7 on both is a candidate for elimination.
3. **The Subtraction Exercise** — Instead of asking "what should I add to my business/life?" ask "what should I remove?" Often the path to improvement is through elimination, not addition.
4. **The Time = Priority Test** — Look at last month's calendar. Whatever dominated your time is your actual priority, regardless of what you tell yourself. If the calendar doesn't match stated priorities, something needs to change.

## Quotable Lines

- "If you're not saying 'HELL YEAH!' about something, say 'no.'"
- "When you say no to most things, you leave room in your life to really throw yourself completely into that rare thing that makes you say 'HELL YEAH!'"
- "Busy is a decision. You have chosen to be busy."
- "Judge a plan by how well it would work if things went wrong."
- "Don't pursue something because it's a popular goal. Pursue it only if it's yours."
- "The more you say no, the more you can say yes to what really matters."

## Application to Board Decisions

- **Strategic focus** — Apply the Hell Yeah filter to strategic proposals at the board level. If a new market, product, or partnership doesn't generate genuine excitement from the leadership team, it's probably a distraction.
- **Portfolio pruning** — Regularly review the company's product portfolio, partnerships, and initiatives using the Subtraction Exercise. What should be killed to free resources for what matters?
- **Board commitment audit** — Each board member should periodically assess whether their board service is still a Hell Yeah. Disengaged board members are worse than empty seats.
- **M&A discipline** — Most acquisitions are not Hell Yeah opportunities—they're "interesting" or "synergistic." Apply the filter to dramatically reduce acquisition frequency and increase acquisition quality.
- **Time allocation** — Audit how the CEO spends their time. If the calendar doesn't reflect stated strategic priorities, that's a governance conversation the board must have.
