# The Almanack of Naval Ravikant — Eric Jorgenson

## Summary

A curated compilation of Naval Ravikant's insights on wealth creation, happiness, and decision-making drawn from tweets, podcasts, and interviews. Naval argues that wealth is built through specific knowledge, leverage, and long-term thinking—not through trading time for money. The second half addresses happiness as a skill that can be developed through awareness, presence, and the deliberate reduction of desire. The book bridges Silicon Valley pragmatism with philosophical depth, offering frameworks that apply equally to a seed-stage founder and a seasoned board member.

## Key Principles

- **Specific knowledge cannot be taught** — It's found by pursuing your genuine curiosity. It feels like play to you but looks like work to others. This is your unfair advantage.
- **Leverage comes in three forms** — Labor (people working for you), capital (money working for you), and products with zero marginal cost of replication (code, media). The last is the most powerful and most accessible.
- **Accountability earns equity** — People who take risk and bear consequences under their own name build reputations that attract resources. Hiding behind committees destroys personal upside.
- **Play long-term games with long-term people** — Compound interest applies to relationships, reputation, and knowledge, not just capital.
- **Arm yourself with specific knowledge, accountability, and leverage** — These three together create asymmetric outcomes.
- **Happiness is a choice and a skill** — It improves when you reduce your attachment to outcomes and increase your awareness of the present.
- **Read to build mental models, not to collect facts** — Read foundational texts deeply rather than consuming news shallowly.

## Frameworks Extracted

1. **The Wealth Creation Stack** — Specific knowledge + Accountability + Leverage = Wealth. Missing any one element caps your upside. Board members should evaluate founders on all three.
2. **Leverage Hierarchy** — (1) Code/Media (best: infinite scale, no permission needed), (2) Capital (good: scales, but requires trust), (3) Labor (limited: scales linearly, management overhead). Evaluate business models by which leverage they primarily use.
3. **The Long-Term Game Filter** — Before any partnership, hire, or deal: would you do this if you knew you'd be working with this person for 20 years? If not, don't start.
4. **The Specific Knowledge Test** — Can this person's contribution be trained into someone else in 6 months? If yes, it's not specific knowledge—it's a commodity skill. Boards should protect and retain people with genuine specific knowledge.

## Application to Board Decisions

- **Founder evaluation** — Use the Wealth Creation Stack to assess whether a founder has specific knowledge, is operating with accountability, and is deploying the right leverage for their market.
- **Business model analysis** — Apply the Leverage Hierarchy. Businesses built on code/media leverage will outscale those built on labor leverage. Advise accordingly.
- **Talent retention** — Identify who in the organization holds specific knowledge. These people are irreplaceable and should be retained through equity, autonomy, and mission alignment—not just salary.
- **Partnership decisions** — Apply the Long-Term Game Filter. Short-term transactional deals with untrustworthy counterparties destroy more value than they create.
- **Board member selection** — Seek board members who bring specific knowledge the existing team lacks, who have skin in the game, and who play long-term games.
