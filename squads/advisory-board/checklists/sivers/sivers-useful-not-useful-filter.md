# Sivers Useful Not Useful Filter Checklist

## Purpose
Applies Sivers' pragmatic philosophy of evaluating everything by its usefulness rather than its cleverness, popularity, or theoretical elegance. In organizations, many activities, processes, and strategies persist because they seem sophisticated or because everyone does them, not because they are actually useful. This checklist forces a practical assessment of utility across the organization.

## When to Use
- During strategy reviews to assess whether initiatives are producing useful results.
- When evaluating processes, tools, and practices for continued investment.
- When the organization seems busy but not productive.
- When simplification efforts need a clear framework for what to keep and what to cut.
- During budget reviews to assess whether spending is generating useful outcomes.

## Gate Criteria

### Utility Assessment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each major initiative can demonstrate specific, measurable usefulness (not just activity).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The intended user or beneficiary of each activity can confirm it is useful to them.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The cost of each activity is proportional to its demonstrated usefulness.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Activities that are useful in theory but not in practice are identified and reformed or eliminated.

### Decision Application
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: "Is this useful?" is a routine question in decision-making and prioritization.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Usefulness is measured by outcomes (customer value, revenue, learning) not by outputs (reports, meetings, emails).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization can distinguish between activities that feel productive and activities that are productive.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Strategies and plans are assessed for practical utility, not just intellectual sophistication.

### Organizational Hygiene
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Reports, meetings, and processes that no one finds useful are eliminated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Tools and platforms are assessed for actual usage, not just license count.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Metrics that are collected but never used for decisions are discontinued.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Communication that informs no one and changes no behavior is stopped.

### Continuous Filtering
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A regular review cycle exists to reassess the usefulness of ongoing activities.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The filter is applied consistently, not just to politically weak initiatives.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Sacred cows (activities protected by tradition or seniority) are subjected to the same filter.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization gets better over time at predicting what will be useful before investing.

## Red Flags
- The organization produces extensive analysis that no one reads.
- Meetings occur on schedule regardless of whether there is useful content to discuss.
- Processes exist because they were once useful but the conditions have changed.
- The team is proud of the complexity of their work rather than the impact.
- No one has asked "who finds this useful?" for the organization's major activities.
- Budgets include line items that have been carried forward for years without reassessment.

## Escalation Triggers
- More than 30% of organizational capacity is consumed by activities that fail the usefulness test.
- A major initiative is completed but produces no measurable useful outcome.
- Teams resist the usefulness filter because it threatens activities tied to their identity or power.
- The organization's overhead ratio (non-useful activity to useful activity) is increasing.
- Customer value metrics decline while internal activity levels remain high.
