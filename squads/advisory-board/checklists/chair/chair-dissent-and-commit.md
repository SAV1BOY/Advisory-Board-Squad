# Chair Dissent and Commit Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the advisory board practices genuine dissent-and-commit: encouraging robust debate before a decision and requiring unified execution after. This is not about forcing consensus; it is about ensuring all perspectives are heard, the best argument wins, and once the decision is made, everyone commits to making it succeed rather than undermining it through passive resistance.

## When to Use
- During board deliberations on contested decisions.
- When decisions are being relitigated after they were made.
- When board alignment after decisions is inconsistent.
- When establishing decision-making culture for a new board.

## Gate Criteria

### Dissent Encouragement
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The chair explicitly invites dissenting views before calling for a decision.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Quieter board members are specifically asked for their perspective.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Dissent is treated as a contribution to better decisions, not as obstruction.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The strongest argument against the proposed decision is articulated and addressed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board members feel safe expressing minority opinions without social penalty.

### Decision Clarity
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The decision point is clearly stated before deliberation begins.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The options under consideration are defined and understood by all.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The criteria for making the decision are agreed upon before evaluating options.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The decision is formally stated and recorded, including any conditions or contingencies.

### Commitment After Decision
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: All board members publicly commit to supporting the decision once made.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board members who dissented communicate the decision to their stakeholders without undermining it.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Dissent is recorded in the minutes for institutional learning, but does not become ongoing opposition.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The chair follows up with dissenters to ensure they are genuinely committed, not just silent.

### Anti-Relitigating Discipline
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decisions are only reopened when genuinely new information emerges, not because of buyer's remorse.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The threshold for reopening a decided matter is defined and high.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The chair redirects attempts to relitigate decided matters.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: If a decision must be revisited, it goes through the full decision process again.

## Red Flags
- Decisions are unanimous without any debate, suggesting false consensus.
- Board members agree in the meeting but undermine decisions afterward.
- The same decision is discussed at three or more consecutive meetings without resolution.
- Dissent is expressed through passive-aggressive behavior rather than direct advocacy.
- The chair does not ask for dissenting views before calling for a vote.
- Minority opinions are dismissed with "we already decided" rather than "here is why we decided."

## Escalation Triggers
- A board member actively sabotages a decision they disagreed with.
- The board cannot reach a decision on a material matter after multiple sessions.
- A pattern emerges where decisions are relitigated at every meeting.
- Board members express dissent to external parties rather than within the board process.
- The chair is unable to facilitate productive conflict, resulting in either false harmony or chronic disagreement.
