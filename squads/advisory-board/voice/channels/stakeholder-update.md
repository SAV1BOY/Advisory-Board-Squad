# Channel Guide: Stakeholder Update

## When to Use

A stakeholder update is a periodic communication to external stakeholders — investors, partners, advisors, or key customers — providing a structured view of company progress, challenges, and outlook. Use it for:

- Quarterly investor updates.
- Partner relationship check-ins.
- Advisory Board external communications.
- Annual review summaries for key stakeholders.

## Format

### Structure

```
STAKEHOLDER UPDATE — [PERIOD]
[Company Name] | [Date]
[Confidentiality: Confidential / For Authorized Recipients Only]

HIGHLIGHTS
[3-5 bullet points: the most important things that happened]

PERFORMANCE
[Key metrics with comparisons to plan and prior period]

STRATEGIC PROGRESS
[Update on strategic priorities — what advanced, what stalled]

CHALLENGES & RISKS
[2-3 honest items: what's not going well and what you're doing about it]

OUTLOOK
[Forward-looking view for the next quarter/period]

ASKS
[If applicable: what you need from this stakeholder group]
```

### Length
- Quarterly investor update: 2-3 pages.
- Partner update: 1-2 pages.
- Annual summary: 3-5 pages.

## Writing Guidance by Section

### Highlights
- Lead with the single most important development.
- Mix wins and challenges. A highlights section that is 100 % positive is not credible.
- Each bullet is one sentence. Specific, not vague.

**Yes:**
- "Closed Series B at $15M on $60M pre-money, led by [Investor]."
- "Enterprise retention dropped to 86 %, driven by onboarding issues we are actively addressing."
- "Launched v2.0 of the API platform; 40 % of customers adopted within 6 weeks."

**No:**
- "Great quarter with lots of progress across multiple dimensions."
- "Continued to execute against our strategic plan."

### Performance
- Present 3-5 key metrics. Not 20. Choose the metrics that best represent company health.
- Always include comparison: actual vs. plan, actual vs. prior period.
- Use a simple table:

```
| Metric          | Q3 Actual | Q3 Plan | Q2 Actual | YoY Change |
|-----------------|-----------|---------|-----------|------------|
| ARR             | $8.2M     | $8.5M   | $7.4M     | +42 %      |
| Net retention   | 108 %     | 115 %   | 112 %     | -4 pts     |
| Gross margin    | 72 %      | 70 %    | 71 %      | +1 pt      |
| Cash runway     | 22 months | 24 mo   | 26 months | —          |
| Headcount       | 48        | 52      | 43        | +67 %      |
```

- Below the table, 2-3 sentences explaining the most significant variances. What happened and why.

### Strategic Progress
- Map directly to the strategic priorities set in the annual plan.
- For each priority: one sentence on status, one sentence on the most important development.
- Use a clear status indicator: On Track, At Risk, Behind, Completed.

```
Priority 1: Enterprise expansion — ON TRACK
Signed 4 new enterprise accounts, including [notable name].
Pipeline for Q4 is 2.3x target.

Priority 2: Platform v2.0 launch — COMPLETED
Launched on schedule in August. Adoption rate exceeding projections.

Priority 3: Hire VP Sales — BEHIND
Search is active but the finalist declined. Revised timeline: Q1 next year.
```

### Challenges & Risks
- This section builds trust. Stakeholders who only hear good news stop trusting the updates.
- Be specific about what's not working.
- For each challenge, state what you're doing about it.
- Do not surprise. If a risk was flagged in a prior update, reference it.

**Yes:** "Enterprise retention dropped for the second consecutive quarter. Root cause is onboarding capacity. We are hiring two implementation engineers (target: 60 days) and have engaged a contractor in the interim."

**No:** "We are monitoring some softness in retention metrics."

### Outlook
- Forward-looking but honest. State what you expect and the key assumptions.
- Flag any changes to the plan.
- If fundraising, M&A, or major strategic shifts are on the horizon, telegraph them here.

### Asks
- If you need introductions, references, advice, or action from stakeholders, ask explicitly.
- "We are seeking introductions to enterprise buyers in healthcare. If you have relevant contacts, please connect us."
- Do not bury asks in other sections. Give them their own space.

## Tone

- **Credible, not promotional.** Stakeholders see through spin. Honest communication builds long-term trust.
- **Balanced.** Every update should include both progress and challenges.
- **Data-driven.** Metrics before narrative. Numbers before adjectives.
- **Concise.** Stakeholders are busy. Earn every sentence.
- **Forward-looking.** Don't just report the past — connect it to the future.

## Cadence

| Stakeholder | Frequency | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| Investors | Quarterly | Email + secure document |
| Board advisors | Monthly (light), quarterly (full) | Email |
| Strategic partners | Quarterly or per agreement | Email or portal |
| Key customers | As warranted by relationship | Direct from account team |

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Victory lap | Every metric is celebrated, no challenges mentioned | Include at least 2 genuine challenges per update |
| Data dump | 20 metrics with no narrative | Pick 3-5 metrics; explain the variances |
| Surprise bad news | Stakeholders learn about problems for the first time | Telegraph risks early; update on known challenges each quarter |
| Generic language | "We continue to make progress" | Specific: what progress, measured how, compared to what |
| Missing the ask | Stakeholders want to help but aren't told how | Explicit asks section |
| Inconsistent cadence | Updates come sporadically | Lock the calendar; send on the same date each quarter |
