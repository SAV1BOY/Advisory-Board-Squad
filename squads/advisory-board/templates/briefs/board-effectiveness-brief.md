# Board Effectiveness Review Brief

## Session Type
Board Governance and Effectiveness Assessment

## Purpose
Evaluate the board's performance across its core responsibilities: strategic oversight, fiduciary duty, CEO accountability, and stakeholder representation. Identify areas where the board is adding value and areas where its composition, processes, or engagement need improvement.

## Context

Effective boards are a competitive advantage. Ineffective boards create risk through poor oversight, rubber-stamping, or micromanagement. Annual effectiveness reviews are best practice for boards at every stage.

## Pre-Session Preparation

### For the Board Chair / Lead Director
- [ ] Review board composition against current strategic needs
- [ ] Compile board meeting attendance and participation records
- [ ] Gather feedback from CEO on board interactions and value
- [ ] Review committee charters and assess whether they remain fit for purpose
- [ ] Prepare summary of board decisions made in the past 12 months
- [ ] Engage external facilitator if conducting review for the first time

### For Board Members
- [ ] Complete the self-assessment questionnaire below before the session
- [ ] Reflect on the quality of information received in board materials
- [ ] Consider whether the right topics are receiving adequate board time
- [ ] Assess your own contribution and engagement level honestly
- [ ] Identify skills or perspectives missing from the current board

## Session Agenda (2.5 Hours)

| Time | Activity | Duration |
|------|----------|----------|
| 0:00 | Opening: review purpose and confidentiality norms | 10 min |
| 0:10 | Self-assessment results review (aggregated) | 20 min |
| 0:30 | Discussion: strategic oversight effectiveness | 25 min |
| 0:55 | Break | 10 min |
| 1:05 | Discussion: board composition and skills gaps | 25 min |
| 1:30 | Discussion: information quality and meeting effectiveness | 20 min |
| 1:50 | Discussion: CEO oversight and succession readiness | 20 min |
| 2:10 | Priority improvements and action items | 15 min |
| 2:25 | Close: commitments and next review date | 5 min |

## Common Findings and Remedies

| Finding | Typical Remedy |
|---------|---------------|
| Too much time on operations, not enough on strategy | Restructure agenda; require management to handle operations in written updates |
| Board materials arrive too late or are too dense | Establish minimum lead time; mandate executive summaries |
| Insufficient board diversity | Develop a skills matrix; engage search firm for targeted recruitment |
| Weak CEO evaluation process | Formalize annual evaluation with 360 input and written assessment |
| No succession plan | Task the governance committee with developing succession framework |
| Groupthink in decision-making | Assign devil's advocate role; conduct pre-mortems on major decisions |
| Committee charters outdated | Annual committee charter review and refresh |

## Output: Board Effectiveness Report

1. **Aggregate self-assessment scores** with trend comparison (if prior year data exists)
2. **Top 3 strengths** the board should maintain
3. **Top 3 improvement areas** with specific action plans
4. **Composition gap analysis** with recruiting priorities
5. **Process improvements** for meetings, materials, and communication
6. **Commitments** with owners and timelines
7. **Date for next annual review**

## Confidentiality Note

Individual self-assessment responses are confidential and shared only in aggregate. The effectiveness report is a board-confidential document not shared beyond the board unless the board explicitly approves broader distribution.
