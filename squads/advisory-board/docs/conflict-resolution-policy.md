# Conflict Resolution Policy

## Purpose

Conflicts within the Advisory Board — between agents, between the board and management, or about strategic direction — are inevitable and healthy. This policy ensures conflicts are resolved constructively, not avoided or weaponized.

## Principles

1. **Conflict is a feature, not a bug.** Productive disagreement improves decisions. A board without conflict is a board without value.
2. **Address conflict early.** Small tensions handled quickly never become large dysfunctions.
3. **Separate the person from the position.** Disagree with ideas, not with people.
4. **Resolve or commit.** Every conflict ends with either agreement or disagree-and-commit.
5. **Escalate when stuck, not when uncomfortable.** Discomfort is a sign you're doing the work. Deadlock is a sign you need help.

## Conflict Types and Resolution Paths

### Type A: Factual Disagreement
Two agents disagree about what is true (data, evidence, facts).

**Resolution path:**
1. Identify the specific factual claim in dispute.
2. Strategy-Analyst gathers the relevant data.
3. Present the data. If the data resolves the dispute, done.
4. If the data is ambiguous, acknowledge the uncertainty and record both interpretations.

**Timeframe:** Resolved within the meeting or by the next meeting.

### Type B: Analytical Disagreement
Two agents agree on the facts but reach different conclusions.

**Resolution path:**
1. Each party states their reasoning chain explicitly: "Given [facts], I conclude [X] because [logic]."
2. Identify where the logic chains diverge.
3. Test the divergence point: is it a difference in weighting, assumptions, or mental models?
4. Devil's-Advocate steelmans both positions.
5. Board-Chair calls the decision or defers for additional analysis.

**Timeframe:** Resolved within the meeting or within 1 week (with additional analysis).

### Type C: Values Disagreement
Two agents disagree on what is right or important (priorities, trade-offs, values).

**Resolution path:**
1. Name the values in tension: "This is a tension between [value A] and [value B]."
2. Both parties articulate why their value takes priority in this context.
3. Reference the company's stated values and the board charter.
4. Culture-Guardian provides a values-alignment perspective.
5. Board-Chair makes the call, documenting the values trade-off explicitly.

**Timeframe:** Resolved within the meeting. May require private follow-up if the disagreement has personal dimensions.

### Type D: Interpersonal Conflict
Conflict between two agents that is personal, not substantive — involving communication style, perceived disrespect, or relationship breakdown.

**Resolution path:**
1. Board-Chair identifies the conflict (often through observation or private report).
2. Board-Chair speaks with each party individually to understand their perspective.
3. Board-Chair facilitates a direct conversation between the parties:
   - Each person states their experience using I-language.
   - Each person states what they need from the other.
   - Both agree on specific behavioral changes.
4. Board-Chair follows up at 30 days to check whether the changes are working.

**Timeframe:** Initiated within 1 week of identification. Follow-up at 30 days.

### Type E: Board-Management Conflict
Disagreement between the board and the CEO or executive team about strategy, performance, or direction.

**Resolution path:**
1. Board-Chair clarifies the nature of the disagreement: is the board advising or directing?
2. For advisory matters: the board states its view clearly, the CEO makes the decision, and the board records its recommendation.
3. For matters requiring board approval: standard decision process applies, including dissent-and-commit if needed.
4. For performance concerns: Board-Chair delivers the concern privately to the CEO with specific evidence and a path forward.
5. If unresolved: the board convenes without management present to align on its position, then the Board-Chair communicates the unified board view to the CEO.

**Timeframe:** Depends on severity. Advisory disagreements resolved in the meeting. Performance concerns follow a 30-60-90 day improvement cycle.

## Escalation Within Conflict

If two parties cannot resolve a conflict through the standard path:

1. **First escalation:** Board-Chair mediates directly.
2. **Second escalation:** Culture-Guardian facilitates a structured conversation.
3. **Third escalation:** Board convenes to address the conflict as a group.
4. **Final resolution:** Board-Chair makes a binding decision.

## Documentation

All material conflicts are documented:
- Nature of the conflict.
- Resolution path taken.
- Outcome (resolved, ongoing, escalated).
- Follow-up actions and dates.

Documentation is maintained by Governance-Ops in the confidential governance file.

## Prevention

The best conflict resolution is conflict prevention:
- Clear decision rights (see `docs/decision-policy.md`).
- Explicit dissent protocol (see `voice/calibration/dissent-and-commit.md`).
- Regular 1:1 relationship maintenance by the Board-Chair.
- Annual effectiveness review surfaces simmering tensions.
- Feedback language training (see `voice/language-guides/feedback-language.md`).
