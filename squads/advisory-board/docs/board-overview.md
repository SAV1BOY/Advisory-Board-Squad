# Board Overview

## Purpose

The Advisory Board exists to improve the quality of strategic decisions. It provides independent counsel, challenges assumptions, manages risk awareness, and holds leadership accountable for outcomes. It does not manage — it advises, questions, and governs.

## How the Board Adds Value

The board adds value in five ways:

1. **Strategic challenge.** Tests strategies against evidence, alternative scenarios, and contrarian perspectives. Ensures the company is making choices, not drifting.

2. **Decision quality.** Improves decisions through structured frameworks, devil's advocacy, and explicit documentation of rationale and dissent.

3. **Risk awareness.** Maintains a living risk register, conducts stress tests, and ensures material risks are identified, assessed, and mitigated.

4. **Accountability.** Tracks commitments, monitors execution, and holds the CEO and executive team accountable for follow-through.

5. **Network and pattern recognition.** Brings cross-industry experience, pattern recognition from similar situations, and connections that accelerate outcomes.

## Board Composition

### Core Agents

| Agent | Primary Function | Key Output |
|---|---|---|
| Board-Chair | Facilitates the board, drives agenda, ensures follow-through | Agendas, meeting summaries, follow-through scorecards |
| Strategy-Analyst | Analyzes data, builds models, produces strategic briefs | Pre-read memos, financial models, diagnostic reports |
| Devil's-Advocate | Challenges recommendations, argues the counter-case | Counter-arguments, pre-mortems, stress tests |
| Risk-Analyst | Assesses and monitors risks across all domains | Risk register, stress test results, risk briefs |
| Culture-Guardian | Evaluates values alignment, organizational health | Culture audit reports, values-alignment assessments |
| Governance-Ops | Manages logistics, templates, tracking, archiving | Meeting logistics, document management, compliance tracking |
| Execution-Tracker | Monitors action items and initiative progress | Action tracker, follow-through scorecards, status dashboards |
| Decision-Tracker | Logs decisions with rationale, dissent, and review dates | Decision log, decision quality reviews |

### Agent Interaction Model

Agents operate in a hub-and-spoke model with the Board-Chair at the center:

- **Board-Chair** coordinates all agents and sets the agenda.
- **Strategy-Analyst** and **Risk-Analyst** provide the analytical foundation.
- **Devil's-Advocate** stress-tests outputs from all analytical agents.
- **Culture-Guardian** provides the values lens across all activities.
- **Governance-Ops**, **Execution-Tracker**, and **Decision-Tracker** provide operational infrastructure.

No agent operates in isolation. Every workflow specifies which agents are involved and how they interact.

## Operating Model

### Meeting Cadence

| Meeting Type | Frequency | Duration | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quarterly board session | Every 3 months | 90-120 min | Strategy, decisions, risk review |
| Monthly chair check-in | Monthly | 60 min | Action tracking, emerging issues |
| Ad-hoc crisis call | As needed | 60 min | Crisis response |
| Annual planning session | Annually | Half-day | Strategic planning, OKRs |
| Annual effectiveness review | Annually | 90 min | Board self-assessment |

### Decision-Making Process

1. Proposals come to the board as structured pre-reads (2-page max).
2. Devil's-Advocate prepares counter-arguments for each recommendation.
3. The board discusses with explicit facilitation from the Chair.
4. Decisions are formally recorded: decision, rationale, dissent, owner, review date.
5. Execution-Tracker monitors follow-through.
6. Decision quality is reviewed quarterly (lightweight) and annually (deep dive).

### Communication Standards

All board communication follows the voice guidelines in `voice/`:
- **Tone:** Direct, evidence-based, caring. (See `voice/tone-profiles/`.)
- **Written format:** Bottom-line first, data-driven, scannable. (See `voice/language-guides/memo-writing-style.md`.)
- **Channels:** Board email, executive summaries, internal memos, stakeholder updates, crisis statements. (See `voice/channels/`.)

## What the Board Does Not Do

- **Does not manage.** The board advises; the executive team executes. The line is at strategy and oversight, not operations.
- **Does not rubber-stamp.** If the board always agrees with management, it is not doing its job.
- **Does not surprise.** Material concerns are raised directly, not saved for board meetings.
- **Does not micromanage.** The board specifies intent and success criteria, not methods.
- **Does not act on stale information.** Pre-reads are mandatory, data must be current, and outdated assumptions must be flagged.
