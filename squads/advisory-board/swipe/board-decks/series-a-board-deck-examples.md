# Series A Board Deck Examples

## Overview
Series A board decks reflect a company that has found product-market fit and is now scaling. Decks should demonstrate repeatable go-to-market, improving unit economics, and clear strategic priorities. More structured than seed, but still focused on velocity and learning.

## Recommended Structure (15-20 slides)

### Slide 1: Executive Summary
- State of the business in 5 bullet points
- Key wins since last board meeting
- Key challenges and risks
- Specific decisions needed from the board

### Slides 2-3: KPI Dashboard

### Slide 4: Revenue Deep-Dive
- Revenue by segment, product, geography
- Pipeline analysis: coverage ratio, conversion rates, deal velocity
- Expansion revenue vs. new business mix

### Slides 5-6: Product Update
- Key features shipped and their impact on engagement/retention
- Roadmap for next quarter with strategic rationale
- Technical debt assessment: is the architecture scaling?

### Slides 7-8: Go-to-Market
- Channel performance comparison
- Sales team performance: ramp time, quota attainment, pipeline per rep
- Marketing: CAC by channel, content performance, brand awareness indicators

### Slide 9: Team and Organization
- Headcount plan vs. actual
- Key hires and open positions
- Employee engagement pulse (NPS, retention, sentiment)
- Organizational structure updates

### Slides 10-11: Financial Review
- P&L: actual vs. plan with variance analysis
- Cash flow statement: operating, investing, financing
- Balance sheet: cash position, debt, runway
- Forecast update: any changes to annual plan?

### Slide 12: Competitive Landscape
- Key competitive moves since last meeting
- Positioning assessment: where are we winning and losing?
- Moat assessment: is our competitive advantage widening or narrowing?

### Slide 13: Risk Register
| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation | Owner |
|------|------------|--------|------------|-------|
| | | | | |

### Slide 14: Strategic Priorities
- Top 3 priorities for next quarter
- What we're explicitly NOT doing (and why)
- Resources allocated to each priority

### Slide 15: Board Asks
- Specific decisions requiring board approval
- Introductions or connections needed
- Areas where board input would be most valuable

## Board Application
- Series A boards should spend 60% of time on strategy and 40% on operations (not the reverse)
- Challenge management on unit economics trajectory, not just growth rate
- Ensure the company is building scalable processes, not just adding more people
