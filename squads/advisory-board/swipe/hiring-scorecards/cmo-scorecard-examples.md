# CMO Scorecard Examples

## CMO Evaluation Dimensions

### Growth and Demand Generation (Weight: 25%)
- Builds scalable, measurable demand generation engine across channels
- Achieves customer acquisition targets within CAC guardrails
- Diversifies acquisition channels to reduce dependency on any single source
- Optimizes full-funnel conversion from awareness through activation
- **Rating scale:** 1 (no repeatable acquisition engine) to 5 (predictable, scalable growth machine with multi-channel diversification)

### Brand and Positioning (Weight: 20%)
- Articulates clear brand positioning that differentiates from competitors
- Builds brand awareness and preference in target segments
- Ensures brand consistency across all touchpoints (product, marketing, sales, support)
- Develops brand that commands pricing power and reduces CAC over time
- **Rating scale:** 1 (undifferentiated brand, no recognition) to 5 (category-defining brand that is a strategic asset)

### Marketing-Sales Alignment (Weight: 20%)
- Delivers qualified pipeline that sales can convert at acceptable rates
- Establishes shared definitions (MQL, SQL, opportunity) with sales leadership
- Creates feedback loops: sales insights inform marketing targeting, content, and messaging
- Owns joint pipeline metrics and holds both teams accountable
- **Rating scale:** 1 (marketing and sales blame each other) to 5 (integrated revenue team with shared metrics and mutual trust)

### Data and Analytics (Weight: 15%)
- Builds attribution models that connect marketing spend to revenue outcomes
- Makes resource allocation decisions based on data, not intuition
- Conducts rigorous experimentation (A/B testing, holdout groups, incrementality studies)
- Reports marketing ROI in language the board and CFO understand
- **Rating scale:** 1 (marketing spend is a black box) to 5 (fully attributed, data-driven marketing organization)

### Product Marketing and Go-to-Market (Weight: 10%)
- Translates product capabilities into customer value propositions
- Launches new products and features with measurable adoption targets
- Develops competitive intelligence and equips sales with battle cards and positioning
- Aligns messaging to buyer personas with evidence of resonance
- **Rating scale:** 1 (product launches fall flat, no competitive positioning) to 5 (GTM launches consistently exceed adoption targets)

### Team Building and Culture (Weight: 10%)
- Recruits and retains top marketing talent across specializations
- Builds a balanced team: creative and analytical, brand and performance, strategic and tactical
- Develops marketing leaders who can own channels and functions independently
- Creates a culture of experimentation, accountability, and continuous learning
- **Rating scale:** 1 (high attrition, skill gaps) to 5 (best-in-class marketing team that is a recruiting magnet)

## Stage-Specific CMO Criteria

### Early-Stage CMO (Pre-Series B)
- Willingness to execute, not just strategize — the CMO is often the first marketer
- Strong product marketing instincts: can they find and articulate product-market fit?
- Scrappiness and capital efficiency: generate awareness on a limited budget
- Founder-level passion for the product and customer problem

### Growth-Stage CMO (Series B-D)
- Scaling acquisition channels while maintaining or improving unit economics
- Building the marketing team from generalists to specialists
- Developing the brand as a strategic asset, not just a logo and color palette
- International and multi-segment expansion of marketing programs

### Enterprise / Late-Stage CMO
- Executive presence and thought leadership at industry events and with analysts
- Sophisticated demand generation with account-based marketing capabilities
- Brand management at scale with crisis communication readiness
- Marketing operations and technology stack maturity (MAP, CRM, analytics)

## Common CMO Hiring Mistakes

| Mistake | Consequence |
|---------|------------|
| Hiring a brand CMO when you need a growth CMO (or vice versa) | Misaligned skills produce neither brand nor growth |
| Hiring too senior too early | Enterprise CMOs struggle without infrastructure and team |
| Expecting the CMO to fix a product problem | No amount of marketing rescues weak product-market fit |
| Not defining the CMO-CRO boundary | Territory conflicts undermine both functions |
| Hiring for channel expertise that is already commoditized | Channels shift; hire for strategic thinking and adaptability |

## Board Application
- Require the CMO to present customer acquisition cost trends, channel diversification, and marketing ROI at least quarterly
- Evaluate whether the company needs a CMO or a VP of Growth — these are different roles with different skill sets
- Ask: "If we cut marketing spend by 50%, what would happen to revenue in 6 months?" The answer reveals how much growth is organic vs. purchased
- Challenge any marketing plan that concentrates more than 40% of spend in a single channel
- Monitor the CAC-to-LTV ratio as the primary marketing health metric
