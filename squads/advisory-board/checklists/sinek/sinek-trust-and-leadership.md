# Sinek Trust and Leadership Checklist

## Purpose
Evaluates whether leaders build trust through consistent behavior rather than demanding it through authority. Sinek argues that trust is not a quality; it is a feeling of safety that emerges when people believe their leaders have their best interests at heart. This checklist assesses whether the organization's leadership creates the conditions for trust or erodes it through inconsistency, self-interest, or opacity.

## When to Use
- During leadership effectiveness reviews and 360 feedback processes.
- When trust indicators (engagement, retention, candor) are declining.
- When evaluating candidates for leadership positions.
- After a trust breach to assess what needs to change.
- Annually as part of organizational health assessment.

## Gate Criteria

### Safety and Belonging
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Employees report feeling psychologically safe to take risks and speak up (measured via survey).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders protect their teams from external pressures rather than passing them through.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: People are treated as whole humans, not just resources for output.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization invests in relationships, not just transactions.

### Consistency and Integrity
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders' actions match their words over time (walk-the-talk assessment).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Promises made are tracked and kept; broken promises are acknowledged and explained.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders apply standards consistently across people and situations, without favoritism.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders admit mistakes openly rather than hiding or reframing them.

### Servant Leadership Indicators
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders prioritize team success over personal recognition.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders invest in developing others, not just driving output.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders remove obstacles for their teams rather than creating additional ones.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders make personal sacrifices when necessary for the team's wellbeing.

### Trust Measurement
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Trust is measured through behavioral indicators (information sharing, risk-taking, collaboration), not just self-reporting.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Trust trends are tracked over time and correlated with organizational performance.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Low-trust relationships or teams are identified and intervention plans exist.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Trust-building is recognized and rewarded as a leadership competency.

## Red Flags
- Leaders demand trust based on their position rather than earning it through behavior.
- Employees sanitize information before it reaches leadership, indicating lack of safety.
- High performers leave citing management rather than compensation or role.
- Leaders take credit for successes but blame the team for failures.
- Policies and rules multiply because trust is low and control is substituted.
- There is a visible gap between how leaders treat senior and junior team members.

## Escalation Triggers
- Employee trust scores drop significantly in any team or at any level.
- A credible allegation of a leader betraying team trust surfaces.
- Multiple resignations from a single team cite leadership trust issues.
- Internal information flow stalls because teams do not trust how information will be used.
- The organization's reputation as an employer deteriorates in external channels.
