# International Expansion Memos — Swipe File

## Overview
International expansion is among the most capital-intensive and operationally complex strategic moves a company can make. It introduces currency risk, regulatory complexity, cultural adaptation requirements, and management attention dilution. These memos provide frameworks for rigorous expansion analysis and common patterns of success and failure.

## International Expansion Memo Template

### Section 1: Strategic Rationale
- **Why expand internationally?** Market saturation at home, customer demand, competitive pressure, talent access, or diversification?
- **Why this market?** Market size, growth rate, competitive landscape, regulatory environment, cultural fit
- **Why now?** What has changed to make this the right time? New trade agreements, competitor entry, customer pull, funding availability?
- **Opportunity cost:** What domestic investments are we deferring to fund this expansion?

### Section 2: Market Assessment
- **Market sizing:** TAM/SAM/SOM with local methodology (do not simply apply US assumptions to foreign markets)
- **Customer behavior:** How do target customers buy in this market? Direct sales, partners, online, government procurement?
- **Competitive landscape:** Local incumbents, other international entrants, substitutes, and informal market alternatives
- **Regulatory environment:** Data residency, employment law, tax implications, licensing requirements, local content rules

### Section 3: Localization Requirements
- **Product localization:** Language, currency, payment methods, compliance features, local integrations
- **Go-to-market localization:** Messaging adaptation (not just translation), local case studies, reference customers
- **Legal entity:** Subsidiary, branch office, distributor agreement, or employer of record?
- **Cultural adaptation:** Business practices, negotiation styles, relationship expectations, meeting etiquette

### Section 4: Operating Model
- **Team structure:** Local team vs. remote management from HQ vs. hybrid
- **Key hires:** Country manager profile, initial team composition, hiring timeline
- **Partner strategy:** Local distribution partner, systems integrator, reseller, or direct
- **Support model:** Local support team, follow-the-sun from HQ, or partner-delivered support?

### Section 5: Financial Model
- **Investment required:** Year 1 and Year 2 fully loaded costs (people, legal, marketing, product, infrastructure)
- **Revenue projections:** Conservative, base, and optimistic scenarios with assumptions documented
- **Unit economics:** Expected CAC, LTV, and payback period in the new market (typically worse than home market for 12-18 months)
- **Currency risk:** Revenue in local currency vs. costs in multiple currencies; hedging strategy if material
- **Breakeven timeline:** When does the market become self-sustaining? What triggers additional investment?

### Section 6: Risk Analysis and Kill Criteria
- **Top 5 risks:** Regulatory change, slower-than-expected adoption, partner failure, talent scarcity, currency depreciation
- **Mitigation plans:** Specific actions for each risk
- **Kill criteria:** Defined before entry. Example: "If after 18 months we have fewer than 30 paying customers and monthly revenue below $50K, we exit the market and redeploy resources."
- **Exit plan:** How do we wind down? Customer migration, employee obligations, lease terminations, partner notifications

## Example: European Expansion for US SaaS Company

**Rationale:** 30% of inbound leads originate from Europe. Three enterprise customers have requested EU data residency. Two competitors have opened London offices. The window to establish a European beachhead is closing.

**Market selection:** UK first (language, legal system, tech adoption), followed by DACH (Germany, Austria, Switzerland) in Year 2. Nordics as Year 3 opportunity.

**Localization:** EU data residency (Frankfurt AWS region). GDPR compliance (already 80% complete). Pricing in EUR and GBP. Local payment processing (SEPA, direct debit). UK English product localization.

**Operating model:** Hire a UK-based GM with enterprise SaaS experience. Initial team of 5: GM, 2 AEs, 1 CSM, 1 solutions engineer. Remote support from US team for first 6 months.

**Financial model:** Year 1 investment: $1.2M. Expected Year 1 revenue: $400K. Breakeven: Month 20. Kill criteria: fewer than 15 paying customers after 12 months.

## International Expansion Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails |
|-------------|-------------|
| "Copy-paste the US playbook" | Local markets have different buying behaviors, channels, and expectations |
| Sending a US executive to "run" the market part-time | International markets require full-time, local leadership |
| Entering multiple markets simultaneously | Dilutes resources and attention; master one market before expanding |
| Underestimating regulatory complexity | GDPR, local employment law, and tax compliance consume more time and money than planned |
| Treating international as a sales problem | It is a product, operations, legal, and cultural problem. Sales is the last mile. |
| No kill criteria | Without exit criteria, failing markets consume resources indefinitely |

## Board Application
- Require a structured international expansion memo before approving any new market entry
- Challenge the "why now?" and opportunity cost analysis — international expansion often delays domestic growth
- Insist on kill criteria defined before entry, reviewed quarterly, and enforced without sentiment
- Monitor the home market during expansion: if domestic growth slows, question whether management attention is adequately balanced
- Ask: "What have we learned from our first international market before entering the second?"
