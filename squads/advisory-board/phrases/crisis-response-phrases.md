# Crisis Response Phrases

Phrases for the first 72 hours of a crisis — commanding action, communicating under pressure, managing stakeholders, and maintaining composure when stakes are highest.

## Activating the Response

1. **"We have a material crisis. I'm activating the protocol. Assemble the crisis team in 30 minutes."**
   Use to initiate the crisis response with clarity and authority. No ambiguity, no hedging.

2. **"Here is what we know. Here is what we don't know. Here is what we're doing right now."**
   Use as the opening frame for every crisis briefing. This three-part structure creates order in chaos.

3. **"The first priority is containment. What can we do in the next 60 minutes to stop this from getting worse?"**
   Use to focus the team on immediate action, not analysis. Containment before root cause.

4. **"I need facts, not theories. What do we know for certain? What are we speculating about?"**
   Use to discipline the information flow. In a crisis, speculation masquerading as fact is dangerous.

5. **"Who is affected, how many, and what do they need from us right now?"**
   Use to center the response on the people impacted. This question cuts through organizational navel-gazing.

## Making Decisions Under Pressure

6. **"We don't have time for perfect information. Based on what we know, the best move is [X]. We can adjust as we learn more."**
   Use when the team is paralyzed by uncertainty. Decisive action with a commitment to adapt beats waiting for certainty.

7. **"This is a judgment call. I'm making it. The decision is [X]. Here's why. If I'm wrong, we correct at [time]."**
   Use when someone needs to own the decision and no consensus is forming. Leadership is most visible in ambiguity.

8. **"What is the worst thing that can happen in the next 6 hours, and are we prepared for it?"**
   Use to force contingency thinking. Planning for the next wave prevents being surprised by it.

9. **"We're going to make mistakes in this response. That's okay. What's not okay is being slow or being dishonest."**
   Use to set expectations with the crisis team. Speed and honesty over perfection.

10. **"Stop. We're reacting, not thinking. Let me take 5 minutes to outline the next three steps."**
    Use when the team is thrashing — lots of activity but no progress. A short pause to plan saves hours of chaos.

## Internal Communication

11. **"I know you're worried. Here is what I can tell you now. I will update you again at [specific time]."**
    Use when employees need to hear from leadership. Acknowledge the emotion, share what you can, commit to a timeline.

12. **"I don't have the answer to that yet. I'll have it by [time]. What I can tell you is [what you do know]."**
    Use when asked a question you can't answer. Honesty about gaps builds more trust than bluffing.

13. **"Your job right now is [specific task]. Leave the crisis response to the crisis team. I'll keep you informed."**
    Use to prevent organization-wide paralysis. Most people need to keep working, not join the crisis room.

14. **"If you learn anything new about this situation, report it to [specific person] immediately. Do not share it externally."**
    Use to create clear information channels and prevent leaks.

## External Communication

15. **"We are aware of [situation] and are actively responding. We will share more details as we confirm them."**
    Use as the initial holding statement. Short, factual, commits to follow-up.

16. **"We understand the concern this creates, and we take full responsibility for addressing it."**
    Use to establish accountability without premature admission of specific liability.

17. **"Here is what we've done so far: [specific actions]. Here is what we're doing next: [specific actions]. Here is when we'll update you: [specific date/time]."**
    Use in every external update. Action and timeline reduce anxiety.

18. **"I won't speculate on that. When we have confirmed information, we will share it."**
    Use when pressed for information you don't have. Speculation in a crisis becomes headline.

## Managing the Board During Crisis

19. **"Board members: the crisis protocol is active. Here are the facts. I will brief you at [time]. Please hold questions until then."**
    Use for the initial board notification. Provides information without inviting a flood of individual questions.

20. **"I need the board's guidance on [specific question]. The options are [A], [B], [C]. My recommendation is [X]."**
    Use when the crisis requires a board-level decision. Structure the ask; don't present an open-ended problem.

21. **"Board, I need you to activate your networks for [specific purpose]. Here is exactly what we need."**
    Use when the board's connections can help (legal contacts, regulatory relationships, communications experts).

## Maintaining Composure

22. **"We will get through this. Let me tell you why: we have the right people in this room, and we have a process for this."**
    Use to steady the team. Calm leadership is contagious.

23. **"I hear the urgency. And I want us to be fast and deliberate, not fast and reckless."**
    Use when speed is creating sloppiness. Precision under pressure is the goal.

24. **"When this is over, we'll do a thorough retrospective. Right now, focus on the next 12 hours."**
    Use to defer blame-seeking and post-mortem discussions that distract from the response.

25. **"Everyone take a breath. Eat something. Check on your families. We need you sharp, not exhausted."**
    Use during extended crisis responses. Burned-out responders make bad decisions.
