# Chouinard 1% for the Planet

## Purpose

Yvon Chouinard co-founded "1% for the Planet," a commitment to donate 1% of total sales (not profit) to environmental causes. This framework explores the principle that businesses should build giving into their operating model from day one — not as charity but as a cost of doing business on the planet. For advisory boards, this framework provides a model for structured, systematic corporate giving that aligns with mission and creates stakeholder value.

## When to Use

- When designing corporate social responsibility programs
- When a company wants to formalize giving without making it dependent on profitability
- When building brand trust through authentic environmental or social commitment
- When evaluating impact investment or B-Corp structures
- When advising on stakeholder capitalism principles

## Core Model

### The 1% Principle
- 1% of total sales (not profit) goes to environmental organizations
- From sales, not profit, because: companies can manipulate profit figures; sales-based giving is simple, transparent, and consistent
- This creates a permanent, non-negotiable commitment that survives downturns
- Chouinard's framing: "The Earth is our most important stakeholder. 1% is the rent we pay for using the planet."

### Why Sales-Based Giving Is Superior
- **Consistent:** Happens in good years and bad, unlike profit-based giving
- **Simple:** No complex calculations or loopholes
- **Transparent:** Easily verifiable by stakeholders
- **Structural:** Built into the P&L, not discretionary
- **Motivating:** Gives every employee a tangible connection to impact

### The Business Case for 1%
- Customer loyalty increases (customers prefer to buy from companies that give back)
- Employee engagement increases (people want to work for companies with purpose)
- Brand differentiation (in commodity markets, mission is the differentiator)
- Community goodwill (local environmental investments create local brand advocacy)

## Steps

1. **Commit.** Decide on a percentage of sales to dedicate. 1% is the standard; some companies do more.
2. **Choose recipients.** Select organizations aligned with your mission. Verify their impact and efficiency.
3. **Formalize the commitment.** Build it into the operating budget, not the discretionary budget. It is a cost of doing business.
4. **Involve employees.** Let teams choose recipients, volunteer, and see the impact directly.
5. **Report transparently.** Publish annual giving reports. Show what was given, to whom, and what impact resulted.
6. **Never pause during downturns.** The commitment is permanent. Consistency during hard times builds the most trust.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Help portfolio companies build giving into their models early. It is easier to start at founding than to add later.
- Frame giving as brand investment, not expense. The ROI on authentic giving exceeds most marketing spend.
- Use the 1% model as a benchmark for corporate responsibility commitments.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Profit-based giving.** Too easily manipulated and suspended. Sales-based is more honest.
2. **Pausing during downturns.** This destroys credibility built during good times.
3. **Giving without strategy.** Random donations have less impact than focused giving aligned with mission.
4. **Performative giving.** Giving for PR without genuine commitment. Stakeholders see through it.
