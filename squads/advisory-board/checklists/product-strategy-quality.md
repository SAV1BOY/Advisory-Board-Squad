# Product Strategy Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures that product strategy is grounded in customer needs, aligned with business strategy, and structured to create sustainable competitive advantage. A product strategy is not a feature roadmap; it is a set of choices about which problems to solve, for whom, and why the company is uniquely positioned to solve them. This checklist prevents product teams from building things nobody asked for or that do not create business value.

## When to Use
- During annual and quarterly product planning.
- When proposing a new product line or major product bet.
- When evaluating product-market fit for an existing product.
- Before significant engineering investment in a new direction.
- When product metrics suggest stagnation or decline.

## Gate Criteria

### Customer and Market
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Target customer segments are defined with specificity (not "everyone" or "SMBs").
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer problems are validated through direct research (interviews, data), not assumed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The urgency and frequency of the problem are assessed (vitamins versus painkillers).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Market size is estimated using bottom-up methodology.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer willingness to pay is validated, not assumed.

### Differentiation and Positioning
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The product's unique value proposition is articulated in one sentence a customer would understand.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Competitive alternatives (including doing nothing) are mapped and the differentiation is defensible.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The differentiation is based on structural advantages, not just features that can be copied.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Positioning is tested with actual customers or prospects.

### Business Model Alignment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Revenue model is defined and unit economics are modeled (CAC, LTV, margins).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Product strategy aligns with company strategy on where-to-play and how-to-win.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Cross-product interactions (cannibalization, complementarity) are assessed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The product contributes to the company's moat, not just to revenue.

### Execution Feasibility
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Technical feasibility is assessed by engineering leadership with estimated effort.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Dependencies on other teams, partners, or technologies are identified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The 90-day plan includes specific deliverables, not just objectives.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Success metrics are defined before building starts, including leading indicators.

### Risk and Learning
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Key assumptions are ranked by impact and uncertainty; the riskiest are tested first.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The plan includes explicit learning milestones where direction can be adjusted.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Kill criteria are defined: what evidence would cause us to stop investing.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The cost of being wrong is quantified and acceptable.

## Red Flags
- Product strategy is a feature list with no articulation of the problem being solved.
- Customer research consists of opinions from internal stakeholders, not actual customers.
- The product strategy changes every quarter without evidence justifying the change.
- Engineering is building features no customer has requested.
- Product metrics are vanity metrics (downloads, signups) rather than value metrics (retention, NPS, revenue).
- No kill criteria exist; zombie products persist indefinitely.

## Escalation Triggers
- Product-market fit evidence deteriorates for two consecutive quarters.
- Engineering investment exceeds the approved budget without corresponding milestone achievement.
- Customer churn increases in a segment where the product was assumed to have strong fit.
- A competitor launches a superior alternative and the product team lacks a credible response.
- The product team and sales team have fundamentally different understandings of the target customer.
