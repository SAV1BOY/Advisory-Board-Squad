# Hoffman Alliance Strategy Check Checklist

## Purpose
Evaluates whether the organization is building and managing strategic alliances effectively. Hoffman views alliances as force multipliers that allow companies to achieve objectives beyond their individual capability. This checklist ensures alliances are formed for genuine strategic value, managed with appropriate governance, and assessed honestly for their contribution versus their cost.

## When to Use
- When evaluating potential strategic partnerships or alliances.
- During annual reviews of existing alliance performance.
- When an alliance relationship shows signs of strain or underperformance.
- When the competitive landscape shifts and alliance strategy needs to adapt.

## Gate Criteria

### Strategic Rationale
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each alliance addresses a specific strategic gap that cannot be filled internally within the required timeframe.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The combined value of the alliance exceeds what either party could achieve independently.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The alliance aligns with the company's long-term strategy, not just a short-term opportunity.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Alternative approaches (build, buy, different partner) were evaluated.

### Partner Fitness
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The partner's strategic direction is compatible with the alliance objectives.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The partner's culture and operating style have been assessed for compatibility.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The partner's financial stability and commitment to the alliance are verified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The partner's reputation and ethical standards are consistent with the company's values.

### Governance and Operations
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A governance structure with clear roles, decision rights, and escalation paths is documented.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Joint objectives and metrics are defined and reviewed on a regular cadence.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Communication protocols between alliance partners are established and functioning.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Conflict resolution mechanisms are defined before conflicts arise.

### Value Assessment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The alliance's contribution to company objectives is measured quantitatively.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The total cost of the alliance (financial, management attention, opportunity cost) is tracked.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The value exchange between partners is balanced and perceived as fair by both sides.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Alliances that are underperforming are restructured or terminated.

## Red Flags
- Alliances are formed based on personal relationships without strategic justification.
- The company has more alliances than it can actively manage.
- Alliance governance meetings are skipped or canceled regularly.
- One partner is extracting disproportionate value from the alliance.
- The alliance creates competitive intelligence risks that are not managed.
- The company cannot quantify the value any of its alliances provide.

## Escalation Triggers
- An alliance partner is acquired by a competitor.
- An alliance fails to deliver on committed objectives for two consecutive review periods.
- A conflict between alliance partners escalates beyond the defined governance process.
- The alliance creates reputational risk due to the partner's actions.
- The company's strategic direction diverges from the alliance's original premise.
