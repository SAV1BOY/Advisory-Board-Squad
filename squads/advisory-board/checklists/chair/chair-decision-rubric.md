# Chair Decision Rubric Checklist

## Purpose
Provides the advisory board chair with a framework for routing decisions through the appropriate process based on their characteristics: urgency, reversibility, materiality, and complexity. Not all decisions deserve the same process. This rubric ensures that the chair allocates governance attention proportionally to decision significance.

## When to Use
- When a decision request is received to determine the appropriate process.
- When the board's time is being consumed by decisions that do not warrant full board deliberation.
- When high-stakes decisions are not receiving adequate board attention.
- When establishing decision-making norms for a new or restructured board.

## Gate Criteria

### Decision Classification
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each decision is classified by materiality (high, medium, low) using defined thresholds.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each decision is classified by reversibility (one-way door, two-way door).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each decision is classified by urgency (immediate, this quarter, no deadline).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each decision is classified by complexity (straightforward, moderate, highly complex).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The classification determines the decision process, not the other way around.

### Process Assignment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: High-materiality, irreversible decisions receive full board deliberation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Low-materiality, reversible decisions are delegated to management with reporting.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Urgent decisions have a fast-track process that does not skip essential diligence.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Complex decisions are assigned adequate preparation time and expert input.

### Quality Standards by Category
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decision memos match the rigor expected for the decision's classification.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Discussion time allocated matches the decision's significance.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The number of approvers matches the decision's materiality and reversibility.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Post-decision review depth matches the decision's importance.

### Chair Facilitation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The chair ensures balanced participation in high-stakes discussions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The chair prevents low-stakes items from consuming high-stakes meeting time.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The chair ensures dissenting views are heard before decisions are finalized.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The chair confirms the decision and next steps before moving to the next agenda item.

## Red Flags
- Every decision receives the same process regardless of significance.
- The board deliberates on decisions that management should handle independently.
- Material, irreversible decisions are rushed through without adequate analysis.
- The chair does not manage discussion time, allowing trivial topics to crowd out important ones.
- No classification framework exists; decisions are routed based on who brings them.
- Dissent is not surfaced before votes are taken.

## Escalation Triggers
- A decision that should have received board deliberation was made without it.
- A decision process is challenged by a board member as inappropriate for the decision's significance.
- The board is consistently spending more than 30% of meeting time on low-materiality items.
- A material decision is reversed shortly after being made, suggesting inadequate process.
- The chair is unable to maintain discussion discipline, and meetings consistently run over time.
