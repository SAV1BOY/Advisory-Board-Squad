# Hindsight Bias in Post-Mortems

## Overview
Hindsight bias ("I knew it all along") distorts our ability to learn from both successes and failures. After an outcome is known, people overestimate their ability to have predicted it. In post-mortems, this bias prevents honest assessment of decision quality and leads to false lessons.

## How It Distorts Learning

### After Failures
- "We should have known this would fail" — But the evidence at the time was genuinely ambiguous
- Blame is assigned to individuals for not seeing what only became visible in hindsight
- The real lesson (the decision process was flawed, or the outcome was genuinely unforeseeable) is obscured

### After Successes
- "Our strategy was brilliant" — But luck, timing, or competitor mistakes may have been the primary driver
- Credit is assigned to the decision-maker's foresight rather than to favorable circumstances
- False confidence in the decision-making process leads to overconfidence in future decisions

## Countermeasures

1. **Document reasoning contemporaneously** — Write down why you're making a decision BEFORE the outcome is known. Review the reasoning, not just the outcome.
2. **Evaluate decisions on process, not just outcome** — A good decision with a bad outcome is still a good decision. A bad decision with a good outcome is still a bad decision.
3. **Pre-mortem before, post-mortem after** — The pre-mortem captures uncertainty. The post-mortem can reference it.
4. **Multiple attribution analysis** — In post-mortems, list all possible causes (including luck and external factors) before assigning credit or blame.
5. **Outcome-blind review** — Have someone evaluate the decision without knowing the outcome. Would they have approved it?

## Post-Mortem Template (Hindsight-Aware)

1. **What was the decision and when was it made?**
2. **What information was available at the time?** (Not what we know now—what we knew then)
3. **What was the reasoning?** (Reference contemporaneous documentation)
4. **What was the outcome?**
5. **Was the outcome primarily driven by:** (a) Decision quality, (b) Execution quality, (c) External factors, (d) Luck
6. **What would we do differently in the PROCESS?** (Not "what would we decide differently knowing the outcome")
7. **What genuine learning can we extract?**

## Board Application
- Adopt the hindsight-aware post-mortem template for all major initiative reviews
- Maintain a decision journal documenting the reasoning at the time of decision
- Evaluate management on decision process quality, not just outcome quality
- When reviewing failures, distinguish between bad decisions and bad luck
