# Status Quo Bias in Strategy

## Overview
Status quo bias is the preference for the current state of affairs. Any change from the baseline is perceived as a loss, triggering loss aversion. In strategic contexts, this bias causes organizations to continue with failing strategies, resist necessary pivots, and underinvest in transformation.

## Strategic Manifestations

### "It's Working" Inertia
- Revenue is growing, so the strategy must be right
- **Reality:** Growth may be masking deteriorating fundamentals (declining retention, rising CAC, narrowing moats)
- **Test:** Would we choose this strategy if starting from scratch today?

### Change Resistance
- Proposals for new markets, new products, or new business models face a higher burden of proof than maintaining the status quo
- **Reality:** The status quo has risks too—they're just less visible because they're familiar

### Incremental Optimization Trap
- Optimizing the existing strategy 5% per year feels safe but may miss 10x opportunities that require fundamental change
- **Reality:** Kodak optimized film processing right up until digital photography made it irrelevant

### Benchmark Against Current Performance
- New initiatives are compared against current (known) performance rather than against the full range of possibilities
- **Reality:** The right comparison is the opportunity cost of NOT pursuing the new initiative

## Countermeasures

1. **Zero-based strategy** — Periodically rebuild the strategy from scratch. Don't start from "what are we doing today?"
2. **The newspaper test** — "If we read about a competitor doing this while we stood still, would we feel foolish?"
3. **Inversion** — "What would a new CEO with no attachment to our history do?"
4. **Time-shifted perspective** — "Looking back from 5 years in the future, what will we wish we had changed today?"
5. **External pattern matching** — Study companies in similar positions that failed to change vs. those that transformed

## Board Application
- Schedule an annual "zero-based strategy" session where the current strategy must be rejustified
- When the board rejects a change proposal, explicitly check whether status quo bias is influencing the decision
- Ask: "What are the risks of NOT changing?" alongside "What are the risks of changing?"
- Track industry disruption patterns and compare to the company's current trajectory
