# Research on Exit Strategies and Timing

## Overview

Exit timing and strategy selection are among the highest-stakes decisions a founder or board will make. This research synthesizes patterns from successful and failed exits across acquisition, IPO, and secondary sale pathways.

## Exit Pathway Comparison

### Strategic Acquisition
**Best when:**
- Acquirer has distribution, brand, or technology that multiplies the target's value
- Founders are ready for a transition and willing to accept earnout structures
- Market window for premium valuations may be closing
- Company lacks the scale to compete independently long-term

**Typical timeline:** 6-12 months from first serious conversation to close
**Valuation range:** 3-15x revenue for SaaS; 8-20x EBITDA for profitable businesses
**Founder outcome:** Cash + stock + earnout; typically 2-3 year retention commitment

### IPO
**Best when:**
- Company has $100M+ revenue with predictable growth
- Business can withstand quarterly earnings scrutiny
- Public market comparable companies trade at premium multiples
- Leadership team has the depth to manage public company obligations

**Typical timeline:** 12-18 months of preparation; 3-6 month execution window
**Considerations:** SOX compliance, auditor requirements, investor relations infrastructure
**Founder outcome:** Liquidity through secondary sales over time; ongoing operational commitment

### Secondary Sale / Private Equity Buyout
**Best when:**
- Founders want partial liquidity without full exit
- Company is profitable but growing moderately (15-30% annually)
- PE firm can add operational value through professionalization
- Public markets are unreceptive or company is below IPO scale

**Typical timeline:** 4-8 months from LOI to close
**Valuation range:** 10-25x EBITDA for quality SaaS; lower for non-recurring revenue
**Founder outcome:** Partial cash-out; "second bite of the apple" through rolled equity

## Timing Patterns and Research

### The "Too Early" vs "Too Late" Problem
Research across 1,200 venture-backed exits (PitchBook 2020-2024):
- Companies that exited within 12 months of peak growth rate achieved median 2.3x higher multiples than those that waited until growth decelerated
- 68% of founders who rejected acquisition offers later exited at lower valuations
- The optimal exit window is typically 6-18 months before the market recognizes a growth slowdown

### Market Cycle Sensitivity
- IPO windows open and close unpredictably; preparation should be continuous
- Strategic acquisition premiums peak when the acquirer's stock is at highs (they can pay with expensive currency)
- PE buyout activity is counter-cyclical; PE firms deploy most aggressively when public markets contract

### Signal-Based Timing Framework
**Consider exiting when:**
- Customer acquisition costs are rising faster than lifetime value
- Key competitive advantages are being commoditized
- Market consolidation is accelerating (be the consolidator or get consolidated)
- Regulatory changes threaten the business model
- The founding team's energy and conviction are declining

**Consider waiting when:**
- Unit economics are improving quarter over quarter
- Network effects or switching costs are strengthening
- A large adjacent market is opening that the company is uniquely positioned to capture
- The competitive moat is widening, not narrowing

## Acquisition Premium Drivers

Factors that increase acquisition multiples (ranked by impact):
1. **Strategic scarcity** — Few alternatives for the acquirer to achieve the same objective
2. **Revenue quality** — Recurring, contractual, high-retention revenue
3. **Competitive tension** — Multiple credible bidders in the process
4. **Growth trajectory** — Accelerating growth commands exponential premium
5. **Integration simplicity** — Clean technology stack, standard contracts, minimal regulatory complexity
6. **Team retention confidence** — Acquirer believes key talent will stay

## Common Exit Mistakes

1. **Anchoring to a number** — Founders fixate on a valuation rather than evaluating the full package
2. **Running a fake process** — Using acquisition interest as a negotiating chip without genuine intent to sell
3. **Ignoring tax planning** — QSBS eligibility, long-term capital gains timing, and entity structure matter enormously
4. **Insufficient preparation** — Data rooms, financial audits, and IP clean-up should precede any process by 6+ months
5. **Emotional decision-making** — Confusing personal identity with company ownership leads to suboptimal timing
6. **Neglecting the team** — Employees learn about an exit and disengage if not properly communicated with and incentivized

## Advisory Framework for Exit Conversations

1. Begin discussing exit philosophy in Year 1 — not as a plan, but as a framework
2. Maintain a rolling "state of readiness" assessment updated quarterly
3. Build relationships with potential acquirers years before any transaction
4. Ensure the cap table is clean, IP assignments are complete, and financials are auditable
5. When the window opens, move decisively — hesitation in exit execution destroys value
