# Incentives Questions

Phrases for uncovering the incentive structures driving behavior — inside the company, among partners, and in the market. "Show me the incentive and I will show you the outcome."

## Internal Incentive Alignment

1. **"What is this person incentivized to do, and is that what we want them to do?"**
   Use when reviewing executive compensation, team structures, or individual performance. Misaligned incentives produce perfectly predictable bad outcomes.

2. **"If we pay for X, why are we surprised when people optimize for X instead of Y?"**
   Use when behavior diverges from stated goals. The incentive structure is always the first diagnostic to check.

3. **"What happens to someone who fails at this initiative? If the answer is 'nothing different,' we don't actually want it to succeed."**
   Use to test whether accountability is real or theatrical. Initiatives without consequences for failure are wishes, not priorities.

4. **"Who gets promoted here, and what does that tell us about what we actually value?"**
   Use during culture audits. Promotion criteria are the loudest signal of real values — louder than any values statement on the wall.

5. **"If we changed the compensation structure to [X], how would behavior change tomorrow?"**
   Use to model the impact of proposed incentive changes before implementing them. Run the thought experiment before running the experiment.

## Organizational Incentives

6. **"Which team has an incentive to make this project fail?"**
   Use when a cross-functional initiative is stalling. Not every blocker is a resource issue — some are incentive issues.

7. **"Does this structure create incentives for cooperation or competition between these teams?"**
   Use when designing org structure or cross-team processes. The answer is often "competition" when "cooperation" is what's needed.

8. **"What behavior are we accidentally rewarding?"**
   Use to surface unintended consequences of existing policies. People are rational actors — they respond to the actual incentives, not the intended ones.

9. **"If we measure [metric], what will people optimize for at the expense of everything else?"**
   Use when choosing KPIs. Every metric creates a distortion. The question is whether the distortion is acceptable.

10. **"Is this person giving us their honest assessment, or the assessment that benefits them?"**
    Use when evaluating recommendations from internal advocates. A VP requesting $5M for their team is not a disinterested analyst.

## Market and Partner Incentives

11. **"What is our partner's incentive in this deal, and how does it differ from ours?"**
    Use during partnership evaluation. Partners who are not incentive-aligned will eventually act on their own incentives, not yours.

12. **"Who is paying this advisor, and how does that affect their advice?"**
    Use when evaluating external counsel, bankers, or consultants. Fee structures create predictable biases.

13. **"Why is the seller selling? What do they know that we don't?"**
    Use during M&A evaluation. A willing seller is information. The question is what information.

14. **"If we are the customer, what is our vendor incentivized to do after we sign the contract?"**
    Use when evaluating vendor relationships. Pre-sale and post-sale incentives are often completely different.

15. **"What would happen if we made our interests fully transparent to the other side?"**
    Use to test whether a negotiation strategy depends on information asymmetry. Strategies that require deception are fragile.

## Incentive Design

16. **"Can this incentive be gamed? If so, it will be."**
    Use when designing any incentive structure. The first question is always: how will a smart, self-interested person exploit this?

17. **"Does this incentive reward outcomes or effort?"**
    Use to calibrate whether the incentive drives the right behavior. Outcome-based incentives create accountability but can also create reckless risk-taking. Effort-based incentives reduce risk but can also reduce ambition.

18. **"What is the time horizon of this incentive? Does it match the time horizon of the outcome we want?"**
    Use to check for temporal misalignment. Annual bonuses incentivize annual thinking. If you want long-term behavior, you need long-term incentives.

19. **"If we applied the Golden Rule of incentives — would we accept this incentive structure if we were on the other side?"**
    Use to test fairness and durability. Incentive structures that one side would reject if roles were reversed are unstable.

20. **"What is the cheapest version of this incentive that produces the same behavior?"**
    Use to avoid over-spending on incentives. Sometimes recognition, autonomy, or purpose is more effective than money — and cheaper.

## The Meta-Question

21. **"What are the incentives of the person recommending this incentive structure?"**
    Use always. The person designing the incentive system has their own incentives. Make them explicit.
