# Decision Types Taxonomy

## Types

### By Reversibility
1. **One-Way Door (Irreversible)** — Decisions that cannot be meaningfully undone once executed. The cost of reversal is prohibitive or the consequences are permanent.
2. **Two-Way Door (Reversible)** — Decisions that can be undone or significantly altered with manageable cost and acceptable consequences.
3. **Partially Reversible** — Decisions where some elements can be unwound but others leave permanent effects (e.g., a public announcement can be retracted but reputational impact remains).

### By Scope
4. **Strategic** — Decisions that define the company's direction, market position, or fundamental business model. Affect the entire organization for 1+ years.
5. **Operational** — Decisions about how work gets done within an established strategy. Affect specific teams or functions for weeks to months.
6. **Tactical** — Day-to-day decisions within established operational frameworks. Affect immediate work for days to weeks.

### By Urgency
7. **Time-Critical** — Must be made within hours or days. Delay itself is a decision with consequences.
8. **Time-Sensitive** — Has a deadline but allows for weeks of deliberation.
9. **Evergreen** — No external deadline. Can be made when the team is ready.

### By Stakeholder Impact
10. **Unilateral** — Affects primarily the decision-maker's domain.
11. **Cross-Functional** — Affects multiple teams or functions that must coordinate.
12. **External-Facing** — Affects customers, partners, investors, or the public.

## Definitions

| Type | Definition | Decision Authority | Process Weight |
|------|-----------|-------------------|----------------|
| One-Way Door | Consequences are permanent or reversal cost exceeds 50% of the original investment | Senior leadership or board | Heavy — full decision memo, dissent capture, pre-mortem |
| Two-Way Door | Can be reversed at less than 10% of original cost within a reasonable timeframe | Person closest to the work | Light — brief rationale, time-boxed experiment |
| Partially Reversible | Some consequences are permanent (reputation, relationships) even if operational elements are reversible | One level above the implementer | Moderate — abbreviated memo, stakeholder review |
| Strategic | Changes the company's trajectory or competitive position | CEO with board input | Heavy — strategy blocks, scenario analysis |
| Operational | Optimizes within current strategy | Functional leader | Moderate — options analysis, owner accountability |
| Tactical | Executes within operational frameworks | Individual contributor or team lead | Light — decide and act |
| Time-Critical | Delay causes material harm | Fastest available authority | Emergency — decide, act, document after |
| Time-Sensitive | Has a deadline | Normal authority for decision type | Normal process compressed to fit timeline |
| Evergreen | No forcing function | Normal authority | Normal or enhanced (use the time for better analysis) |

## Examples

| Decision | Primary Type | Secondary Type | Recommended Process |
|----------|-------------|----------------|-------------------|
| Acquire a competitor | One-Way Door, Strategic | External-Facing | Full decision memo, board vote, pre-mortem, dissent capture |
| Choose Q3 sprint priorities | Two-Way Door, Operational | Cross-Functional | Product lead decides with team input, 1-week review |
| Respond to a PR crisis | One-Way Door, Time-Critical | External-Facing | Crisis lead decides within hours, document after |
| Change the pricing page | Two-Way Door, Tactical | External-Facing | Product manager runs experiment, 2-week review |
| Hire a VP Engineering | Partially Reversible, Strategic | Cross-Functional | Full hiring process, scorecard, board-level decision |
| Select a cloud provider | Partially Reversible, Operational | Cross-Functional | Technical lead with advisory input, 3-year commitment |
| Pivot the business model | One-Way Door, Strategic | External-Facing | Full pivot pattern, board approval, pre-mortem |
| Approve a $5K marketing spend | Two-Way Door, Tactical | Unilateral | Marketing manager decides within budget authority |
| Terminate a partnership | Partially Reversible, Strategic | External-Facing | Decision memo, stakeholder analysis, board awareness |
| Choose team meeting time | Two-Way Door, Tactical | Unilateral | Team lead decides, consent-based |

## When to Use Each

**Use One-Way Door process when:**
- The decision involves significant capital deployment (> 10% of reserves)
- The decision affects the company's public identity or reputation
- The decision involves personnel at VP level or above
- The decision creates long-term contractual or legal obligations
- The decision cannot be reversed within 6 months at reasonable cost

**Use Two-Way Door process when:**
- The decision can be tested with a time-boxed experiment
- Reversal cost is minimal (< 10% of investment)
- The learning from making the decision quickly exceeds the risk of being wrong
- Analysis paralysis is the greater danger than a wrong call

**Use Partially Reversible process when:**
- Operational elements are reversible but reputational or relational elements are not
- The decision involves external stakeholders who will be affected by changes
- Some consequences persist even if the decision is reversed (e.g., team trust after a reorg)

**Default rule:** When in doubt about classification, treat the decision as one category more serious than your instinct suggests. The cost of over-processing a reversible decision is low; the cost of under-processing an irreversible one is high.
