# Board Positioning One-Liners — Swipe File

## Purpose
Short, precise statements that board advisors and founders can use to reframe discussions, cut through ambiguity, and anchor critical conversations. These are not slogans — they are cognitive tools for redirecting board-level discourse. A well-placed one-liner can cut through an hour of circular discussion.

## On Decision-Making
- "Is this a one-way door or a two-way door?" — Forces classification of reversibility before debating the decision. (Bezos)
- "What would we have to believe for this to be true?" — Surfaces hidden assumptions behind any recommendation.
- "We are not choosing between good and bad. We are choosing between trade-offs." — Resets conversations that have become falsely binary.
- "The opposite of this decision is also defensible. What are we giving up?" — Forces acknowledgment of opportunity cost.
- "If we had to decide today with the information we have, what would we choose?" — Breaks analysis paralysis.
- "Who is the single decision-maker here? Not a committee — a name." — Ensures accountability before the discussion begins.
- "We can disagree and commit, but we cannot disagree and delay." — Unblocks decisions where consensus is impossible.
- "What would we do if we had no sunk costs in this decision?" — Clears anchoring bias.
- "Don't tell me what you think. Tell me what's in your portfolio." — Tests conviction vs. opinion. (Taleb)
- "If we read about a competitor making this move, would we think they were smart or desperate?" — Provides outside-in perspective.

## On Strategy
- "Strategy is what you say no to, not what you say yes to." — Refocuses strategy discussions that have become wish lists.
- "If everything is a priority, nothing is a priority." — Pushes back on bloated strategic plans.
- "What would a new CEO do on day one? We should do that now." — Challenges legacy decisions that no one would make fresh.
- "Are we optimizing for the current game or positioning for the next one?" — Distinguishes operational excellence from strategic positioning.
- "The plan is not the strategy. The choices are the strategy." — Prevents confusion between operational plans and strategic decisions.
- "Where do we have a right to win that others don't?" — Forces honest assessment of competitive advantage.
- "Competition is for losers. Where is our monopoly?" — Reframes competitive analysis toward differentiation. (Thiel)
- "Are we building for Day 1 or managing Day 2?" — Tests whether the organization has maintained urgency. (Bezos)
- "Would we enter this market if we were starting from scratch today?" — Separates strategic logic from historical momentum.

## On Risk and Uncertainty
- "What is the cost of being wrong, and can we survive it?" — Sizes downside before evaluating upside.
- "We are confusing uncertainty with risk. Uncertainty cannot be measured. Risk can." — Clarifies thinking when the team is paralyzed.
- "The base rate for this type of bet is X. Why do we think we are different?" — Grounds optimism in reference class data.
- "What would make us change our mind? If nothing, we are not thinking — we are hoping." — Tests genuine intellectual honesty.
- "The risk of inaction is not zero. It just feels like zero." — Counters status quo bias in conservative boards.
- "We are not being conservative. We are being passive. Those are different things." — Distinguishes thoughtful caution from avoidance.
- "The biggest risk is the one we haven't identified yet." — Redirects from known risks to blind spots.
- "Are we fragile, robust, or antifragile to this scenario?" — Forces structural analysis of exposure. (Taleb)
- "This time it's different — the four most dangerous words in business." — Anchors against cycle-blindness. (Marks)

## On Capital and Resources
- "Every dollar we spend here is a dollar we cannot spend there. What are we not doing?" — Forces explicit opportunity cost accounting.
- "Are we buying growth or earning it?" — Distinguishes sustainable from subsidized growth.
- "Runway is not a number. It is the number of experiments we can run before we need to be right." — Reframes burn rate as learning capacity.
- "Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity, cash flow is reality." — Cuts through top-line obsession.
- "Growth that destroys unit economics is just accelerated death." — Tests whether growth is value-creating or value-destroying.
- "The most dangerous number in business is one — one customer, one product, one channel." — Highlights concentration risk.

## On People and Organization
- "Would you enthusiastically rehire this person today?" — Cuts through sunk cost of tenure and loyalty.
- "We do not have a performance problem. We have a clarity problem." — Reframes underperformance as a leadership failure before a personnel failure.
- "Culture is what happens when the CEO is not in the room." — Grounds culture discussions in observable behavior.
- "The team you have is not the team you need. What is the gap?" — Forces honest organizational assessment.
- "A players hire A players. B players hire C players." — Frames why first hires at each level determine trajectory.
- "First who, then what." — Get the right people before deciding direction. (Collins)
- "Show me the incentive and I'll show you the outcome." — Root-causes behavior through structure. (Munger)
- "Clear is kind. Unclear is unkind." — Reframes difficult feedback as an act of respect. (Brown)
- "The cost of a bad hire at the executive level is measured in years, not dollars." — Sizes the true impact of hiring mistakes.

## On Board Dynamics
- "Our job is to ask the questions management is not asking themselves." — Defines the board's role as challenge, not validation.
- "If we all agree immediately, we have not thought about it hard enough." — Invites constructive dissent.
- "What does management know that we don't? What do we see that management can't?" — Clarifies the information asymmetry.
- "The best boards make the CEO braver, not more cautious." — Reframes the board's role from risk management to courage amplification.
- "Are we governing or advising right now? Let's be explicit." — Separates governance from advisory conversations.
- "No surprises. Ever. In either direction." — Sets the foundational expectation for board-management trust.
- "If the board always agrees with management, one of them is unnecessary." — Tests whether the board is adding value.

## On Crisis
- "The cover-up is always worse than the crisis." — Anchors crisis response in transparency.
- "We have 72 hours to define the narrative. After that, someone else defines it for us." — Creates urgency.
- "What would we want to have done if this is on the front page tomorrow?" — The newspaper test.
- "This is not a communications problem. This is an operations problem." — Redirects from PR to root cause.
- "Pain plus reflection equals progress." — Reframes crisis as a learning opportunity. (Dalio)

## How to Use These One-Liners
1. **Before a board meeting:** Review the relevant category and select 2-3 that apply to agenda topics
2. **In the moment:** Use to redirect a conversation that is off-track or stuck in a loop
3. **In written memos:** Use as epigraphs or framing devices at the top of decision memos
4. **Build shared vocabulary:** When the board shares shorthand, communication accelerates across meetings
