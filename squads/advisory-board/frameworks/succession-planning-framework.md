# Succession Planning Framework

## Purpose

Succession planning ensures organizational continuity by identifying, developing, and preparing leaders to fill critical roles when transitions occur — planned or unplanned. Most organizations treat succession as an emergency response rather than an ongoing process. This framework transforms succession from crisis management into strategic capability building, ensuring the organization is never dependent on any single individual.

## When to Use

- As an ongoing process for all critical roles (not just CEO)
- When a key leader announces departure
- When evaluating organizational resilience and risk
- During board reviews of leadership depth
- When a founder is considering long-term transition
- When rapid growth requires more leadership capacity than currently exists

## Core Model

### The Succession Readiness Matrix

| Role | Emergency Successor (Now) | Developed Successor (6-12 months) | Pipeline Candidate (1-3 years) |
|------|--------------------------|----------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| CEO | | | |
| CTO | | | |
| VP Sales | | | |

### Three Succession Scenarios

**1. Planned Transition:** Leader is leaving on a known timeline. Maximum preparation time. Ideal scenario.
**2. Unexpected Departure:** Leader leaves suddenly (resignation, health, termination). Emergency plan activates.
**3. Growth Transition:** Role outgrows the person. The leader is still present but the role needs different capabilities.

### Succession Development Components
- **Exposure:** Give candidates visibility to board-level and strategic decisions
- **Experience:** Rotate candidates through adjacent responsibilities
- **Education:** Targeted development in gap areas
- **Evaluation:** Regular assessment of readiness with honest feedback

## Steps

1. **Identify critical roles.** Which roles, if vacant, would cause significant disruption?
2. **Assess current bench strength.** For each critical role: who could step in today? Who could step in with 6 months of development?
3. **Develop succession candidates.** Create individualized development plans for each successor candidate.
4. **Create emergency succession plans.** For each critical role: if the person left tomorrow, who takes over and what is the 90-day plan?
5. **Review with the board annually.** Succession planning is a board-level responsibility.
6. **Update continuously.** As people join, leave, and develop, the succession map changes.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Succession planning is the board's responsibility, not just management's. Push for annual succession review.
- For founder-led companies, the hardest succession is the founder's. Start the conversation early.
- Evaluate whether the company is building leadership depth or relying on individual heroics.

## Common Mistakes

1. **No plan at all.** The most common failure.
2. **Paper plan only.** A succession plan without development investment is fiction.
3. **Single successor.** Having only one candidate for each role creates fragility.
4. **Avoiding the founder succession conversation.** Every founder will eventually transition. Planning early creates options.
