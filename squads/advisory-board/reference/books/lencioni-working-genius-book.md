# The 6 Types of Working Genius — Patrick Lencioni

## Summary

Lencioni identifies six fundamental types of work that every project requires: Wonder, Invention, Discernment, Galvanizing, Enablement, and Tenacity. Each person has two areas of genius (energizing), two of competency (neutral), and two of frustration (draining). The model explains why some people thrive in brainstorming but wilt during execution, or why great project managers feel drained by open-ended ideation. For teams and boards, the framework diagnoses productivity gaps: when a project stalls, it's usually because a required genius type is missing from the team, not because people aren't working hard enough.

## Key Principles

- **Six types of work are required for any initiative** — Wonder (pondering possibility), Invention (creating solutions), Discernment (evaluating ideas with intuition), Galvanizing (rallying people to action), Enablement (providing support and assistance), Tenacity (pushing through to completion).
- **Genius, competency, frustration** — Everyone has 2 geniuses, 2 competencies, 2 frustrations. Working in your frustration zone drains energy and produces mediocre results. Working in your genius zone energizes and produces exceptional results.
- **Guilt and shame come from misidentification** — People who are forced to work in their frustration areas often feel guilty for struggling. Understanding the model replaces guilt with clarity.
- **Teams need all six** — A team of Inventors and Wonders will generate brilliant ideas but never ship. A team of Enablers and Tenacity types will execute flawlessly on the wrong things. Balance is essential.
- **The phases flow sequentially** — Wonder → Invention → Discernment → Galvanizing → Enablement → Tenacity. Skipping phases or reversing order creates dysfunction.

## Frameworks Extracted

1. **The Working Genius Assessment** — Map each team member's two geniuses, two competencies, and two frustrations. Overlay these on the team to find gaps and overlaps.
2. **The Genius Map** — Visualize the team on a 2x3 grid. Identify which phases of work are covered by genius (strong), competency (adequate), or frustration (weak). Weak phases will be the bottleneck.
3. **Project Phase Audit** — When a project stalls, diagnose which phase it's stuck in: (1) Not enough wondering (no one asking "what if?"), (2) Not enough invention (no creative solutions), (3) Not enough discernment (bad ideas not filtered), (4) Not enough galvanizing (no one rallying the team), (5) Not enough enablement (no one supporting execution), (6) Not enough tenacity (no one pushing to finish).
4. **Role Redesign Protocol** — After assessment, realign roles so people spend maximum time in their genius zones. This often means splitting roles that combine genius and frustration work.

## Quotable Lines

- "The problem isn't that people aren't working hard. It's that they're working on the wrong type of work."
- "When people work in their areas of genius, productivity rises and guilt disappears."
- "Every team dysfunction can be traced to a missing or underrepresented genius type."
- "Knowing your frustration is as important as knowing your genius."
- "The most common source of team conflict is not personality differences—it's genius differences."

## Application to Board Decisions

- **Board composition** — Map the board's Working Genius profile. Most boards over-index on Discernment (evaluating proposals) and under-index on Wonder (asking "what if?" about the future) and Galvanizing (energizing the organization post-decision).
- **Executive team gaps** — Use the Genius Map to identify gaps in the leadership team. If no one on the executive team has Tenacity as a genius, execution will consistently fall short of planning.
- **Meeting design** — Structure board meetings to activate different geniuses at different times: Wonder time (open exploration), Invention time (solution design), Discernment time (evaluation), Galvanizing time (alignment and energy).
- **Hiring decisions** — When the board approves a senior hire, evaluate what genius type the role requires and whether the candidate matches. Hiring a brilliant Inventor for a role that requires Tenacity guarantees failure.
- **Succession planning** — Map the CEO's genius profile against the company's current needs. A company in the idea phase needs Wonder/Invention leadership; a company in the scaling phase needs Galvanizing/Tenacity leadership.
