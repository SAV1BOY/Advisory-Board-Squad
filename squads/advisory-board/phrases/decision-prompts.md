# Decision Prompts

Phrases to sharpen decision-making, force clarity, and prevent decision drift. Use these when the board is evaluating options, about to commit, or avoiding a decision.

## Framing the Decision

1. **"What is the actual decision we are making right now?"**
   Use when discussion has been running for 10+ minutes without a clear question on the table. Forces the group to articulate the specific choice.

2. **"Is this a one-way door or a two-way door?"**
   Use to calibrate how much deliberation a decision warrants. One-way (irreversible) doors deserve deep analysis. Two-way (reversible) doors should be made fast.

3. **"What would we have to believe for Option A to be the right choice? And for Option B?"**
   Use when the group is debating options without making assumptions explicit. Forces each position to state its underlying beliefs.

4. **"If we do nothing, what happens?"**
   Use to test whether the decision is urgent or manufactured. Sometimes the best decision is no decision — and sometimes inaction is the riskiest path.

5. **"What is the cost of being wrong in each direction?"**
   Use to assess asymmetric risk. Being wrong by acting is different from being wrong by not acting. Quantify both.

## Sharpening Analysis

6. **"What is the expected value of this decision?"**
   Use when the discussion is qualitative but could benefit from quantification. Forces probability-weighted thinking.

7. **"What would a disconfirming data point look like?"**
   Use to test whether a recommendation is falsifiable. If no evidence could disprove it, the analysis isn't rigorous enough.

8. **"Are we anchored to the first option we heard?"**
   Use when the group converges quickly on the earliest proposal. Anchoring bias is common and invisible.

9. **"Separate the decision from the outcome. Was this a good process even if the outcome is uncertain?"**
   Use when fear of a bad outcome is preventing a well-reasoned decision. Good process does not guarantee good outcomes, but it is the only thing we can control.

10. **"If we learned this information a year from now, would it change our decision today?"**
    Use to identify information that is decision-relevant vs. merely interesting. If it wouldn't change the decision, we don't need it yet.

## Forcing Commitment

11. **"We have discussed this for [X] minutes. Are we ready to decide, or do we need specific additional information?"**
    Use when discussion is circling. Forces the group to either commit or name precisely what is missing.

12. **"I'm going to call the decision in 60 seconds. If you have an objection, raise it now."**
    Use when consensus is near but nobody will call it. Creates a healthy forcing function.

13. **"We are choosing Option B. The rationale is [X]. The owner is [name]. The review date is [date]. Does anyone object?"**
    Use to formally close a decision. Stating the full decision record forces clarity and surfaces hidden disagreement.

14. **"What kill criteria should we set now, before we are emotionally invested?"**
    Use at the moment of commitment. Pre-commitment kill criteria prevent sunk-cost bias later.

15. **"Who disagrees? I want to record the dissent before we move on."**
    Use before closing any significant decision. Explicit invitation to dissent catches objections that politeness would suppress.

## Avoiding Decision Traps

16. **"Are we making this decision because it is right, or because it is comfortable?"**
    Use when the "obvious" choice is also the easiest one. Comfort bias is the enemy of strategic clarity.

17. **"What would our harshest critic say about this decision?"**
    Use to stress-test externally. If you can't anticipate the criticism, you haven't thought through the angles.

18. **"Is this decision consistent with the last three decisions we made? If not, what changed?"**
    Use to check for strategic coherence. Inconsistency isn't always wrong, but it should be intentional and explained.

19. **"Whose interests are not represented in this room right now?"**
    Use to check for stakeholder blind spots. Customers, employees, partners, and future team members may be affected by a decision made without their perspective.

20. **"If we had to make this decision with 70 % of the information, what would we choose?"**
    Use when the group is waiting for perfect information. Most good decisions are made with incomplete data. The question is whether more data would actually change the direction.
