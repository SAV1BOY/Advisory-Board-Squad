# Framing Effects in Memos

## Overview
The way information is presented (framed) systematically changes decisions, even when the underlying facts are identical. "95% survival rate" and "5% mortality rate" describe the same reality but produce different choices. In board memos, framing effects can make mediocre proposals look compelling and strong proposals look risky.

## Common Framing Tactics in Business Memos

### Gain vs. Loss Framing
- **Gain frame:** "This investment will generate $5M in new revenue"
- **Loss frame:** "Without this investment, we'll lose $5M in potential revenue"
- **Impact:** Loss framing triggers stronger emotional response and is more persuasive

### Absolute vs. Relative Numbers
- **Impressive:** "Revenue grew 50% this quarter"
- **Less impressive:** "Revenue grew from $2M to $3M" (the same fact)
- **Impact:** Percentage growth sounds more impressive for small absolute numbers

### Time Horizon Manipulation
- **Short frame:** "This project will cost $1M this quarter" (sounds expensive)
- **Long frame:** "This project will generate $10M over 5 years" (sounds cheap)
- **Impact:** Costs feel bigger in short frames; benefits feel bigger in long frames

### Selective Comparison
- **Favorable comp:** "We're growing faster than Competitor X" (who is struggling)
- **Unfavorable comp omitted:** Competitor Y (who is growing 3x faster) isn't mentioned
- **Impact:** Selective comparison creates an illusion of strong performance

### Base Rate Omission
- **Presented:** "Our conversion rate improved 20%"
- **Missing context:** From 1% to 1.2% (barely meaningful in absolute terms)
- **Impact:** Relative improvement sounds dramatic; absolute reality is modest

## Detection Protocol

For every memo presented to the board:
1. **Reframe gains as losses and losses as gains** — Does the recommendation change?
2. **Convert percentages to absolute numbers** — Is the impact actually meaningful?
3. **Extend or shorten the time horizon** — Does the proposal still look good at 1 year? At 10 years?
4. **Add missing comparisons** — Who else should we compare against?
5. **Check base rates** — What's the absolute starting point for any relative claim?

## Board Application
- Train board members to recognize framing effects in management presentations
- Require memos to present both gain and loss framings for key proposals
- Insist on absolute AND relative numbers for any metric
- When a memo is particularly persuasive, check whether the framing is doing the persuading rather than the substance
