# Meeting Control Phrases

Phrases for managing board meeting flow — keeping discussions on track, managing time, handling tangents, and ensuring productive outcomes.

## Time Management

1. **"We have 15 minutes left on this topic. Let's focus on what we need to decide, not what we'd like to discuss."**
   Use when a discussion is running long. Separates the decision from the broader conversation.

2. **"I'm going to call time on this discussion. We can continue offline or at the next meeting."**
   Use when a topic has consumed its allocated time without resolution.

3. **"We are halfway through the agenda and we've covered one item. I'm going to tighten the time boxes."**
   Use early when pacing is off. Better to adjust at the midpoint than to rush the final items.

4. **"This is a 2-minute answer, not a 10-minute answer. Give me the bottom line."**
   Use when a presenter or advisor is over-explaining. Direct but not hostile.

5. **"Let's table the implementation details. The board's role is to decide the direction, not design the solution."**
   Use when the board drifts from governance into management. The most common meeting time-sink.

## Redirecting Discussion

6. **"That's an important point, but it belongs in a different conversation. Let me note it for follow-up."**
   Use to validate the point while redirecting the discussion. The "parking lot" approach works when the note is actually followed up.

7. **"We're solving the wrong problem right now. The question on the table is [restate]."**
   Use when the discussion has drifted from the original question. Restating the question is the fastest way to refocus.

8. **"I've heard three perspectives that I want to capture before we hear a fourth."**
   Use to prevent discussion pile-up. Pausing to summarize creates structure and prevents repetition.

9. **"We're relitigating a decision we made in [month]. Has new information emerged, or are we reopening for a different reason?"**
   Use when a settled decision is being re-debated. Distinguishes between legitimate re-evaluation and chronic second-guessing.

10. **"This conversation is between two people. Let's take it offline and bring back the conclusion."**
    Use when a bilateral debate has consumed group time. Respect everyone's time by narrowing the conversation.

## Ensuring Participation

11. **"I want to hear from everyone on this. Let's do a 60-second round-robin, starting with [name]."**
    Use to break the silence or balance participation. Round-robins with time limits are efficient and inclusive.

12. **"[Name], you have direct experience with this. What's your read?"**
    Use to invite specific expertise into the conversation. Named invitations are more effective than open-ended "any thoughts?"

13. **"We've heard from the advocates. Now I want to hear from the skeptics."**
    Use to balance the discussion when early speakers have set an overly positive or negative tone.

14. **"I'm going to pause the verbal discussion. Everyone write down your position in one sentence. Then we'll share."**
    Use when dominant voices are anchoring the group. Written responses before verbal sharing reduce anchoring bias.

15. **"Before we move on, does anyone have something they haven't said? Last call."**
    Use before closing any agenda item. Gives permission for one final thought without reopening the discussion.

## Managing Conflict

16. **"Both positions have merit. Let me see if I can find the underlying question where you disagree."**
    Use when two advisors are talking past each other. Identifying the root disagreement is more productive than mediating surface positions.

17. **"The temperature is rising. Let's take 5 minutes and come back with fresher heads."**
    Use when emotions are overtaking reason. A short break resets the dynamic.

18. **"I'm going to ask you both to do something hard: state the other person's position as they would state it."**
    Use to build mutual understanding in a disagreement. If neither can articulate the other's view, they are not actually disagreeing — they are talking past each other.

19. **"We can disagree on this and still make a decision. Disagreement is healthy. Deadlock is not."**
    Use when perfectionism about consensus is blocking action.

## Driving to Decision

20. **"I'm hearing convergence around [X]. Is that accurate, or am I projecting?"**
    Use to test whether consensus is real or imagined. Asking explicitly prevents false consensus.

21. **"We need a decision now. I'm going to propose one. Push back if you disagree."**
    Use when discussion has been thorough but nobody is willing to call the question.

22. **"The decision is [X]. Let me read back: rationale, owner, review date, dissent. Confirm."**
    Use to close every decision with a formal record. Forces clarity and catches misunderstandings.

23. **"We are not ready to decide. The specific information we need is [X]. [Name] will bring it to the next meeting. Moving on."**
    Use when a decision genuinely requires more information. Names the gap specifically rather than deferring vaguely.

## Closing

24. **"Summary: we made [N] decisions today. [List them.] Action items: [list with owners and dates]. Next meeting: [date]."**
    Use to close every meeting. A crisp summary ensures everyone leaves with the same understanding.

25. **"What went well in today's meeting? What should we improve?"**
    Use as a 2-minute closing ritual. Continuous improvement of meeting quality compounds over time.
