# AI Disruption Landscape

## Key Findings

1. **Uneven disruption velocity.** AI adoption follows a power-law pattern across industries — financial services, healthcare diagnostics, and software development are experiencing 3-5x faster disruption than manufacturing, construction, or agriculture. Board oversight should prioritize sectors where the company's customers or competitors face imminent AI-driven displacement.
2. **Incumbents with data moats are defending better than expected.** Despite predictions of startup dominance, incumbents with proprietary data assets (healthcare records, financial transaction histories, supply chain logs) are integrating AI faster than startups can acquire equivalent data. The window for AI-native startups to displace incumbents narrows by 12-18 months with each generation of foundation models.
3. **Workflow automation precedes job displacement.** The pattern across industries is: (a) AI augments individual tasks, (b) AI automates full workflows, (c) organizational structures change, (d) job categories shift. Most industries are between stages (a) and (b) as of early 2026.
4. **Build vs. buy is resolving toward buy.** 78% of enterprises now prefer purchasing AI capabilities through existing vendor integrations over building custom solutions. This favors platform companies that embed AI into workflow tools over pure-play AI companies.
5. **Regulatory fragmentation creates moats.** Divergent AI regulation across the EU (AI Act), US (sector-specific), and Asia creates compliance complexity that favors companies with established regulatory infrastructure.

## Implications for Board

- **Portfolio exposure assessment.** Every board meeting should include a 5-minute update on AI disruption signals relevant to the company's industry. Use the competitive-intelligence-template.md to structure this monitoring.
- **Talent strategy.** Companies that embedded AI literacy programs in 2024 are showing 2.1x faster internal AI adoption than those starting in 2025. The board should verify the company has an AI upskilling plan with measurable milestones.
- **Build vs. buy decisions.** The board should challenge any proposal to build custom AI infrastructure unless the company has a clear, defensible data advantage. Default recommendation: buy or partner.
- **Regulatory preparedness.** Companies operating across jurisdictions need an AI governance framework reviewed quarterly. The board should ensure legal and compliance teams are resourced for AI-specific regulatory tracking.
- **Competitive moat evaluation.** AI commoditizes capabilities quickly. The board should ask: "What does our company do that AI makes harder to replicate, not easier?" If the answer is unclear, strategic risk is high.
