# Unit Economics

## Core Metrics

### Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
- **Formula:** Total sales and marketing spend / Number of new customers acquired
- **Fully-loaded CAC:** Include salaries, tools, content, events, overhead — not just ad spend
- **By channel:** Calculate CAC per acquisition channel to identify efficient vs. wasteful spend
- **Benchmarks:** SaaS CAC payback < 12 months (good), < 18 months (acceptable), > 24 months (concerning)

### Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)
- **Simple formula:** Average revenue per customer per month x Gross margin % x Average customer lifespan (months)
- **With expansion:** Include upsell and cross-sell revenue in average revenue per customer
- **Cohort-based:** Calculate LTV by acquisition cohort to identify whether newer cohorts are better or worse than older ones
- **Benchmarks:** LTV/CAC > 3x (healthy), 1-3x (investable but tight), < 1x (unsustainable)

### LTV/CAC Ratio
- **The single most important unit economics metric** for subscription businesses
- **> 3x:** Strong economics, typically can invest more in growth
- **1-3x:** Viable but watch for deterioration; may need to improve retention or reduce CAC
- **< 1x:** Destroying value with every customer acquired; stop spending on acquisition until fixed

### Payback Period
- **Formula:** CAC / (Monthly revenue per customer x Gross margin %)
- **Why it matters:** Faster payback = less capital required to fund growth = less dilution
- **Cash flow implication:** A 12-month payback means you need 12 months of capital per customer before they become profitable

## Contribution Margin

### Revenue per Unit — Cost of Goods per Unit = Contribution Margin
- For SaaS: hosting, support, onboarding costs per customer
- For marketplaces: take rate minus payment processing, fraud, support per transaction
- For e-commerce: product cost, shipping, returns, warehouse per order
- **Contribution margin must be positive** before scaling. Scaling a negative contribution margin business is burning money faster.

## Cohort Analysis

### What to Track
- **Revenue retention:** What % of revenue from a cohort is retained at month 6, 12, 24?
- **Logo retention:** What % of customers from a cohort are still active?
- **Net revenue retention (NRR):** Revenue from existing customers including expansion minus churn. NRR > 100% means you grow even without new customers.
- **Cohort behavior changes:** Are newer cohorts retaining better or worse? This reveals whether product-market fit is strengthening or weakening.

### Red Flags
- Declining retention in newer cohorts (PMF may be weakening)
- CAC increasing while LTV is flat (market saturation)
- High logo churn masked by expansion revenue from remaining customers
- Payback period lengthening over time

## Board Application
- Review unit economics quarterly, not just revenue and growth
- Demand cohort-level analysis, not just blended averages
- Challenge management when unit economics deteriorate but growth is maintained (growth can mask decay)
- Use unit economics to evaluate whether growth spending should increase, decrease, or reallocate
