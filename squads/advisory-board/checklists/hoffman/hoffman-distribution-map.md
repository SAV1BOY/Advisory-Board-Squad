# Hoffman Distribution Map Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the organization has a clear, defensible distribution strategy. Reid Hoffman emphasizes that great products without great distribution fail. This checklist verifies that distribution channels are identified, tested, and optimized, and that the organization does not rely on the "build it and they will come" fallacy. Distribution is not an afterthought; it is a core strategic capability.

## When to Use
- When launching a new product or entering a new market.
- During go-to-market strategy reviews.
- When customer acquisition costs are rising or growth is decelerating.
- When evaluating channel partnerships and distribution agreements.
- During annual strategic reviews of growth strategy.

## Gate Criteria

### Channel Identification
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: All current and potential distribution channels are mapped and assessed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each channel's customer acquisition cost (CAC) is measured or estimated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each channel's scalability ceiling is assessed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Channel-customer fit is verified: the target customer actually uses this channel.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Emerging distribution channels relevant to the market are monitored.

### Channel Dependency
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: No single channel accounts for more than 50% of customer acquisition without explicit risk acceptance.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Channel ownership is assessed: does the company control the channel or depend on a third party.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Platform risk is evaluated: what happens if a key channel changes its rules or economics.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The cost and timeline to diversify channels is quantified.

### Distribution Effectiveness
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Conversion rates at each stage of the distribution funnel are measured.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: CAC is tracked against LTV to ensure unit economics are positive by channel.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Channel performance is benchmarked against industry standards.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A/B testing and experimentation are conducted to optimize channel performance.

### Distribution-Product Fit
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The product's distribution mechanism is designed into the product, not bolted on.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Viral loops, referral mechanisms, or built-in sharing features are evaluated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The product experience reinforces distribution: satisfied users become distribution channels.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Distribution strategy aligns with the product's pricing and positioning.

## Red Flags
- The company has no distribution strategy beyond "organic growth."
- Customer acquisition cost is rising faster than customer lifetime value.
- The company depends entirely on a single platform (Google, Apple, Facebook) for distribution.
- Sales and marketing are treated as costs to minimize rather than strategic capabilities to develop.
- The product has strong engagement metrics but weak distribution metrics.
- No one on the leadership team has deep expertise in the company's primary distribution channel.

## Escalation Triggers
- A primary distribution channel changes its terms or algorithm, materially impacting growth.
- CAC exceeds LTV for the primary channel and the trend is worsening.
- A competitor achieves superior distribution that threatens market share.
- Growth decelerates for two consecutive quarters with no diagnosed cause.
- The company's distribution strategy requires more capital than is available.
