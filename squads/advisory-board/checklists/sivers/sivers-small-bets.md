# Sivers Small Bets Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the organization takes a portfolio approach to uncertainty by making many small bets rather than a few large ones when operating in areas of high uncertainty. Sivers advocates for keeping options open, learning from small experiments, and only scaling what works. This checklist prevents the failure mode of going all-in on unvalidated assumptions while also preventing excessive caution that avoids any risk.

## When to Use
- When entering new markets or launching new products with high uncertainty.
- When deciding how to allocate resources between proven and unproven initiatives.
- When the organization has been burned by large bets that failed.
- During innovation and exploration planning.
- When evaluating the risk profile of the initiative portfolio.

## Gate Criteria

### Bet Sizing
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The initial investment in unproven initiatives is sized as a small bet (loss is acceptable if it fails).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The portfolio includes multiple small bets rather than a single large one.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each small bet has a defined investment cap before a go/no-go decision.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The total exposure to small bets is within an acceptable risk budget.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Follow-on investment is contingent on demonstrated results, not on initial enthusiasm.

### Learning Design
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each small bet is designed to answer a specific question or validate a specific assumption.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Success and failure criteria are defined before the bet is placed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The timeline for evaluation is short enough to be actionable (weeks or months, not years).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Data collection is built into the bet design so results can be evaluated.

### Scaling Discipline
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Only bets that demonstrate clear positive signals are scaled.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The scaling decision is based on evidence, not on the team's desire to continue.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Failed bets are shut down cleanly and resources are reallocated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The path from small bet to scaled initiative is mapped with clear milestones.

### Portfolio Management
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The portfolio of small bets is reviewed on a regular cadence.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization maintains capacity to pursue new small bets as old ones resolve.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Learnings from all bets (successful and failed) are captured and shared.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization tracks its hit rate and uses it to calibrate future bet sizing.

## Red Flags
- The organization makes one large bet instead of multiple small ones in areas of high uncertainty.
- Small bets are never killed; they accumulate and drain resources.
- The organization waits for certainty before placing any bets, missing opportunities.
- No learning is extracted from failed bets.
- The team confuses small bets with half-hearted efforts (small bets are intentionally small, not poorly resourced).
- Success is measured only by the outcome, not by the quality of learning.

## Escalation Triggers
- The total investment in small bets exceeds the risk budget without board awareness.
- Multiple small bets are failing with no corrective learning being applied.
- A small bet that should be killed continues to receive resources.
- The organization has no active small bets, suggesting excessive risk aversion.
- A competitor succeeds with an approach the organization tested as a small bet but abandoned prematurely.
