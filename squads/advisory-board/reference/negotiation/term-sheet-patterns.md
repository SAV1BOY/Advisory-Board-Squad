# Term Sheet Patterns

## Overview
Term sheets set the framework for investment deals. Understanding common terms, their implications, and negotiation leverage points is essential for boards advising on fundraising. This file covers the key terms, what's standard, and where founders should push back.

## Economic Terms

### Valuation
- **Pre-money valuation:** Company value before new investment
- **Post-money valuation:** Pre-money + new investment
- **Negotiation:** Higher pre-money = less dilution for founders. But valuation isn't everything—terms matter more.

### Liquidation Preference
- **1x Non-participating (standard):** Investor gets their money back OR converts to common (whichever is better)
- **1x Participating:** Investor gets money back AND shares in remaining proceeds. Significantly worse for founders.
- **Multiple preferences (2x, 3x):** Investor gets 2-3x their investment before common shareholders receive anything. Avoid if possible.
- **Stacking:** Multiple funding rounds with preferences create a "preference stack" that founders must clear before receiving proceeds

### Anti-Dilution
- **Broad-based weighted average (standard):** Adjusts conversion price based on the economics of the down round, weighted by shares outstanding
- **Full ratchet (aggressive):** Adjusts conversion price to the new lower price regardless of round size. Very investor-friendly; avoid.

### Option Pool
- **Pre-money option pool increase:** Investors often require expanding the option pool before their investment, effectively reducing the true pre-money valuation
- **Negotiation:** Negotiate the option pool size based on an actual hiring plan, not an arbitrary percentage

## Control Terms

### Board Composition
- **Typical seed:** 3 seats (1 founder, 1 investor, 1 independent)
- **Typical Series A:** 5 seats (2 founders, 2 investors, 1 independent)
- **Negotiation:** Maintain founder control as long as possible. Independent directors should be mutually agreed.

### Protective Provisions
- **Standard:** Investor approval required for: issuing new shares, changing the charter, selling the company, taking on significant debt, changing board size
- **Aggressive:** Approval required for: annual budget, any hire above $X salary, any contract above $Y value
- **Negotiation:** Accept standard protections; push back on operational micro-management provisions

### Information Rights
- **Standard:** Monthly or quarterly financials, annual budget, access to books and records
- **Negotiation:** Generally reasonable; ensure the reporting burden is manageable

## Other Key Terms

### Drag-Along
- If a supermajority of shareholders approve a sale, remaining shareholders must participate
- **Purpose:** Prevents minority shareholders from blocking an exit
- **Negotiation:** Ensure the threshold is high enough (usually 60-75% of preferred) and includes minimum price protections

### Right of First Refusal (ROFR)
- Company and/or investors have the right to purchase shares before they're sold to outsiders
- **Purpose:** Controls the cap table; prevents unwanted shareholders
- **Negotiation:** Standard and generally reasonable. Ensure process isn't so cumbersome it blocks secondary transactions.

### Pay-to-Play
- Investors who don't participate in future rounds lose their preferred status (convert to common)
- **Founder advantage:** Encourages investor commitment; prevents free-rider problem
- **Negotiation:** Founders should generally favor pay-to-play provisions

## Board Application
- Review every term sheet before the founder signs; pay attention to terms, not just valuation
- Flag participating liquidation preferences, full ratchet anti-dilution, and aggressive protective provisions
- Model the cap table through multiple future rounds to understand cumulative dilution and preference stack
- Ensure the option pool is right-sized for the actual hiring plan, not inflated to benefit investor economics
