# Fundraising Timeline Framework

## Purpose

This framework provides a structured timeline and process for planning and executing fundraising rounds. Fundraising is a project that competes with building the business, and poor planning leads to either desperate, poorly-timed raises or unnecessary dilution. This framework helps founders and advisors plan fundraising with the same rigor they apply to product launches.

## When to Use

- 6-9 months before the company needs to raise
- When planning the annual financial calendar
- When advising founders on fundraising strategy and timing
- When evaluating whether to raise now, later, or not at all

## Core Model

### The Fundraising Timeline (18 weeks typical)

**Months -6 to -3: Preparation Phase**
- Finalize metrics and narrative (what story do the numbers tell?)
- Build target investor list (50-100 names for seed, 30-50 for Series A+)
- Create materials: deck, data room, financial model
- Begin warm introductions (not pitching — building relationships)
- Set board meeting to align on terms and strategy

**Months -3 to -1: Warm-up Phase**
- Coffee meetings with target investors (no ask — just relationship building)
- Refine pitch based on feedback from warm-up conversations
- Identify lead investor targets (the first check matters most)
- Finalize data room and due diligence materials

**Weeks 1-4: Active Fundraise**
- Launch: pitch to all targets within a compressed 2-week window
- Create competitive dynamics by running a parallel process
- First meetings → Partner meetings → Term sheets (ideally within 4 weeks)
- Maintain business momentum (assign a fundraising lead so CEO isn't 100% distracted)

**Weeks 4-8: Closing**
- Negotiate terms with preferred investor(s)
- Due diligence and legal documentation
- Sign and wire

**Post-Close**
- Announce to team, customers, and market (if appropriate)
- Onboard new board member (if applicable)
- Set milestones for the capital deployment plan

### Key Principles
1. **Raise from a position of strength.** The best time to raise is when you don't need to.
2. **Compressed timelines create urgency.** Run a parallel process to generate competitive dynamics.
3. **The lead investor matters most.** Focus energy on finding the right lead; followers follow.
4. **Don't let fundraising stop the business.** Assign clear roles: who fundraises, who runs the business.

## Steps

1. **Determine whether to raise.** Is fundraising the right move? (See Opportunity Cost Framework)
2. **Set the timeline.** Work backward from when you need the money. Add 3 months buffer.
3. **Build materials.** Deck, model, data room, target list.
4. **Execute the warm-up.** Relationship building before asking.
5. **Launch compressed.** All pitches within a 2-week window.
6. **Close efficiently.** Don't let due diligence drag. Set deadlines.
7. **Deploy capital against milestones.** The money is a tool, not a trophy.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Starting too late.** If you start fundraising when you need money, you're already desperate.
2. **Raising too much or too little.** Raise 18-24 months of runway. More creates complacency; less creates desperation.
3. **No parallel process.** Talking to one investor at a time has no competitive dynamics.
4. **Letting fundraising consume the CEO.** The business must continue during the raise.
