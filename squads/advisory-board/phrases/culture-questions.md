# Culture Questions

Phrases for probing organizational culture — revealing the gap between stated values and lived reality, diagnosing cultural health, and guiding cultural evolution.

## Diagnosing Culture

1. **"What behavior gets rewarded here that shouldn't? What behavior should be rewarded that isn't?"**
   Use to identify the gap between stated values and actual incentives. This single question reveals more about culture than any survey.

2. **"Tell me about the last person who was fired. What did they do, and how long did it take?"**
   Use to assess accountability culture. Slow firing signals high tolerance for underperformance. Arbitrary firing signals low psychological safety.

3. **"What would happen to someone who publicly disagreed with the CEO in a meeting?"**
   Use to gauge psychological safety. If the answer involves any form of punishment or career risk, dissent is not safe.

4. **"What story does the company tell about itself that is no longer true?"**
   Use to identify cultural mythology that has diverged from reality. Founding stories, "how we work" narratives, and identity claims all need periodic testing.

5. **"What do new hires figure out in the first 30 days that nobody tells them explicitly?"**
   Use to surface the unwritten rules. The shadow culture — the one nobody formally teaches — is often more powerful than the official one.

## Testing Values

6. **"When was the last time we made a decision that cost us money because of our values?"**
   Use to test whether values are real or decorative. Values that have never cost anything have never been tested.

7. **"Which of our stated values is hardest to live by? When have we failed at it?"**
   Use to identify the value under the most strain. Honest acknowledgment of failure builds more trust than pretending perfection.

8. **"If a journalist embedded in our company for a month, what would they write?"**
   Use as a reality check. The observer test reveals what insiders have stopped noticing.

9. **"Do people here optimize for the company's success or their personal success? How would you know?"**
   Use to assess alignment. In healthy cultures, the two are aligned. In unhealthy cultures, they diverge.

10. **"What is the most common reason people leave voluntarily? And is that a problem or a feature?"**
    Use to distinguish healthy attrition (misfit departures) from unhealthy attrition (culture-driven exodus).

## Evolving Culture

11. **"What cultural trait helped us get here but will hurt us going forward?"**
    Use during growth transitions. The scrappy, do-everything mentality that builds a startup can destroy a scaling company.

12. **"If we could change one thing about how we work together, what would have the biggest impact?"**
    Use to surface the single highest-leverage cultural intervention. One change is more achievable and more measurable than ten.

13. **"What meeting behaviors would we change if we watched a recording of ourselves?"**
    Use to create self-awareness about communication patterns. Most teams are unaware of their own dysfunctions.

14. **"Are we hiring people who will reinforce our culture or challenge it? Which do we need right now?"**
    Use when defining hiring criteria. Both reinforcement and challenge have value — the question is which one is more needed at this stage.

15. **"What would a culture of genuine accountability look like here — not punishment, but ownership?"**
    Use to reframe accountability positively. Many organizations confuse accountability with blame, which makes people hide problems.

## Psychological Safety

16. **"When was the last time someone shared bad news early? What happened to them?"**
    Use to test whether the environment encourages or punishes transparency. Early bad news is a gift; organizations that shoot the messenger stop receiving gifts.

17. **"Is it safe to say 'I don't know' in this organization?"**
    Use to assess intellectual honesty norms. If leaders cannot admit uncertainty, the organization will fill the gap with fiction.

18. **"What is the worst thing that could happen to someone who makes an honest mistake here?"**
    Use to calibrate the organization's relationship with failure. Learning cultures treat honest mistakes as data. Fear cultures treat them as crimes.

19. **"Do people here ask for help, or do they suffer in silence?"**
    Use to assess vulnerability norms. Asking for help requires trust. If people don't ask, they don't trust.

20. **"When decisions are made, do people leave the room feeling heard — even if they disagreed?"**
    Use to assess inclusion in decision-making. People can accept decisions they disagree with if they felt genuinely heard in the process.
