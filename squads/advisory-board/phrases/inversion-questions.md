# Inversion Questions

Phrases that flip the question to reveal what forward-looking analysis misses. Instead of asking "How do we succeed?" ask "How do we fail?" and then avoid those paths.

## Classic Inversions

1. **"Instead of asking how to make this succeed, let's ask: how could we guarantee this fails?"**
   Use to launch a pre-mortem exercise. Listing failure modes is easier and more productive than predicting success.

2. **"What would we have to do to destroy the most value in this company in the shortest time?"**
   Use as a thought experiment to identify the highest-leverage risks. The inverse of the answer reveals the highest-leverage protections.

3. **"If we wanted our best people to leave, what would we do? Are we doing any of those things?"**
   Use during culture or retention discussions. The answers are usually painfully obvious and immediately actionable.

4. **"What would make a customer choose our competitor over us today?"**
   Use to identify competitive vulnerabilities from the customer's perspective, not from internal assumptions.

5. **"If we wanted to ensure this partnership fails, what terms would we negotiate?"**
   Use during deal negotiation to identify toxic terms. Then check whether any of those terms are in the current proposal.

## Strategic Inversions

6. **"What would make this market unattractive to enter? Do those conditions exist?"**
   Use before committing to a new market. The inverse reveals barriers, risks, and unfavorable economics that enthusiasm may mask.

7. **"If we wanted to make sure nobody uses this product, how would we design it?"**
   Use in product strategy discussions. Common answers: make onboarding complex, hide the value, require 10 steps for the core action. Then check if the product does any of those things.

8. **"What would cause this strategy to be completely wrong in two years?"**
   Use to identify the assumptions most vulnerable to change. These are the assumptions that need monitoring, not just documentation.

9. **"If we were our own competitor, where would we attack us?"**
   Use to identify defensive priorities. The weakest point in your position is where the attack will come.

10. **"What is the one thing that, if it stopped working, would collapse the business?"**
    Use to identify existential dependencies. The answer reveals the single point of failure that deserves the most protection.

## Operational Inversions

11. **"If we wanted to slow down decision-making, what processes would we add?"**
    Use when evaluating process complexity. If the answers sound like your current processes, you have a problem.

12. **"If we wanted to ensure nobody reads the pre-read, how would we format it?"**
    Use to audit communication effectiveness. Make it long, dense, jargon-filled, and poorly structured. Then check your current pre-reads.

13. **"What would make this meeting a complete waste of everyone's time?"**
    Use before designing an agenda. Common answers: no clear purpose, no decisions, wrong people in the room, no pre-reads, no follow-up.

14. **"If we wanted to maximize team dysfunction, what would we do to this org structure?"**
    Use during org design discussions. The inverse reveals the structural failure modes to avoid.

15. **"What process, if we removed it entirely, would nobody notice?"**
    Use during simplification sprints. If removal causes no harm, the process is not adding value.

## People Inversions

16. **"What would cause our best hire to fail in this role?"**
    Use when onboarding new executives. Design the onboarding plan to prevent every failure mode on the list.

17. **"If we wanted to build a toxic culture, what would we reward?"**
    Use during culture audits. Then check what you actually reward. The gap between the two lists reveals cultural risk.

18. **"What kind of person should we absolutely not hire for this role?"**
    Use to sharpen hiring criteria. The anti-profile is often more revealing than the ideal profile.

19. **"What would cause this team to lose all trust in leadership?"**
    Use when preparing difficult communications. The answers guide both what to say and how to say it.

20. **"If we wanted advisors to disengage from this board, what would we do?"**
    Use during board effectiveness reviews. Common answers: ignore their advice, waste their time with poor preparation, make decisions before asking for input.

## How to Use Inversions in Practice

1. State the goal: "We want to [X]."
2. Invert: "How would we guarantee we don't [X]?"
3. List the failure modes (aim for 5-10).
4. Check: are we doing any of these things currently?
5. For each failure mode we are exhibiting, design a specific fix.
6. For each failure mode we are not exhibiting, add it to the watch list.

The power of inversion is that failure modes are concrete and specific, while success is abstract and general. Working backward from failure produces more actionable insight than working forward toward success.
