# Product-Market Fit Evidence Checklist

## Purpose
Provides a rigorous framework for assessing whether the organization has achieved or is approaching product-market fit. PMF is not a binary state; it is a spectrum. This checklist evaluates the quality and quantity of evidence for PMF rather than relying on gut feeling or a single metric.

## When to Use
- Before committing significant resources to scaling a product.
- During product reviews when growth stalls or churn increases.
- When evaluating whether to invest more or pivot.
- When presenting PMF claims to the board or investors.

## Gate Criteria
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer retention (cohort-based) exceeds the threshold for the product category.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customers would be "very disappointed" if the product disappeared (Sean Ellis test > 40%).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Organic growth exists: customers refer others without incentive.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Usage depth increases over time (customers use more features, more frequently).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customers pay and renew without heavy discounting or concessions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer acquisition becomes easier over time, not harder.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer feedback is about expanding the product, not about fixing basic functionality.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The product solves a real problem confirmed by customer behavior, not just stated intent.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: PMF evidence exists across multiple customer segments, not just early adopters.

## Red Flags
- Growth requires heavy discounting or free tier extensions.
- Customers sign up but do not actively use the product.
- Every customer requires custom work to get value.
- The product team is in constant feature request mode with no stable core.
- Customer descriptions of the product's value vary wildly.

## Escalation Triggers
- Retention drops below the category benchmark for two consecutive cohorts.
- Organic referral rate declines quarter over quarter.
- The Sean Ellis test score drops below 40%.
- Customer acquisition cost increases for three consecutive quarters.
