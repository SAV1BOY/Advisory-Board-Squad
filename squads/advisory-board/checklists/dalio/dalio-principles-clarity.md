# Dalio Principles Clarity Checklist

## Purpose
Verifies that the organization has codified its operating principles and that these principles are understood, referenced, and applied consistently in decision-making. Inspired by Ray Dalio's practice at Bridgewater of writing down principles so they can be debated, refined, and systematically applied. Without explicit principles, organizations make decisions inconsistently and cannot learn from their patterns.

## When to Use
- When establishing or updating the organization's operating principles.
- During onboarding of new leaders to assess their understanding of principles.
- When a decision outcome suggests principles were not followed.
- Annually to review whether principles remain relevant and complete.

## Gate Criteria

### Documentation and Accessibility
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Operating principles are written down in a single, accessible document.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each principle includes the reasoning behind it, not just the statement.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Principles are organized by domain (decision-making, conflict resolution, hiring, strategy).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The document is version-controlled with a changelog showing how principles have evolved.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Every team member knows where to find the principles document.

### Understanding and Adoption
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders can articulate the top five principles without consulting the document.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Principles are referenced in decision memos and meeting discussions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: New hires are trained on principles with examples and case studies.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Team members can identify which principle applies to a given situation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Performance reviews include assessment of adherence to principles.

### Application and Testing
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Principles have been tested against real decisions and refined based on outcomes.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Conflicting principles are identified and a hierarchy or resolution framework exists.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Exceptions to principles are documented with the rationale for the exception.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A feedback mechanism allows anyone to challenge or propose amendments to principles.

### Continuous Improvement
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Principles are reviewed at least annually for relevance and completeness.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Post-mortems reference principles to assess whether they were followed and whether they were correct.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: New principles are added when recurring situations lack guidance.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Obsolete or counterproductive principles are retired explicitly.

## Red Flags
- Principles exist on a poster but are never referenced in actual decision-making.
- Leadership cannot articulate the principles or disagrees about what they are.
- Principles are so generic ("be excellent") that they provide no practical guidance.
- Principles have not been updated since the company's founding despite material changes.
- Exceptions to principles are frequent and not documented, suggesting the principles are aspirational fiction.
- The principles document was written by one person without team input or debate.

## Escalation Triggers
- A major decision is made that directly contradicts a stated principle with no acknowledgment.
- New hires report they were unaware of operating principles after 90 days.
- A post-mortem reveals that a failure could have been prevented by applying existing principles.
- A leadership conflict reveals fundamentally different interpretations of core principles.
- Employee surveys indicate principles are perceived as performative rather than genuine.
