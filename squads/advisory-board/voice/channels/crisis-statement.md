# Channel Guide: Crisis Statement

## When to Use

A crisis statement is a formal public or semi-public communication issued during a material crisis. It is the company's official voice to affected stakeholders. Use it for:

- Security incidents or data breaches.
- Product failures or safety issues.
- Leadership misconduct.
- Regulatory actions.
- Financial disclosures during crisis events.
- Any situation where stakeholders need to hear from the company formally.

## Format

### Structure

```
[COMPANY NAME] STATEMENT REGARDING [EVENT]
[Date] | [Time, if time-sensitive]

[Opening: What happened — facts only]

[Impact: Who is affected and how]

[Response: What we are doing about it]

[Support: What affected parties can do / where to get help]

[Commitment: What happens next and when to expect updates]

[Contact information]
```

### Length
- External/public: 150-300 words. Brevity is critical.
- Customer notification: 300-500 words. More detail is appropriate.
- Employee communication: 400-600 words. Context and empathy warrant more space.
- Regulatory filing: Per requirements.

## Writing Rules

### Opening (1-2 sentences)
- State what happened in plain language.
- Use active voice. "We discovered..." not "It was discovered..."
- Include the date and time if relevant.
- Do not editorialize. Facts only.

**Yes:** "On March 8, 2026, we discovered unauthorized access to a database containing customer account information."
**No:** "We regret to inform you that an unfortunate cybersecurity event occurred."

### Impact (2-3 sentences)
- State who is affected, specifically.
- State what data, services, or operations were impacted.
- State what was NOT affected if that reduces uncertainty.
- Do not minimize. Do not exaggerate.

**Yes:** "Approximately 12,000 customer accounts were potentially exposed. The data included names and email addresses. Payment information was not stored in the affected database and was not compromised."
**No:** "A small number of accounts may have been affected by this incident."

### Response (3-5 sentences)
- State what concrete actions have been taken.
- Use past tense for completed actions, present tense for ongoing actions.
- Be specific about the response, not generic.
- Name the types of external support engaged (forensic investigators, legal counsel) without naming specific firms.

**Yes:** "We immediately isolated the affected system, engaged a leading cybersecurity firm to conduct a forensic investigation, and notified law enforcement. We have implemented additional monitoring on all systems. Affected customers are being notified individually."
**No:** "We are taking this very seriously and have implemented enhanced security measures."

### Support (2-3 sentences)
- Tell affected parties exactly what they should do.
- Provide a specific resource: phone number, email, web page.
- If offering remediation (credit monitoring, refunds, etc.), state it clearly.

**Yes:** "If your account was affected, you will receive a direct notification with instructions for securing your account. We are providing 12 months of free credit monitoring. For questions, contact our dedicated support line: [number]."

### Commitment (1-2 sentences)
- State when the next update will come.
- Be specific about the date/time.
- State the channel (email, website, press release).

**Yes:** "We will provide an update by March 12, 2026, with the results of our investigation. Updates will be posted at [URL] and sent directly to affected customers."

## Tone Calibration

| Dimension | Target |
|---|---|
| Empathy | High. Acknowledge human impact genuinely. |
| Accountability | High. Own what happened. No blame-shifting. |
| Specificity | High. Facts, numbers, dates, actions. |
| Legal caution | Moderate. Don't admit liability prematurely, but don't sound lawyerly. |
| Corporate-speak | Zero. Plain language, short sentences. |
| Defensiveness | Zero. No "but" or "however" after an acknowledgment. |

## Words and Phrases

### Use
- "We discovered..."
- "We are taking the following actions..."
- "Approximately [number] customers were affected..."
- "We are sorry for the concern this causes."
- "We will update you by [date]."
- "Here is what you can do..."

### Avoid
- "We take security very seriously" (show, don't tell).
- "There is no evidence that..." (sounds like you haven't looked hard enough).
- "We apologize for any inconvenience" (crisis is not an "inconvenience").
- "At this time" (implies you'll change the story later).
- "Isolated incident" (unless forensically confirmed).
- "Moving forward" (focus on now, not on getting past it).

## Review Chain

Before any crisis statement is released:

1. **Author** (usually Communications lead) drafts.
2. **Legal counsel** reviews for liability exposure and regulatory compliance.
3. **Board-Chair** reviews for accuracy, tone, and strategic alignment.
4. **CEO** approves and signs.
5. **Governance-Ops** manages distribution and logging.

Turnaround target: 2 hours from draft to approval. For critical crises, compress to 1 hour.

## Post-Release

- Monitor response across all channels.
- Track incoming questions and prepare FAQ.
- Do not engage in social media debates about the statement.
- Prepare the follow-up statement immediately — do not wait until the deadline.
- Archive the statement, all review comments, and the distribution log.
