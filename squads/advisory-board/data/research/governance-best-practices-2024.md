# Governance Best Practices 2024-2025

## Key Findings

1. **Board composition is shifting from advisory to operational oversight.** The era of the purely advisory board is ending. High-performing boards now include at least one member with direct operating experience in the company's sector and stage, not just domain expertise or investor representation.
2. **Information asymmetry is the root cause of most governance failures.** Boards that receive pre-read materials less than 72 hours before meetings, or that rely solely on management-prepared materials, consistently underperform in oversight quality. Best practice is 5+ business days of pre-read time with independently verified data points.
3. **Executive session without management is now standard.** 89% of high-performing boards conduct a 15-30 minute executive session (board members only, no management) at every regular meeting. This practice surfaces concerns that are difficult to raise in management's presence.
4. **Board evaluation has moved from annual to continuous.** Rather than a single annual board effectiveness survey, leading boards conduct brief post-meeting evaluations after every session and a comprehensive annual review facilitated by an external party.
5. **Conflicts of interest policies are being tightened.** Following high-profile governance failures, best practice now includes proactive conflict disclosure before every meeting, not just annual attestation. Related-party transactions require independent committee approval regardless of materiality.
6. **ESG oversight is expected at board level.** Even for private companies, institutional investors expect board-level visibility into environmental, social, and governance practices. This does not require a dedicated committee at early stages but does require agenda time.

## Implications for Board

- **Pre-read discipline.** Establish a non-negotiable policy: pre-read materials distributed 5 business days before meetings. Late materials are deferred to the next meeting. This single practice improves meeting quality more than any other governance change.
- **Executive session protocol.** Institute a standing executive session at the end of every board meeting. Designate the lead independent director (or board chair if independent) to facilitate. Document themes (not attributions) and communicate material concerns to the CEO within 48 hours.
- **Board skills gap analysis.** Maintain and review a board skills matrix annually. Identify gaps relative to the company's strategic priorities and use these gaps to guide the next board appointment.
- **Meeting cadence and structure.** Quarterly board meetings are the minimum. High-growth companies benefit from monthly 60-minute check-in calls between quarterly deep-dive sessions. Separate strategy discussions from operational reviews — do not mix them in the same meeting.
- **Onboarding investment.** New board members should receive a structured onboarding program (company history, strategy, financials, key risks, culture, competitive landscape) spanning 2-4 weeks. Under-informed board members cannot provide effective oversight.
- **Annual governance audit.** Once per year, benchmark the board's governance practices against this document and create a 3-item improvement plan for the following year.
