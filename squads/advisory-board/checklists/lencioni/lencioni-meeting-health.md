# Lencioni Meeting Health Checklist

## Purpose
Evaluates whether the organization's meeting structure follows Lencioni's model of differentiated meeting types, each designed for a specific purpose. Lencioni argues that most meetings fail because they try to accomplish too many different things in one session. Effective meeting architecture separates daily check-ins, weekly tactical meetings, monthly strategic meetings, and quarterly offsites, each with distinct rules and rhythms.

## When to Use
- When meetings feel unproductive and people complain about meeting overload.
- When important decisions are not being made because there is no appropriate forum.
- When resetting organizational meeting culture.
- During annual reviews of organizational effectiveness.
- When meeting satisfaction scores are low.

## Gate Criteria

### Meeting Architecture
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A daily standup or check-in exists for operational alignment (5-10 minutes maximum).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A weekly tactical meeting exists for reviewing priorities and removing obstacles (45-90 minutes).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A monthly or biweekly strategic meeting exists for in-depth discussion of one or two topics (2-4 hours).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A quarterly offsite exists for team building, strategic review, and organizational assessment.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each meeting type has a distinct purpose and rules that are understood by participants.

### Tactical Meeting Discipline
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Tactical meetings start with a lightning round of priorities and end with action items.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Tactical meetings do not devolve into strategic discussions (strategic topics are parking-lotted).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Tactical meetings review the scorecard of key metrics.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Tactical meetings are decision-oriented, not just information-sharing.

### Strategic Meeting Depth
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Strategic meetings focus on one or two topics with enough time for genuine debate.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Preparation materials are distributed in advance for strategic meetings.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Strategic meetings produce clear decisions or next steps, not just discussion.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Strategic topics are selected based on importance, not urgency.

### Offsite Quality
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Quarterly offsites include personal check-ins, strategic review, team assessment, and planning.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Offsites address team health and dynamics, not just business topics.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Offsites produce commitments that are tracked in subsequent meetings.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The offsite location and format support the objective (not a conference room relabeled as an offsite).

### Meeting Hygiene
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Meetings start and end on time.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Attendee lists are intentional: only people who need to be there are invited.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Action items from all meetings are captured and tracked.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Meeting effectiveness is assessed periodically by participants.

## Red Flags
- All meetings have the same structure regardless of purpose.
- The leadership team spends more than 25% of their time in meetings that do not produce decisions.
- Strategic topics are discussed in 15-minute agenda items with no resolution.
- Daily standups become 30-minute discussions.
- The team has never had an offsite, or offsites are vacation-like with no actionable outcomes.
- People attend meetings out of obligation, not because they contribute or benefit.

## Escalation Triggers
- Decision-making is visibly slowed because there is no appropriate meeting forum.
- Meeting overload is cited as a top concern in employee satisfaction surveys.
- Critical issues are not addressed for weeks because meetings are consumed by tactical items.
- Team health deteriorates because no forum exists for addressing interpersonal dynamics.
- Meeting quality has been raised as an issue but no structural changes have been made.
