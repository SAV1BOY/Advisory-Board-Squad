# Chouinard Mission Over Margin

## Purpose

Yvon Chouinard, founder of Patagonia, demonstrates that prioritizing mission over short-term margin creates a more durable, more valuable, and more resilient business. This framework helps leaders align business decisions with a core environmental or social mission without sacrificing long-term profitability — and often enhancing it. The counterintuitive truth: companies that genuinely prioritize mission often outperform purely profit-driven competitors because mission creates brand loyalty, employee engagement, and customer trust that money cannot buy.

## When to Use

- When business decisions conflict with stated mission or values
- When building a mission-driven company from the ground up
- When evaluating whether a company's mission is genuine or performative
- When short-term profit pressure threatens long-term brand trust
- When advising founders who want to build purpose-driven organizations

## Core Model

### The Chouinard Principle
"I never wanted to be a businessman. But when I saw how business could be used as a tool for change, I decided to use Patagonia as an experiment — to see if we could make the best products while doing the least harm."

### Mission-Margin Integration (Not Tradeoff)
- Mission drives product quality → Premium pricing → Higher margins
- Mission drives employee loyalty → Lower turnover → Lower costs
- Mission drives customer trust → Lower CAC, higher LTV
- Mission drives brand differentiation → Competitive moat
- The apparent tradeoff between mission and margin is a false dichotomy over longer time horizons

### The Patagonia Playbook
1. **Build the best product.** Quality is the first commitment. A product that fails serves no mission.
2. **Cause no unnecessary harm.** Evaluate every business decision through the lens of environmental and social impact.
3. **Use business to inspire and implement solutions.** The business is a vehicle for the mission, not the other way around.
4. **Be transparent about failures.** Patagonia publishes its supply chain flaws. Transparency builds trust.

## Steps

1. **Define the mission.** One sentence: what change are you trying to create in the world?
2. **Audit every business decision against the mission.** For each decision, ask: "Does this advance or compromise the mission?"
3. **Accept short-term costs for long-term alignment.** Mission-margin conflicts will arise. Choose mission when the long-term brand value exceeds the short-term cost.
4. **Measure mission impact alongside financial performance.** If you only measure profit, you will only optimize for profit.
5. **Communicate the mission constantly.** Internally and externally. Repetition builds belief.
6. **Hold yourself accountable publicly.** Publish mission metrics. Admit failures. Transparency is the price of credibility.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Help founders distinguish between genuine mission and marketing. The test: would they pursue this mission if it meant less profit?
- When margin pressure mounts, advocate for mission preservation. Short-term margin sacrifice for mission integrity is almost always the right long-term decision.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Mission as marketing.** Using mission language without mission commitment. Customers and employees detect this instantly.
2. **Mission without quality.** A mission-driven product that doesn't work is just a bad product with good intentions.
3. **Ignoring financial sustainability.** Mission without margin is a hobby. The business must be financially sustainable to serve the mission.
4. **Changing the mission when convenient.** The mission is permanent. Tactics change; mission does not.
