# Sivers Hell Yes or No Checklist

## Purpose
Applies Derek Sivers' decision filter: if a decision does not feel like "hell yes," then the answer is no. This principle protects against the accumulation of mediocre commitments that crowd out exceptional ones. Organizations that say yes to everything end up spread thin, overwhelmed, and unable to execute on the things that truly matter. This checklist provides discipline for saying no to good opportunities so that great ones have room.

## When to Use
- When evaluating new initiatives, partnerships, or commitments.
- When the organization feels overcommitted and unable to focus.
- When a new opportunity competes for existing resources.
- During annual priority-setting to cull the initiative list.
- When decision-makers feel pressure to say yes to avoid conflict or missing out.

## Gate Criteria

### Enthusiasm Test
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The decision-maker(s) feel genuine excitement about the opportunity, not just obligation or fear of missing out.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The opportunity aligns with the organization's stated priorities, not just its capability.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team can articulate why this opportunity is better than alternatives for the same resources.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The opportunity is worth the displacement cost: what will not get done if this is approved.

### Opportunity Cost Assessment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The resources required (time, money, attention) are explicitly quantified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: What must be deprioritized or stopped to pursue this opportunity is identified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team has assessed whether the displaced activities are less valuable than the new opportunity.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The opportunity is compared against doing nothing and using the resources for existing commitments.

### Commitment Discipline
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The number of active initiatives is within the organization's demonstrated capacity.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Every "yes" includes a corresponding "no" to maintain focus.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization tracks its win rate: do things it says yes to actually produce exceptional results.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: There is a defined process for saying no gracefully without burning bridges.

### Review and Pruning
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Existing commitments are reviewed periodically and those that no longer feel like "hell yes" are reconsidered.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Sunk cost does not keep the organization committed to declining-value activities.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization's capacity for new commitments is known and respected.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Past decisions are reviewed: did the "hell yes" opportunities outperform the reluctant ones.

## Red Flags
- The organization has more active initiatives than it can staff with its best people.
- Leaders say yes to avoid confrontation or to please stakeholders.
- The word "strategic" is used to justify commitments that have no clear return.
- New opportunities are added but nothing is ever removed.
- The team cannot name what they will stop doing to make room for new work.
- Fear of missing out (FOMO) drives more decisions than genuine enthusiasm.

## Escalation Triggers
- The organization is spread so thin that none of its initiatives are succeeding.
- Key talent is burned out from managing too many concurrent priorities.
- A genuinely exceptional opportunity cannot be pursued because all resources are committed to mediocre ones.
- The leadership team cannot agree on what to stop doing to free capacity.
- The organization's initiative failure rate exceeds 50%, suggesting too many marginal commitments.
