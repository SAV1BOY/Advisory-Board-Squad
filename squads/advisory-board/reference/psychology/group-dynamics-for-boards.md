# Group Dynamics for Boards

## Overview
Boards are small groups, and they're subject to all the dynamics—productive and destructive—that govern small-group behavior. Understanding these dynamics is the difference between a high-performing board and an expensive rubber stamp.

## Constructive Group Dynamics

### Psychological Safety (Edmondson)
- Team members feel safe to take interpersonal risks: disagree, admit mistakes, ask questions
- **Board marker:** Members freely express concern about a proposal without fear of social penalty
- **How to build:** Board chair models vulnerability. Explicitly reward dissent. Never punish honest mistakes.

### Productive Conflict
- Task-focused disagreement that improves decision quality
- **Board marker:** Vigorous debate about strategy that remains respectful and data-driven
- **How to cultivate:** Separate idea evaluation from person evaluation. Argue positions, not people.

### Cognitive Diversity
- Different thinking styles, backgrounds, and expertise create better collective judgment
- **Board marker:** Members regularly introduce perspectives others hadn't considered
- **How to cultivate:** Diverse composition by background, expertise, age, gender, and thinking style.

## Destructive Group Dynamics

### Groupthink (Janis)
- Desire for harmony overrides realistic evaluation of alternatives
- **Symptoms:** Illusion of invulnerability, collective rationalization, stereotyping of opponents, self-censorship, illusion of unanimity, direct pressure on dissenters
- **Prevention:** Devil's advocate role, anonymous voting, external perspectives, explicit encouragement of dissent

### Social Loafing
- Individual effort decreases in group settings
- **Board marker:** Members come unprepared, contribute minimally, defer to others
- **Prevention:** Individual accountability (assigned questions, rotating presentations), regular evaluation

### Authority Gradient
- Deference to the most senior, wealthiest, or most prestigious member
- **Board marker:** When the board chair or lead investor speaks first, others align. Silence from junior members.
- **Prevention:** Junior members speak first. Written pre-meeting positions. Structured round-robin discussions.

### Abilene Paradox
- A group collectively decides on a course of action that no individual member actually wants
- **Board marker:** Everyone agrees to a proposal, but in private conversations, several express doubt
- **Prevention:** Anonymous polling before final votes. Explicit permission to say "I'm not enthusiastic about this."

### Polarization
- Groups tend to make more extreme decisions than individuals
- **Board marker:** The board takes a more aggressive (or more conservative) position than any individual member would independently
- **Prevention:** Document individual positions before group discussion. Compare individual and group outcomes.

## Structural Interventions

### Meeting Design
- **Pre-meeting written input:** Each member submits independent views before discussion begins
- **Round-robin:** Each member speaks in turn before open discussion
- **Anonymous voting:** For sensitive decisions, use anonymous polling to prevent conformity pressure
- **Executive session:** Regular time without management to discuss sensitive dynamics

### Role Assignment
- **Chair/Facilitator:** Manages discussion, ensures all voices are heard
- **Devil's Advocate:** Assigned to argue against the prevailing view
- **Process Observer:** Monitors group dynamics and calls out patterns
- **Timekeeper:** Prevents any single topic or person from dominating

## Board Application
- Conduct an annual assessment of board group dynamics
- The board chair is responsible for managing dynamics, not just agenda
- When decisions are too easy (quick unanimity), investigate whether groupthink is at work
- Invest in board relationships outside of meetings; trust built informally enables productive conflict formally
