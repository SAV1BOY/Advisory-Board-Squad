# Partnership Negotiation Patterns

## Overview
Strategic partnerships—with distribution partners, technology partners, co-development partners, and channel partners—require different negotiation approaches than M&A or employment. This file covers common partnership structures, negotiation patterns, and governance frameworks.

## Partnership Types

### Distribution Partnership
- **Structure:** One party provides the product, the other provides access to customers
- **Key terms:** Revenue share (typically 15-30% to distributor), exclusivity, territory, minimum commitments
- **Risk:** Dependency on partner for customer access; loss of direct customer relationship

### Technology Partnership
- **Structure:** Two parties integrate their technologies to create a combined offering
- **Key terms:** API access, data sharing, co-development costs, IP ownership, maintenance obligations
- **Risk:** Technical dependency; partner's roadmap changes breaking integration

### Co-Marketing Partnership
- **Structure:** Two parties jointly market to their combined audiences
- **Key terms:** Lead sharing, brand guidelines, cost allocation, measurement, duration
- **Risk:** Brand association; one partner's reputation affecting the other

### Strategic Alliance
- **Structure:** Deep collaboration across multiple dimensions (technology, distribution, co-development)
- **Key terms:** Governance structure, investment terms, IP sharing, non-compete, exit mechanisms
- **Risk:** Complexity, alignment drift over time, competitive dynamics

## Negotiation Patterns

### The Pilot-to-Scale Pattern
- Start with a small, time-limited pilot with clear success criteria
- If successful, expand to full partnership with negotiated terms
- **Advantage:** Reduces risk for both sides; creates real data for negotiation
- **Board role:** Approve pilot criteria and ensure expansion terms are pre-negotiated

### The Mutual Hostage Pattern
- Each side makes investments specific to the partnership that would be lost if it ends
- **Example:** Co-developed IP, shared infrastructure, integrated systems
- **Advantage:** Creates mutual commitment; neither side wants to walk away
- **Risk:** If the partnership sours, unwinding is painful. Negotiate exit terms upfront.

### The Option Pattern
- Structure the deal as a series of options: initial collaboration → deeper integration → potential acquisition
- **Advantage:** Flexibility; each stage unlocks the next only if value is demonstrated
- **Board role:** Ensure each option has clear strike conditions and that the company isn't giving away too much optionality cheaply

## Governance Framework

### Partnership Steering Committee
- Representatives from both sides meeting regularly (monthly/quarterly)
- **Responsibilities:** Review performance metrics, resolve operational issues, plan joint roadmap
- **Escalation path:** Clear process for escalating disputes to executives and ultimately to boards

### Key Performance Metrics
- Revenue generated through partnership
- Customer satisfaction with the integrated offering
- Operational efficiency (time to resolve issues, integration uptime)
- Strategic value (market access, brand enhancement, data shared)

### Exit Mechanisms
- **Termination for convenience:** Either party can exit with 90-180 day notice
- **Termination for cause:** Immediate termination for material breach
- **Transition support:** Obligations to support the other party's transition to alternatives
- **IP disentanglement:** Clear rules for separating jointly created assets

## Board Application
- Every significant partnership should have board awareness if not board approval
- Evaluate partnerships for strategic fit, not just incremental revenue
- Ensure exit mechanisms are negotiated upfront, not when the relationship is deteriorating
- Monitor partnership health quarterly; partnerships that drift often become liabilities
