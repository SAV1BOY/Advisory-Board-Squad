# Fundraising Patterns

## Stage-by-Stage Patterns

### Pre-Seed ($100K-$1M)
- **Sources:** Personal savings, friends and family, angel investors, pre-seed funds
- **What you need:** A compelling vision, a credible founding team, early signal of market demand
- **Typical terms:** SAFE or convertible note, $3-8M cap
- **Board impact:** Usually no formal board seats; advisory relationships begin

### Seed ($1-4M)
- **Sources:** Seed funds, angel syndicates, some Series A funds doing seed
- **What you need:** Product prototype or MVP, early user/customer traction, clear target market
- **Typical terms:** Priced round or SAFE, $8-20M post-money valuation
- **Board impact:** Lead investor may take a board seat or observer seat

### Series A ($5-15M)
- **Sources:** Series A venture funds
- **What you need:** Product-market fit evidence, repeatable customer acquisition, $1-3M ARR trajectory
- **Typical terms:** Preferred equity, $20-60M post-money, 1x non-participating liquidation preference standard
- **Board impact:** Lead investor takes a board seat; formal board governance begins

### Series B ($15-50M)
- **Sources:** Growth-stage venture funds, existing investors following on
- **What you need:** Proven unit economics, scalable go-to-market, path to profitability visible
- **Typical terms:** Preferred equity, $80-250M post-money
- **Board impact:** Additional investor director; independent director expected

### Series C+ and Growth ($50M+)
- **Sources:** Growth equity firms, crossover funds, sovereign wealth
- **What you need:** Market leadership position, strong unit economics, large addressable market
- **Typical terms:** Varies widely; may include secondary sales, structured terms
- **Board impact:** Board professionalization; majority independent directors expected

## Key Term Sheet Concepts

### Valuation and Dilution
- **Pre-money vs. post-money:** Post-money = Pre-money + New investment. Dilution = New shares / Total shares post-investment.
- **Option pool shuffle:** Investors often require an option pool increase pre-money, which dilutes founders more than the headline valuation suggests.

### Liquidation Preferences
- **1x non-participating:** Investor gets their money back OR converts to common (whichever is better). Standard and founder-friendly.
- **1x participating:** Investor gets their money back AND converts to common. More investor-friendly; doubles their return in moderate outcomes.
- **Multiple liquidation preferences:** 2x or 3x preferences. Significant downside for founders; avoid if possible.

### Governance Terms
- Board composition and voting rights
- Protective provisions (investor veto on specific actions)
- Information rights (financial reporting requirements)
- Pro-rata rights (right to invest in future rounds)

## Fundraising Strategy

### Timing
- Raise when you don't need to (you have leverage)
- The best time to raise is when you're growing fast and have 12+ months of runway
- Never let runway drop below 6 months without a funded plan

### Process
- Build relationships with investors 6-12 months before you need capital
- Run a structured process (not sequential conversations) to create competitive pressure
- Target 10-15 introductory meetings, aim for 3-5 term sheets
- The best investors add value beyond money: network, recruiting, strategy, credibility

## Board Application
- Advise founders on fundraising timing and strategy
- Review term sheets for unfavorable governance or economic terms
- Ensure the company doesn't accept unfavorable terms out of desperation
- Help founders understand dilution implications across multiple rounds
- Maintain relationships with potential investors as a board resource
