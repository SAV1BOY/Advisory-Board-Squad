# Leadership Alignment Check Checklist

## Purpose
Assesses whether the leadership team is genuinely aligned on strategy, priorities, and operating norms. Misalignment at the top cascades through the entire organization, creating confusion, conflicting priorities, and wasted effort. This checklist detects alignment gaps that leadership teams often cannot see from within.

## When to Use
- After strategic planning to verify alignment on decisions made.
- When cross-functional execution problems suggest leadership misalignment.
- When new leaders join the executive team.
- Quarterly as part of leadership team health assessment.
- When employee surveys indicate confusion about direction.

## Gate Criteria

### Strategic Alignment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: All leaders can state the company's top three priorities consistently.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: All leaders can explain the strategy in their own words and the explanations are compatible.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Resource allocation across departments reflects agreed-upon strategic priorities.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders do not pursue department-level strategies that conflict with company strategy.

### Operational Alignment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Cross-functional dependencies are acknowledged and managed collaboratively.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders resolve conflicts among themselves rather than escalating to the CEO.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Meeting commitments and action items from one leader to another are honored.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Messaging to employees from different leaders is consistent.

### Values and Behavioral Alignment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders model the same values and behaviors they expect from their teams.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Performance standards are consistent across departments.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders support each other publicly and resolve disagreements privately.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The leadership team has agreed on behavioral norms and holds each other accountable.

### Alignment Maintenance
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Regular alignment check-ins occur (not just annual planning).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: New information that could affect alignment is shared promptly among leaders.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The CEO actively manages alignment rather than assuming it.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Alignment is assessed through observable behavior, not just stated agreement.

## Red Flags
- Leaders give different explanations of company strategy to their teams.
- Cross-functional projects stall because leaders disagree on priorities.
- The CEO is the only connector between otherwise siloed executives.
- Leaders publicly agree but privately pursue different agendas.
- Employee surveys indicate confusion about direction or conflicting mandates.

## Escalation Triggers
- Strategic misalignment causes a material execution failure.
- Two leaders' teams are working at cross-purposes on a significant initiative.
- A new executive is not aligned within 90 days and the gap is not addressed.
- Employee engagement drops in teams led by misaligned executives.
- The CEO cannot resolve a leadership alignment issue and it persists for over 60 days.
