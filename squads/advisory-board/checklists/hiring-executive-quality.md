# Hiring Executive Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures that executive hiring decisions are made with the rigor they deserve, given that a wrong executive hire is one of the most costly and disruptive mistakes an organization can make. This checklist prevents the common failure modes of hiring for charisma over competence, cultural fit over cultural contribution, and credentials over demonstrated results.

## When to Use
- When hiring any C-suite, VP, or equivalent senior leadership role.
- When promoting an internal candidate to an executive position.
- When evaluating whether an executive search firm's candidate slate is adequate.
- During 90-day reviews of newly hired executives.

## Gate Criteria

### Role Definition
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A written role scorecard exists that defines outcomes expected in the first 12 months, not just responsibilities.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The scorecard distinguishes between must-have and nice-to-have competencies.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The role's reporting structure, decision authority, and budget responsibility are specified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The cultural attributes needed are described in behavioral terms, not abstract values.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The role definition reflects input from the CEO, board, and key cross-functional peers.

### Candidate Assessment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: At least three finalist candidates have been evaluated (internal and external).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each candidate has been assessed against the scorecard with evidence-based ratings.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Structured interviews were conducted using behavioral and situational questions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: At least one interview probed for past failures and what the candidate learned.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A work-sample or case exercise relevant to the actual role was included.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The interview panel included perspectives from direct reports, peers, and the hiring manager.

### Reference and Background
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: At least five references were checked, including back-channel references not provided by the candidate.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: References include former direct reports, peers, and supervisors (360-degree reference check).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Specific questions about weaknesses, conflict style, and performance under pressure were asked.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Any gaps or inconsistencies in the candidate's history have been satisfactorily explained.

### Strategic Fit
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The candidate's leadership style matches what the organization needs at its current stage.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The candidate has demonstrated the ability to operate at the required scale (team size, revenue, complexity).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The candidate's values alignment has been assessed through scenario-based questions, not self-reporting.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The candidate brings capabilities the current team lacks, not just more of the same.

### Onboarding and Integration
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A structured 90-day onboarding plan exists with milestones and check-ins.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Success metrics for the first 6 and 12 months are defined before the hire starts.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A board member or senior leader is assigned as an onboarding sponsor.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The compensation structure aligns with long-term incentives and is approved by the compensation committee.

## Red Flags
- The hiring decision is driven by urgency rather than fit ("we need someone now").
- Only one candidate was seriously considered.
- References are glowing with no nuance or mention of weaknesses.
- The candidate's track record is from organizations at a very different scale or stage.
- The candidate cannot articulate specific failures and lessons learned.
- The hiring manager overrides the structured scorecard based on gut feeling.
- No internal candidates were considered for the role.

## Escalation Triggers
- A finalist candidate has a material undisclosed conflict of interest.
- Reference checks reveal a pattern of behavior inconsistent with the candidate's self-representation.
- The new executive fails to meet 90-day milestones and is not receiving corrective coaching.
- Team attrition increases significantly within six months of the executive joining.
- The executive's behavior contradicts stated company values and the issue is not being addressed.
