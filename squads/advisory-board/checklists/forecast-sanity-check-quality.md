# Forecast Sanity Check Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures that financial and operational forecasts are grounded in reality, internally consistent, and useful for decision-making. Forecasts are not predictions; they are disciplined statements of what we expect to happen given explicit assumptions. This checklist prevents the twin failures of sandbagging (deliberately conservative forecasts) and hockey-stick projections (unrealistic optimism).

## When to Use
- Before presenting any forecast to the board, investors, or leadership team.
- During quarterly forecast update cycles.
- When a forecast miss exceeds 20% in either direction.
- When evaluating the forecasts embedded in decision memos or business cases.

## Gate Criteria

### Assumption Quality
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Key assumptions are listed explicitly and numbered for reference.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each assumption is tagged as validated (evidence-based), estimated (judgment-based), or speculative.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Historical accuracy of the same assumptions in prior forecasts is documented.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Assumptions are internally consistent (growth rate, hiring plan, and capacity are aligned).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: External assumptions (market growth, competitor behavior, regulatory environment) are sourced.

### Scenario Coverage
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Base, bull, and bear scenarios are presented with probability weightings.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Scenarios differ on the most impactful variables, not just across the board.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The bear case is genuinely pessimistic, not just base minus 10%.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: At least one scenario contemplates a structural change, not just a magnitude change.

### Internal Consistency
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Revenue forecast is consistent with sales pipeline, conversion rates, and capacity.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Expense forecast is consistent with hiring plan, vendor commitments, and capital expenditure.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Cash flow forecast reconciles with P&L and balance sheet assumptions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Growth rates implied are consistent with historical trajectory or the variance is explained.

### Bias Detection
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The forecast has been reviewed by someone other than the team responsible for the results.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The forecast is compared against external benchmarks (industry growth rates, peer performance).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Prior forecast accuracy is reported alongside the new forecast.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Incentive-driven bias is assessed: do the forecasters benefit from specific outcomes.

### Usefulness and Communication
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The forecast is presented with enough context for the audience to evaluate credibility.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Key sensitivities are highlighted: which variable changes have the largest impact.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The forecast includes specific action triggers tied to scenarios.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The forecast update cadence is defined and committed to.

## Red Flags
- The forecast exactly hits a round number or convenient target.
- Revenue grows at 40% but no additional sales resources are planned.
- The forecast has not been updated despite material changes in the business.
- Every quarter's forecast has been missed in the same direction.
- The bear case has never been modeled.
- The CFO or finance team has not reviewed the forecast.

## Escalation Triggers
- Actual results deviate from the forecast by more than 25% for two consecutive periods.
- The forecast presented to the board differs from the internal operating forecast.
- A forecast assumption is discovered to be materially incorrect after the fact.
- Revenue recognition timing is being manipulated to match forecast.
- The forecast does not reflect known contractual commitments or losses.
