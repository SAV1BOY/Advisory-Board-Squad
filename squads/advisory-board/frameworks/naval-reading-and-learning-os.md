# Naval Reading and Learning Operating System

## Purpose

Naval Ravikant is one of the most well-read people in technology and investing. His learning operating system prioritizes depth over breadth, foundational knowledge over trending topics, and re-reading over consuming new material. This framework provides a structured approach to continuous learning that builds the intellectual foundation for superior judgment, specific knowledge, and worldly wisdom.

## When to Use

- When designing a personal or organizational learning curriculum
- When an advisor or founder wants to deepen their intellectual toolkit
- When building a culture of continuous learning within a team
- When feeling overwhelmed by information and needing a filter
- When the goal is wisdom rather than just knowledge

## Core Model

### Naval's Learning Principles

**1. Read what you love until you love to read.** Don't force yourself through books you hate. Follow your curiosity — it knows something you don't.

**2. Read the foundational texts, not the summaries.** Read Adam Smith, not a book about Adam Smith. Read Darwin, not a podcast about Darwin. Original sources build deeper understanding.

**3. Re-read the great books.** A book you read once is a book that changed you temporarily. A book you read five times is a book that changed you permanently.

**4. Read across disciplines.** The best insights come from connecting ideas across domains. A framework from biology applied to business is more valuable than another business book.

**5. Focus on mental models, not facts.** Facts change. Mental models persist. Learn the models, not the data.

**6. Math and science are the foundations.** Basic mathematics, physics, and biology provide the most transferable mental models. Everything else is built on top.

**7. If a book is boring, put it down.** Life is too short to read bad books. No guilt. Move on.

**8. Read to develop judgment, not to accumulate information.** The goal is not to know more but to think better.

### The Learning Stack
1. **Foundation**: Mathematics, physics, biology, philosophy (timeless, most transferable)
2. **Core**: Economics, psychology, history (essential for business judgment)
3. **Applied**: Strategy, management, technology (domain-specific)
4. **Current**: Industry news, market trends (perishable, consume sparingly)

### Reading Modes
- **Deep reading**: Books, long papers — for building models (2-4 hours/week)
- **Reflective reading**: Re-reading, journaling about what you read — for integration (1-2 hours/week)
- **Broad scanning**: Articles, newsletters, podcasts — for awareness (30 min/day max)

## Steps

1. **Audit your current learning diet.** How much time do you spend on each layer of the learning stack? Most people over-invest in "Current" and under-invest in "Foundation."

2. **Build a reading list weighted toward foundations.** For every current-events article, read a page of a foundational text. The ratio should be at least 3:1 foundation to current.

3. **Identify 5-10 books to re-read this year.** The books that changed your thinking the most deserve repeated engagement.

4. **Create a learning ritual.** Daily or weekly protected time for deep reading. Non-negotiable, scheduled, and distraction-free.

5. **Keep a commonplace book.** Record key insights, mental models, and connections between ideas. This is your personal latticework in progress.

6. **Teach what you learn.** Share a model per week with your team, advisory group, or public audience. Teaching deepens understanding.

7. **Apply what you learn.** Each mental model should be tested against a real decision within one week of learning it.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Share relevant reading with portfolio companies. Curated recommendations are a high-value, low-cost advisory contribution.
- Build a shared reading culture in your advisory practice. When all advisors share a common intellectual foundation, discussions are richer.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Reading for volume.** "I read 100 books this year" is vanity. 5 deeply understood books > 100 skimmed books.
2. **Only reading within your domain.** Business people reading only business books get incremental insights. Cross-disciplinary reading produces breakthrough insights.
3. **Never re-reading.** One reading retains ~10% of a book's insight. Five readings may retain 70%.
4. **Consuming current events excessively.** News is designed to be urgent, not important. Limit consumption.
