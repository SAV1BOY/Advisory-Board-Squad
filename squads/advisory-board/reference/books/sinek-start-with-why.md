# Start with Why — Simon Sinek

## Summary

Sinek introduces the Golden Circle—Why, How, What—arguing that the most inspiring leaders and organizations start from purpose (Why) and work outward, while most start with What and never reach Why. Using examples from Apple, Martin Luther King Jr., and the Wright Brothers, he demonstrates that people don't buy what you do, they buy why you do it. The book makes the case that purpose-driven organizations attract more loyal customers, more committed employees, and make more consistent decisions because every choice is filtered through a clear Why.

## Key Principles

- **The Golden Circle** — Why (purpose/belief) → How (process/values) → What (products/results). Communication and strategy should flow from inside out.
- **People don't buy what you do, they buy why you do it** — Features and benefits persuade the rational brain. Purpose and belief recruit the emotional brain where decisions actually happen.
- **The Law of Diffusion of Innovation** — Early adopters buy the Why. The early majority follows once social proof is established. You must win the 15-18% of innovators and early adopters before you can cross the chasm.
- **The Why must come from the founder** — It cannot be manufactured by a marketing team. Authentic purpose originates from the founder's personal story and beliefs.
- **Consistency creates trust** — When What and How consistently align with Why, people trust the organization. When they diverge, trust erodes even if individual products are excellent.
- **The split** — As organizations grow, the Why can become diluted. The founder's departure or the pressure of scale can cause the organization to lose its purpose and become purely transactional.

## Frameworks Extracted

1. **The Golden Circle** — Three concentric rings. Every strategy document, pitch, and board discussion should start with Why before addressing How and What.
2. **The Celery Test** — If your Why is health, then choosing celery over M&Ms at the supermarket is obvious. Your Why should make strategic decisions self-evident. If a proposed initiative doesn't pass the Celery Test, it's off-strategy.
3. **The Split Diagnostic** — Warning signs that an organization has lost its Why: decisions are made purely on financial metrics, messaging focuses on features rather than purpose, employees can't articulate why the company exists beyond making money.
4. **The Megaphone Model** — The founder is the Why. The CEO and executive team are the How. The organization and products are the What. Each layer amplifies the one above it.

## Application to Board Decisions

- **Strategic alignment** — Every major decision should be tested against the company's Why using the Celery Test. If it doesn't serve the purpose, it dilutes the brand regardless of short-term revenue.
- **CEO selection** — The next CEO must embody the Why. Boards that hire operators without purpose alignment risk the Split—the slow death of organizational identity.
- **Brand governance** — The board should periodically audit whether external messaging and internal culture still align with the founding Why. Drift is gradual and often invisible from the inside.
- **M&A evaluation** — Acquisitions that don't share the Why create integration problems that no amount of operational synergy can solve. Cultural misalignment is the top reason acquisitions fail.
- **Investor communications** — Shareholder letters and board decks should lead with Why before presenting What (financials). This frames numbers within purpose and builds long-term investor loyalty.
