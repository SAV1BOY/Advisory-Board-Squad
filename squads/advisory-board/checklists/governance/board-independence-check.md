# Board Independence Check Checklist

## Purpose
Assesses whether the board maintains sufficient independence from management and other conflicting interests to provide genuine governance oversight. Independence is not just a structural attribute; it is a behavioral one. Directors may be technically independent but functionally captured by management relationships, compensation dependency, or social bonds.

## When to Use
- When evaluating board composition and nominations.
- Annually during board effectiveness reviews.
- When potential conflicts of interest arise.
- When governance effectiveness is questioned by stakeholders.

## Gate Criteria

### Structural Independence
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A majority of board members meet the relevant independence criteria for the organization type.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: No board member has a material financial relationship with the company beyond board compensation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: No board member has a familial relationship with management.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board member tenure is not so long that independence is compromised by familiarity.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board chair is independent from the CEO role or a lead independent director exists.

### Behavioral Independence
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board members challenge management proposals with substantive questions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board has made decisions that went against management's recommendation in the last 24 months.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board members form opinions before meetings rather than accepting management framing.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Executive sessions without management occur regularly and produce genuine governance discussion.

### Process Independence
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board controls its own agenda, not just responds to management's agenda.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board has access to independent advisors (legal, financial) separate from management.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board member compensation is set independently of management input.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board nominations consider independence as a criterion alongside expertise.

### Independence Maintenance
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Independence is reassessed when circumstances change (new business relationships, personal connections).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board refreshment ensures that independence does not erode over time.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Potential conflicts are proactively identified and managed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board evaluates its own independence as part of annual self-assessment.

## Red Flags
- The board unanimously agrees with every management proposal.
- Board members have consulting or vendor relationships with the company.
- The CEO handpicks board members who are personal friends.
- No executive session has been held in over six months.
- Board compensation is tied to short-term company performance in ways that compromise objectivity.

## Escalation Triggers
- A board member's conflict of interest is discovered after they voted on a related matter.
- Stakeholders formally question the board's independence.
- The board fails to exercise oversight on a matter that results in material harm.
- A board member's independence status changes and the board does not acknowledge or address it.
- Regulatory requirements for independence are not met.
