# Board Decision Memo Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Ensure every decision memo presented to the board meets the standard of clarity, completeness, and intellectual rigor required for sound governance. A weak memo leads to uninformed votes, wasted board time, and decisions that unravel under scrutiny.

## When to Use
- Before any decision memo is circulated to board members for review.
- When a previously rejected memo is being revised for resubmission.
- During quarterly audits of decision-making documentation quality.

## Gate Criteria

### Structure and Clarity
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The memo opens with a single-sentence recommendation that a reader can understand without context.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The problem statement is framed in terms of business impact, not internal process.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Background section is concise (under 500 words) and does not repeat information already known to the board.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The memo follows a consistent template used across all board materials.

### Options and Analysis
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: At least three distinct options are presented, including the status quo.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each option includes projected financial impact with explicit assumptions stated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Trade-offs between options are articulated honestly, not skewed toward the preferred recommendation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Second-order effects of each option are addressed (market reaction, team morale, competitive response).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Reversibility of each option is explicitly rated (easily reversible, partially reversible, irreversible).

### Evidence and Data
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Claims are supported by data with cited sources, not unsupported assertions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Data is current (within the last 90 days unless historical context is required).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Sensitivity analysis or scenario modeling is included for decisions involving financial commitments above the materiality threshold.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer or market data is included where the decision has external impact.

### Risk and Downside
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Key risks are listed with likelihood and severity ratings.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Mitigation strategies are specific and actionable, not generic platitudes.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The worst-case scenario is described in concrete terms, not abstract language.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Legal, regulatory, and reputational risks are addressed where applicable.

### Decision Mechanics
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The memo specifies who has decision authority (board vote, CEO with board input, committee).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Timeline for decision and implementation is explicit with milestones.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Success metrics and review date are defined before the decision is made.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Dissenting views from the management team are included, not suppressed.

### Readability and Respect for Board Time
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Total memo length is under 5 pages (excluding appendices).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Appendices contain supporting detail but the memo stands alone without them.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Memo was circulated at least 72 hours before the board meeting.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: An executive summary of no more than 3 bullet points is included at the top.

## Red Flags
- The memo reads as a justification for a decision already made rather than a genuine request for input.
- Only one option is presented, framed as the only viable path.
- Financial projections lack assumptions or use best-case-only modeling.
- The memo was sent to the board less than 24 hours before the meeting.
- Dissenting voices within the management team are absent or minimized.
- The memo uses jargon or acronyms without definition, signaling insider-only communication.
- Risk section is a single paragraph with no specifics.

## Escalation Triggers
- A board member flags that the memo is misleading or materially incomplete.
- The same quality failures recur across three consecutive board meetings.
- A decision made from a weak memo results in a material negative outcome that better analysis would have prevented.
- Management refuses to include dissenting views in the memo.
- Legal counsel raises concerns about the accuracy of representations in the memo.
