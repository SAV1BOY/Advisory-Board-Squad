# Naval Specific Knowledge Check Checklist

## Purpose
Evaluates whether the organization and its leaders are building and leveraging specific knowledge: the kind of knowledge that cannot be trained for, that comes from genuine curiosity and passion, and that feels like play to the possessor but looks like work to others. Naval Ravikant argues that specific knowledge is the foundation of outsized value creation. This checklist ensures the organization identifies, cultivates, and deploys specific knowledge as a competitive advantage.

## When to Use
- During talent reviews and succession planning.
- When evaluating what makes the company uniquely capable.
- When building teams for new initiatives that require distinctive capability.
- When assessing whether the organization's advantage is replicable by competitors.
- During strategic planning to identify where specific knowledge creates defensibility.

## Gate Criteria

### Identification
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization can articulate its specific knowledge: what it knows that others do not and cannot easily learn.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Key individuals' specific knowledge is documented and understood by leadership.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The specific knowledge is connected to customer value, not just internal processes.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The source of specific knowledge is identified: unique experience, pattern recognition, or deep domain obsession.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The specific knowledge is distinguished from general skills that are widely available.

### Cultivation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization creates environments where specific knowledge can deepen (unstructured time, exploration budgets, cross-domain exposure).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Hiring prioritizes candidates with specific knowledge that complements the existing team.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: People are deployed in roles that leverage their specific knowledge, not in roles that waste it.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Learning from the market and customers continuously sharpens specific knowledge.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization protects specific knowledge from being diluted by excessive standardization.

### Deployment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Strategic bets are aligned with the organization's specific knowledge.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Product and service design leverages specific knowledge in ways customers can perceive.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization avoids competing in areas where it has no specific knowledge advantage.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Specific knowledge informs decision-making at the board and leadership level.

### Protection and Sustainability
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Specific knowledge is not concentrated in a single individual without knowledge-sharing mechanisms.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Retention strategies target holders of critical specific knowledge.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization invests in deepening specific knowledge rather than only exploiting existing knowledge.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Competitive threats to the organization's specific knowledge advantage are monitored.

## Red Flags
- The company cannot articulate what it knows that competitors do not.
- Specific knowledge holders are leaving and the organization does not recognize the loss.
- The company is competing in areas where it has no distinctive knowledge advantage.
- Hiring emphasizes credentials over demonstrated specific knowledge.
- The organization's processes are so standardized that no room exists for specific knowledge application.
- Leaders confuse specific knowledge with general industry experience.

## Escalation Triggers
- A competitor hires away a key specific knowledge holder.
- The organization enters a market where it lacks specific knowledge and struggles to compete.
- Product or service quality declines because specific knowledge is not being applied.
- The organization cannot explain to investors or the board what makes it uniquely capable.
- Specific knowledge that once differentiated the company is becoming commoditized.
