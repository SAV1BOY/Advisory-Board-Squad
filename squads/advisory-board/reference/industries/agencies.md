# Agencies Industry Reference

## Business Model Characteristics
- **Revenue models:** Retainer fees, project fees, performance fees, hybrid models
- **Margins:** Gross margins 40-60%. Net margins 10-20% when well-managed. The primary cost is talent.
- **Key constraint:** Revenue is limited by headcount. Linear scaling unless productized services are introduced.
- **Client concentration risk:** Agencies often depend on a few large clients. Loss of one can be catastrophic.

## Key Metrics
- **Revenue per employee:** $100-150K (average), $150-200K (good), > $200K (excellent)
- **Utilization rate:** Billable hours / total available hours. Target: 70-80%.
- **Client retention rate:** Annual retention > 85% is healthy
- **Client concentration:** No single client > 20% of revenue (ideal)
- **Gross margin per project:** Track project profitability, not just revenue
- **Pipeline coverage:** 3x pipeline to target is standard; < 2x is concerning

## Scaling Patterns
- **Productized services:** Package repeatable deliverables at fixed prices. Increases margins, reduces customization burden.
- **Technology leverage:** Use automation, AI, and templates to increase output per person.
- **Niche specialization:** Industry or function specialization commands premium pricing and reduces competition.
- **Offshore/nearshore:** Lower-cost talent for execution while retaining strategic and client roles locally.
- **SaaS transition:** Some agencies evolve into software companies by productizing their expertise. Difficult but transformative.

## Board Considerations
- Monitor client concentration relentlessly. The loss of a top-3 client should have a contingency plan.
- Evaluate whether the agency is scaling linearly (more people = more revenue) or creating leverage (technology, productization).
- Talent retention is the #1 strategic concern. The agency's product walks out the door every evening.
- Assess whether AI is a threat (automating agency deliverables) or an opportunity (enabling the agency to do more with fewer people).
