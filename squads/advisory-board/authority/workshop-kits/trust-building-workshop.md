# Workshop Kit: Building Team Trust

## Workshop Overview

**Duration:** 3 hours
**Participants:** 6-15 team members (works best with intact teams)
**Facilitator:** External facilitator recommended for first session; internal leader can facilitate subsequent sessions
**Materials:** Whiteboard/flip charts, sticky notes, pens, printed handouts of exercises

## Purpose

Trust is the foundation of high-performing teams. Without it, feedback is filtered, conflict is avoided, accountability erodes, and collaboration becomes transactional. This workshop surfaces the current state of trust within a team, identifies specific behaviors that build or erode trust, and creates commitments for improvement.

## Pre-Workshop Preparation

### For the Facilitator
- [ ] Conduct brief 1:1 conversations with 3-4 team members to understand trust dynamics
- [ ] Prepare the room: chairs in a circle or U-shape (not classroom style)
- [ ] Print the Trust Equation handout (one per participant)
- [ ] Prepare flip charts with exercise templates
- [ ] Review ground rules and be prepared to enforce them
- [ ] Have a plan for managing emotional moments

### For Participants
- [ ] Reflect on a time when you trusted a colleague deeply — what created that trust?
- [ ] Reflect on a time when trust was broken — what caused it?
- [ ] Come prepared to be honest and vulnerable (within your comfort zone)

## Ground Rules

1. **Confidentiality:** What is shared here stays here. Specifics are not repeated outside the room.
2. **Speak from experience:** Use "I" statements, not accusations or generalizations.
3. **Assume positive intent:** Believe that your colleagues want the team to succeed.
4. **Discomfort is expected:** Trust-building requires vulnerability, which is uncomfortable.
5. **No devices:** Full presence and attention for the entire session.

## Exercise Details

### Exercise 1: The Trust Equation (25 min)

Introduce the Trust Equation framework:

**Trust = (Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy) / Self-Orientation**

- **Credibility:** Do I believe what you say? (expertise, accuracy, honesty)
- **Reliability:** Do you do what you say you will do? (consistency, follow-through)
- **Intimacy:** Do I feel safe sharing with you? (discretion, empathy, care)
- **Self-Orientation:** Are you focused on yourself or on us? (the denominator — higher self-orientation means lower trust)

**Activity:** Each participant privately rates themselves on each dimension (1-10). Then, in pairs, share one dimension you want to strengthen and one you believe is a strength.

### Exercise 2: Personal Histories (30 min)

**Purpose:** Build understanding and empathy by sharing formative experiences.

**Activity:** Each person shares (2-3 minutes):
- Where you grew up
- How many siblings and your birth order
- One unique or defining challenge from your childhood or early career

**Why this works:** Patrick Lencioni's research shows that even basic personal sharing creates meaningful connection. People who understand each other's context extend more grace and assume better intent.

### Exercise 3: Trust Behaviors Mapping (35 min)

**Activity (15 min individual, 20 min group):**

Each participant writes on sticky notes:
- 3 specific behaviors that BUILD trust on this team (green sticky notes)
- 3 specific behaviors that ERODE trust on this team (red sticky notes)

**Rules for sticky notes:**
- Be specific: "Responding to messages within 4 hours" not "Being responsive"
- Focus on behaviors, not people
- Draw from actual experience, not hypotheticals

**Group discussion:** Cluster the sticky notes by theme. Identify the top 3 trust-building behaviors to amplify and the top 3 trust-eroding behaviors to address.

### Exercise 4: Vulnerability Practice (30 min)

**Activity:** Each person shares one thing with the group from this list (choose the one that feels most relevant):

- "Something I struggle with professionally that I rarely admit..."
- "A mistake I made on this team that I have not fully addressed..."
- "Something I need from this team that I have been reluctant to ask for..."
- "A strength I see in someone on this team that I have never told them..."

**Facilitator note:** Model vulnerability by going first or sharing your own example. Silence after someone shares is OK — do not rush to fill it.

### Exercise 5: Trust Commitments (20 min)

**Activity:** Based on the session, each person writes two commitments:

1. **I will start:** One new behavior I commit to that will build trust
2. **I will stop:** One behavior I commit to changing that has been eroding trust

Commitments are shared with the group and written on a flip chart. The facilitator photographs the chart and distributes it after the session.

## Post-Workshop Follow-Up

### Within 1 Week
- Distribute commitment list to all participants
- Facilitator sends thank-you note and reinforces confidentiality

### At 30 Days
- Brief check-in (15-20 minutes in a team meeting): "How are we doing on our commitments?"
- Celebrate observed improvements
- Address any commitments that have not been honored

### At 90 Days
- Consider a follow-up half-day session to go deeper
- Administer a brief trust survey to measure change
- Determine if regular trust-building sessions should become part of team cadence

## Facilitator Tips

- **Do not force vulnerability.** Offer choices so people can engage at their comfort level.
- **Watch for dominant voices.** Ensure quieter team members have equal space.
- **Name what is happening.** If tension arises, acknowledge it: "I notice this is uncomfortable. That is a sign we are doing important work."
- **Protect the space.** If someone violates ground rules, address it immediately and gently.
- **This is a beginning, not an event.** Trust is built through consistent daily behavior, not workshops. Frame this as a starting point.
