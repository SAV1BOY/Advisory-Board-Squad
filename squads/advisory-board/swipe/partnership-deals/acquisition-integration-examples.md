# Acquisition Integration Examples — Swipe File

## Overview
Most acquisitions fail to deliver expected value, and the primary cause is poor integration. These examples illustrate integration patterns, common failures, and best practices.

## Integration Models

### Model 1: Full Integration
- **When:** The acquisition is for technology or market position that will be folded into the acquirer's product and operations
- **Timeline:** 6-12 months for core integration, 12-24 months for full integration
- **Risks:** Cultural clash, talent loss, customer disruption, operational complexity
- **Example pattern:** Acquirer absorbs the product, brand, and team. Target brand is retired.

### Model 2: Autonomous Operation
- **When:** The acquisition is for a new market or category that benefits from independence
- **Timeline:** Indefinite autonomy with shared services and reporting
- **Risks:** Lack of synergy realization, duplicate costs, cultural drift between parent and subsidiary
- **Example pattern:** Acquired company maintains its brand, culture, and operations. Parent provides capital and shared services.

### Model 3: Gradual Integration
- **When:** The acquisition benefits from some integration (shared customers, shared technology) but full integration would destroy value
- **Timeline:** 12-36 months of phased integration
- **Risks:** "Worst of both worlds" — too integrated to maintain independence, too independent to capture synergies
- **Example pattern:** Shared back-office, independent product and sales, gradually merged over time

## The 100-Day Integration Playbook

### Days 1-30: Stabilize
- Day 1 communication to all stakeholders (employees, customers, partners)
- Interim organizational structure announced
- Key person retention conversations completed
- Customer retention program launched
- Critical business processes documented and maintained

### Days 31-60: Assess
- Detailed operational assessment of the acquired business
- Cultural assessment: what's working, what's different, what creates friction
- Technology audit: compatibility, technical debt, integration requirements
- Customer feedback: how are they experiencing the transition?

### Days 61-100: Plan
- Detailed integration plan with timelines, owners, and milestones
- Organizational design for the combined entity
- Technology integration roadmap
- Synergy capture plan with specific targets and timeline
- Communication plan for the next 12 months

## Common Integration Failures

### Failure 1: Talent Exodus
- **Cause:** Acquired employees feel uncertain, undervalued, or culturally misaligned
- **Prevention:** Retention packages (vesting over 2-3 years), clear role definition, cultural integration workshops, genuine listening to acquired team

### Failure 2: Customer Defection
- **Cause:** Service disruption, product changes, or relationship loss during transition
- **Prevention:** Dedicated customer success team for transition, proactive communication, service level guarantees

### Failure 3: Cultural Collision
- **Cause:** Attempting to impose acquirer's culture on acquired company (or vice versa)
- **Prevention:** Explicitly acknowledge cultural differences. Define which elements of each culture to preserve. Create a new, shared culture for the combined entity.

### Failure 4: Integration Fatigue
- **Cause:** Integration drains resources and attention from running the core business
- **Prevention:** Dedicated integration team separate from operational leadership. Protect core business operations.

## Board Application
- Appoint a board member to oversee integration and report regularly
- Conduct 30-60-90 day reviews and a comprehensive 12-month post-acquisition assessment
- Track synergy realization against the assumptions in the original investment memo
- If integration is failing, address it early. Delayed course correction compounds the damage.
