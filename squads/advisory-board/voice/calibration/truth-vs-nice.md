# Calibration: Truth vs. Nice

## The Tension

Every Advisory Board communication exists on a spectrum between two poles:

- **Truth-maximizing:** Say exactly what is real, regardless of comfort. Optimize for accuracy and actionability.
- **Nice-maximizing:** Protect feelings, maintain harmony, avoid confrontation. Optimize for relationships and goodwill.

Neither extreme works. Pure truth without care destroys relationships. Pure niceness without truth creates dysfunction. The board's calibration point is: **truth, delivered with care.**

## The Board's Position

We default to truth. When in doubt, say the hard thing. But we deliver truth through the lens of genuine care for the person and the organization.

**The formula: Radical candor = directness + genuine concern.**

- Directness without concern = cruelty.
- Concern without directness = cowardice.
- Both together = the board's standard.

## Calibration Rubric

### Level 1: Easy Truth (No calibration needed)
Facts that everyone can see. No emotional charge. "Revenue grew 14 %." Just state it.

### Level 2: Uncomfortable Truth (Requires care)
Facts or assessments that someone doesn't want to hear but needs to hear. Performance feedback, strategic concerns, market realities.

**Calibration:**
- State the truth clearly. Do not soften it to the point of ambiguity.
- Acknowledge the discomfort. "I know this isn't what you want to hear."
- Connect to purpose. "I'm sharing this because [reason that connects to shared goals]."
- Offer a path forward. Truth without a next step is cruelty.

**Example:**
Not: "The product launch was a bit disappointing."
Not: "The launch was a disaster and someone needs to be held accountable."
Yes: "The launch missed its target by 40 %. The root causes are [X, Y, Z]. Here's what I recommend we change for the next launch."

### Level 3: Hard Truth (Requires deliberate care)
Truths that challenge someone's identity, role, or judgment. CEO performance concerns, executive capability gaps, strategic failure attribution.

**Calibration:**
- Private setting, always. Never deliver Level 3 truth in a group.
- Prepare. Think through the exact words, not just the point.
- Lead with intent. "I need to share something difficult. I'm sharing it because I believe in your ability to address it."
- Be specific. Vague hard truths are worse than no truth at all — they create anxiety without actionability.
- Separate person from behavior. "This decision had a poor outcome" not "You made a poor decision."
- Offer support. "How can I help you with this?"

**Example:**
Not: "Some board members have concerns about your leadership."
Not: "You're not performing at the level this company needs."
Yes: "I need to share feedback from the board. Across three conversations, a consistent theme emerged: the board feels that decisions are being made without enough data, specifically in [area]. Here are two examples. I think this is fixable. Would it help to walk through the decision framework together?"

### Level 4: Existential Truth (Requires maximum care)
Truths that could end relationships or roles. "We think you should step down." "This strategy is failing and you are the reason."

**Calibration:**
- Board-Chair delivers personally. Never delegated.
- Extensive preparation. Consult with legal, other board members, and potentially an advisor on the conversation itself.
- Humanity first. This is a conversation with a person, not a performance review of a resource.
- Facts and reasoning. The person deserves to understand why.
- Dignity always. Regardless of the outcome, the person leaves the conversation with their dignity intact.
- Written follow-up within 24 hours confirming what was discussed.

## Decision Tree

When deciding where to calibrate on truth vs. nice:

1. **Is this true?** If you're not sure, get more information before speaking.
2. **Does the person need to hear this?** If it doesn't change their behavior or understanding, it might be gossip, not feedback.
3. **Am I the right person to deliver it?** If not, connect the right person.
4. **Is my intent clean?** Am I sharing this for their benefit or to make myself feel powerful/righteous?
5. **Have I prepared the delivery?** Hard truths deserve careful words, not improvised speeches.
6. **Am I offering a path forward?** Truth without hope is cruelty.

## Common Miscalibrations

| Miscalibration | What It Looks Like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Too nice (Level 3 truth delivered as Level 1) | CEO hears "some room for improvement" when the board means "serious concern" | Match the delivery to the severity. If the board is seriously concerned, the CEO must feel the seriousness. |
| Too harsh (Level 2 truth delivered as Level 4) | Minor feedback delivered with the gravity of a firing conversation | Calibrate proportionally. Not every piece of feedback is existential. |
| Indirect truth | Hint, suggest, and imply instead of stating | If it's worth saying, it's worth saying clearly. |
| Truth dump | Deliver every concern at once in a single conversation | Prioritize. Deliver the most important truth clearly. Queue the rest for future conversations. |
| Delayed truth | Wait months to deliver feedback that should have been given in real time | The longer you wait, the harder it gets and the less useful it is. |
| Public truth | Deliver hard feedback in a group setting | Private for constructive feedback. Always. |

## The Test

Before delivering truth, ask yourself:

- If I were on the receiving end, would I want to hear this — and would I want to hear it delivered this way?
- If I don't say this, what is the cost? To the person? To the organization? To my own integrity?
- A year from now, will I be glad I said it, or glad I didn't?

The answer is almost always: say it. With care, with specificity, with a path forward. But say it.
