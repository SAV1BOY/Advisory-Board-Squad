# Bezos Shareholder Letter Highlights — Swipe File

## Best Opening Concepts
- The 1997 letter: "This is Day 1 for the Internet." Attached to every subsequent letter as an appendix. Radical consistency.
- "Customers are always beautifully, wonderfully dissatisfied, even when they report being happy." (2016)
- "Failure and invention are inseparable twins." (2015)

## Best Passages on Decision-Making
- "Most decisions should probably be made with somewhere around 70% of the information you wish you had. If you wait for 90%, in most cases, you're probably being slow."
- "Some decisions are consequential and irreversible or nearly irreversible—one-way doors—and these decisions must be made methodically, carefully, slowly, with great deliberation and consultation. Type 1 decisions."
- "Disagree and commit. If you have conviction on a particular direction even though there's no consensus, say 'Look, I know we disagree on this but will you gamble with me on it? Disagree and commit?'"

## Best Passages on Customer Obsession
- "We're not competitor obsessed, we're customer obsessed. We start with what the customer needs and we work backwards."
- "There are many ways to center a business. You can be competitor focused, you can be product focused, you can be technology focused, you can be business model focused. But in my view, obsessive customer focus is by far the most protective of Day 1 vitality."
- "We see our customers as invited guests to a party, and we are the hosts. It's our job every day to make every important aspect of the customer experience a little bit better."

## Best Passages on Standards
- "High standards are contagious. Bring a new person onto a high standards team, and they'll adapt and learn quickly."
- "Another thing I've noticed is that high standards are domain specific. If you have high standards in one area, you don't automatically have high standards in another."
- "You can consider yourself a person of high standards in general and still have terribly low standards in areas you just haven't identified."

## Best Passages on Long-Term Thinking
- "We are willing to be misunderstood for long periods of time."
- "If everything you do needs to work on a three-year time horizon, then you're competing against a lot of people. But if you're willing to invest on a seven-year time horizon, you're now competing against a fraction of those people."

## Swipe Application
- Use the Type 1/Type 2 framework to calibrate decision speed in board discussions
- Adopt "disagree and commit" as a board norm to prevent paralysis
- Apply the "domain-specific high standards" insight to board self-evaluation
