# Incentives Alignment Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Verify that the incentive structures surrounding a decision, initiative, or organizational design genuinely align individual and team behavior with desired outcomes. Misaligned incentives are the root cause of most organizational dysfunction. As Charlie Munger observed, "Show me the incentive and I will show you the outcome."

## When to Use
- When designing or revising compensation structures, bonus plans, or equity programs.
- Before approving any initiative where multiple stakeholders have potentially divergent interests.
- When reviewing partnership or vendor agreements.
- When diagnosing why an initiative is producing unintended behavior.
- During annual reviews of executive and team incentive structures.

## Gate Criteria

### Structural Alignment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Every key participant's incentive structure has been mapped and documented.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Individual incentives are tested against company-level objectives for conflicts.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Short-term incentives (quarterly bonuses, sales commissions) do not undermine long-term value creation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Incentives reward outcomes, not activity or effort metrics that can be gamed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The incentive structure accounts for both upside rewards and downside consequences.

### Gaming and Perverse Outcomes
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A red-team exercise has been conducted to identify how a rational actor would game the incentive system.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Metrics used for incentive calculation are resistant to manipulation or Goodhart's Law effects.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The system does not create incentives to shift costs or risks to other teams, future periods, or external parties.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: There is no incentive to withhold information or delay bad news.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Collaboration across teams is incentivized, not just individual or team-level performance.

### Stakeholder Coverage
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer interests are protected by the incentive design, not just shareholder or management interests.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Employee incentives at all levels (not just executives) have been reviewed for alignment.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Partner and vendor incentive structures have been examined for misalignment with company goals.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board member incentives (compensation, equity, reputation) are aligned with long-term shareholder value.

### Transparency and Communication
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Incentive structures are clearly communicated to all affected parties.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The link between behavior, metrics, and rewards is understandable without specialized knowledge.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Changes to incentive structures are announced with sufficient lead time for behavioral adjustment.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: There are no hidden or informal incentive structures operating alongside the formal ones.

### Monitoring and Adjustment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leading indicators of incentive misalignment are defined and monitored.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A review cadence is established to assess whether incentives are producing intended behavior.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: There is a mechanism to adjust incentives mid-cycle if material misalignment is discovered.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Historical data from previous incentive structures is used to inform the current design.

## Red Flags
- People are meeting their individual targets while the company is underperforming.
- Top performers are optimizing for their bonus at the expense of team or company goals.
- Employees are not able to explain how their incentives connect to company strategy.
- The incentive structure has not been changed in over two years despite significant strategic shifts.
- Sales incentives reward revenue without regard to margin, churn, or customer satisfaction.
- Executives have asymmetric upside (big bonuses for wins) with no meaningful downside (no clawbacks for losses).

## Escalation Triggers
- Evidence emerges that incentive gaming is causing material financial or reputational harm.
- An executive's personal incentives are found to conflict with their fiduciary duties.
- Employee survey data reveals widespread perception that incentives are unfair or misaligned.
- A pattern of sandbagging targets or manipulating timing of revenue recognition is detected.
- Partner or customer complaints trace back to incentive-driven behavior by company employees.
