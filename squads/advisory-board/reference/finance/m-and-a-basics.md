# M&A Basics

## Acquisition Rationale Types

### Revenue Synergies (High Risk)
- Cross-sell products to each other's customer base
- Enter new markets through acquisition
- **Warning:** Revenue synergies almost always take longer and deliver less than projected. Discount them 50-70%.

### Cost Synergies (Medium Risk)
- Eliminate duplicate functions (finance, HR, IT)
- Achieve purchasing scale
- Consolidate facilities
- **Warning:** Cost synergies are more reliable but still require execution. Discount them 20-30%.

### Capability Acquisition (Variable Risk)
- Acquire technology, talent, or IP that would take years to build internally
- **Warning:** The talent often leaves after the acquisition, taking the capability with them. Build retention into the deal.

### Market Position / Competitive (Strategic)
- Acquire a competitor to increase market share and pricing power
- Remove a disruptive threat before it matures
- **Warning:** Antitrust regulators increasingly scrutinize competitive acquisitions.

## Due Diligence Checklist

### Financial
- 3 years audited financials (or reviewed if not audited)
- Revenue quality: recurring vs. one-time, concentration, churn
- Working capital needs and seasonality
- Debt and off-balance-sheet liabilities
- Tax compliance and exposure

### Legal
- Corporate structure and capitalization
- Material contracts and their assignability
- Litigation pending or threatened
- IP ownership and freedom to operate
- Regulatory compliance

### Commercial
- Customer interviews (not just references provided by the target)
- Market position and competitive dynamics
- Product roadmap and technical debt
- Sales pipeline quality and conversion rates

### People
- Key employee identification and retention risk
- Employment agreements, non-competes, equity obligations
- Culture assessment: how does this organization actually work?
- Management team quality and bench strength

### Integration
- Systems compatibility
- Process alignment
- Cultural integration plan
- Communication plan for customers, employees, and partners

## Valuation Approaches for M&A

### For the Acquirer
- **Maximum price = Standalone value + Realizable synergies - Integration costs - Risk premium**
- **The discipline:** Never pay more than the value you can create. The seller's alternative (BATNA) should not set your price.

### Common Valuation Errors
- Overvaluing revenue synergies
- Underestimating integration costs (typically 2-3x initial estimates)
- Ignoring cultural integration challenges
- Anchor bias from the seller's asking price
- Winner's curse in competitive processes

## Integration Best Practices

### Day 1 Readiness
- Communication plan for all stakeholders (employees, customers, partners)
- Interim reporting structure clear
- Critical business processes maintained
- Key employee retention conversations completed

### First 90 Days
- Integration team with dedicated leader and clear authority
- Cultural integration workshops (not just org chart merging)
- Quick wins identified and executed to build momentum
- Customer retention program activated

### Common Integration Failures
- Waiting too long to integrate (drift kills value)
- Over-integrating (destroying the acquired company's culture and capabilities)
- Neglecting the core business during integration (the acquirer stumbles)
- Losing key talent (the #1 integration risk in capability acquisitions)

## Board Application
- Every acquisition proposal must include a written investment memo with explicit synergy assumptions and integration plan
- The board should independently evaluate the valuation, not just ratify management's numbers
- Require a post-acquisition review at 6 and 12 months to assess whether the stated rationale is materializing
- Appoint a board member to oversee the integration process and report regularly
