# Buffett Shareholder Letter Highlights — Swipe File

## Best Opening Lines
- "Our gain in net worth during 1979 was $215.7 million, or 35.7% per share." — Immediate, specific, transparent.
- "We had a good year in 2021. But we had a bad year in how we deployed our capital." — Leads with honesty about weakness.
- "Berkshire's performance in all four of our major areas of operation was good last year." — Simple, declarative, sets the frame.

## Best Passages on Capital Allocation
- "The first law of capital allocation—whether for a public company, a private company, or a portfolio of securities—is that what is smart at one price is dumb at another."
- "Charlie and I have not learned how to solve difficult business problems. What we have learned is to avoid them."
- "We try to exert a long-term, but not a permanent, hold. We buy with the thought of holding forever, but we sell when facts change."

## Best Passages on Management
- "When a management with a reputation for brilliance tackles a business with a reputation for bad economics, it is the reputation of the business that remains intact."
- "We want managers who think about what can go wrong rather than just about what can go right."
- "Somebody once said that in looking for people to hire, you look for three qualities: integrity, intelligence, and energy. And if you don't have the first, the other two will kill you."

## Best Passages on Mistakes
- "I made a big mistake not buying Walmart stock when it was cheap. The mistake cost us about $10 billion."
- "We will never apologize for the mistake of omission... but we should and do criticize ourselves for errors of commission."
- "Our insurance business has had some terrible years. When I make mistakes, they tend to be mega-mistakes."

## Best Passages on Long-Term Thinking
- "Our favorite holding period is forever."
- "Someone is sitting in the shade today because someone planted a tree a long time ago."
- "Time is the friend of the wonderful company, the enemy of the mediocre."

## Swipe Application
- Use Buffett's opening line technique: lead with the most important fact, stated simply
- Model mistake acknowledgment on Buffett's approach: specific, quantified, and without excuses
- Adopt the "reputation of the business" framing when evaluating management's ability to fix a struggling unit
