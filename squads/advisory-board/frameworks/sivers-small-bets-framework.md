# Sivers Small Bets Framework

## Purpose

Derek Sivers advocates for making many small bets rather than a few large ones. Small bets provide information, build skills, create optionality, and limit downside — all while preserving the ability to discover unexpected opportunities. This framework applies to product development, career decisions, investments, and strategic experiments.

## When to Use

- When uncertainty is high and prediction is unreliable
- When a team wants to "go big" before validating the idea
- When building a portfolio of experiments rather than betting on one path
- When the cost of failure on a big bet would be catastrophic
- When looking for product-market fit or exploring new markets

## Core Model

### Small Bets Principles
1. **Bet small, learn fast.** Each experiment should be small enough that failure is cheap and learning is fast.
2. **Many bets, not one big bet.** In uncertain environments, the probability of any single bet succeeding is low. A portfolio of small bets has a much higher probability of finding a winner.
3. **Kill fast, double down fast.** When a small bet fails, stop immediately. When one shows promise, increase investment.
4. **The discovery is the goal.** You are not trying to be right — you are trying to learn what works.
5. **Serendipity favors action.** The more experiments you run, the more likely you are to stumble onto something unexpected and valuable.

### Bet Sizing
- **Maximum bet size:** Small enough that complete failure does not threaten the enterprise
- **Minimum bet count:** Enough bets to have a reasonable probability of finding a winner
- **Typical ratio:** 70% of resources on core business, 20% on adjacent bets, 10% on wild-card experiments

## Steps

1. **Define the hypothesis.** What are you testing? Be specific: "We believe X will lead to Y."
2. **Design the smallest possible experiment.** What is the minimum investment that can test the hypothesis?
3. **Set success/failure criteria in advance.** What result would make you double down? What result would make you stop?
4. **Run the experiment.** Execute with full commitment despite small size.
5. **Evaluate honestly.** Did it meet the criteria? What did you learn?
6. **Iterate.** Kill the failures, double down on the successes, run new experiments.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Push founders toward small bets when uncertainty is high. "Why not test this with a small experiment before committing $1M?"
- Help founders build a portfolio of experiments rather than betting everything on one hypothesis.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Bets too big.** If a single experiment failing would cause significant harm, the bet is too big.
2. **Not enough bets.** One or two experiments is not a portfolio. Run 5-10 simultaneous small bets.
3. **Not killing failures.** Emotional attachment to failed experiments wastes resources.
4. **Not doubling down on successes.** When a small bet works, increase investment immediately.

## Related Frameworks

- **Expected Value and Asymmetry** — Small bets are convex: limited downside, optionality upside
- **Power Law Thinking** — A portfolio of small bets is how you find the power-law winner
- **Hoffman Speed vs. Certainty Matrix** — Small bets are the rapid-learning quadrant
- **Reversible-Irreversible Decisions** — Small bets are Type 2 decisions by design
