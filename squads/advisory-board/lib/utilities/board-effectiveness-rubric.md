# Board Effectiveness Rubric

## Purpose

A comprehensive scoring rubric for evaluating the overall effectiveness of a board or advisory board. This rubric goes beyond meeting quality to assess whether the board is fulfilling its core functions: providing strategic oversight, ensuring accountability, surfacing risks, and making high-quality decisions. Use this rubric for quarterly self-assessments and annual board evaluations.

## How to Use

Conduct a full board effectiveness assessment annually (recommended: during the first board meeting of the fiscal year). Conduct a light-touch self-assessment quarterly. Each board member scores independently, then scores are discussed collectively to surface perception gaps. Feed results into `data/metrics/board-effectiveness-score.md`.

## Scale/Criteria

### Dimension 1: Strategic Value (Weight: 25%)
Does the board contribute meaningfully to strategic thinking?

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | Board members bring unique strategic perspectives that materially improve decisions. Board challenges assumptions constructively and introduces frameworks the team would not have reached alone. Strategic discussions are the most valuable part of board time. |
| 4 | Board provides strong strategic input. Most members contribute perspectives that improve thinking. Occasional redundancy with management's own analysis. |
| 3 | Board engages in strategic discussion but often at a surface level. Input is sometimes useful but rarely changes the direction of thinking. |
| 2 | Board reviews strategy but does not add to it. Discussion is confirmatory rather than challenging. Board lacks the context or expertise to push thinking forward. |
| 1 | Board does not engage in strategy or engages only to second-guess without adding value. Strategic discussions are avoided or perfunctory. |

### Dimension 2: Decision Quality (Weight: 25%)
Does the board make good decisions and improve organizational decision-making?

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | Board decisions are well-structured, evidence-based, and properly debated. Dissent is captured and honored. Decision processes are calibrated to decision type. The board actively improves the organization's decision-making capability. |
| 4 | Most board decisions follow good process. Decision quality rubric scores average 4+. Some decisions could benefit from more structured dissent. |
| 3 | Decision quality is inconsistent. Some decisions are well-made; others are rushed or politically driven. Decision processes are not systematically applied. |
| 2 | Board decisions are frequently driven by the loudest voice, recency bias, or political dynamics rather than evidence. Little structured process. |
| 1 | Board decision-making is dysfunctional. Decisions are either rubber-stamped or endlessly debated without resolution. |

### Dimension 3: Accountability and Oversight (Weight: 20%)
Does the board hold the organization accountable and fulfill its oversight function?

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | Board tracks commitments rigorously. Action items are followed up every meeting. Performance against plan is reviewed with candor. Difficult conversations about underperformance happen promptly. The board holds itself to the same standard. |
| 4 | Good accountability practices. Most commitments are tracked. Difficult conversations happen, though sometimes with delay. |
| 3 | Some commitments are tracked but others fade. Accountability is inconsistent — depends on the topic or the person. Difficult conversations are sometimes avoided. |
| 2 | Accountability is weak. Commitments are made but follow-through is not systematically tracked. Underperformance is acknowledged but not addressed. |
| 1 | No meaningful accountability. The same issues appear on the agenda repeatedly without resolution. Underperformance is tolerated indefinitely. |

### Dimension 4: Risk and Crisis Readiness (Weight: 15%)
Does the board effectively identify and manage risk?

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | Board proactively identifies emerging risks before they become crises. Risk register is reviewed and updated regularly. Crisis response protocols are defined and tested. The board surfaces uncomfortable truths. |
| 4 | Good risk awareness. Risk register exists and is reviewed. Most material risks are identified. Crisis protocols exist. |
| 3 | Risk is discussed when it appears on the agenda but not proactively surfaced. Risk register exists but is not consistently updated. Crisis protocols are informal. |
| 2 | Risk management is reactive. Issues are addressed only after they become problems. No formal risk register or crisis protocols. |
| 1 | Board is blind to risk or actively dismisses concerns. No systematic risk management. The organization is repeatedly surprised by foreseeable events. |

### Dimension 5: Composition and Dynamics (Weight: 15%)
Does the board have the right people working together effectively?

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | Board composition covers all critical competency areas. Diversity of perspective is high. Members trust each other and engage in productive conflict. Power dynamics are healthy — no single member dominates. |
| 4 | Good composition with minor gaps. Dynamics are mostly healthy. Occasional imbalance in participation or influence. |
| 3 | Some competency gaps in board composition. Dynamics are functional but one or two relationships create friction or avoidance. Some voices are consistently underrepresented. |
| 2 | Significant competency gaps. Dynamics are strained — either overly deferential or overly combative. Key perspectives are missing. |
| 1 | Board composition does not match the company's needs. Dynamics are toxic — deep distrust, persistent conflict, or complete disengagement. |

## Scoring Guide

### Calculating the Overall Score
**Overall = (Strategic x 0.25) + (Decisions x 0.25) + (Accountability x 0.20) + (Risk x 0.15) + (Composition x 0.15)**

### Interpretation
| Score Range | Rating | Action |
|-------------|--------|--------|
| 4.5 - 5.0 | World-class | Maintain and evolve. Document practices for organizational learning. |
| 3.5 - 4.4 | Effective | Strong board. Target the weakest dimension for focused improvement. |
| 2.5 - 3.4 | Adequate | Board is functional but not adding maximum value. Two or more dimensions need attention. Consider a board retreat to address. |
| 1.5 - 2.4 | Underperforming | Board is a net cost rather than a net asset. Structural changes needed — composition, process, or both. External facilitation recommended. |
| 1.0 - 1.4 | Dysfunctional | Board is actively harming the organization. Immediate intervention: external board evaluation, potential composition changes, governance overhaul. |

### Annual Assessment Process
1. Each board member completes the rubric independently (allow 1 week).
2. An external facilitator aggregates scores and identifies perception gaps (where scores differ by 2+ points across members).
3. A 90-minute board session discusses the results, focusing on gaps and the lowest-scoring dimension.
4. One structural improvement is committed to per quarter.
5. Progress is reviewed at the next quarterly assessment.

### Perception Gap Analysis
When individual member scores differ significantly:
- **Member scores high, CEO scores low:** Board may be out of touch with operational reality.
- **CEO scores high, members score low:** CEO may not be getting the challenge they need, or members feel their input is not valued.
- **New members score lower than tenured members:** Fresh perspectives are often more accurate — tenured members may have normalized dysfunction.
