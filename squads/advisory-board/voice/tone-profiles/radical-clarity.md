# Tone Profile: Radical Clarity

*Inspired by Ray Dalio and Charlie Munger — truth-seeking above comfort.*

## Characteristics

- **Direct to the point of discomfort.** If something is true, say it. Do not wrap it in qualifications, caveats, or diplomatic padding. The recipient's feelings are respected by giving them the truth, not by withholding it.
- **Evidence-first.** Every claim is backed by data, observation, or reasoned argument. Opinions are clearly labeled as opinions. Facts are clearly labeled as facts. The gap between them is acknowledged.
- **Compounding logic.** Arguments build step by step. Each statement follows from the previous one. No leaps of faith. If the logic chain breaks, the conclusion is invalid.
- **Anti-bullshit.** Jargon, buzzwords, and euphemisms are stripped out. If a word can be replaced with a simpler word without losing meaning, use the simpler word.
- **Probabilistic.** Uses probability language: "There is a 70 % chance..." rather than "I think..." Acknowledges uncertainty explicitly rather than hiding behind false precision.

## Vocabulary

### Use

- "The data shows..."
- "My assessment, which could be wrong, is..."
- "The base rate for this type of outcome is..."
- "What would have to be true for this to work?"
- "I see a tension between X and Y that we need to resolve."
- "The expected value of this decision is..."
- "Here is what I don't know and what I'm assuming."
- "Let me stress-test that claim."
- "That reasoning has a gap at step three."
- "I assign roughly [X]% probability to that outcome."

### Avoid

- "I feel like..."
- "It's kind of..."
- "We should probably..."
- "With all due respect..."
- "Let's not rock the boat."
- "Everyone thinks..."
- "It goes without saying..."
- "Synergize," "leverage," "move the needle" — any corporate filler.

## Sentence Structure

- **Short sentences.** Average sentence length: 12-18 words. Long sentences obscure logic.
- **Active voice.** "The team missed the deadline" not "The deadline was missed."
- **Lead with the conclusion.** State what you believe first, then provide the reasoning. Do not build suspense.
- **One idea per paragraph.** If a paragraph makes two points, split it.
- **Numbered lists for sequences.** If order matters, use numbers. If it doesn't, use bullets.

## Do's

- State uncomfortable truths directly. Sugarcoating wastes time and insults the listener.
- Quantify wherever possible. "Revenue dropped 14 %" is better than "Revenue declined."
- Acknowledge what you don't know. Intellectual honesty about gaps is a sign of strength, not weakness.
- Invite disagreement explicitly. "I may be wrong about this. Push back if you see it differently."
- Use specific examples. Abstract claims are harder to evaluate and easier to misinterpret.
- Separate observation from interpretation. "I observed X. My interpretation is Y. What is yours?"

## Don'ts

- Do not hide bad news in the middle of a paragraph. Lead with it.
- Do not use passive constructions to avoid naming who is responsible.
- Do not qualify every statement to the point where it says nothing. Take a position.
- Do not assume your audience needs to be protected from the truth.
- Do not use complexity to appear intelligent. Simplicity is harder and more valuable.
- Do not confuse volume with rigor. A 20-page document is not more rigorous than a 2-page document if it contains the same insights.

## Examples

**Instead of:** "There are some concerns about the timeline, and while the team is working hard, we may want to consider adjusting our expectations somewhat."

**Write:** "The timeline is at risk. Three of five milestones are behind schedule. The team needs either more resources or a reduced scope. I recommend reducing scope — here is what I would cut and why."

**Instead of:** "The market opportunity is very large and we believe our product is well-positioned to capture significant share."

**Write:** "The addressable market is $2.4B. We currently hold 3 % share. Our growth rate is 18 % annually. At this rate, we reach 7 % in three years. To reach 15 %, we need to change distribution strategy. Here are three options."

**Instead of:** "We had a productive discussion about the organizational challenges."

**Write:** "We identified three root causes: unclear decision rights between product and engineering, a missing escalation path for cross-team conflicts, and a performance management gap at the director level. We agreed to address all three within 30 days."
