# Hoffman Alliance Framework

## Purpose

Reid Hoffman's Alliance Framework reconceptualizes the employer-employee relationship as a mutually beneficial alliance with defined "tours of duty" rather than a permanent family or transactional contract. Each tour has explicit goals for both sides — what the company needs from the person AND what the person needs from the company. This creates honest, productive relationships that acknowledge modern career realities while building genuine mutual commitment.

## When to Use

- When hiring senior talent and structuring role expectations
- When an employee seems disengaged or considering leaving
- When designing retention and development programs
- When building a culture of honest career conversations
- When structuring advisory and board member relationships

## Core Model

### Three Types of Tours of Duty

**Rotational:** Entry-level, not personalized. Standard program for learning and assessment. Duration: 1-2 years. Both sides evaluating fit.

**Transformational:** Personalized mission aligned with the company's needs and the employee's growth goals. Duration: 2-4 years. The sweet spot where both sides invest deeply.

**Foundational:** The employee's personal mission and the company's mission are essentially identical. Rare. Applies to founders, long-tenured executives, and mission-aligned leaders. Duration: indefinite.

### The Alliance Compact
For each tour, both sides make explicit:
- **Company's mission for the employee:** What transformation the company needs delivered
- **Employee's mission for themselves:** What skills, experiences, and advancement they seek
- **Alignment zone:** Where these overlap is the basis of the alliance
- **Honest timeline:** An open conversation about duration expectations

## Steps

1. **Define the tour type** for each key hire or existing employee
2. **Articulate both sides' missions** explicitly in writing
3. **Find the alignment zone** — where organizational and personal goals overlap
4. **Set success criteria** for both sides
5. **Check in quarterly** on alignment and progress
6. **Plan transitions honestly** — when a tour is ending, design the next one together
7. **Maintain the alumni network** — former employees are extended alliance members

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Advisory relationships should be structured as tours of duty with clear missions and timelines.
- Help founders have honest conversations with key employees about career trajectories.
- Evaluate retention strategy through the alliance lens: are key people in well-aligned tours?

## Common Mistakes

1. **The family myth.** Companies are not families. Families don't have performance reviews.
2. **Avoiding timeline conversations.** Not discussing duration doesn't make people stay longer.
3. **One-sided alliance.** If only the company's needs are discussed, the employee feels used.
4. **Ignoring alumni.** Former employees are referral sources, customers, and future re-hires.

## Output Format

```markdown
# Alliance Tour: [Person Name]

## Tour Type: Rotational / Transformational / Foundational
## Expected Duration: [Timeframe]

## Company's Mission for This Person
[What the company needs accomplished]

## Person's Mission for Themselves
[What they want to learn, achieve, become]

## Alignment Zone
[Where missions overlap]

## Success Criteria (Both Sides)
[Measurable outcomes]

## Quarterly Check-in Schedule: [Dates]
```
