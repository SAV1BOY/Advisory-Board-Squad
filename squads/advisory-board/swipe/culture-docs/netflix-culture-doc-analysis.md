# Netflix Culture Doc Analysis

## Overview
Netflix's culture deck (originally published 2009, updated multiple times) became one of the most influential documents in Silicon Valley. Sheryl Sandberg called it "the most important document ever to come out of the Valley." This analysis extracts the principles, techniques, and board-relevant insights.

## Core Principles

### Freedom and Responsibility
- Netflix provides extraordinary freedom to top performers and expects extraordinary responsibility in return.
- No vacation policy (unlimited PTO). No expense policy beyond "act in Netflix's best interest."
- The freedom attracts top talent. The responsibility filter retains the right talent and exits the wrong.

### Context, Not Control
- Leaders provide strategic context so team members can make good decisions independently.
- If people are making bad decisions, the problem is context (not enough information, unclear strategy), not character.
- Micromanagement is a leadership failure, not a team failure.

### The Keeper Test
- Managers regularly ask themselves: "If this person told me they were leaving, would I fight to keep them?" If the answer is no, they should be given a generous severance package and let go.
- Controversial but effective: it maintains the talent density that makes everything else work.

### Highly Aligned, Loosely Coupled
- High alignment on strategy and goals (everyone knows the direction).
- Loose coupling on execution (teams have autonomy to execute in the way they see fit).
- This requires exceptional communication and trust.

### Candor and Feedback
- Feedback is given directly, not through HR or performance review systems.
- "Only say things about someone that you would say to their face."
- 360 feedback is done publicly, in writing, signed.

## What the Culture Doc Gets Right
- It's honest about the tradeoffs. Netflix explicitly says this culture isn't for everyone.
- It operationalizes values into specific behaviors and decisions (not vague aspirations).
- It treats adults like adults—trusting them with freedom and holding them to high standards.
- It creates a self-selecting culture: people who thrive under these conditions stay; those who don't, leave.

## What the Culture Doc Gets Wrong (or Omits)
- The "Keeper Test" can create anxiety and short-termism if poorly implemented
- Freedom without sufficient context leads to chaos in less disciplined teams
- The culture works well for high-performing knowledge workers; it's less applicable to operational roles
- Candor without empathy becomes brutality. The doc underemphasizes the emotional intelligence required.

## Board Application
- Use Netflix's approach to operationalizing values: every value should have specific behavioral examples
- The Keeper Test is a useful mental model for board composition: would you fight to keep each board member?
- "Context, not control" applies to board governance: provide strategic context to management, don't micromanage
- Consider whether your company's culture doc would survive public scrutiny—if not, there's a gap between stated and actual culture
