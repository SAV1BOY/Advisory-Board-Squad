# Sivers Opposite Check Checklist

## Purpose
Applies Sivers' technique of considering the opposite of your initial instinct or the conventional approach. Often the best answer is the reverse of what seems obvious. This checklist is a structured prompt to invert assumptions, challenge defaults, and explore whether doing the opposite of what everyone else does could create distinctive value.

## When to Use
- When stuck on a problem and conventional solutions are not working.
- During brainstorming and ideation sessions.
- When evaluating whether to follow industry convention or chart a different path.
- When a decision seems obvious and no one is questioning it.
- During strategy sessions to generate contrarian options.

## Gate Criteria

### Inversion Practice
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: For each major decision, the opposite option has been explicitly considered and analyzed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Industry conventions that the company follows have been questioned: would doing the opposite create value.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Assumptions labeled as "obvious" have been tested by imagining their inverse.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team has asked: "What if the customer wants the opposite of what we think?"
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Historical decisions that defied convention and succeeded are studied for patterns.

### Analysis Quality
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The opposite option is analyzed with the same rigor as the conventional option, not dismissed reflexively.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Potential advantages of the opposite approach are specifically identified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The risks of the opposite approach are assessed, but do not automatically disqualify it.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Someone is assigned to advocate for the opposite position to ensure it gets a fair hearing.

### Application Discipline
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The opposite check is applied to strategy, product, pricing, hiring, and culture decisions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: When the opposite approach is chosen, it is committed to fully, not implemented half-heartedly.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Results from opposite-of-convention decisions are tracked to build institutional knowledge.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The practice is a regular habit, not a one-time exercise.

### Intellectual Courage
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team is willing to pursue the opposite approach even if it draws criticism.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization values original thinking, not just best-practice adoption.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decisions to go against convention are based on analysis, not contrarianism for its own sake.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team can articulate why the opposite approach is better in this context.

## Red Flags
- The organization always follows industry best practices without questioning whether they apply.
- Contrarian ideas are dismissed because "that's not how it's done."
- The team has never considered doing the opposite of its default approach.
- Convention is followed because it is safe, not because it is optimal.
- The team confuses being different with being better (contrarianism without analysis).
- No one on the team is willing to advocate for an unconventional approach.

## Escalation Triggers
- The organization is failing with conventional approaches but refuses to consider alternatives.
- A competitor succeeds by doing the opposite of the industry norm.
- The team is stuck in a rut and generates the same types of solutions repeatedly.
- Innovation scores decline because the team only generates conventional ideas.
- The opposite check reveals a significantly better approach but political pressure prevents adoption.
