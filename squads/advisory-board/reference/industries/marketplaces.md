# Marketplaces Industry Reference

## Business Model Characteristics
- **Revenue model:** Transaction fee (take rate) on GMV, subscriptions for premium features, or advertising
- **Take rates:** 5-30% depending on value added, market maturity, and competition
- **Key dynamic:** Chicken-and-egg problem. Need supply to attract demand and demand to attract supply.
- **Network effects:** Cross-side (more supply → more demand → more supply). The fundamental moat.
- **Liquidity:** The % of listings that result in a transaction. The critical metric for marketplace health.

## Key Metrics
- **GMV (Gross Merchandise Value):** Total transaction value on the platform
- **Net revenue:** GMV x take rate (after refunds and incentives)
- **Liquidity rate:** Transactions / listings. Higher = healthier marketplace.
- **Supply-side retention:** What % of suppliers remain active after 12 months?
- **Buyer repeat rate:** What % of buyers transact again within 12 months?
- **CAC by side:** Track acquisition cost separately for supply and demand

## Growth Strategy Patterns
- **Constrain geography or category initially.** Win one market before expanding. Uber: San Francisco. Airbnb: New York.
- **Subsidize the hard side.** Usually supply in the early days. Pay or incentivize supply to join, then attract demand organically.
- **Single-player mode.** Provide value to one side even without the other side (SaaS for suppliers, content for buyers).
- **Managed marketplace.** Add services (vetting, curation, fulfillment) to increase take rate and trust.

## Competitive Dynamics
- **Winner-take-most in commodity markets** (ridesharing, food delivery). Multi-tenanting is easy, so moat comes from density and efficiency.
- **Room for niche winners in specialized markets** (B2B, vertical-specific). Deep domain expertise matters more than scale.
- **Disintermediation risk:** Buyers and sellers transact off-platform after initial matchmaking.

## Board Considerations
- Focus on liquidity and retention, not just GMV growth. Growing GMV through subsidies without improving liquidity is unsustainable.
- Monitor take rate trajectory: increasing take rate signals pricing power; decreasing signals competition.
- Evaluate disintermediation risk: if the platform's value is only matchmaking, it's vulnerable.
- Assess which side of the marketplace the company "owns" and whether that side is defensible.
