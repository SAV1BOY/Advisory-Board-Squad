# Information Asymmetry Check Checklist

## Purpose
Detects and manages information asymmetry between the board and management. Management always knows more about the business than the board. Healthy governance manages this asymmetry by ensuring the board receives the information it needs to govern effectively. Unhealthy governance exploits this asymmetry by filtering information to control board decisions.

## When to Use
- During board meeting preparation to assess information completeness.
- When board members feel they lack sufficient information to make decisions.
- After a surprise negative outcome that the board was not prepared for.
- During annual governance effectiveness reviews.

## Gate Criteria

### Information Completeness
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board receives both good and bad news in equal detail.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Financial information includes leading indicators, not just lagging results.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Risk information is presented proactively, not just when problems materialize.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Competitive and market context is provided to help the board assess performance.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Employee, customer, and operational health metrics are included in board reporting.

### Information Quality
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Data presented to the board is independently verified or auditable.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Management narrative is tested against underlying data.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board has access to functional leaders (not just the CEO) for domain-specific questions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: External benchmarks and data sources supplement management-provided information.

### Access and Timeliness
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board materials are delivered with adequate time for review and questioning.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board members can request additional information between meetings and receive timely responses.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Material developments are communicated to the board promptly, not held for the next meeting.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board has a direct relationship with the CFO and General Counsel.

### Safeguards
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board periodically assesses whether it is receiving sufficient information.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Executive sessions allow the board to discuss concerns about information quality.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: External auditors have direct access to the board for reporting concerns.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Whistleblower channels allow the board to receive information that may be filtered by management.

## Red Flags
- The board is consistently surprised by developments they should have been informed about.
- Management presents only favorable data and the board does not ask about unfavorable trends.
- Board members do not ask questions that would reveal information gaps.
- The CEO controls all information flow to the board with no independent channels.
- Financial reports are formatted to obscure rather than illuminate.

## Escalation Triggers
- The board discovers material information that management withheld.
- External auditors raise concerns about information provided to the board.
- A whistleblower report alleges management is misrepresenting information to the board.
- Multiple board members independently express concern about information quality.
- A significant event occurs that the board should have been warned about based on available data.
