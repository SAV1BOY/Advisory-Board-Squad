# Growth Channel Dependency Check Checklist

## Purpose
Evaluates the organization's dependency on specific growth channels and the risk that creates. Channel concentration is a hidden fragility; when a single channel falters, growth can collapse overnight. This checklist ensures channel diversification is intentional and that dependency risks are managed.

## When to Use
- Quarterly during growth and marketing reviews.
- When a channel's performance changes significantly.
- When evaluating new channel investments.
- When a platform or partner that controls a channel makes policy changes.

## Gate Criteria
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: All growth channels are mapped with their contribution to total growth.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: No single channel accounts for more than 40% of new customer acquisition without explicit risk acceptance.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Owned channels (email, direct, product-led) are developed alongside rented channels (paid ads, platform).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Channel economics (CAC, conversion, LTV by channel) are tracked independently.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Channel diversification targets are set and progress is tracked.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Platform risk for each channel is assessed (what if Google/Apple/Facebook changes its rules).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A contingency plan exists for the loss of the primary channel.

## Red Flags
- Over 50% of growth comes from a single channel the company does not control.
- A channel's economics are deteriorating but investment continues because alternatives are not developed.
- The company has never tested growth through channels beyond its primary one.
- Channel dependency has increased over the past 12 months.

## Escalation Triggers
- A primary channel's performance drops by more than 20% in a single quarter.
- A platform changes rules or pricing that materially affects the primary channel.
- A competitor gains control of a channel the company depends on.
- Total growth declines because channel diversification efforts are behind plan.
