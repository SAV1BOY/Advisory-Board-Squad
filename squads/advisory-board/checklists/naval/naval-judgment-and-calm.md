# Naval Judgment and Calm Checklist

## Purpose
Evaluates whether the organization's decision-makers exercise good judgment under uncertainty and maintain composure during high-stakes situations. Naval argues that judgment, especially in the face of uncertainty, is the most valuable skill a leader can have. Good judgment requires clarity of thought, emotional regulation, and the ability to resist both panic and overconfidence. This checklist detects when judgment quality is compromised.

## When to Use
- Before and during high-stakes decisions.
- When the organization faces a crisis or sudden change in circumstances.
- When evaluating leadership capability for promotion or hiring.
- After a decision that produced a significantly unexpected outcome.
- When the decision-making environment is characterized by high emotion or pressure.

## Gate Criteria

### Decision Quality Under Pressure
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decision-makers can articulate the key variables and their relative importance calmly.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Emotional state is assessed before major decisions: decisions are deferred if decision-makers are not in a clear state.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The pace of decision-making matches the actual urgency, not the perceived urgency.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decisions are framed around what matters long-term, not just what feels urgent now.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team distinguishes between decisions that need to be made now and those that benefit from delay.

### Cognitive Clarity
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decision-makers seek first principles rather than reasoning by analogy or convention.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Complex problems are broken down into components before attempting to solve them.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team resists narrative fallacy: constructing a simple story to explain complex events.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Uncertainty is acknowledged explicitly rather than filled with false confidence.

### Emotional Regulation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders model calm during crises, preventing panic from cascading through the organization.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organizational culture allows for acknowledging difficulty without catastrophizing.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decision-making processes have cooling-off periods for high-emotion situations.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders recognize when their own emotions are influencing their judgment and seek input.

### Judgment Calibration
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders track their prediction accuracy over time and calibrate their confidence.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Past judgments are reviewed against outcomes to improve future judgment.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization values probabilistic thinking over binary predictions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders seek out information that challenges their judgment, not just confirmatory data.

## Red Flags
- Every situation is treated as urgent, with no prioritization or calm assessment.
- Leaders make dramatic decisions during moments of high emotion (fear, anger, euphoria).
- The organization oscillates between panic and overconfidence based on the latest data point.
- Leaders cannot distinguish between signal and noise in incoming information.
- Decision-making quality visibly deteriorates under time pressure.
- No one questions the pace of decision-making, even when there is no genuine urgency.

## Escalation Triggers
- A major decision made under emotional pressure produces a material negative outcome.
- Leadership's emotional state is visibly affecting employee morale and organizational stability.
- The team is making decisions faster than their information allows, leading to frequent reversals.
- A pattern of reactive, crisis-driven decision-making displaces proactive strategic work.
- External stakeholders express concern about leadership stability or judgment quality.
