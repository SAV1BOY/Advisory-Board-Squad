# Fiduciary Duties — Practical Guide

## Overview
Board members owe fiduciary duties to the company and its shareholders. Understanding these duties is not optional—it's the legal and ethical foundation of board service. This guide presents fiduciary duties in practical, actionable terms for advisory board members.

## The Three Core Duties

### Duty of Care
- **What it means:** Make informed decisions. Act with the care a reasonably prudent person would use in similar circumstances.
- **In practice:** Read the materials. Attend meetings. Ask questions. If you don't understand something, say so. Hiring experts when needed is part of care, not a sign of weakness.
- **Common violations:** Rubber-stamping decisions without review. Missing multiple meetings. Failing to investigate red flags.
- **Protection:** The Business Judgment Rule protects directors who act in good faith, on an informed basis, with a reasonable belief that their actions serve the company's best interest.

### Duty of Loyalty
- **What it means:** Put the company's interests above your own. No self-dealing, no competing opportunities, no conflicts of interest.
- **In practice:** Disclose all potential conflicts. Recuse yourself from votes where you have a personal interest. Don't use company information for personal gain.
- **Common violations:** Related-party transactions without disclosure. Taking a corporate opportunity for yourself. Providing insider information to third parties.
- **Protection:** Full disclosure and board approval of any conflicted transaction. Transparency is the antidote.

### Duty of Obedience (or Good Faith)
- **What it means:** Act within the company's legal authority and in accordance with its governing documents (articles, bylaws, shareholder agreements).
- **In practice:** Ensure the company complies with applicable laws and regulations. Don't authorize illegal actions. Maintain the company's stated mission and purpose.
- **Common violations:** Ignoring regulatory requirements. Authorizing actions that violate the company's charter. Knowingly allowing law-breaking.

## Advisory Board vs. Formal Board

### Advisory Board Members
- Typically no formal fiduciary duty (unless the advisory board agreement creates one)
- Best practice: act AS IF you have fiduciary duties, even if legally you don't
- Reputation and ethical obligation substitute for legal obligation
- Avoid giving advice that would violate fiduciary duties if you were a formal director

### Formal Board Members
- Full fiduciary duties apply
- Personal liability potential (mitigated by D&O insurance, indemnification, and the Business Judgment Rule)
- Minutes, votes, and documented reasoning are your protection

## Practical Fiduciary Checklist

Before every major board decision:
1. Have I reviewed the relevant materials and understood them?
2. Do I have any personal conflict of interest? If so, have I disclosed it?
3. Am I acting in the best interest of the company and its shareholders?
4. Have I asked the hard questions, or am I going along to avoid conflict?
5. Would I be comfortable if this decision were made public?
6. Is this decision within the company's legal authority?
7. Have we considered the impact on all stakeholders (not just the loudest ones)?

## Key Risk Areas
- **Interested transactions** — Any deal where a board member or their associate benefits personally
- **Executive compensation** — Must be justified by market data and performance, not friendship
- **Change of control** — Selling the company requires the highest standard of care (Revlon duties in Delaware)
- **Insolvency zone** — When the company approaches insolvency, fiduciary duties may shift to include creditors
