# Lencioni Clarity Stack Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the leadership team has achieved alignment on the six critical questions that Lencioni identifies as the foundation of organizational clarity. Without alignment on these questions, teams pull in different directions, employees receive contradictory messages, and execution suffers. This checklist verifies that clarity exists and is communicated consistently throughout the organization.

## When to Use
- During leadership team alignment sessions.
- When the organization is experiencing confusion about direction or priorities.
- When onboarding new leadership team members.
- Quarterly to verify that clarity has not eroded.
- When employee surveys indicate confusion about strategy or priorities.

## Gate Criteria

### Question 1: Why Do We Exist?
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization's core purpose is articulated in a single statement.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: All leadership team members can state the purpose consistently.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The purpose is aspirational and enduring, not tied to a specific product or timeframe.

### Question 2: How Do We Behave?
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Core values (2-3 maximum) are defined as behavioral descriptions, not abstract words.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Values are genuinely distinctive, not generic virtues everyone claims.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Hiring, firing, and promotion decisions are visibly influenced by values.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The leadership team can provide recent examples of values in action and values being enforced.

### Question 3: What Do We Do?
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization's business can be described in one plain-language sentence.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Employees at all levels can describe what the organization does consistently.

### Question 4: How Will We Succeed?
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The strategy is articulated as a set of intentional choices, not a laundry list of initiatives.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Strategic anchors (3 maximum) define how the company differentiates and wins.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: All leadership team members can explain the strategy consistently.

### Question 5: What Is Most Important Right Now?
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A single thematic goal for the current period is identified and communicated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The thematic goal is qualitative and rallying, with supporting objectives that are measurable.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: All team members know the thematic goal and how their work contributes to it.

### Question 6: Who Must Do What?
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each leadership team member's accountability is clear and non-overlapping.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Roles are defined by outcomes, not just functional descriptions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team can state each other's core accountability without hesitation.

## Red Flags
- Leadership team members give different answers to these six questions.
- The organization has more than three values, and no one can remember them all.
- The strategy changes every quarter without a clear reason.
- There is no single priority that the whole organization is aligned around.
- Role definitions overlap, creating confusion and conflict about ownership.
- New hires learn what the company actually does from peers, not from onboarding.

## Escalation Triggers
- A leadership team alignment session reveals fundamental disagreement on core questions.
- Employee surveys consistently show confusion about strategy, values, or priorities.
- Cross-functional conflicts trace back to misalignment on one of the six questions.
- The organization cannot articulate a single thematic priority for the current period.
- Multiple teams are working on initiatives that contradict each other due to lack of clarity.
