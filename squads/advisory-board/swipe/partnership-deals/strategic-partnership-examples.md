# Strategic Partnership Examples — Swipe File

## Overview
Strategic partnerships create value when both parties contribute complementary assets that neither could replicate independently. These examples illustrate partnership structures, success factors, and warning signs.

## Partnership Pattern 1: Distribution Partnership

### Example: Startup + Enterprise Distribution
- **Structure:** Enterprise company integrates startup's technology into their product, offers it to their customer base
- **Terms:** 70/30 revenue share (startup keeps 70%), enterprise gets co-marketing rights, startup provides integration support
- **Success factors:** Clear API integration, joint customer success process, aligned sales incentives
- **Warning signs:** Enterprise partner demands exclusivity without guaranteed minimums, integration deprioritized by partner's engineering team

### Key Terms to Negotiate
- Minimum performance commitments (leads generated, deals closed)
- Exclusivity scope and duration (narrow and short if at all)
- Data sharing and customer ownership
- Termination rights and transition support

## Partnership Pattern 2: Technology Integration

### Example: Two SaaS Companies Building Shared Workflow
- **Structure:** Bidirectional API integration. Each company maintains independence but creates a combined experience.
- **Terms:** No revenue share. Each company sells independently. Joint marketing investment. Shared roadmap for integration features.
- **Success factors:** Dedicated integration engineers on both sides, shared customer feedback loop, regular joint roadmap reviews
- **Warning signs:** One-sided investment (only one company is building the integration), competitive overlap emerging over time

### Key Terms to Negotiate
- API stability commitments (minimum notice for breaking changes)
- Data handling and privacy compliance for shared data
- Co-marketing rights and brand guidelines
- Competitive restrictions (can either party partner with a direct competitor?)

## Partnership Pattern 3: Go-to-Market Alliance

### Example: Complementary Products Selling Together
- **Structure:** Joint sales motion targeting shared customer profile. Co-branded offering.
- **Terms:** Lead sharing agreement, joint selling process, shared customer success playbook
- **Success factors:** Compatible sales processes, aligned customer profiles, executive sponsorship on both sides
- **Warning signs:** Unequal lead quality (one side sends garbage leads), no accountability for referral quality

## Partnership Governance Best Practices

1. **Executive sponsor on each side** — Senior leader responsible for the partnership's success
2. **Quarterly business reviews** — Review metrics, pipeline, issues, and roadmap alignment
3. **Clear escalation path** — When operational issues arise, how do they get resolved?
4. **Annual renewal decision** — Partnerships should be actively renewed, not passively continued
5. **Exit planning** — Before you need it, agree on how to unwind gracefully

## Board Application
- Evaluate every proposed partnership for strategic fit and operational feasibility
- Require clear success metrics and review cadence for all partnerships
- Monitor for dependency risk: if a partnership becomes critical to revenue, it's a strategic vulnerability
- Ensure exit terms are negotiated upfront
