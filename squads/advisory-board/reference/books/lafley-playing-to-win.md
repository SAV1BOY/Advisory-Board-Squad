# Playing to Win — A.G. Lafley & Roger Martin

## Summary

Lafley and Martin present strategy as five cascading choices drawn from Lafley's transformation of Procter & Gamble. The framework moves from aspiration to execution: What is our winning aspiration? Where will we play? How will we win? What capabilities must we have? What management systems are required? The central insight is that strategy is not a plan—it's a set of interrelated choices, and the most critical choice is where NOT to play. Companies that try to play everywhere win nowhere.

## Key Principles

- **Strategy is choice** — Strategy means making explicit decisions about what to do and what not to do. A strategy that doesn't exclude options isn't a strategy.
- **The five choices cascade** — Each choice constrains and enables the next. Changing one requires reconsidering all downstream choices.
- **Where to play is more important than how to win** — Competing in the right market with an average strategy beats competing in the wrong market with a brilliant strategy.
- **Winning aspiration must be specific** — Not "be the best" but "be the leading provider of X to Y in Z." Specificity creates alignment.
- **Capabilities and systems are the foundation** — Without the right capabilities and management systems, upper-level choices are aspirational fiction.

## Frameworks Extracted

1. **The Five Cascading Choices** — (1) Winning aspiration, (2) Where to play (geography, segment, channel, category), (3) How to win (value proposition, competitive advantage), (4) Capabilities required, (5) Management systems needed. All five must be coherent.
2. **The Where-to-Play Matrix** — Map opportunities on: attractiveness (size, growth, margins) vs. right to win (capability fit, competitive position). Only enter quadrants where both are high.
3. **The Strategy Logic Flow** — For any strategic choice: (1) What must be true for this to work? (2) What are the biggest risks? (3) How would we test our assumptions before full commitment?
4. **The Reverse Engineering Test** — Analyze successful competitors by mapping their five choices. Reveals the coherence that drives their performance and identifies gaps you can exploit.

## Quotable Lines

- "Strategy is about making choices, not making plans."
- "If you try to win everywhere, you'll win nowhere."
- "A strategy that cannot be wrong cannot be right—because it hasn't made real choices."
- "The best strategies feel uncomfortable because they require giving things up."

## Application to Board Decisions

- **Strategic review** — Require the CEO to present all five choices and demonstrate coherence between them. Incoherent choices are the root of most strategic failures.
- **Market entry/exit** — Use the Where-to-Play Matrix to evaluate new opportunities and audit whether current markets still deserve resources.
- **Capability investment** — Oversee whether the company is building capabilities its strategy requires. Strategy without capability-building is fantasy.
- **M&A alignment** — Every acquisition should strengthen at least one of the five choices without weakening the others.
