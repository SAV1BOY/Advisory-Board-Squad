# Sinek Infinite Game Check Checklist

## Purpose
Evaluates whether the organization is playing the infinite game: competing to stay in the game indefinitely rather than to win a finite contest. Sinek distinguishes between finite games (fixed rules, known players, agreed-upon objectives) and infinite games (changing rules, evolving players, the purpose is to keep playing). Organizations that apply finite game thinking to infinite game contexts exhaust themselves chasing victories that do not matter.

## When to Use
- During strategic planning to assess the time horizon and nature of the competitive frame.
- When the organization is making decisions that sacrifice long-term viability for short-term wins.
- When evaluating competitive strategy: are we trying to beat a rival or to advance a cause.
- When organizational energy is focused on defeating competitors rather than serving customers.

## Gate Criteria

### Game Recognition
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization explicitly recognizes that its industry operates as an infinite game.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Success is defined in terms of continued relevance and impact, not defeating a specific competitor.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Strategy accounts for evolving players, rules, and conditions over a 10+ year horizon.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization has a Just Cause that transcends any individual product, market, or competitive battle.

### Long-Term Orientation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Investment decisions favor long-term capability building over short-term gains.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization resists sacrificing trust, culture, or relationships for quarterly results.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Relationships with employees, customers, and partners are treated as ongoing, not transactional.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization invests in sustainability and adaptability, not just current performance.

### Competitive Frame
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization studies competitors to improve itself, not to obsess over relative ranking.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Resources are not allocated primarily to beat a specific rival.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization welcomes worthy rivals who push it to improve.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Market share is a metric, not the goal; the goal is advancing the cause.

### Resilience and Adaptability
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization is prepared to pivot its strategy while maintaining its purpose.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Business model changes are considered as the market evolves, rather than clinging to a static model.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization builds reserves and optionality to survive setbacks.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leadership succession ensures the cause survives any individual leader.

## Red Flags
- Every strategic discussion references "beating" a specific competitor.
- Quarterly results drive all major decisions, even at the expense of long-term health.
- The organization measures success primarily by market share rank.
- Long-term investments are consistently cut to meet short-term financial targets.
- The organization has no vision beyond the next two years.
- Leadership cannot articulate why the organization should exist in 20 years.

## Escalation Triggers
- Short-term financial pressures cause decisions that undermine long-term viability.
- The organization exhausts itself chasing a rival and loses sight of its purpose.
- Key talent leaves because the organization has lost its sense of mission.
- The organization is disrupted by a new entrant playing a different game.
- The board pushes for finite game metrics at the expense of infinite game resilience.
