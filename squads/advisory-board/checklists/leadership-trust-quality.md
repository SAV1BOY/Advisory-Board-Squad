# Leadership Trust Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Assesses the level of trust within and around the leadership team. Trust is the foundation of speed, collaboration, and candor. Without it, every interaction requires more overhead, every decision takes longer, and every conflict becomes destructive. This checklist measures trust through observable behaviors, not self-reporting.

## When to Use
- Quarterly as part of leadership team health assessments.
- When new leaders join the executive team and team dynamics shift.
- When leadership conflicts surface that impede organizational performance.
- After a crisis where trust may have been damaged.
- During board evaluations of the CEO and leadership team effectiveness.

## Gate Criteria

### Behavioral Indicators of Trust
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leadership team members engage in productive conflict about ideas without personal attacks.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders admit mistakes and share failures openly in team settings.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders ask for help from peers without perceiving it as weakness.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Feedback flows both up and down the hierarchy regularly.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders give each other the benefit of the doubt before assuming negative intent.

### Commitment and Accountability
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: After decisions are made, all leaders commit publicly even if they initially disagreed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders hold each other accountable for commitments without relying on the CEO to mediate.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Commitments are tracked and reviewed; broken commitments are addressed, not ignored.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders prioritize collective results over individual department metrics.

### Transparency and Information Sharing
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders share relevant information proactively rather than hoarding it for advantage.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Bad news is surfaced quickly and without blame being the first response.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders provide context for their decisions rather than issuing directives.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: There are no persistent back-channel conversations that undermine formal decision processes.

### Trust Between Board and Management
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Management provides the board with complete and timely information, including unfavorable data.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board provides constructive challenge without micromanaging.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: CEO communicates difficult issues to the board before they become crises.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board members maintain confidentiality of sensitive information.

### Recovery Mechanisms
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A process exists for addressing trust breaches when they occur.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Team retrospectives or offsites include explicit trust-building activities.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: External facilitation is available for trust issues that the team cannot resolve internally.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Trust-related issues are addressed directly, not allowed to fester.

## Red Flags
- Leaders talk differently about each other when the person is not in the room.
- Decisions are relitigated after meetings, indicating lack of genuine commitment.
- The CEO is the only person who holds executives accountable (peer accountability is absent).
- Information silos persist despite stated values of transparency.
- New leaders are excluded from informal communication channels.
- Conflicts are avoided entirely, creating artificial harmony that masks real disagreement.

## Escalation Triggers
- A leadership team member deliberately withholds information that leads to a material negative outcome.
- Trust erosion causes a senior leader to resign citing team dysfunction.
- Board members lose confidence in management's candor based on discovering unreported issues.
- A pattern of blame-shifting after failures becomes entrenched in the leadership culture.
- The leadership team is unable to make decisions without CEO arbitration on routine matters.
