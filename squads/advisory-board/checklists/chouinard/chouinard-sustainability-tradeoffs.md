# Chouinard Sustainability Tradeoffs Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the organization honestly confronts tradeoffs between sustainability and other business objectives rather than pretending they do not exist. Chouinard demonstrated that sustainability requires genuine sacrifice and creativity, not greenwashing. This checklist provides a framework for making sustainability tradeoffs transparently and with integrity.

## When to Use
- When a product, process, or partnership decision involves environmental or social sustainability considerations.
- When evaluating supplier choices with different sustainability profiles.
- When cost pressures tempt the organization to cut sustainability investments.
- During annual sustainability reviews.
- When marketing sustainability claims that need substantiation.

## Gate Criteria

### Tradeoff Identification
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The sustainability implications of the decision are explicitly identified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The financial cost of the sustainable option versus the less sustainable option is quantified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The long-term value of sustainability (brand, risk reduction, customer loyalty) is estimated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The tradeoff is discussed openly, not hidden or assumed away.

### Honest Assessment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Sustainability claims are backed by data, not aspirational language.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization acknowledges where it falls short of sustainability goals.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Third-party validation or certification is pursued for material sustainability claims.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization does not cherry-pick sustainability metrics while ignoring negative impacts.

### Decision Framework
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A clear threshold exists for when sustainability considerations override financial ones.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The decision-making process includes sustainability as a weighted criterion, not an afterthought.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decisions to compromise on sustainability are documented with rationale and mitigation plans.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The cumulative effect of sustainability tradeoffs is tracked to prevent death by a thousand cuts.

### Continuous Improvement
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Sustainability targets are set with year-over-year improvement expectations.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization invests in innovation to reduce sustainability tradeoffs over time.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Industry best practices for sustainability are monitored and adopted.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization shares sustainability learnings with the broader community.

## Red Flags
- Sustainability is discussed in marketing but not in supply chain or operations decisions.
- The organization makes green claims that cannot be substantiated.
- Cost is always chosen over sustainability without genuine deliberation.
- Sustainability targets have not improved in two or more years.
- The organization has no mechanism for measuring its environmental or social impact.
- Sustainability is delegated to a team with no decision-making authority.

## Escalation Triggers
- A sustainability claim is challenged by a credible third party or regulator.
- The organization's sustainability performance deteriorates significantly.
- A supplier or partner is discovered to violate sustainability standards the organization claims.
- Customer or employee pressure for sustainability action exceeds the organization's response.
- A sustainability-related incident causes reputational damage.
