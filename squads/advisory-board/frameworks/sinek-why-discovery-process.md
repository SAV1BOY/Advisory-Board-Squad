# Sinek Why Discovery Process

## Purpose

The Why Discovery Process is Simon Sinek's structured method for uncovering an individual's or organization's authentic WHY — the purpose, cause, or belief that drives everything they do. Unlike strategic mission statements crafted in boardrooms, the authentic WHY is discovered by examining stories of past impact and identifying the common thread. It is not invented; it is excavated.

## When to Use

- When a company lacks a clear sense of purpose beyond profit
- When rebranding or repositioning requires deeper identity clarity
- When a founder cannot articulate why they started the company
- When hiring and the company needs to attract purpose-aligned talent
- During organizational transitions (mergers, pivots, leadership changes)
- When personal coaching requires clarity of individual purpose

## Core Model

### The Discovery Principle
Your WHY already exists — it is the pattern in your past actions and moments of greatest impact. It is not something you choose; it is something you find by looking at what you have always done when at your best.

### The Process for Individuals
1. Gather stories: Identify 5-10 specific moments when you felt most fulfilled, energized, or proud of your contribution
2. Share stories: Tell them to a partner who listens for patterns you cannot see yourself
3. Identify themes: What common thread runs through all the stories?
4. Draft the WHY statement: "To [contribution] so that [impact]"
5. Test and refine: Does this feel authentically yours? Would you do this even if not paid?

### The Process for Organizations
1. Gather stories from the founding team and early employees about moments when the organization was at its best
2. Identify the patterns: What was the common thread?
3. Articulate the organizational WHY: separate from the founder's personal WHY but often related
4. Validate with the broader team: Does this resonate as authentic?

### WHY Statement Format
"To _______ [your contribution] so that _______ [the impact on others]"

Example: "To inspire people to do the things that inspire them so that, together, we can change our world." (Sinek's own WHY)

## Steps

1. **Gather 5-10 stories** of specific moments when you/the organization felt most alive and impactful
2. **Tell the stories to a partner** — someone who can listen without judgment and identify patterns
3. **Partner identifies recurring themes** across the stories: what actions, feelings, or contributions keep appearing?
4. **Draft the WHY statement** using the "To ___ so that ___" format
5. **Test the draft** — does it feel authentic? Does it apply to most of your stories?
6. **Refine through iteration** — the WHY may take several drafts to feel right
7. **Validate externally** — do people who know you well recognize this as authentic?

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Help founders discover their authentic WHY rather than crafting a marketing-oriented mission statement. The authentic WHY inspires; the crafted one falls flat.
- Organizations where the founder's WHY and the company's WHY are aligned perform better long-term.
- When evaluating new portfolio companies, assess WHY alignment between founders, team, and market.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Inventing instead of discovering.** The WHY is not aspirational — it is already in your past. Look for it; don't create it.
2. **Making it about money.** Money is a result, not a WHY. "To maximize shareholder value" is not a WHY.
3. **Too vague.** "To make the world a better place" is not actionable. Be specific about your contribution and impact.
4. **Changing it when times are hard.** The WHY is durable. It doesn't change when the market does.
