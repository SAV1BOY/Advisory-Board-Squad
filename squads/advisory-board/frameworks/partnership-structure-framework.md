# Partnership Structure Framework

## Purpose

This framework provides a structured approach to evaluating, designing, and governing business partnerships — from strategic alliances and joint ventures to distribution agreements and co-development deals. Most partnerships fail not because the opportunity was wrong but because the structure was wrong: misaligned incentives, unclear governance, or imbalanced contributions. This framework prevents those structural failures.

## When to Use

- When evaluating a potential partnership opportunity
- When structuring terms for a strategic alliance or joint venture
- When a partnership is underperforming and needs restructuring
- When deciding between partnership and internal development (build vs. partner)
- When advising on partnership strategy

## Core Model

### Partnership Assessment Matrix

**1. Strategic Fit:** Do the partners' strategies align? Are they complementary or competitive?
**2. Contribution Balance:** Does each partner contribute something the other cannot easily obtain?
**3. Incentive Alignment:** Do both partners benefit from the same outcomes?
**4. Governance Clarity:** Who makes what decisions? How are disputes resolved?
**5. Exit Provisions:** How does the partnership end if it's not working?

### Partnership Types
- **Distribution partnership:** One partner makes, the other sells. Clear value exchange.
- **Co-development:** Both partners build together. High alignment needed.
- **Strategic alliance:** Informal cooperation on shared goals. Flexible but fragile.
- **Joint venture:** Separate legal entity. Maximum commitment and complexity.
- **Platform partnership:** One partner builds on the other's platform.

## Steps

1. **Assess strategic fit.** Draw a Venn diagram: what does each partner bring? Where is the overlap?
2. **Define contributions explicitly.** What does each partner provide? Money, technology, distribution, brand, expertise?
3. **Align incentives.** Structure the deal so both partners are rewarded for the partnership's success, not just their own.
4. **Design governance.** Decision-making authority, meeting cadence, escalation process, KPIs.
5. **Include exit provisions.** Every partnership should have a defined exit process: triggers, timeline, IP ownership upon exit.
6. **Start with a pilot.** Test the partnership small before committing large.
7. **Review quarterly.** Is the partnership delivering value for both sides?

## Common Mistakes

1. **Partnerships of convenience.** Partnering because it's easy, not because it's strategic.
2. **No exit clause.** Partnerships that cannot be exited become traps.
3. **Unbalanced contributions.** If one partner contributes 80% and receives 50%, resentment builds.
4. **Assuming alignment.** Partners may smile in meetings and compete in markets. Verify alignment through structure.
