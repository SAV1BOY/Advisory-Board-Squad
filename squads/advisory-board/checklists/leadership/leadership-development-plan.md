# Leadership Development Plan Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures that leadership development is intentional, resourced, and connected to organizational needs. Development is not training; it is the combination of experience, exposure, and education that builds the judgment, skills, and relationships leaders need. This checklist prevents the failure of assuming leaders will develop naturally without structured support.

## When to Use
- When creating or updating individual development plans for current and aspiring leaders.
- During annual talent reviews and succession planning.
- When a leader transitions to a significantly larger or different role.
- When leadership capability gaps are identified through assessment.

## Gate Criteria

### Plan Quality
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each leader has a documented development plan reviewed within the last 12 months.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The plan targets specific capabilities needed for the leader's next-level role, not generic skills.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The plan includes experience (stretch assignments), exposure (mentors, board interaction), and education (courses, reading).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The plan has measurable milestones, not just activities.

### Organizational Investment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Budget is allocated for leadership development and actually spent.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Senior leaders actively participate as mentors, sponsors, and teachers.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Stretch assignments are provided even when it would be easier to assign the most experienced person.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization creates safe-to-fail environments for developing leaders.

### Progress Tracking
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Development progress is reviewed at least semi-annually with the developing leader and their manager.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: 360-degree feedback is used to assess leadership development progress.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Development plans are adjusted based on results and changing needs.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization tracks aggregate leadership development metrics (readiness levels, time to readiness).

### Integration with Business Needs
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Development plans are connected to the succession planning process.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Development priorities reflect the capabilities the organization will need in 2-3 years.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Development includes exposure to the organization's most pressing strategic challenges.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Developing leaders are given increasing decision authority as they demonstrate readiness.

## Red Flags
- Development plans exist but no development experiences are actually provided.
- Development is only classroom training with no experiential component.
- The same leaders attend development programs year after year with no observable growth.
- Development budgets are the first line item cut in a cost reduction.
- Senior leaders view development as HR's job rather than their own responsibility.

## Escalation Triggers
- A critical leadership gap exists that development should have addressed but did not.
- High-potential leaders leave citing lack of development opportunities.
- Development plans for succession candidates are consistently deprioritized.
- 360-degree feedback shows stagnation in key development areas for senior leaders.
- The organization cannot fill a senior role internally despite years of development investment.
