# Culture Health Rubric

## Purpose

A scoring rubric for assessing the lived reality of organizational culture versus its stated aspirations. Culture health is a leading indicator of organizational performance — culture problems surface in engagement, retention, and decision quality 6-12 months before they appear in financial results. This rubric makes culture measurable and trackable.

## How to Use

Conduct a culture health assessment quarterly using a combination of anonymous surveys, skip-level interviews, and artifact analysis. Score each dimension using the criteria below. Track trends over time and feed results into `data/metrics/culture-health-score.md`.

## Scale/Criteria

### Dimension 1: Psychological Safety (Weight: 25%)
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | People regularly share bad news, admit mistakes, and challenge leadership without fear. Dissent is visibly rewarded. Leaders publicly acknowledge their own errors. |
| 4 | Most people feel safe speaking up. Occasional hesitation on sensitive topics. Leaders generally receptive to feedback. |
| 3 | Safety is inconsistent — depends on the team or the topic. Some leaders are approachable, others are not. Bad news travels slowly. |
| 2 | People self-censor on important issues. Criticism is perceived as career-limiting. Mistakes are hidden rather than surfaced. |
| 1 | Fear-based culture. People agree publicly and complain privately. Messengers are shot. Problems are discovered by customers before leadership. |

### Dimension 2: Trust and Transparency (Weight: 20%)
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | Information flows freely across levels and teams. Financial and strategic context is shared by default. Decisions and their rationale are communicated proactively. People trust each other's intentions even during disagreement. |
| 4 | Good information flow with occasional gaps. Strategic context is shared regularly. Most decisions are transparent. |
| 3 | Information is available but not proactively shared. Some teams operate in silos. Decisions sometimes feel opaque. |
| 2 | Information hoarding is common. Leadership communicates selectively. People question each other's motives. Rumor mill is active. |
| 1 | Systematic opacity. Critical information is restricted as a power tool. Trust is absent — people assume bad intent. |

### Dimension 3: Accountability (Weight: 20%)
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | People own their commitments and follow through. Missed commitments are addressed promptly and constructively. Performance standards are high and consistently applied regardless of seniority. |
| 4 | Strong accountability on most teams. Occasional follow-through gaps addressed within one cycle. Standards are mostly consistent. |
| 3 | Accountability varies by team. Some commitments slip without consequence. Standards applied inconsistently — high performers held to different standard than others. |
| 2 | Commitments are frequently missed without accountability. Poor performance is tolerated for long periods. "That is just how [person] is" is a common phrase. |
| 1 | No accountability culture. Commitments are suggestions. Poor performers are carried indefinitely. The hardest workers burn out while others coast. |

### Dimension 4: Alignment and Clarity (Weight: 15%)
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | Every person can articulate the company's top priorities and how their work connects. Strategy is translated into team and individual objectives that are clear and measurable. |
| 4 | Most people understand the top priorities. Connection to individual work is clear for most but not all roles. |
| 3 | Leadership is aligned but translation to teams is inconsistent. Some people are working on things that do not connect to stated priorities. |
| 2 | Conflicting priorities across teams. People are unsure what the real priorities are. Strategy changes frequently without clear communication. |
| 1 | No shared sense of direction. Every team is optimizing locally. Conflicting directives from different leaders. |

### Dimension 5: Growth and Development (Weight: 10%)
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | People are stretched with challenging work and supported with coaching. Feedback is specific, timely, and development-oriented. Career paths are clear and achievable. |
| 4 | Good development culture. Regular feedback cycles. Most people see a path for growth. |
| 3 | Development is available but not systematic. Feedback is periodic but shallow. Career paths are unclear for some roles. |
| 2 | Development is an afterthought. Feedback is rare or generic. People grow by leaving, not by staying. |
| 1 | No development culture. Feedback is absent or purely critical. People feel stuck. Top talent leaves for growth opportunities. |

### Dimension 6: Resilience and Adaptability (Weight: 10%)
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | The organization responds to setbacks with energy rather than blame. Change is embraced as opportunity. Teams self-organize around problems without waiting for direction. |
| 4 | Good resilience overall. Change is managed well with occasional friction. Most teams adapt independently. |
| 3 | Mixed resilience. Some teams bounce back quickly; others spiral. Change is accepted but not embraced. |
| 2 | Setbacks trigger blame cycles. Change is resisted. People wait for instructions rather than taking initiative. |
| 1 | Fragile culture. Setbacks cause paralysis. Any change triggers anxiety. Learned helplessness is pervasive. |

## Scoring Guide

### Calculating the Overall Score
**Overall = (Safety x 0.25) + (Trust x 0.20) + (Accountability x 0.20) + (Alignment x 0.15) + (Growth x 0.10) + (Resilience x 0.10)**

### Interpretation
| Score Range | Rating | Interpretation |
|-------------|--------|----------------|
| 4.5 - 5.0 | Thriving | Culture is a competitive advantage. Protect and evolve it deliberately. |
| 3.5 - 4.4 | Healthy | Culture is functional and positive. Focus on the weakest dimension. |
| 2.5 - 3.4 | Strained | Culture has meaningful gaps affecting performance. Two or more dimensions need intervention. |
| 1.5 - 2.4 | Damaged | Culture is actively harming the business. Urgent leadership intervention required. Retention risk is high. |
| 1.0 - 1.4 | Toxic | Culture is the primary business risk. Executive change may be required. Expect talent exodus. |

### Assessment Methods by Dimension
| Dimension | Best Assessment Method |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Psychological Safety | Skip-level interviews; anonymous survey question: "I can share bad news without fear" |
| Trust and Transparency | Survey; artifact analysis (are financials shared, are decision logs accessible) |
| Accountability | Manager interviews; action item completion rate data |
| Alignment | Random sampling: ask 10 people "what are our top 3 priorities" and measure consistency |
| Growth and Development | Survey; exit interview analysis; promotion and internal mobility data |
| Resilience | Observe response to the most recent setback; time-to-recovery data |
