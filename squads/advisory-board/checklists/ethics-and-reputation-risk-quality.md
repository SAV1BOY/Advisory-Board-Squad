# Ethics and Reputation Risk Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures that ethical considerations and reputational risks are systematically evaluated before decisions are made, not after damage is done. Reputation is a fragile asset that takes years to build and moments to destroy. This checklist prevents the failure mode of optimizing for short-term gain while creating long-term ethical and reputational liabilities.

## When to Use
- Before any decision that could affect public perception, customer trust, or employee morale.
- When evaluating partnerships, marketing campaigns, or public statements.
- When an ethical gray area is identified in business operations.
- During annual ethics and compliance reviews.
- When media or social media attention creates reputational risk.

## Gate Criteria

### Ethical Assessment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The decision has been evaluated through a stakeholder impact lens: customers, employees, community, environment.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The newspaper test has been applied: would you be comfortable seeing this decision reported on the front page.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The decision complies with both the letter and spirit of applicable laws and regulations.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The decision is consistent with the company's stated values and code of conduct.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Vulnerable or disadvantaged stakeholders who may be disproportionately affected are identified.

### Reputation Risk Analysis
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Reputational risk is assessed across customers, employees, investors, regulators, and media.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The probability and severity of reputational damage are estimated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Social media and public sentiment risks are evaluated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The analysis considers how critics or adversaries could frame the decision negatively.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Historical precedents of reputational crises in the industry are reviewed for parallels.

### Mitigation and Preparedness
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Proactive communication plans exist for high-risk decisions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A response plan is prepared for likely negative reactions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Legal review of public-facing communications is completed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Employee communication addresses the ethical rationale for the decision.

### Systemic Prevention
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: An ethics reporting mechanism exists and is known to employees.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Ethics training is conducted at least annually for all employees.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Incentive structures are reviewed for potential to encourage unethical behavior.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Third-party relationships (vendors, partners, contractors) are assessed for ethical risks.

## Red Flags
- The rationale for the decision relies on "everyone in the industry does it."
- Ethics review is treated as a checkbox rather than a genuine deliberation.
- The decision benefits the company at a clear cost to customers or employees.
- Legal advice is used to determine what the company can get away with rather than what is right.
- No one has been willing to raise ethical concerns despite the decision being controversial.
- The company has no formal ethics review process.

## Escalation Triggers
- An employee reports a potential ethical violation through formal channels.
- Media inquiry suggests an upcoming negative story about company practices.
- A regulatory agency opens an investigation into company conduct.
- Customer complaints reveal a pattern of harm caused by company practices.
- A senior leader is credibly accused of personal conduct that reflects on the company.
- Social media attention reaches a volume that indicates viral reputational risk.
