# Dalio Idea Meritocracy Audit Checklist

## Purpose
Evaluates whether the organization operates as a genuine idea meritocracy where the best ideas win regardless of their source. An idea meritocracy requires three elements: radical truth (speaking honestly), radical transparency (sharing information openly), and believability-weighted decision-making (weighting input by demonstrated competence). This checklist detects when an organization claims to be meritocratic but actually operates on hierarchy, politics, or popularity.

## When to Use
- During annual culture and governance reviews.
- When decisions consistently favor ideas from certain individuals or groups.
- When innovation slows and the best ideas seem to come only from the top.
- When evaluating whether the organization's decision-making processes are genuinely inclusive.
- When building or resetting team decision-making norms.

## Gate Criteria

### Idea Generation Inclusivity
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Mechanisms exist for anyone in the organization to propose ideas regardless of role.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Ideas are evaluated on merit, and the evaluation criteria are explicit and consistent.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The origin of an idea does not affect how it is evaluated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Quiet or introverted team members have structured opportunities to contribute ideas.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Ideas from outside the organization (customers, advisors, peers) are actively solicited.

### Evaluation Rigor
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Ideas are tested against evidence, not just debated based on opinions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The burden of proof is on the idea, not on the person's authority.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Competing ideas are evaluated side-by-side using consistent criteria.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Small experiments or pilots are used to test ideas before full commitment.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The evaluation process is transparent; rejected ideas receive an explanation.

### Decision Quality
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decisions reflect the quality of reasoning, not the loudest voice.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Disagreements are resolved through structured dialogue, not authority pulls.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Believability weighting is applied: those with relevant track records carry more weight.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decision rationale is documented so it can be reviewed and learned from.

### Feedback and Iteration
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The outcomes of implemented ideas are tracked and reported.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Failed ideas are analyzed for what was wrong with the thesis or execution.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Contributors receive feedback on their ideas regardless of adoption.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The idea meritocracy itself is subject to improvement based on outcomes.

## Red Flags
- The same three people generate every approved idea.
- Junior employees report that their ideas are dismissed without consideration.
- "That won't work here" is a common response to new ideas without analysis.
- Idea evaluation criteria shift to justify the preferred option.
- No mechanism exists for anonymous idea submission.
- Ideas that challenge the status quo are labeled as disruptive rather than innovative.

## Escalation Triggers
- A competitor implements an idea that was previously proposed and rejected internally.
- Employee engagement surveys reveal that people feel their ideas are not valued.
- Innovation metrics (new products, process improvements, patents) are declining.
- A pattern emerges where ideas from certain demographics or teams are systematically undervalued.
- The organization's best thinkers are leaving for environments where they feel heard.
