# Culture Audit Session Brief

## Session Type
Organizational Culture Assessment

## Purpose
Assess the current organizational culture against stated values and strategic needs. Identify gaps between espoused culture and lived experience, surface cultural strengths to protect, and pinpoint cultural risks that threaten performance or retention.

## Why Culture Audits Matter

- Culture predicts employee retention more accurately than compensation
- Cultural misalignment is the top reason cited for M&A integration failure
- Rapid scaling creates culture drift that compounds without deliberate attention
- Board-level culture oversight is increasingly expected by investors and regulators

## Pre-Session Preparation

### For the Facilitator
- [ ] Review stated company values and cultural principles
- [ ] Collect recent employee survey data (engagement, satisfaction, eNPS)
- [ ] Gather exit interview themes from the past 12 months
- [ ] Review Glassdoor and public employer brand feedback
- [ ] Compile retention and turnover data by team and tenure
- [ ] Prepare anonymous input mechanism for sensitive topics
- [ ] Distribute pre-read to participants 1 week before session

### For Participants
- [ ] Reflect on the gap between stated values and daily behavior
- [ ] Identify one cultural strength that drives results
- [ ] Identify one cultural weakness that creates friction or risk
- [ ] Consider what new employees experience in their first 90 days
- [ ] Think about what is rewarded vs. what is stated to be valued

## Culture Assessment Framework

### Dimension 1: Values Alignment
**Question:** Do people experience the stated values in their daily work?
- List each stated value
- For each: provide evidence of alignment and evidence of misalignment
- Identify values that are strongly lived and values that are aspirational only

### Dimension 2: Decision-Making Norms
**Question:** How are decisions actually made, and does that match how we say they are made?
- Who has real decision authority vs. stated authority?
- How much autonomy do individual contributors have?
- Are decisions made transparently or behind closed doors?
- Is speed or consensus prioritized?

### Dimension 3: Communication Patterns
**Question:** How does information flow, and what is not being said?
- Is bad news shared freely or suppressed?
- Do people communicate directly or through intermediaries?
- Are meetings the primary venue for real discussion or for performance?
- What topics are undiscussable?

### Dimension 4: Accountability and Consequences
**Question:** What happens when people succeed and when they fail?
- Are high performers recognized visibly?
- Are underperformers addressed or tolerated?
- Do consequences apply equally regardless of seniority or tenure?
- Is accountability seen as punitive or developmental?

### Dimension 5: Inclusion and Belonging
**Question:** Do all team members feel they belong and can contribute fully?
- Which voices are amplified and which are marginalized?
- Do people from different backgrounds report similar experiences?
- Are diverse perspectives actively sought in decision-making?
- Is there a gap between inclusion rhetoric and lived experience?

### Dimension 6: Innovation and Risk Tolerance
**Question:** Does the culture encourage experimentation and tolerate failure?
- Are people rewarded for taking smart risks, even when outcomes are poor?
- Is there a blame culture around mistakes?
- Do new ideas come from all levels or only leadership?
- How quickly does the organization adopt change?

## Output: Culture Audit Report

### Structure
1. **Executive Summary** — Key findings in 5-7 bullet points
2. **Cultural Strengths** — What to protect and amplify (with evidence)
3. **Cultural Risks** — What to address urgently (with evidence)
4. **Gap Analysis** — Stated values vs. observed behavior for each value
5. **Recommendations** — Specific, actionable interventions ranked by priority
6. **Measurement Plan** — How to track cultural improvement over time

## Ground Rules

1. This is a diagnostic exercise, not a blame session
2. Specific behaviors and patterns matter more than individual names
3. Confidentiality: individual contributions are not attributed
4. Honesty is the most valuable contribution anyone can make
5. The goal is improvement, not perfection
