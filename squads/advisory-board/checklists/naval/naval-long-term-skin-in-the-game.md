# Naval Long-Term Skin in the Game Checklist

## Purpose
Verifies that key decision-makers have genuine long-term skin in the game: personal exposure to the consequences of their decisions over meaningful time horizons. Naval argues that accountability and skin in the game are essential for good outcomes because they align incentives with results. This checklist ensures that decisions are not made by people who can walk away from consequences while others bear the burden.

## When to Use
- When evaluating executive and board member incentive structures.
- Before approving long-term strategic commitments.
- When assessing whether advisors and consultants have appropriate accountability.
- When designing partnership and vendor agreements.
- During governance reviews of decision-making authority.

## Gate Criteria

### Economic Alignment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Executives have meaningful equity or deferred compensation that vests over multiple years.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board members have personal financial exposure to company performance.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Advisors who influence strategy have some form of outcome-based compensation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decision-makers bear downside risk, not just upside benefit.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Lock-up periods or holding requirements prevent short-term extraction.

### Reputational Alignment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Key decision-makers' professional reputations are tied to the outcome of their decisions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Track records are transparent and accessible to stakeholders.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders cannot easily distance themselves from failed decisions they championed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Public accountability exists for commitments made.

### Temporal Alignment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Incentive structures reward long-term outcomes (3-5 years), not just quarterly performance.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Employment or engagement terms discourage premature departure after major decisions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Clawback provisions exist for compensation tied to results that later prove unsustainable.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decision-makers expect to be present when the consequences of their decisions materialize.

### Stakeholder Protection
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customers, employees, and partners are not bearing disproportionate risk relative to the decision-makers.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Asymmetric risk arrangements (heads I win, tails you lose) are identified and corrected.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Risk-sharing between the organization and its stakeholders is fair and transparent.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Those most affected by a decision have input into the decision-making process.

## Red Flags
- Executives can earn full bonuses even when the company underperforms.
- Board members have no personal financial investment in the company.
- Advisors receive large fees regardless of outcome quality.
- Leaders who championed failed initiatives face no career consequences.
- Employment contracts allow executives to leave with full benefits immediately after a major decision.
- The people making the decision will not be around to see the consequences.

## Escalation Triggers
- An executive takes an action that enriches them personally while harming the company.
- Incentive structures are discovered that create asymmetric risk exposure.
- Key decision-makers exit shortly after a major commitment, leaving others to execute.
- Partners or vendors extract value with no performance accountability.
- Stakeholder trust erodes because decision-makers are perceived as not having skin in the game.
