# 09 — Annual Strategic Planning

## Overview

The company's annual planning process — a comprehensive cycle that reviews the prior year, analyzes the competitive landscape, sets strategic priorities, defines OKRs, and establishes the monitoring cadence for the coming year. This workflow integrates with the strategy refresh (workflow 02) and capital allocation (workflow 03) cycles.

## Trigger

- Annual planning calendar (Q3 kickoff for next fiscal year).
- New fiscal year approaching within 90 days.

## Agents

| Agent | Role |
|---|---|
| board-chair (Board Chair) | Sponsors the process, facilitates board sessions |
| strategic-advisor (Peter Thiel) | Produces analysis, builds the plan, tracks OKRs |
| capital-allocator (Charlie Munger) | Challenges priorities and targets |
| growth-navigator (Naval Ravikant) | Converts the plan into trackable OKRs and dashboards |
| culture-steward (Brene Brown) | Ensures plan alignment with company values |

## Phases

### Phase 1 — Prior Year Review (Weeks 1-2)

1. strategic-advisor compiles the annual review package:
   - Financial performance vs. plan: revenue, margins, cash flow, runway.
   - Strategic initiative scorecard: what was planned, what was delivered, what was abandoned.
   - OKR achievement rates by team and function.
   - Customer metrics: acquisition, retention, NPS, churn.
   - Competitive position: market share changes, competitive moves, new entrants.
2. Each business unit submits a 2-page self-assessment: wins, misses, lessons, resource constraints.
3. board-chair and strategic-advisor identify the 3-5 biggest lessons from the prior year.
4. Package distributed to the board for review at T-5 before the annual review session.

### Phase 2 — Environmental Analysis (Weeks 2-4)

1. strategic-advisor produces an external environment scan:
   - Market trends: growth rates, buyer behavior shifts, technology trends.
   - Competitive landscape: who gained, who lost, who entered, who pivoted.
   - Regulatory environment: new regulations, enforcement trends, policy risks.
   - Macro factors: economic outlook, capital markets, talent market.
2. Conduct a structured SWOT or equivalent framework:
   - Strengths: what advantages do we have that competitors don't?
   - Weaknesses: where are we objectively behind?
   - Opportunities: what can we exploit in the next 12 months?
   - Threats: what could materially hurt us?
3. capital-allocator challenges each element: "Are our stated strengths actually defensible? Are we missing a threat?"
4. Synthesize into a 3-page environmental brief.

### Phase 3 — Priority Setting (Weeks 4-6)

1. board-chair convenes a full-day planning session with the executive team and board:
   - Morning: review prior year performance and environmental analysis (90 min).
   - Mid-morning: identify the 5-7 strategic questions for the coming year (60 min).
   - Afternoon: for each question, debate options and make choices (120 min).
   - Late afternoon: agree on 3-5 annual priorities with owners (60 min).
2. For each priority, define:
   - The outcome we are targeting (specific, measurable).
   - The key initiatives required to achieve it.
   - The resource requirements (budget, headcount, technology).
   - The trade-offs: what are we choosing NOT to do?
3. capital-allocator presents a "what could go wrong" analysis for each priority.
4. Board votes on the final priority set. Record decisions and dissent.

### Phase 4 — OKR Definition (Weeks 6-8)

1. Each priority owner drafts OKRs:
   - Objective: qualitative, inspiring, time-bound.
   - Key Results: 3-5 per objective, quantitative, measurable, challenging but achievable.
2. strategic-advisor reviews OKRs for consistency across priorities (no conflicts, no gaps).
3. growth-navigator validates measurability: can we actually track these? Do we have the data?
4. CEO and board-chair review the full OKR set for coherence and ambition.
5. Board ratifies OKRs in a dedicated 60-minute session.
6. growth-navigator builds the monitoring dashboard.

### Phase 5 — Monitoring Setup (Weeks 8-10)

1. Define the review cadence:
   - Weekly: team-level check-ins against key results (internal, not board-level).
   - Monthly: executive team reviews OKR progress, flags blockers.
   - Quarterly: board reviews OKR progress, strategic assumption validity, and resource allocation.
2. Set assumption checkpoints: specific dates to revisit the planning assumptions.
3. Define escalation triggers: what level of off-track performance triggers an escalation to the board?
4. growth-navigator publishes the full monitoring calendar and dashboard access.
5. board-chair sends the "year ahead" memo to the full board: priorities, OKRs, cadence, expectations.

## Quality Gates

| Gate | Criteria | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| G1 — Prior year review complete | Performance data, initiative scorecard, lessons documented | strategic-advisor (Peter Thiel) |
| G2 — Environmental analysis complete | External scan, SWOT, 3-page brief | strategic-advisor (Peter Thiel) |
| G3 — Priorities set | 3-5 priorities with owners, outcomes, resources, trade-offs | board-chair (Board Chair) |
| G4 — OKRs ratified | OKRs reviewed for consistency, measurability, and ambition | board-chair (Board Chair) |
| G5 — Monitoring live | Dashboard, cadence, escalation triggers all operational | growth-navigator (Naval Ravikant) |

## Outputs

- Annual review package (financial performance, initiative scorecard, OKR achievement).
- Environmental analysis brief (3 pages).
- Annual priorities document with trade-offs.
- Full OKR set with owners.
- Monitoring dashboard.
- Year-ahead memo from board-chair.

## Metrics

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Prior year review coverage | 100 % of business units submit self-assessment |
| Planning session attendance | ≥90 % of board and executive team |
| Priorities count | 3-5 (hard cap) |
| OKR measurability | 100 % of key results have a data source and baseline |
| Quarterly review completion | 4/4 quarters reviewed on schedule |
| OKR achievement rate | ≥70 % of key results met or exceeded |

## Common Failures

| Failure Mode | Symptom | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Planning as ritual | Process runs but produces no real choices | board-chair demands the "what we are NOT doing" document |
| Too many priorities | 10+ priorities dilute focus | Hard cap at 5; force-rank and cut |
| Vanity OKRs | Key results measure activity, not outcomes | growth-navigator challenges every KR: "Can this be gamed?" |
| Stale plan | Plan is written in Q3 and never updated | Quarterly board reviews with assumption checkpoints |
| Missing the "so what" | Environmental analysis is informative but doesn't drive action | Every insight must link to a priority or a watch item |
| Optimistic targets | Every OKR assumes perfect execution | capital-allocator requires a "what breaks at 80 % delivery" scenario |
