# Loss Aversion in Strategy

## Overview
People feel losses approximately twice as intensely as equivalent gains (Kahneman & Tversky). In strategic decision-making, this asymmetry causes boards to reject positive-expected-value opportunities, over-invest in protecting existing positions, and delay necessary changes.

## Strategic Manifestations

### Status Quo Preservation
- Boards prefer maintaining current strategy even when evidence suggests change is needed
- The pain of giving up the current approach (a loss) outweighs the potential gain of a new approach

### Escalation of Commitment
- Continuing to invest in failing initiatives because stopping feels like "losing" the investment
- The sunk cost creates an emotional attachment that distorts rational assessment

### Risk-Averse Growth Decisions
- Rejecting strategic investments with positive expected value because the downside scenario feels more painful than the upside feels attractive
- This systematically underinvests in growth and innovation

### Defensive Strategy Bias
- Allocating disproportionate resources to defending current market position vs. capturing new opportunities
- Defense feels like preventing a loss; offense feels like risking what you have

## Countermeasures

1. **Reframe the status quo as a choice** — Not changing is a decision with its own risks. What will you lose by staying still?
2. **Tabula rasa test** — "If we were starting from scratch today, would we make this same choice?"
3. **Portfolio thinking** — Evaluate bets as a portfolio, not individually. Some will fail; the portfolio must win.
4. **Explicit expected value calculation** — Force numerical assessment: (probability of gain x size of gain) vs. (probability of loss x size of loss)
5. **Separate decision quality from outcome quality** — A good decision can have a bad outcome. Judge the process, not just the result.

## Board Application
- When the board rejects a growth investment, explicitly check whether loss aversion is driving the decision
- Frame strategic options in both gain and loss terms; the frame changes the decision
- Use portfolio thinking for capital allocation: the overall portfolio should generate positive expected value even if individual bets fail
