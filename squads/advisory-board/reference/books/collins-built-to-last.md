# Built to Last — Jim Collins & Jerry Porras

## Summary

Collins and Porras studied 18 visionary companies that have endured for 50+ years, comparing each against a matched competitor. The central finding: enduring companies are driven by core ideology (core values + purpose) AND stimulated by progress (BHAGs, innovation, evolution). They don't choose between stability and change—they do both. The book debunks myths about needing a charismatic founder, a great initial idea, or maximizing profits. Instead, lasting companies build organizational systems that transcend any individual and preserve the core while stimulating progress across generations.

## Key Principles

- **Clock building, not time telling** — Great founders build organizations that can thrive without them, not organizations dependent on their personal genius.
- **Preserve the core AND stimulate progress** — The core ideology never changes; everything else is open to change. Companies that confuse core with practices become rigid. Companies that abandon core become rudderless.
- **BHAGs (Big Hairy Audacious Goals)** — Visionary companies set 10-25 year goals that seem almost impossible but are achievable with commitment. BHAGs provide long-range direction and catalyze team spirit.
- **Try a lot of stuff and keep what works** — Evolutionary progress through experimentation, not central planning. Many of the best products and strategies emerged from accidents and experiments.
- **Cult-like cultures** — Visionary companies have strong cultures with intense selection pressure. They're great places to work for people who fit and terrible for those who don't.
- **Home-grown management** — 94% of visionary company CEOs were promoted from within. External CEOs correlate with decline.

## Frameworks Extracted

1. **Core Ideology Framework** — Core Values (3-5 timeless principles) + Core Purpose (the fundamental reason for existence beyond profit). These never change. Everything else—strategies, structures, products—can and should evolve.
2. **The BHAG Template** — A goal that is: (1) 10-25 years out, (2) Specific and measurable, (3) Requiring significant stretch, (4) Aligned with core ideology, (5) So compelling it needs little explanation. Examples: Boeing's bet on the 747, Sony's pocket radio.
3. **The Preserve/Stimulate Matrix** — For any proposed change: Does it touch core ideology (preserve it) or operational practice (open to change)? This prevents both ossification and drift.
4. **The Succession Test** — Can the next CEO be promoted from within? If not, the organization has failed to develop leadership depth—a critical board oversight failure.

- "A visionary company is like a great work of art. You can't point to any single item that makes the whole thing work; it's the entire work that creates the magic."

## Application to Board Decisions

- **Succession planning** — Prioritize internal succession. The board's most important job is developing the next generation of leaders within the company.
- **Core ideology governance** — The board should be the guardian of core ideology. When strategic proposals conflict with core values or purpose, the board must say no, regardless of financial appeal.
- **BHAG setting** — Boards should participate in setting BHAGs at major inflection points. A company without a BHAG drifts; a company with one has direction.
- **Clock-building mindset** — Evaluate the CEO on organizational capability building, not just current results. A great CEO who leaves behind a weak organization has failed.
- **M&A cultural fit** — Before approving acquisitions, assess core ideology compatibility. Merging companies with conflicting core ideologies creates lasting dysfunction.
