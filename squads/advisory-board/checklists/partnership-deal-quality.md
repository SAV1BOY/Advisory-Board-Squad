# Partnership Deal Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures that partnership agreements are evaluated with rigor, structured to create genuine mutual value, and contain appropriate protections against misalignment. Partnerships fail when the initial enthusiasm overshadows due diligence on incentive alignment, dependency risk, and exit provisions. This checklist prevents partnerships that look good on a press release but destroy value in execution.

## When to Use
- Before signing any strategic partnership, joint venture, or material vendor agreement.
- When renewing or renegotiating existing partnership terms.
- When evaluating partnership performance at annual review.
- When a partner's strategy or ownership changes materially.

## Gate Criteria

### Strategic Rationale
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The partnership addresses a specific strategic need that cannot be met through internal capabilities within the required timeframe.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The partnership creates value for both parties that neither could achieve alone.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The strategic rationale is documented with measurable success criteria.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Alternative options (build, other partners, acquisition) were evaluated and the rationale for partnership is clear.

### Incentive Alignment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Both parties' economic incentives are mapped and tested for conflicts.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Revenue sharing, cost allocation, and investment responsibilities are explicit.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Performance incentives reward shared success rather than individual extraction.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The agreement addresses what happens when one party's strategy diverges from the partnership's goals.

### Dependency and Risk
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The level of operational dependency on the partner is assessed and acceptable.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Concentration risk is evaluated: what percentage of revenue or capability depends on this partner.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The partner's financial stability and strategic commitment are assessed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: IP ownership, data rights, and customer relationship ownership are unambiguously defined.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Competitive restrictions (exclusivity, non-compete) are reasonable and time-bound.

### Governance and Operations
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A governance structure with defined decision rights, escalation paths, and review cadence is documented.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Day-to-day operational interfaces are mapped with named owners on both sides.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: SLAs and performance metrics are defined with consequences for underperformance.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Communication protocols and information sharing boundaries are agreed upon.

### Exit Provisions
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Termination clauses are clear with defined notice periods and transition support.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Exit costs are quantified and acceptable.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Data portability and transition provisions protect the company's ability to operate post-partnership.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The company can survive the partnership ending without catastrophic disruption.

## Red Flags
- The partnership is driven by a personal relationship between executives rather than structural strategic logic.
- One party has significantly more to lose, creating an asymmetric dependency.
- Exclusivity clauses restrict the company's strategic flexibility disproportionately.
- Due diligence on the partner is superficial or skipped due to time pressure.
- The partnership creates customer confusion about who owns the relationship.
- No exit provisions exist or they are prohibitively expensive.

## Escalation Triggers
- The partner is acquired by a competitor or hostile party.
- Partner underperformance materially impacts the company's customer commitments.
- The partner breaches confidentiality, data agreements, or competitive restrictions.
- Dependency on the partner exceeds the threshold defined at deal signing.
- The partnership economics shift to become unfavorable due to changes in either party's business model.
