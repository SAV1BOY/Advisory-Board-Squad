# Capital Allocation Phrases

Phrases for evaluating investments, allocating resources, and maintaining financial discipline. Use during capital allocation cycles, budget reviews, and investment decisions.

## Evaluating Investments

1. **"What is the expected value of this investment? Show me the math, not the story."**
   Use when a proposal relies on narrative rather than quantification. Every investment should have a probability-weighted expected return.

2. **"What is the opportunity cost? If we fund this, what don't we fund?"**
   Use to prevent evaluation in isolation. Every dollar spent here is a dollar not spent elsewhere.

3. **"What is the minimum return this investment needs to generate to justify the capital, the attention, and the risk?"**
   Use to set a hurdle rate that includes the hidden costs: management attention, organizational complexity, and execution risk.

4. **"Is this a bet on the jockey or the horse? And is that the right bet to make?"**
   Use when evaluating investments where the team quality and the market opportunity are mismatched. Know which you're betting on.

5. **"How much would we invest if this were our own money, not the company's?"**
   Use to invoke the ownership test. When people spend other people's money, the bar drops.

## Challenging Projections

6. **"These projections show a hockey stick. Walk me through specifically what happens at the inflection point and why."**
   Use to challenge unrealistic growth curves. The inflection point is where most projections break down.

7. **"What is the base rate for this type of investment? How often does it produce the projected return?"**
   Use to anchor projections in reality. Historical base rates are more reliable than optimistic forecasts.

8. **"Which assumptions in this model, if wrong by 20 %, would change the conclusion?"**
   Use to identify sensitivity — the inputs that matter most. If the model breaks with a 20 % miss, it's fragile.

9. **"Strip out the synergies. What is this worth on a standalone basis?"**
   Use during M&A and partnership evaluations. Synergies are often the most optimistic part of any model.

10. **"When was the last time one of our projections was accurate? What was the typical variance?"**
    Use to calibrate confidence in forecasting. Organizations that don't track projection accuracy are guessing with confidence.

## Making Allocation Decisions

11. **"Rank these investments by expected value per dollar invested. What does the ranking tell us?"**
    Use to force-rank competing investments on a common metric. Equal distribution is the enemy of strategic allocation.

12. **"How much of our capital is going to maintenance versus growth? Is that the right ratio for where we are?"**
    Use to check portfolio balance. Growth-stage companies that starve maintenance create technical and organizational debt.

13. **"What percentage of our capital budget is reserved for experiments? Is it enough to learn, or so much that it's wasteful?"**
    Use to calibrate the exploration budget. Too little and you stop learning; too much and you stop executing.

14. **"This investment is projected to break even in 24 months. What happens if it takes 36? Can we afford that?"**
    Use to test the downside timeline. Most investments take longer than projected.

15. **"We've been funding this for [X] months and it's [Y]% behind plan. Do we double down, restructure, or kill it?"**
    Use for reallocation decisions on underperforming investments. The three options — not just "continue" — force a real choice.

## Financial Discipline

16. **"Every dollar we spend should have a measurable return — financial, strategic, or risk-reduction. What is this dollar's return?"**
    Use to enforce spending discipline. Unmeasured spending grows unchecked.

17. **"Our runway is [X] months. Every capital decision should be evaluated against that constraint."**
    Use to keep runway awareness in every allocation discussion. Capital discipline intensifies as runway shortens.

18. **"Before we approve new spending, show me what we're cutting to fund it."**
    Use to enforce budget discipline. New spending without corresponding cuts inflates the budget.

19. **"What are the kill criteria for this investment? Define them now, before we're emotionally invested."**
    Use to pre-commit to exit conditions. Kill criteria set during allocation prevent sunk-cost bias during review.

20. **"We've committed 85 % of next year's budget. The remaining 15 % is our strategic flexibility. Protect it."**
    Use to preserve optionality. Fully committed budgets cannot respond to opportunities or threats.

## Post-Allocation Review

21. **"This investment was approved 12 months ago with projected return of [X]. Actual return is [Y]. What happened?"**
    Use during annual retrospectives. Tracking projection accuracy is how allocation skill improves.

22. **"Across all investments approved last year, what was the average return vs. projection? Are we systematically biased?"**
    Use to identify organizational biases. Most companies are systematically over-optimistic on timelines and returns.

23. **"Of the investments we killed this year, which was the hardest to kill? What made it hard, and was that a good reason?"**
    Use to improve kill-decision quality. Hard-to-kill investments often reveal emotional attachment masquerading as strategic rationale.
