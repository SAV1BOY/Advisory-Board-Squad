# Language Guide: Feedback Language

## Purpose

This guide provides language patterns for giving and receiving feedback in the Advisory Board context — from the board to the CEO, between advisors, and from the board about its own effectiveness. The standard is: feedback should be specific, timely, actionable, and delivered with care.

## The Feedback Framework: SBI-A

Every piece of feedback follows four steps:

1. **Situation:** When and where did the behavior occur?
2. **Behavior:** What specifically did the person do (observable, factual)?
3. **Impact:** What was the effect on you, the team, the outcome?
4. **Ask:** What do you want to happen next?

### Example

"During yesterday's board meeting [situation], when you presented the financial model without addressing the downside scenario [behavior], several board members lost confidence in the analysis and the discussion stalled for 15 minutes [impact]. For the next presentation, I'd like you to lead with the risks, not just the upside [ask]."

## Giving Positive Feedback

Positive feedback is not "Good job." It is specific recognition that reinforces the exact behavior you want to see repeated.

### Language patterns
- "I want to call out something [name] did well: [specific behavior]. The impact was [specific impact]. This is the standard we should hold ourselves to."
- "The pre-read for today's meeting was excellent, specifically because [specific quality]. It made the decision 10 minutes faster."
- "I noticed that [name] challenged the consensus during the strategy discussion. That took courage, and the alternative they raised ultimately strengthened the decision."
- "Your memo was a model of the standard we set in our writing guidelines. I'm going to use it as an example for future pre-reads."

### Rules
- Be specific. Name the behavior, not just the outcome.
- Be timely. Deliver within 24 hours of the behavior.
- Be public when appropriate. Positive feedback in front of peers reinforces norms.
- Don't dilute. Positive feedback followed by "but..." is not positive feedback — it's a criticism sandwich. Keep them separate.

## Giving Constructive Feedback

### Language for opening
- "I have some feedback that I think will help. Is now a good time, or should we schedule 15 minutes?"
- "I want to share an observation from [meeting/memo/decision]. My intent is to help, and I want to make sure you hear it that way."
- "Something came up that I think is worth addressing directly rather than letting it linger."

### Language for delivering
- "In [situation], I observed [specific behavior]. The impact was [specific impact]. I'm raising this because I think a small change would make a big difference."
- "The decision memo you submitted [situation] had strong analysis [acknowledge], but the recommendation wasn't clearly stated until page 3 [behavior]. The board spent 10 minutes confused about what you were asking [impact]. Next time, lead with the bottom line [ask]."
- "During the interview process [situation], you gave the candidate a much easier time than the other finalists [behavior]. This makes it harder for us to compare candidates fairly [impact]. I'd like us to agree on a standard level of rigor [ask]."

### Language for difficult feedback
- "This is hard to say, and I'm saying it because I care about your success here."
- "I've been sitting on this for a few days, and I've realized that not sharing it would be a disservice to you."
- "I want to separate the feedback from my feelings about it. The facts are [facts]. My interpretation is [interpretation]. I could be wrong about the interpretation."
- "This feedback is about [specific behavior], not about your character or your competence overall. You are excellent at [specific strength]. This one area needs attention."

## Receiving Feedback

### Language for receiving gracefully
- "Thank you for telling me that. I want to make sure I understand: you're saying [restate]."
- "That's useful. I hadn't seen it from that perspective."
- "I need a moment to process this. Can I come back to you tomorrow with my thoughts?"
- "Can you give me a specific example? I want to make sure I'm addressing the right thing."

### Language when you disagree
- "I hear you, and I see it differently. Can I share my perspective?"
- "I think we may have different information about what happened. Here's what I observed."
- "I appreciate the feedback. I want to understand: is this about one instance, or do you see a pattern?"
- "I'm not sure I agree with the interpretation, but I take the observation seriously. Let me think about it."

### What to avoid when receiving
- Defensiveness: "That's not fair" or "You don't understand the context."
- Deflection: "Other people do the same thing" or "That's how it's always been."
- Counter-attack: "Well, you also..."
- Dismissal: "It's not a big deal" or "You're being too sensitive."

## Board-to-CEO Feedback

This is the highest-stakes feedback loop. The board's ability to give the CEO honest, useful feedback determines the board's value.

### Ongoing feedback
- "I want to share a pattern I've noticed across the last three meetings: [pattern]. My concern is [concern]. What's your perspective?"
- "The board is consistently impressed by your [strength]. One area where we think there's room to grow is [specific area]."
- "I'm hearing from multiple board members that [specific issue]. I want to bring it to you directly rather than let it become an undiscussed concern."

### Annual feedback
- "Here is a summary of the board's assessment: strengths [list], areas for development [list], and one specific goal for the coming year [goal]."
- "This feedback is based on input from all advisors. The themes were consistent, so I'm confident in the pattern."
- "Let's discuss how the board can better support you on [development area]. What do you need from us?"

## Feedback Cadence

| Type | Frequency | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Positive reinforcement | Real-time, every meeting | Public (in meetings or via email) |
| Constructive feedback | Within 48 hours of the behavior | Private (1:1 conversation) |
| Board-to-CEO feedback | Quarterly (light touch), annually (deep dive) | Private (Chair + CEO) |
| Board self-feedback | Annually via effectiveness review | Facilitated session |

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Problem | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Feedback sandwich | "Good / bad / good" — the recipient learns to ignore the positives | Deliver positive and constructive feedback separately |
| Delayed feedback | Months-old feedback feels like ambush | Deliver within 48 hours or on the next interaction |
| Vague feedback | "You need to communicate better" — not actionable | Specify the situation, behavior, and impact |
| Public criticism | Constructive feedback in front of peers triggers shame | Constructive feedback is always private first |
| Feedback without ask | Person knows what's wrong but not what to do differently | Always include a specific ask or suggestion |
| Weaponized feedback | Using feedback to punish rather than to develop | Check your intent before delivering. Is this for their growth or your frustration? |
