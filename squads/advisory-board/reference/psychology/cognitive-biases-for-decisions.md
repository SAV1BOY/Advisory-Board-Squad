# Cognitive Biases for Board Decisions

## Overview
A practical catalog of the biases most dangerous in board and strategic decision contexts. Each bias includes definition, board-specific manifestation, and countermeasure.

## Key Biases

### Confirmation Bias
- **Definition:** Seeking and interpreting information that confirms existing beliefs
- **Board manifestation:** Evaluating M&A targets by looking for evidence the deal is good, ignoring red flags
- **Countermeasure:** Assign a devil's advocate. Require the investment memo to include the strongest case against.

### Anchoring Bias
- **Definition:** Over-relying on the first piece of information encountered
- **Board manifestation:** The CEO's initial valuation of a target sets the anchor for all subsequent negotiation
- **Countermeasure:** Generate independent valuations before sharing any reference numbers. Explore multiple framings.

### Sunk Cost Fallacy
- **Definition:** Continuing a course of action because of past investment rather than future expected value
- **Board manifestation:** Refusing to kill a failing project because 'we've already invested $5M'
- **Countermeasure:** Ask: 'If we were starting fresh today with no prior investment, would we choose this path?'

### Groupthink
- **Definition:** The drive for consensus overrides realistic appraisal of alternatives
- **Board manifestation:** Quick unanimous votes on major decisions. Silence treated as agreement.
- **Countermeasure:** Anonymous pre-vote polling. Mandatory devil's advocate. Require written independent assessments.

### Overconfidence Bias
- **Definition:** Excessive confidence in one's own knowledge, predictions, or abilities
- **Board manifestation:** Approving aggressive projections without demanding sensitivity analysis
- **Countermeasure:** Reference class forecasting. Require confidence intervals, not point estimates. Pre-mortem exercises.

### Availability Bias
- **Definition:** Overweighting information that is easily recalled (recent, vivid, emotional)
- **Board manifestation:** Overreacting to a recent competitor move while ignoring slow structural trends
- **Countermeasure:** Require data-driven analysis. Ask: 'Is this top of mind because it's important, or because it's recent?'

### Status Quo Bias
- **Definition:** Preference for the current state of affairs, resistance to change
- **Board manifestation:** Rejecting strategic pivots because 'what we have is working' (even when it isn't)
- **Countermeasure:** Periodically ask: 'If we were founding this company today, would we do it this way?'

### Authority Bias
- **Definition:** Tendency to defer to perceived authority figures
- **Board manifestation:** Deferring to the most senior board member or the CEO without independent evaluation
- **Countermeasure:** Solicit junior voices first. Use believability-weighted input (Dalio model).

### Survivorship Bias
- **Definition:** Focusing on successes and ignoring failures, creating a distorted view of probability
- **Board manifestation:** 'Company X did this and succeeded, so we should too' (ignoring 100 companies that tried and failed)
- **Countermeasure:** Ask: 'How many companies tried this and failed? What made the successes different?'

### Narrative Fallacy
- **Definition:** Creating a coherent story to explain past events, making them seem predictable and repeatable
- **Board manifestation:** Post-hoc explanations for why a strategy worked, used to justify the next strategy
- **Countermeasure:** Distinguish between correlation and causation. Ask: 'Could this outcome have happened for different reasons?'

## Board Decision Hygiene Protocol
1. Before major decisions, circulate the bias checklist
2. Identify which biases are most likely to affect THIS specific decision
3. Implement the relevant countermeasures
4. Document the decision-making process, including biases considered
