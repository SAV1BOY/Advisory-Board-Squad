# Dalio Radical Truth Check

## Purpose
Assesses whether the organization practices radical truth: the commitment to seeking and speaking truth even when it is uncomfortable. Radical truth means that no one's status, feelings, or political considerations override the need to surface accurate information. This checklist detects the ways organizations systematically suppress uncomfortable truths and provides a framework for building truth-seeking into the culture.

## When to Use
- During leadership team and board meeting effectiveness reviews.
- When suspecting information is being filtered or withheld.
- After a surprise negative outcome that should have been predictable.
- When evaluating the quality of internal debate and decision-making.
- When onboarding new leaders who will set truth-telling norms.

## Gate Criteria

### Truth-Seeking Behaviors
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders actively seek disconfirming evidence for their beliefs and plans.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Bad news reaches leadership within 24 hours of discovery, not weeks.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Questions are encouraged regardless of the questioner's seniority.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Data that contradicts the preferred narrative is presented alongside supporting data.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Assumptions are labeled as assumptions, not stated as facts.

### Truth-Telling Safety
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: No one has been punished, sidelined, or excluded for raising an uncomfortable truth in the last 12 months.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Employees report feeling safe to disagree with their manager (measured via anonymous survey).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Whistleblower protection mechanisms exist and are known.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders publicly thank people who bring them bad news or challenge their thinking.

### Information Integrity
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Reports and presentations to the board are reviewed for accuracy, not just narrative coherence.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Cherry-picking of data to support a predetermined conclusion is actively guarded against.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: External data and benchmarks are used to validate internal assessments.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Disagreements between departments about data or assessments are surfaced and resolved.

### Accountability for Truth
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders who suppress or distort truth face real consequences.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization distinguishes between honest mistakes (acceptable) and dishonest reporting (unacceptable).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Transparency is a stated and enforced cultural value, not just an aspiration.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Regular audits assess whether information flowing to the board is complete and accurate.

## Red Flags
- Everyone agrees in meetings but dissent surfaces in hallway conversations.
- The CEO is consistently the last to learn about problems.
- Reports to the board are systematically more optimistic than internal assessments.
- Messengers of bad news are reassigned, marginalized, or excluded from future meetings.
- The culture values politeness over honesty in professional interactions.
- No one can recall the last time a senior leader said "I was wrong."

## Escalation Triggers
- A material risk or problem is discovered that was known internally but not reported to the board.
- An employee reports retaliation for raising a legitimate concern.
- An external audit or investigation reveals systematic inaccuracies in internal reporting.
- Multiple departing employees cite inability to speak truth as a reason for leaving.
- A decision is made based on information that key participants knew was incomplete or inaccurate.
