# Stakeholder Types Taxonomy

## Types

1. **Decision Makers** — Stakeholders with formal authority to approve, reject, or direct decisions.
2. **Influencers** — Stakeholders without formal authority but with significant ability to shape decisions through expertise, relationships, or political capital.
3. **Implementers** — Stakeholders who must execute the decision and whose capability determines success.
4. **Beneficiaries** — Stakeholders who gain value from the decision's success.
5. **Impacted Parties** — Stakeholders who are affected by the decision but have no role in making or executing it.
6. **Gatekeepers** — Stakeholders who control access to resources, information, or approvals needed for execution.
7. **Advisors** — Stakeholders whose role is to provide counsel without decision authority or execution responsibility.
8. **Opponents** — Stakeholders who actively resist the decision due to disagreement, competing interests, or threatened position.
9. **Observers** — Stakeholders who are aware of the decision but are not directly affected. They may form opinions that matter later.
10. **Champions** — Stakeholders who actively promote and defend the decision, using their influence to build support.

## Definitions

| Type | Role in Decision | Communication Need | Engagement Level |
|------|-----------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Decision Makers | Approve or reject | Full context, options, recommendation, risks | Active — participate in decision process |
| Influencers | Shape the decision indirectly | Understanding of stakes and implications | Active — consulted before the decision |
| Implementers | Execute post-decision | Clear directives, resources, success criteria, timeline | Active — involved in feasibility assessment |
| Beneficiaries | Receive value from success | Awareness of what is coming and when | Moderate — informed of timeline and expectations |
| Impacted Parties | Bear consequences (positive or negative) | Honest, timely communication about what changes and why | Moderate — informed before changes occur |
| Gatekeepers | Control access to needed resources | Specific asks with justification and timeline | Active when their resources are needed |
| Advisors | Provide expert counsel | Relevant context and clear questions | Moderate — consulted on specific aspects |
| Opponents | Resist the decision | Understanding of rationale; forum to voice concerns | Active — engaged directly, not avoided |
| Observers | Watch from a distance | Periodic high-level updates | Low — informed at milestones |
| Champions | Promote and defend the decision | Deep context, talking points, authority to speak | Active — equipped and empowered to advocate |

## Examples

| Stakeholder | Type | Typical Role | Engagement Approach |
|------------|------|-------------|-------------------|
| CEO | Decision Maker | Final authority on strategic decisions | Full decision memo, direct participation in decision sessions |
| Board member | Decision Maker / Advisor | Governance decisions, strategic counsel | Pre-read materials, structured board meetings |
| VP Engineering | Implementer / Influencer | Executes technical decisions, influences product direction | Feasibility review before decision, clear execution brief after |
| Key customer | Beneficiary / Impacted Party | Receives product value, affected by changes | Early communication of changes, feedback collection |
| Investor | Decision Maker (board-level) / Observer | Approves fundraising terms, monitors performance | Quarterly updates, deep engagement on capital decisions |
| Legal counsel | Gatekeeper / Advisor | Controls legal risk assessment, advises on compliance | Consulted on legal implications before commitments |
| Competitor | Observer | Watches market moves | Minimal — but consider what they observe from public actions |
| All-hands employee base | Impacted Party / Observer | Affected by strategic decisions, forms opinions | Transparent communication at appropriate time |
| Industry analyst | Influencer / Observer | Shapes market perception | Briefed on strategy when external narrative matters |
| Executive recruiter | Gatekeeper | Controls access to executive talent | Engaged with clear scorecard and timeline |
| Departing executive | Opponent / Impacted Party | May resist or undermine during transition | Direct, respectful communication; clear transition plan |
| Early adopter customer | Champion / Beneficiary | Promotes the product, provides references | Equipped with information, recognized for support |

## When to Use Each

**During decision preparation:** Map all stakeholders affected by the decision. Classify each using this taxonomy. This determines who is consulted, informed, or involved in the decision process.

**For communication planning:** Different stakeholder types need different messages at different times. Decision Makers need full context early. Impacted Parties need honest communication before changes occur. Observers need only milestone updates.

**RACI-style mapping:** Use this taxonomy to create stakeholder maps for major initiatives:
- **Responsible:** Implementers
- **Accountable:** Decision Makers
- **Consulted:** Influencers, Advisors, Gatekeepers
- **Informed:** Beneficiaries, Impacted Parties, Observers

**For managing resistance:** Identify Opponents early. Understand whether their opposition is based on information gaps (provide data), interest conflicts (redesign incentives), or values disagreement (engage directly). Ignoring Opponents does not make them disappear — it makes them more powerful in informal channels.

**For building support:** Identify and equip Champions. Give them the context and authority to advocate. A decision with three vocal champions and one quiet opponent is more likely to succeed than a decision with universal lukewarm acceptance.

**For board-level decisions:** Map stakeholder types explicitly in every decision memo. Include how each stakeholder type will be communicated with and when.

**Stakeholder dynamics to watch:**
- A Decision Maker who is also an Opponent creates a governance crisis — address it directly.
- An Implementer who is an Opponent will execute poorly — either convert them or reassign.
- A Champion who lacks accurate information will damage credibility — equip them fully.
- A Gatekeeper who is ignored will become an Opponent — engage proactively.
- An Observer who becomes an Impacted Party without notice will lose trust — communicate early.
