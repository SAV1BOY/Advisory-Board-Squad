# Chouinard Mission Integrity Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the organization's operations, products, and decisions genuinely serve its stated mission rather than just paying lip service. Yvon Chouinard built Patagonia by making mission-driven decisions that sometimes sacrificed short-term profits for long-term integrity. This checklist verifies that mission is an operational reality, not a poster on the wall.

## When to Use
- When making decisions that trade off between profitability and mission alignment.
- During annual strategy reviews to assess mission-strategy coherence.
- When new products, markets, or partnerships are evaluated.
- When external stakeholders question the company's commitment to its mission.
- During leadership transitions to preserve mission continuity.

## Gate Criteria

### Mission-Decision Alignment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Every major decision includes an explicit assessment of mission alignment.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization has turned down profitable opportunities that conflicted with the mission in the last 12 months.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Resource allocation reflects mission priorities, not just financial optimization.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Products and services demonstrably advance the mission, not just generate revenue.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The mission guides "how" the company operates, not just "what" it produces.

### Mission Communication
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The mission is stated in concrete terms that employees can apply to daily decisions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: New hires understand the mission and its operational implications within their first 30 days.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders regularly connect decisions and priorities to the mission in communications.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: External stakeholders (customers, partners, investors) can articulate the company's mission accurately.

### Mission Accountability
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Mission-related metrics are tracked with the same rigor as financial metrics.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders are accountable for mission outcomes, not just financial outcomes.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board or advisory body reviews mission performance at least annually.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Trade-off decisions between mission and profit are documented with rationale.

### Mission Evolution
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The mission is reviewed periodically for continued relevance (but not changed frequently).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The mission guides the organization into new areas rather than constraining it.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: As the company grows, mission integrity is actively protected from dilution.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization can articulate how its impact on the mission has grown over time.

## Red Flags
- The mission is referenced in marketing but not in operational decision-making.
- Profitable initiatives that conflict with the mission proceed without discussion.
- Employees view the mission as irrelevant to their daily work.
- Mission metrics are not measured or are measured but not reviewed.
- The organization cannot identify a recent decision where mission considerations overrode financial ones.
- New executives were hired without assessment of mission commitment.

## Escalation Triggers
- A material decision is made that directly contradicts the mission without acknowledgment.
- External stakeholders credibly accuse the organization of mission-washing.
- Employee surveys reveal that the mission is perceived as performative.
- Revenue from mission-inconsistent activities exceeds a defined threshold.
- A leadership dispute about mission interpretation remains unresolved.
