# Market Entry Memos — Swipe File

## Overview
Entering a new market—whether geographic, segment, or product category—is one of the highest-stakes strategic decisions a board will evaluate. These swipe examples illustrate effective market entry reasoning and common pitfalls.

## Market Entry Memo Template

### Section 1: Market Opportunity
- **Market size:** TAM → SAM → SOM with methodology explained
- **Growth rate:** Historical and projected, with drivers identified
- **Competitive landscape:** Who's there, what's their positioning, where are the gaps
- **Timing:** Why now? What has changed to make this market accessible?

### Section 2: Right to Win
- **What unique assets do we bring?** Technology, brand, data, relationships, expertise
- **Where do we have an advantage over existing players?** Be specific and honest.
- **Where are we disadvantaged?** Also be specific and honest.
- **What must we build or acquire?** Capabilities, partnerships, local knowledge

### Section 3: Entry Strategy
- **Beachhead:** Smallest viable segment to prove the model before expanding
- **Go-to-market:** How will we acquire customers? Which channels?
- **Pricing:** Market-based, value-based, or penetration pricing?
- **Partnership:** Any local partners needed for distribution, compliance, or credibility?

### Section 4: Financial Model
- **Investment required:** 12-month and 24-month projections
- **Revenue timeline:** When do we expect first revenue? Break-even?
- **Unit economics:** CAC, LTV, contribution margin expectations for the new market
- **Sensitivity analysis:** What happens if customer acquisition takes 2x longer or costs 2x more?

### Section 5: Risks and Mitigations
- **Top 3 risks** with specific mitigation plans
- **Kill criteria:** What would cause us to exit the market? Define upfront.
- **Reversibility:** How quickly can we unwind if the entry fails?

## Example: SaaS Company Entering Brazil Market

**Market Opportunity:** Brazil has 6M+ SMBs, 70% lack modern software. SaaS adoption is 5-10 years behind the US, creating a timing opportunity. Market growing 25% YoY.

**Right to Win:** Our product already serves Portuguese-speaking customers (Portugal). We have a technology advantage over local competitors. However, we lack: local payment integration (PIX, boleto), local support team, understanding of tax/compliance requirements (nota fiscal).

**Entry Strategy:** Start with São Paulo. Partner with a local accounting software for distribution. Hire a country manager and 3-person team (sales, support, engineering). Price at 60% of US pricing to match local willingness to pay.

**Kill Criteria:** If after 12 months we have fewer than 50 paying customers with < 80% retention, exit the market and redeploy resources.

## Board Application
- Every market entry proposal should follow a structured memo format
- Challenge the "right to win" section most aggressively—this is where wishful thinking hides
- Insist on kill criteria defined before entry, not after
- Monitor the beachhead closely: if it's not working at small scale, scaling won't fix it
