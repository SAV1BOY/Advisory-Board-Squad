# Founder-CEO Transition

## When the Transition Question Arises

### Natural Transition Points
- Post-Series B: company needs professional management systems the founder hasn't built
- Revenue > $20M ARR: operational complexity exceeds founder's management capacity
- Team > 100 people: organizational design and people management become the primary CEO job
- New market entry: geographic or segment expansion requires different leadership skills

### Warning Signs the Founder May Be the Bottleneck
- Every decision flows through the founder; nothing moves when they're absent
- The executive team is weak because the founder can't delegate or hire above themselves
- Product velocity has slowed as the founder is pulled into management
- The board is managing around the founder's limitations rather than addressing them
- Employee engagement is declining, especially among senior hires

### Warning Signs a Transition Is NOT Needed
- Investors are pushing for a "professional CEO" as a default, not because of specific deficiencies
- The founder is still the best product visionary and the company's primary competitive advantage
- The company is in wartime and the founder's urgency is exactly what's needed
- The founder is actively developing management skills and showing improvement

## Transition Models

### Model 1: Founder → CEO Coach
- Founder stays as CEO but gets intensive executive coaching and support
- Board provides mentorship and accountability
- Senior hires brought in to complement founder's weaknesses
- **Best when:** Founder is willing, coachable, and the gaps are addressable

### Model 2: Founder CEO → Founder Chairman + Professional CEO
- Founder maintains strategic influence and board presence
- Professional CEO handles day-to-day operations and management
- Clear delineation of responsibilities is critical
- **Best when:** Founder's value is in vision and product, not management

### Model 3: Founder CEO → Founder CTO/CPO + Professional CEO
- Founder moves to a functional role aligned with their strengths
- Professional CEO leads the organization
- **Best when:** Founder is fundamentally a builder, not a leader of people

### Model 4: Founder Departure
- Founder exits the company entirely (gradually or quickly)
- Most appropriate when the founder-company fit has deteriorated beyond repair
- **Best when:** The founder is actively harming the company or has lost the will to lead

## Managing the Transition

### Pre-Transition
1. Have honest conversations early—don't wait for a crisis
2. Define what the founder values most (vision influence, technical contribution, financial outcome)
3. Identify the specific capabilities the next CEO needs
4. Build a search committee (board + external search firm)
5. Establish transition timeline (3-6 months minimum)

### During Transition
1. Overlap period: outgoing founder and incoming CEO work together for 30-90 days
2. Clear communication to employees, customers, and investors
3. Defined authority transfer: specific date when decision-making authority transfers
4. Founder availability for institutional knowledge transfer

### Post-Transition
1. Clear role for the founder (if staying) with defined boundaries
2. Regular check-ins between founder and new CEO to manage the relationship
3. Board monitors both the new CEO's performance and the founder's adjustment
4. 6-month review of the transition's effectiveness

## Board Application
- The board is responsible for raising the transition conversation—it won't come from the founder
- Approach with empathy: this is deeply personal for the founder. Apply Brene Brown's "clear is kind" principle.
- Define success criteria for the transition before starting the process
- Protect the founder's legacy and dignity while doing what's best for the company
- The worst outcome is a transition handled badly—it can destroy culture and demoralize the team
