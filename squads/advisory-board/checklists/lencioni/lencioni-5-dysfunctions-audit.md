# Lencioni 5 Dysfunctions Audit Checklist

## Purpose
Systematically evaluates the leadership team against Lencioni's Five Dysfunctions model: absence of trust, fear of conflict, lack of commitment, avoidance of accountability, and inattention to results. These dysfunctions are layered: each builds on the one beneath it. Fixing a higher-level dysfunction without addressing the underlying one is futile. This checklist provides a diagnostic that identifies where the team is stuck.

## When to Use
- Quarterly or semi-annually during leadership team health assessments.
- When team performance is below expectations despite individual talent.
- When introducing a new team member to the leadership team.
- During leadership team offsites focused on team effectiveness.
- When persistent team dynamics issues are not responding to surface-level interventions.

## Gate Criteria

### Dysfunction 1: Absence of Trust
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Team members openly admit mistakes and weaknesses to each other.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Team members ask each other for help without hesitation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: People give each other the benefit of the doubt before making negative assumptions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Team members invest time in getting to know each other personally.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Vulnerability is modeled by the team leader first.

### Dysfunction 2: Fear of Conflict
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Team meetings involve robust debate about important issues.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: People express disagreement openly during meetings, not just after them.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team tackles difficult issues head-on rather than avoiding them.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Debate is focused on ideas, not personal attacks.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Silence is not mistaken for agreement.

### Dysfunction 3: Lack of Commitment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decisions are clear and explicitly stated at the end of discussions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: All team members commit to decisions, even those they initially disagreed with.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team moves forward with confidence, not hedging based on unresolved disagreements.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decisions are not revisited after they are made unless genuinely new information emerges.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Team members communicate decisions clearly to their own teams.

### Dysfunction 4: Avoidance of Accountability
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Team members call each other out on behaviors and performance that hurt the team.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Accountability is peer-to-peer, not just top-down from the team leader.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Standards and expectations are clear and agreed upon.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Underperformance is addressed promptly, not allowed to persist.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team has mechanisms for tracking and reviewing commitments.

### Dysfunction 5: Inattention to Results
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Team members prioritize collective results over individual department metrics.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Success is defined by team outcomes, not individual recognition.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Status and ego do not drive decisions about resources or priorities.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team regularly reviews collective results and adjusts behavior accordingly.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Individual incentives are aligned with team-level outcomes.

## Red Flags
- Team meetings are polite but unproductive, with no real debate.
- Decisions are made in meetings but not followed through.
- Team members complain about each other to the leader rather than to each other.
- The team defaults to individual goals when collective goals conflict.
- The team leader is the only one who ever holds anyone accountable.
- Trust-building is seen as a one-time event rather than an ongoing practice.

## Escalation Triggers
- The team scores poorly on three or more dysfunction layers in a self-assessment.
- Dysfunction is visibly impacting business results (missed deadlines, lost customers, talent exodus).
- The team leader cannot or will not model vulnerability, blocking the entire pyramid.
- External facilitator assessment confirms team dysfunction that self-assessment minimizes.
- The leadership team's dysfunction causes cascading dysfunction in their direct teams.
