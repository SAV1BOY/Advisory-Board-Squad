# 04 — Fundraising Cycle

## Overview

End-to-end process for raising external capital — from crafting the narrative through building the investor pipeline, negotiating terms, and closing the round. The board's role is to advise, connect, stress-test, and hold the CEO accountable for process discipline.

## Trigger

- Runway drops below 18-month threshold.
- Strategic opportunity requires capital beyond current budget.
- Board approves a growth acceleration plan that needs external funding.
- Market conditions create a favorable window.

## Agents

| Agent | Role |
|---|---|
| Board-Chair | Coaches the CEO, reviews materials, facilitates introductions |
| Strategy-Analyst | Builds the data room, financial model, and market analysis |
| Devil's-Advocate | Stress-tests the narrative, challenges valuation assumptions |
| Governance-Ops | Manages the pipeline tracker, NDA flow, data room access |
| Risk-Analyst | Assesses dilution scenarios, term sheet risks, downside protection |

## Phases

### Phase 1 — Narrative & Materials (Weeks 1-3)

1. CEO and Board-Chair align on the fundraising thesis: why now, how much, what for, what milestones it unlocks.
2. Strategy-Analyst builds the financial model: historical performance, projections (base/upside/downside), unit economics, use of proceeds.
3. CEO drafts the pitch deck (12-15 slides max):
   - Problem → Solution → Market → Traction → Business model → Team → Financials → Ask.
4. Devil's-Advocate runs the deck through the "skeptical investor" lens: what are the three hardest questions an investor will ask? Prepare clear answers.
5. Board-Chair reviews the narrative for coherence, ambition-credibility balance, and competitive positioning.
6. Strategy-Analyst prepares the data room: financials, cap table, legal docs, key contracts, IP summary, customer references.
7. Governance-Ops sets up the pipeline tracker: investor name, stage, last contact, next step, champion, notes.

### Phase 2 — Pipeline Building (Weeks 3-6)

1. Board-Chair and advisors generate a target investor list (30-50 names), categorized:
   - Tier 1: highest strategic fit and likelihood (8-12 names).
   - Tier 2: strong fit, needs more cultivation (10-15 names).
   - Tier 3: broad outreach (remaining).
2. Map warm introductions: which advisor or board member has a relationship with each target?
3. Prioritize Tier 1 outreach. Warm intros go first. Cold outreach follows for Tier 2-3.
4. CEO runs 3-5 "practice pitches" with friendly investors or advisors to refine delivery.
5. Board-Chair debriefs each practice pitch: what landed, what confused, what got challenged.
6. Governance-Ops tracks pipeline in real time. Weekly pipeline review with CEO and Board-Chair.

### Phase 3 — Term Negotiation (Weeks 6-10)

1. As term sheets arrive, Risk-Analyst evaluates each against a standard checklist:
   - Valuation and dilution.
   - Liquidation preferences and participation rights.
   - Board composition and governance rights.
   - Anti-dilution provisions.
   - Protective provisions and veto rights.
   - Information rights and reporting obligations.
   - Founder vesting and lock-up provisions.
2. Devil's-Advocate models worst-case scenarios for each term sheet: what happens in a down round? In an acqui-hire? In a flat exit?
3. Board-Chair advises on negotiation strategy: which terms to push back on, where to concede, how to create competitive tension.
4. CEO negotiates with guidance. Board-Chair is available for back-channel conversations with lead investors.
5. Legal counsel reviews final term sheet. Board provides final recommendation.

### Phase 4 — Close & Communication (Weeks 10-14)

1. Legal counsel drives definitive documents. Strategy-Analyst verifies financial representations.
2. Board approves the final deal terms in a dedicated session.
3. Signing and funding. Governance-Ops confirms wire receipt.
4. CEO drafts internal announcement. Board-Chair reviews for tone and completeness.
5. External announcement (if applicable) follows the stakeholder communication workflow (17).
6. Update the cap table. File regulatory documents. Archive the data room.

### Phase 5 — Post-Close (Weeks 14-16)

1. CEO sends thank-you notes to every investor who engaged, including those who passed.
2. Board-Chair conducts a fundraising retrospective:
   - What worked in the narrative?
   - Which investor relationships are worth maintaining for future rounds?
   - What feedback did we receive most frequently?
   - How accurate was our timeline estimate?
3. Strategy-Analyst archives the fundraising package and retrospective.
4. Update the board composition if new investors have board or observer seats.
5. Governance-Ops updates the charter and meeting logistics as needed.

## Quality Gates

| Gate | Criteria | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| G1 — Materials ready | Deck, model, data room complete and reviewed | Board-Chair |
| G2 — Pipeline populated | ≥30 investors identified, tiers assigned, warm intros mapped | Governance-Ops |
| G3 — Term sheets evaluated | Each term sheet scored against standard checklist | Risk-Analyst |
| G4 — Board approval | Board votes to approve final deal terms | Board-Chair |
| G5 — Close confirmed | Funds received, cap table updated, docs filed | Governance-Ops |

## Outputs

- Pitch deck (12-15 slides).
- Financial model (base/upside/downside).
- Data room (organized, access-controlled).
- Investor pipeline tracker.
- Term sheet evaluation scorecard.
- Signed deal documents.
- Fundraising retrospective memo.

## Timeline

| Milestone | Week |
|---|---|
| Materials complete | Week 3 |
| Pipeline built, outreach begins | Week 4 |
| First term sheets expected | Week 7 |
| Term negotiation | Week 8-10 |
| Close | Week 12-14 |
| Post-close retrospective | Week 16 |

Total cycle: **14-16 weeks** from kickoff to close.

## Metrics

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Investor meetings booked | ≥20 from Tier 1+2 |
| Conversion rate (meeting → term sheet) | ≥15 % |
| Time from first meeting to term sheet | ≤6 weeks for lead investor |
| Term sheet to close | ≤4 weeks |
| Valuation within target range | ±15 % of board-approved range |
| Post-close investor NPS | ≥8/10 from lead investor |

## Common Failures

| Failure Mode | Symptom | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Narrative mismatch | Investors consistently ask the same confused question | Board-Chair debriefs every meeting; fix the narrative within 48 hours |
| Pipeline too narrow | Relying on 3-5 investors creates zero leverage | Build to 30-50; maintain competitive tension |
| Term sheet panic | Accepting the first term sheet without comparison | Governance-Ops tracks pipeline timing to ensure overlapping processes |
| Data room chaos | Investors request documents that aren't there | Strategy-Analyst audits data room against a standard checklist before outreach begins |
| Founder distraction | CEO stops running the company to fundraise | Time-box fundraising to 30 % of CEO's week; delegate everything possible |
| Post-close amnesia | Lessons from this round are not captured | Board-Chair owns the retrospective; it is a gate, not optional |
