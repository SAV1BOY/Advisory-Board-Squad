# Strategy Memo Examples

## Overview

The best strategy memos are concise, rigorous, and force clarity of thought. This file provides examples and templates for strategy memos that boards should expect from management, modeled on the best practices from Amazon, Bridgewater, McKinsey, and iconic founder communications.

## The One-Page Strategy Memo

### Structure
1. **The Challenge** (2-3 sentences) — What is the critical problem or opportunity we are addressing?
2. **Our Diagnosis** (3-5 sentences) — What is the root cause? What forces are at play? What data supports this diagnosis?
3. **Our Guiding Policy** (1-2 sentences) — What is our overall approach to addressing the challenge?
4. **Coherent Actions** (3-5 bullet points) — What specific, sequenced actions will we take?
5. **Key Assumptions** (3-5 bullet points) — What must be true for this strategy to work?
6. **Risks and Mitigations** (3-5 bullet points) — What could go wrong and how will we respond?
7. **Resource Requirements** — What investment of money, people, and time does this require?
8. **Success Metrics** — How will we know this is working? What are the leading indicators?

### Example: Market Expansion Strategy Memo

**The Challenge:** Our core market (US SMB SaaS) is approaching saturation at our price point. Growth has decelerated from 80% to 35% YoY, and CAC has increased 40% in 18 months.

**Our Diagnosis:** Three converging forces: (1) We've penetrated 40% of the addressable US SMB market, (2) Two well-funded competitors have entered with lower price points, compressing margins and increasing acquisition costs, (3) Our product has outgrown the SMB segment—enterprise features requested by 60% of churned customers.

**Our Guiding Policy:** Move upmarket to mid-market and enterprise while defending the SMB base with a self-serve product tier.

**Coherent Actions:**
- Launch a self-serve tier at $X/month by Q2 to defend SMB with lower CAC
- Build enterprise features (SSO, audit logs, custom integrations) by Q3
- Hire 3 enterprise AEs and 1 enterprise CSM by Q2
- Restructure pricing into three tiers: Self-serve, Pro, Enterprise
- Partner with 2 system integrators for enterprise distribution

**Key Assumptions:**
- Enterprise sales cycle will be 3-6 months (validated by 5 pilot conversations)
- Self-serve tier will maintain 60% of current SMB volume at 30% lower CAC
- Enterprise ACV will be 5-8x current average

**Risks and Mitigations:**
- Risk: Enterprise sales cannibalize mid-market → Mitigation: Feature-gate enterprise tier clearly
- Risk: Self-serve quality drops → Mitigation: Dedicated product owner for self-serve
- Risk: Enterprise sales take longer than expected → Mitigation: 12-month cash runway maintained regardless

**Resource Requirements:** $1.2M additional spend over 12 months (4 new hires, product development, SI partnerships)

**Success Metrics:** Enterprise pipeline of $2M ARR by Q3, Self-serve conversion rate > 8%, Blended CAC payback < 18 months

## The Competitive Response Memo

### Structure
1. **Competitive Action** — What did the competitor do?
2. **Impact Assessment** — How does this affect us specifically?
3. **Response Options** — 3 options with tradeoffs
4. **Recommendation** — Preferred response with reasoning
5. **Speed** — When must we decide?

## The Annual Strategy Refresh Memo

### Structure
1. **State of the Business** — Honest assessment of current position
2. **What Changed** — External and internal changes since last strategy review
3. **What We Got Right** — Validated assumptions and successful bets
4. **What We Got Wrong** — Failed assumptions and course corrections needed
5. **Updated Strategy** — Revised five-choice cascade (Lafley framework)
6. **This Year's Thematic Goal** — Single most important objective (Lencioni framework)

## Board Application

- Require strategy memos in written form at least 48 hours before board meetings
- Use the one-page format as the default; request deeper analysis as needed
- Evaluate memos for intellectual honesty (are risks and assumptions stated clearly?) rather than polish
- The quality of the strategy memo reflects the quality of strategic thinking
