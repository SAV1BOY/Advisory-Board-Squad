# Meeting Agenda Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures that board and advisory meetings are structured for productive deliberation, not performative reporting. A well-crafted agenda is the single greatest lever for meeting effectiveness. This checklist prevents meetings that waste senior leaders' time on updates that could be emails and leave insufficient time for the decisions that require collective wisdom.

## When to Use
- Before finalizing the agenda for any board meeting, advisory session, or leadership offsite.
- When resetting meeting culture after a period of unproductive sessions.
- During annual board effectiveness reviews.

## Gate Criteria

### Purpose and Outcomes
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each agenda item states the desired outcome: decision, discussion, or information.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decision items are prioritized in the first half of the meeting when energy is highest.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Information-only items are limited to 20% of total meeting time or sent as pre-reads.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The overall meeting outcome is stated: what should be true after this meeting that is not true now.

### Time Allocation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each item has a specific time allocation that totals to the meeting duration.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Buffer time is included between items (minimum 5 minutes per transition).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The most important decision item has at least 30 minutes allocated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Time for open discussion or emerging issues is included (minimum 15 minutes).

### Pre-Read Materials
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: All materials are distributed at least 72 hours before the meeting.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Pre-reads include explicit questions for board members to consider.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The total pre-read package is manageable (under 50 pages or equivalent).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each pre-read document has an executive summary on the first page.

### Participant Preparation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Presenters are identified and their role (recommend, inform, facilitate) is clear.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Expected contributions from specific attendees are noted in advance.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Conflicts of interest for any agenda item are identified and recusal plans documented.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Guest attendees (non-regular participants) are limited to their specific agenda items.

### Continuity and Follow-Up
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Carryover items from the previous meeting are included with status updates.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The agenda references the decision log for items that are follow-ups to previous decisions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A consent agenda is used for routine approvals to preserve time for strategic items.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The final agenda item includes next steps, action items, and date for the next meeting.

## Red Flags
- The agenda is a list of presentation topics with no designated outcomes.
- More than 50% of the meeting is allocated to information-sharing rather than discussion or decisions.
- Pre-reads are sent the night before or the morning of the meeting.
- The same items have appeared on the agenda for three consecutive meetings without resolution.
- No time is allocated for open discussion or concerns not on the agenda.
- The agenda was created by a single person without input from board members.

## Escalation Triggers
- Board members consistently arrive unprepared, indicating pre-read materials are inadequate.
- Meetings consistently run over time, indicating agenda planning is unrealistic.
- Critical decisions are deferred repeatedly due to poor agenda management.
- Board members express frustration about meeting effectiveness in feedback surveys.
- Material issues are discovered that should have been on the agenda but were omitted.
