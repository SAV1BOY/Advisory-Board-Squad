# Mission Integrity Phrases

Phrases for keeping the organization true to its mission — connecting daily decisions to purpose, calling out mission drift, and ensuring that commercial success and mission alignment reinforce each other.

## Connecting Decisions to Mission

1. **"How does this decision serve our mission? If the connection is unclear, the decision might be wrong."**
   Use to test mission alignment before committing to any significant choice.

2. **"We exist to [mission statement]. Does this initiative move us closer to that, or is it a distraction?"**
   Use to filter opportunities against purpose. Not everything that makes money advances the mission.

3. **"Who are we doing this for? If the answer is 'us' rather than the people we serve, let's reconsider."**
   Use to recenter on the customer or beneficiary. Organizations drift toward self-service when not deliberately anchored.

4. **"Would the version of ourselves who wrote this mission statement be proud of this decision?"**
   Use as a time-travel test. The founding intent often has more clarity than current complexity allows.

5. **"This is commercially attractive. Is it also mission-aligned? If both, proceed. If only one, think harder."**
   Use to require both commercial viability and mission fit. Neither alone is sufficient.

## Calling Out Drift

6. **"I think we're drifting from our mission, and I want to name it before it becomes permanent."**
   Use when you notice incremental decisions pulling the company away from its stated purpose. Drift is gradual and invisible until someone calls it out.

7. **"We used to be about [original purpose]. Lately, we seem to be about [current behavior]. When did that shift happen, and was it intentional?"**
   Use to surface unconscious mission drift. If the shift was deliberate, it should be documented. If it wasn't, it should be corrected.

8. **"This quarter we made three decisions that prioritized revenue over mission. Is that a temporary trade-off or a permanent shift?"**
   Use to force explicit acknowledgment of mission compromises. Temporary trade-offs are acceptable if they are temporary.

9. **"Our mission says [X]. Our behavior says [Y]. One of them needs to change."**
   Use when the gap between stated mission and actual behavior has become undeniable.

10. **"If we continue on this path for five years, will we still recognize ourselves?"**
    Use to project current decisions into the future. Small compromises compound.

## Protecting Values Under Pressure

11. **"This is a values moment. We can take the easy path or the right path. They are not the same."**
    Use when short-term pressure tempts a values compromise. Name it explicitly.

12. **"The cost of maintaining our values here is [X]. The cost of abandoning them is [Y]. I believe Y is higher, even if it's harder to quantify."**
    Use to frame values as a strategic asset, not just a moral position. Values have economic value through trust, reputation, and talent attraction.

13. **"Our competitors may cut this corner. We won't. That's a choice, not a disadvantage."**
    Use to reframe values-driven constraints as competitive differentiation.

14. **"If this became public, would we be proud of it? If not, we shouldn't do it even if it stays private."**
    Use as the publicity test. Decisions that require secrecy to be acceptable are rarely acceptable.

15. **"We are under pressure to compromise on [value]. Let me remind us why that value exists and what it has cost others to abandon it."**
    Use to provide historical context for values that feel inconvenient in the moment.

## Sustaining Mission in Growth

16. **"As we grow, what parts of our mission must be non-negotiable, and where can we be flexible?"**
    Use during growth transitions. Growth pressures every aspect of culture and mission. Define what's sacred.

17. **"Every new hire dilutes or strengthens our mission culture. Which is this hire doing?"**
    Use to connect hiring decisions to mission integrity. At scale, culture is set by who you hire.

18. **"How do we onboard people into our mission, not just our processes?"**
    Use to ensure new team members understand the why, not just the how.

19. **"What metrics would tell us if we're staying true to our mission? Are we tracking them?"**
    Use to operationalize mission integrity. What gets measured gets managed.

20. **"The day we stop asking whether our decisions align with our mission is the day we stop having one."**
    Use as a cultural statement. Mission alignment is a practice, not a permanent state.

## Mission and Commercial Alignment

21. **"The best businesses are the ones where doing the right thing and doing the profitable thing are the same thing. Are we building that kind of business?"**
    Use to challenge the false dichotomy between mission and money.

22. **"If our mission doesn't eventually create commercial value, it's a hobby, not a business. How does this mission drive our economics?"**
    Use to ground mission in sustainability. Unsustainable missions help no one in the long run.

23. **"What would our most mission-aligned customer want us to do here? And does that also work commercially?"**
    Use to find the intersection of mission and market. The overlap is where the strongest strategies live.
