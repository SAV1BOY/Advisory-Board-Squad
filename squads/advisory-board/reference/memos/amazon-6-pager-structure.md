# Amazon 6-Pager Structure

## Overview

Amazon banned PowerPoint in favor of 6-page narrative memos read in silence at the beginning of each meeting. This format forces clear thinking (you can't hide behind bullet points), ensures all attendees have the same information, and creates a written record of reasoning. This file documents the structure, principles, and adaptation guidelines for advisory board use.

## The Original Format

### Reading Ritual
- First 20-30 minutes of every meeting are spent silently reading the memo
- No pre-reads sent in advance (ensures everyone actually reads it)
- Questions and discussion follow the reading
- The writer is present to answer questions

### Document Structure
1. **Introduction/Context** — Why are we discussing this? What problem are we solving? What's the background a reader needs?
2. **Goals and Non-Goals** — What are we trying to achieve? Equally important: what are we explicitly NOT trying to achieve?
3. **Tenets/Principles** — What beliefs guide our approach? What tradeoffs are we making and why?
4. **Current State** — Where are we today? Data, metrics, customer feedback.
5. **Proposed Approach** — What do we recommend and why? This is the heart of the memo—detailed enough that a reader can evaluate the logic without a presentation.
6. **Alternatives Considered** — What other approaches did we evaluate? Why did we reject them? This section prevents "why didn't you think of X?" questions and demonstrates thoroughness.
7. **Risks and Open Questions** — What could go wrong? What don't we know yet? What decisions are we deferring?
8. **Appendix** — Supporting data, detailed financials, research, FAQs. Can exceed the 6-page limit.

## Writing Principles

### Narratives Over Bullets
- Complete sentences force complete thoughts
- "We will increase revenue" is a bullet point. "We will increase revenue by expanding into the mid-market segment, targeting companies with 100-500 employees, using a land-and-expand motion that begins with a free self-serve tier" is a narrative.

### The "So What?" Test
- Every paragraph should survive the question: "So what? Why does this matter for the decision at hand?"

### Data-Backed Claims
- Assertions without data are opinions. Opinions are labeled as such. Data sources are cited.

### Intellectual Honesty
- The memo should address the strongest counterargument, not just the weakest
- "However, this approach has a significant weakness..." is a sign of strong thinking
- Memos that only present upside are advocacy, not analysis

### Customer Obsession
- How does this affect the customer? Every proposal should connect to customer impact.

## Adaptation for Advisory Boards

### The 3-Pager Variant
For advisory boards with less complex decisions, a 3-page format works:
1. **Context and Challenge** (1 page)
2. **Proposed Approach and Alternatives** (1 page)
3. **Risks, Resources, and Metrics** (1 page)

### Board Meeting Integration
- Send the memo 48 hours in advance (adaptation from Amazon's in-meeting reading)
- Dedicate 10 minutes at the start to re-read and annotate
- Discussion should focus on questions, risks, and alternatives—not restating the memo
- Vote at the end of discussion if decision is required

### Quality Standards for Board Memos
- No memo longer than 6 pages (excl. appendix)
- Every claim backed by data or labeled as assumption
- Every proposal includes alternatives considered
- Every recommendation includes risks and mitigations
- Written in clear prose, not consultant-speak

## Board Application

- Adopt the narrative memo format for all board-level proposals
- Use the reading ritual to ensure deep engagement with the material
- Evaluate memo quality as a proxy for thinking quality
- Keep an archive of memos to track decision reasoning over time
