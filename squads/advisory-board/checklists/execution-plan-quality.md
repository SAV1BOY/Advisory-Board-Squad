# Execution Plan Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures that strategic intentions are translated into concrete, trackable, and resourced execution plans. The gap between strategy and results is almost always an execution gap. This checklist prevents the common failure mode of well-crafted strategies that die in implementation due to vague accountability, unrealistic timelines, or missing dependencies.

## When to Use
- When translating strategic decisions into operational plans.
- During quarterly planning cycles.
- When an initiative is behind schedule and a recovery plan is needed.
- When onboarding a new execution owner for an existing initiative.
- During board reviews of strategic initiative progress.

## Gate Criteria

### Clarity and Scope
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The execution plan states the objective in measurable terms with a specific deadline.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Scope is defined with explicit boundaries: what is included and what is out of scope.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Success criteria are defined before execution begins, not retrofitted to results.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The plan distinguishes between leading indicators (input metrics) and lagging indicators (outcome metrics).

### Accountability and Ownership
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A single accountable owner is named for the overall initiative.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each major workstream or milestone has a named owner.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Owners have the authority and resources to deliver their commitments.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The plan specifies who makes decisions when the owner encounters obstacles or scope changes.

### Timeline and Milestones
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The timeline includes milestones at least every 30 days for the first 90 days.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Dependencies between milestones are mapped with critical path identified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Buffer time is built in for the most uncertain phases (minimum 20% for novel work).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: External dependencies (vendor deliveries, regulatory approvals, partner actions) are identified with lead times.

### Resources and Constraints
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Required resources (people, budget, tools) are identified and confirmed available.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Competing priorities for the same resources are identified and resolved.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Constraints (technical, regulatory, organizational) are documented with mitigation plans.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The plan is feasible given current team capacity, not dependent on heroic effort.

### Risk and Adaptation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Top three execution risks are identified with probability, impact, and mitigation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Trigger points are defined for when the plan needs revision (not just at the end).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A review cadence is established (weekly for high-priority, biweekly for standard).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The plan includes a mechanism for incorporating learnings and adjusting course.

## Red Flags
- The plan has no intermediate milestones between launch and the final deadline.
- All milestones are clustered at the end of the timeline.
- The same people are listed as owners on more than three concurrent initiatives.
- The plan assumes 100% resource availability with no buffer for interruptions.
- Dependencies are listed but no one has confirmed commitments from the dependency owners.
- Past execution plans from the same team have consistently missed deadlines with no process improvement.

## Escalation Triggers
- A critical-path milestone is missed by more than two weeks.
- A key resource becomes unavailable (departure, reallocation) without a replacement plan.
- Scope creep has expanded the initiative beyond original approval without reauthorization.
- The accountable owner signals they cannot deliver but the plan is not revised.
- Three or more dependencies are simultaneously at risk.
