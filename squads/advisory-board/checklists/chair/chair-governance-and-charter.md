# Chair Governance and Charter Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the advisory board operates under a clear charter that defines its authority, scope, composition, and operating procedures. Without a governance framework, advisory boards drift into either rubber-stamping or micromanaging. This checklist ensures the charter is current, understood, and followed.

## When to Use
- When establishing or reconstituting the advisory board.
- Annually during the board effectiveness review.
- When the company's stage or strategy changes significantly enough to warrant charter revision.
- When governance disputes arise about the board's authority or scope.

## Gate Criteria

### Charter Completeness
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A written charter exists that defines the advisory board's purpose, authority, and limitations.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The charter specifies which decisions require board approval versus board input versus no board involvement.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board composition requirements are defined: number of members, expertise mix, independence criteria.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Member term lengths, rotation schedule, and renewal process are specified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Meeting frequency, quorum requirements, and voting procedures are defined.

### Operating Procedures
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Conflict of interest policies are documented and acknowledged by all members.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Confidentiality requirements are specified and signed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The process for adding and removing board members is defined.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board communication protocols (between meetings, with management, with external parties) are established.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board evaluation procedures are defined.

### Authority Boundaries
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The distinction between advisory (input) and governance (approval) authority is clear.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Materiality thresholds for board-level decisions are defined (dollar amounts, strategic significance).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Delegated authority to management is specified with reporting requirements.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Emergency decision-making procedures exist for time-sensitive situations.

### Charter Maintenance
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The charter is reviewed at least annually and updated as needed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: All board members have a current copy and can locate it.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Changes to the charter require a defined approval process.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The charter's provisions are actually followed, not just documented.

## Red Flags
- No written charter exists; the board operates on informal understanding.
- The charter has not been reviewed since the board was formed.
- Board members disagree about the scope of their authority.
- Management selectively involves the board, bypassing the charter when convenient.
- The charter is so vague that it provides no practical guidance.
- New board members are not given the charter or oriented to its provisions.

## Escalation Triggers
- A governance dispute arises that the charter does not adequately address.
- A material decision is made without required board involvement as defined in the charter.
- The charter's composition requirements are not met (missing expertise, lapsed terms).
- A conflict of interest is not disclosed or managed according to charter procedures.
- External legal or regulatory requirements change and the charter is not updated.
