# Board & Advisor Compensation Benchmarks

## Overview

Compensation for board members and advisors varies dramatically by company stage, industry, and the nature of the engagement. This research compiles benchmarks to help founders and CEOs structure competitive, fair compensation packages.

## Startup Advisory Board Compensation

### Equity-Only (Pre-Revenue / Seed Stage)
| Role | Typical Equity | Vesting | Meeting Cadence |
|------|---------------|---------|-----------------|
| Informal Advisor | 0.10 - 0.25% | 2 years, monthly | Ad hoc |
| Advisory Board Member | 0.25 - 0.50% | 2 years, quarterly cliff | Quarterly |
| Strategic Advisor (domain expert) | 0.50 - 1.00% | 2-3 years, monthly | Monthly |
| Advisory Board Chair | 0.50 - 1.00% | 2 years, monthly | Monthly + ad hoc |

### Cash + Equity (Series A-B)
| Role | Annual Cash | Equity | Notes |
|------|-----------|--------|-------|
| Advisory Board Member | $5K - $15K | 0.10 - 0.25% | Cash often per-meeting ($1-2K) |
| Strategic Advisor | $15K - $30K | 0.15 - 0.35% | Monthly retainer model |
| Industry Expert | $10K - $25K | 0.10 - 0.20% | Project-based engagement |

### Standard Advisor Agreement Terms
- **Vesting schedule:** 24 months with monthly vesting, no cliff (or 3-month cliff)
- **Single trigger acceleration:** Common for advisors (uncommon for employees)
- **Time commitment:** 2-5 hours per month
- **Expense reimbursement:** Travel and reasonable expenses covered

### Trends
- Shift from per-meeting fees to flat annual retainers
- Increasing use of RSUs over stock options
- Growing premium for cybersecurity and technology expertise
- ESG committee roles commanding new premiums ($10-15K)

## Compensation Design Principles

### For Early-Stage Companies
1. **Lead with equity** — Cash-constrained companies should offer meaningful equity with clear vesting
2. **Define the time commitment explicitly** — Vague expectations lead to advisor disengagement
3. **Include a termination mechanism** — Either party should be able to end the relationship with 30-day notice
4. **Avoid anti-dilution provisions** — Advisor equity should dilute alongside founders

### For Growth-Stage Companies
1. **Benchmark against peer companies** — Use stage, industry, and geography comparisons
2. **Tie compensation to committee responsibilities** — Extra work deserves extra pay
3. **Review annually** — Adjust as company complexity and director workload increase
4. **Consider D&O insurance** — Directors increasingly require coverage as a condition of service

### For Public Companies
1. **Maintain independence** — Compensation should not create financial dependence on the company
2. **Align with shareholders** — Majority equity-based compensation aligns incentives
3. **Cap meeting fees** — Flat retainers encourage strategic engagement over attendance maximization
4. **Disclose transparently** — Proxy statement disclosures are scrutinized by ISS and Glass Lewis

## Common Mistakes

- Offering too little equity to attract serious advisors, then getting what you paid for
- Granting advisor equity without vesting, creating windfall for minimal contribution
- Failing to define deliverables, making it impossible to evaluate advisor performance
- Over-compensating early advisors relative to employees, creating internal equity issues
- Not having a written agreement, leading to disputes about expectations and ownership
