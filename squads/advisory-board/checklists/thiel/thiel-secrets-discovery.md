# Thiel Secrets Discovery Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the organization actively seeks and capitalizes on secrets: important truths about the world that are not yet widely understood. Thiel distinguishes between conventions (things everyone knows), secrets (hard-won truths few have discovered), and mysteries (things no one can know). Companies that discover and act on secrets create enormous value. This checklist detects whether the organization has a systematic approach to uncovering non-obvious insights.

## When to Use
- During product discovery and innovation processes.
- When developing competitive strategy.
- When evaluating whether the company's thesis is common knowledge or genuinely differentiated.
- During R&D and strategic investment planning.
- When the company feels it has lost its innovative edge.

## Gate Criteria

### Secret Identification
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization can articulate at least one secret it has discovered that competitors have not.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The secret is about something important: a large market, a fundamental behavior, or a technology shift.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The secret is validated through evidence, not just believed as an act of faith.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The reason others have not discovered this secret is understood (too hard, too unfashionable, too counterintuitive).

### Discovery Process
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization has processes for discovering secrets: customer research, technology exploration, market analysis.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: People are incentivized to pursue non-obvious insights, not just optimize known variables.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization interacts with unconventional sources of information (other industries, academia, frontier technology).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Time and resources are allocated to exploration that may not have immediate payoff.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization distinguishes between incremental insights and genuine secrets.

### Secret Protection
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Secrets that are critical to competitive advantage are protected from leaking.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization is selective about what it shares publicly versus keeps proprietary.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: IP protection mechanisms (patents, trade secrets, NDAs) are appropriate for the type of secret.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization moves quickly to capitalize on secrets before they become widely known.

### Secret Exploitation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Discovered secrets are translated into product, strategy, or business model decisions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Investment is allocated proportionally to the size of the secret's potential impact.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization builds on secrets to create compounding advantages.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The transition from secret to competitive advantage is actively managed.

## Red Flags
- The company has no thesis about the world that competitors would find surprising.
- All insights are derived from publicly available data that competitors also analyze.
- Innovation consists of copying competitors' features rather than pursuing original insights.
- The R&D budget is spent entirely on incremental improvement, with nothing allocated to exploratory work.
- Customer conversations are scripted and do not probe for unexpected insights.
- The organization has not made a non-obvious bet in over 12 months.

## Escalation Triggers
- A competitor launches a product or service based on a secret the organization should have discovered.
- The organization's products are becoming commoditized because they are based on common knowledge.
- Innovation pipeline is empty and the organization is recycling old ideas.
- Customer research reveals needs the organization had no awareness of.
- The organization's strategic thesis is now consensus, and no new secrets are being pursued.
