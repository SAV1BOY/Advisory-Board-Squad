# Annual Letters Index

## Overview
A curated index of the most decision-relevant annual shareholder and stakeholder letters. Organized by author with key themes and direct source links.

## Tier 1: Essential Reading

### Warren Buffett — Berkshire Hathaway
- **Timespan:** 1965-present
- **Source:** berkshirehathaway.com/letters/letters.html
- **Key themes:** Capital allocation, competitive moats, management quality, long-term thinking, intellectual honesty
- **Must-read years:** 1977 (inflation), 1985 (restructuring), 2001 (insurance and crisis), 2008 (financial crisis), 2020 (pandemic)

### Jeff Bezos — Amazon
- **Timespan:** 1997-2020
- **Source:** aboutamazon.com/news/company-news (search "letter to shareholders")
- **Key themes:** Customer obsession, long-term thinking, high-velocity decisions, Day 1 mentality
- **Must-read years:** 1997 (foundational), 2004 (free cash flow focus), 2015 (invention and failure), 2016 (Day 1 vs. Day 2)

### Howard Marks — Oaktree Capital
- **Timespan:** 1990-present
- **Source:** oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo
- **Key themes:** Market cycles, risk management, contrarian thinking, investor psychology
- **Must-read memos:** "The Most Important Thing" (2003), "Nobody Knows" (2001), "It's Not Easy" (2015)

### Jamie Dimon — JPMorgan Chase
- **Timespan:** 2005-present
- **Source:** jpmorganchase.com (annual report)
- **Key themes:** Banking operations, risk management, technology investment, macroeconomic outlook
- **Must-read years:** 2008 (financial crisis), 2015 (technology strategy), 2020 (pandemic response)

### Reed Hastings — Netflix

- **Source:** ir.netflix.net (quarterly letters)

### Daniel Ek — Spotify

- **Source:** investors.spotify.com

### Patrick Collison — Stripe
- **Key themes:** Internet infrastructure, developer experience, long-term building
- **Source:** stripe.com/annual-updates

### Tobi Lutke — Shopify
- **Key themes:** Merchant empowerment, platform strategy, organizational design
- **Source:** Shopify annual reports and investor letters

## Board Application
- Assign 2-3 letters per quarter as board reading to develop shared mental models
- Use letter examples as templates for improving the company's own shareholder communications
- Reference specific letter passages when discussing capital allocation, risk, or long-term strategy
