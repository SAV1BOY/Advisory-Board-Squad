# Negotiation Lines

Phrases for board-level negotiations — fundraising, partnerships, M&A, executive compensation, and strategic agreements. These are tools for creating value, protecting interests, and maintaining relationships.

## Opening and Framing

1. **"Before we discuss terms, let's align on what a successful outcome looks like for both sides."**
   Use to frame the negotiation as value-creation, not zero-sum. Establishing shared success criteria early changes the dynamic.

2. **"Help me understand your priorities. Which terms matter most to you, and where do you have flexibility?"**
   Use to gather information before making proposals. The side that understands the other's priorities better usually gets a better deal.

3. **"We want a deal that both sides are happy to honor in five years, not one that either side regrets in six months."**
   Use to signal long-term orientation. Short-term wins that create resentment destroy more value than they capture.

4. **"Let me share our constraints transparently, and I'd like you to share yours."**
   Use to build trust through reciprocal disclosure. Transparent constraints narrow the negotiation space productively.

5. **"I want to be direct about what we can and cannot do. I'd rather lose the deal than make a commitment we can't keep."**
   Use to establish credibility early. Reliability in negotiation is more valuable than cleverness.

## Exploring Options

6. **"What if we structured it differently? Instead of [X], what about [Y]?"**
   Use when a negotiation stalls on a specific term. Restructuring the deal often creates value that didn't exist in the original framing.

7. **"That doesn't work for us as stated. But I think we can get to the same outcome a different way."**
   Use to reject a specific term without rejecting the intent. Preserves the relationship while protecting the interest.

8. **"What would make this an easy yes for your side?"**
   Use to understand the other side's decision criteria. Sometimes a small concession on your part unlocks a large concession on theirs.

9. **"If we give you [X], would you be willing to move on [Y]?"**
   Use to create explicit trades. Linked concessions create value; unlinked concessions are gifts.

10. **"Let's set aside the terms we agree on and focus only on the gaps. What are the two or three items where we're apart?"**
    Use to narrow the negotiation to what actually matters. Most of the deal is usually agreed; the dispute is concentrated in a few terms.

## Holding the Line

11. **"That is beyond our range. I want to be honest rather than counter with something unrealistic."**
    Use when a proposal is far from acceptable. Direct honesty about limits is more effective than theatrical counter-offers.

12. **"We've made significant concessions on [list them]. We need you to move on [specific item]."**
    Use to create reciprocity pressure. Naming your concessions explicitly makes the asymmetry visible.

13. **"This is a deal-breaker for us. I want you to know that clearly so we don't waste each other's time."**
    Use sparingly and honestly. A deal-breaker that turns out to be negotiable destroys your credibility.

14. **"I need to take this back to my board. I can advocate for this position, but only if [condition]."**
    Use to create a legitimate external constraint. The board as a backstop is honest and effective when used genuinely.

15. **"We are prepared to walk away from this deal. We would prefer not to, but we will if the terms don't work."**
    Use when you mean it. Walking-away power is the strongest negotiating tool, but only if the other side believes you.

## Creating Urgency (Without Manipulation)

16. **"We have a timeline constraint: [specific date]. Can we work backward from that?"**
    Use to create legitimate urgency. Real deadlines (board meetings, fiscal year, market windows) drive closure.

17. **"We are in conversations with other parties. I'm telling you because I respect you, not to pressure you."**
    Use when competition is genuine. Transparently acknowledging alternatives is honest; manufacturing them is manipulation.

18. **"The terms of this discussion are valid through [date]. After that, we'll need to reassess."**
    Use to prevent indefinite negotiation without commitment. Expiration creates decision pressure.

## Closing

19. **"Let me summarize where we are: we agree on [list]. We are still apart on [list]. Can we close those gaps today?"**
    Use to force the final push. A clear summary of agreed and open items makes the remaining work visible and achievable.

20. **"I want to confirm: if we agree on [final open item], do we have a deal?"**
    Use to test whether there are hidden issues. If the answer is "yes, but also...", there are more issues than you thought.

21. **"We have a deal. Let me restate the key terms so we are both working from the same understanding."**
    Use immediately after reaching agreement. Verbal agreement should be confirmed before anyone leaves the room.

## Post-Agreement

22. **"The negotiation is over. Now we are partners. How do we make this work?"**
    Use to signal the transition from negotiation mode to collaboration mode. The post-deal relationship is more valuable than the deal itself.

23. **"Let's schedule a 90-day check-in to make sure the agreement is working as intended for both sides."**
    Use to build in a relationship maintenance mechanism. Agreements that are never revisited drift toward dysfunction.
