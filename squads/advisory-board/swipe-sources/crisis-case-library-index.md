# Crisis Case Library Index

## Overview
An index of documented corporate crises organized by type, with key lessons and sources for deeper study. Use this library to pattern-match when advising companies through similar crises.

## Product and Safety Crises

### Boeing 737 MAX (2018-2019)
- **What happened:** MCAS software defect caused two fatal crashes killing 346 people. Global fleet grounding.
- **Response:** Initially defensive — blamed pilot error. Slow to ground fleet. CEO eventually fired.
- **Lesson:** When engineering culture is subordinated to financial targets, catastrophic failures follow. Defensiveness in a safety crisis destroys trust irreparably. Boards must override management instinct to minimize.

### Samsung Galaxy Note 7 (2016)
- **What happened:** Battery defect caused phones to catch fire. Two rounds of recalls.
- **Response:** First recall was partial and insufficient. Second was total — all units pulled. Samsung invested in an 8-point battery safety check.
- **Lesson:** Half-measures in product safety crises extend the crisis. Full recall followed by transparent process improvement rebuilt trust faster.

### CrowdStrike Global Outage (2024)
- **What happened:** A faulty Falcon sensor update caused 8.5 million Windows machines to blue-screen worldwide.
- **Response:** CEO issued a public statement within hours. Full root cause analysis published within days.
- **Lesson:** Speed of acknowledgment matters more than completeness. Publish what you know, commit to a timeline for the rest.

### Theranos (2015-2018)
- **What happened:** Fraudulent medical device claims. Technology did not work as represented.
- **Lesson:** Boards must independently verify core product claims, especially in high-stakes domains.

### Volkswagen Dieselgate (2015)
- **What happened:** Engineers installed defeat devices that cheated emissions tests. $30B+ in fines.
- **Lesson:** When the gap between commitments and engineering reality grows too wide, organizations find shortcuts. Boards must ask: "Is this target achievable?"

## Leadership and Governance Crises

### WeWork / Adam Neumann (2019)
- **What happened:** IPO filing revealed governance failures — related-party transactions, sole voting control, trademark self-dealing.
- **Response:** IPO pulled. Neumann forced out. Valuation collapsed from $47B to under $10B.
- **Lesson:** Board independence is not optional. Boards that rubber-stamp founder excesses become complicit.

### Uber Culture Crisis (2017)
- **What happened:** Susan Fowler's blog post revealed systemic harassment. Followed by executive departures.
- **Response:** Board forced CEO Kalanick out. Eric Holder investigation. Cultural rebuild over 2+ years.
- **Lesson:** Cultural crises require structural change — leadership, governance, policies — not just messaging.

### OpenAI Board Crisis (2023)
- **What happened:** Board fired CEO Sam Altman without consulting investors or employees. 95% of staff threatened to quit.
- **Response:** Board reconstituted. Altman reinstated within 5 days. Governance structure revised.
- **Lesson:** Boards that act without stakeholder alignment create worse crises than the ones they are trying to solve.

### Enron (2001)
- **What happened:** Accounting fraud and board complicity. Company collapsed.
- **Lesson:** The board has a fiduciary duty to understand the financials, not just trust management.

### FTX (2022)
- **What happened:** Commingling of customer funds, governance vacuum. $8B in customer assets lost.
- **Lesson:** Governance infrastructure must scale with the business. No board, no audit, no controls.

## Financial Crises

### SVB / Silicon Valley Bank (2023)
- **What happened:** Duration mismatch plus concentrated depositor base triggered a bank run in 48 hours via social media.
- **Response:** CEO's reassurance letter backfired — it named the problem without a credible solution, triggering panic.
- **Lesson:** Poorly constructed crisis communication can accelerate the crisis. Risk concentration in any single dimension is existential.

### Wirecard (2020)
- **What happened:** EUR 1.9 billion in cash balances did not exist. Massive accounting fraud.
- **Lesson:** Boards must insist on independent verification of material financial claims. "Trust the auditor" is not a control.

### Lehman Brothers (2008)
- **What happened:** Excessive leverage, insufficient liquidity. Bankruptcy triggered global financial crisis.
- **Lesson:** Leverage is the fastest path to destruction. Boards must enforce leverage limits independent of management optimism.

## Reputational Crises

### Wells Fargo Fake Accounts (2016)
- **What happened:** Employees created millions of unauthorized accounts to meet sales targets.
- **Response:** CEO initially blamed low-level employees. Eventually resigned. $3B+ in fines.
- **Lesson:** Incentive structures cause behavior. Boards must audit the incentives, not just the outcomes.

### BP Deepwater Horizon (2010)
- **What happened:** Explosion killed 11 workers. Largest marine oil spill in history. $65B+ costs.
- **Lesson:** "I'd like my life back" is the textbook example of what NOT to say in a crisis.

### Facebook / Cambridge Analytica (2018)
- **What happened:** Data privacy violation affecting 87 million users.
- **Lesson:** Platform responsibility for user data is real. "We didn't know" is not an acceptable defense.
