# Overconfidence in Forecasting

## Overview
Overconfidence is the most consistent and damaging cognitive bias in business decision-making. Research shows experts are worse than random at long-term predictions but maintain high confidence. This file provides tools for recognizing and counteracting overconfidence in board-level forecasting.

## Manifestations

### Planning Fallacy
- Projects consistently take longer and cost more than estimated
- Cause: people plan based on best-case scenarios, not base rates
- **Countermeasure:** Reference class forecasting. Ask: "How long did similar projects take for other companies?"

### Narrow Confidence Intervals
- When asked to estimate a range, people make it too narrow. 90% confidence intervals contain the true value only 50% of the time.
- **Countermeasure:** Require ranges 3x wider than feels comfortable. Track prediction accuracy over time.

### Expert Overconfidence
- Domain expertise increases confidence more than it increases accuracy, especially for complex, long-term predictions
- **Countermeasure:** Weight track record over credentials. Ask experts to state their assumptions explicitly.

### Illusion of Control
- People believe they can influence outcomes more than they actually can
- **Countermeasure:** Identify which variables are genuinely controllable and which are environmental.

## Debiasing Techniques

1. **Pre-mortem** — Imagine the plan has failed. What went wrong? This activates skeptical thinking.
2. **Reference class forecasting** — Start with base rates from similar situations before adjusting for specifics.
3. **Track record review** — How accurate were past forecasts? Most organizations never check.
4. **Three-scenario planning** — Require optimistic, base, and pessimistic scenarios with probabilities for each.
5. **Outside view solicitation** — Ask someone with no emotional investment to evaluate the forecast.

## Board Application
- Never accept single-point forecasts for major decisions. Require ranges with explicit assumptions.
- Maintain a "prediction log" tracking board-level forecasts against actual outcomes.
- When management is highly confident, increase scrutiny. Confidence and accuracy are often inversely correlated.
- Apply reference class forecasting to fundraising projections, product launch timelines, and M&A integration plans.
