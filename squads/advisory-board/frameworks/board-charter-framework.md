# Board Charter Framework

## Purpose

A Board Charter defines the governance structure, roles, responsibilities, and operating norms of an advisory or governing board. It is the constitutional document that establishes how the board functions, what authority it has, and how it interfaces with management. Without a charter, boards operate on implicit assumptions that lead to role confusion, overreach, or abdication. This framework provides a comprehensive template for creating a clear, enforceable charter.

## When to Use

- When forming a new advisory board or board of directors
- When reforming an underperforming board
- When there is confusion about board vs. management authority
- When onboarding new board members
- When governance needs to be formalized for fundraising or regulatory requirements

## Core Model

### Charter Components

**1. Board Purpose and Authority**
- Why does this board exist?
- What decisions does the board make vs. advise on vs. have no role in?
- What is the board's relationship to the CEO/founder?

**2. Board Composition**
- How many members? (Optimal: 5-7 for advisory; 5-9 for governing)
- What expertise domains must be represented?
- Term lengths and rotation schedules
- Independence requirements

**3. Roles and Responsibilities**
- Chair role and responsibilities
- Individual member expectations (preparation, attendance, contribution)
- Committee structure (if any): audit, compensation, nominating

**4. Meeting Operating System**
- Frequency (quarterly minimum for advisory; monthly for governing)
- Agenda setting process
- Pre-read requirements and deadlines
- Decision-making process
- Minutes and documentation

**5. Information Rights**
- What information does the board receive and when?
- Financial reporting cadence and format
- Access to management and employees

**6. Performance and Accountability**
- Board self-assessment process
- Member evaluation
- Removal process for underperforming members
- Annual effectiveness review

## Steps

1. **Draft the charter** using the components above as a template.
2. **Review with all members** and incorporate feedback.
3. **Ratify formally** — all members sign the charter.
4. **Distribute to management** so they understand board expectations.
5. **Review annually** and update as the organization evolves.
6. **Use as onboarding material** for every new board member.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Insist on a written charter before joining a board. Verbal agreements about roles inevitably lead to confusion.
- Use the charter to push back on scope creep (board overreaching into operations) or abdication (board rubber-stamping without analysis).

## Common Mistakes

1. **No charter at all.** The most common failure. Implicit governance fails as organizations grow.
2. **Charter without enforcement.** A charter that nobody references is decoration.
3. **Over-detailed charter.** The charter should be a framework, not a rulebook. 5-10 pages maximum.
4. **Not updating.** A seed-stage charter is inappropriate for a Series C company.

## Output Format

```markdown
# Board Charter: [Organization]

**Effective Date:** YYYY-MM-DD
**Version:** X.X

## 1. Purpose and Authority
[Board's role and decision authority]

## 2. Composition
- Members: [Count]
- Required expertise: [Domains]
- Term length: [Duration]

## 3. Roles
- Chair: [Responsibilities]
- Members: [Expectations]

## 4. Meetings
- Frequency: [Cadence]
- Pre-read deadline: [Hours before meeting]
- Decision process: [Consensus / Vote / Chair decides]

## 5. Information Rights
[What board receives and when]

## 6. Accountability
- Self-assessment: [Annual]
- Member review: [Process]
```
