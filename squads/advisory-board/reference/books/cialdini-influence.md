# Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion — Robert Cialdini

## Summary

Cialdini identifies six universal principles of persuasion based on decades of experimental research: Reciprocity, Commitment & Consistency, Social Proof, Authority, Liking, and Scarcity. These principles operate automatically and are used—consciously or unconsciously—in every negotiation, sale, and boardroom interaction. The book serves dual purposes: understanding how to ethically persuade others and defending against manipulation. For board members, recognizing these principles in action is essential for evaluating proposals, managing stakeholders, and making unbiased decisions.

## Key Principles

- **Reciprocity** — People feel obligated to return favors. A small gift or concession triggers an outsized return obligation.
- **Commitment and Consistency** — Once people commit to something (especially publicly), they feel internal pressure to behave consistently with that commitment.
- **Social Proof** — In uncertainty, people look to what others are doing. The more people doing something, the more "correct" it seems.
- **Authority** — People defer to perceived experts and authority figures, often without evaluating the substance of their claims.
- **Liking** — People are more persuaded by people they like. Liking is driven by similarity, compliments, cooperation, and physical attractiveness.
- **Scarcity** — Things seem more valuable when they're scarce or about to become unavailable. Deadlines and limited availability trigger urgency.

## Frameworks Extracted

1. **The Persuasion Audit** — For any proposal or pitch: which of the six principles is being deployed? Is the deployment ethical (genuine) or manipulative (manufactured)?
2. **The Defense Protocol** — When you feel an unexplained urge to comply: (1) Identify which principle is being triggered, (2) Separate the principle from the substance, (3) Evaluate the substance on its own merits.
3. **The Ethical Influence Design** — To persuade ethically: (1) Be genuinely helpful first (reciprocity), (2) Seek small initial commitments (consistency), (3) Demonstrate proven results (social proof), (4) Establish genuine expertise (authority), (5) Find genuine common ground (liking), (6) Be honest about genuine constraints (scarcity).
4. **The Groupthink Detector** — Social proof is most dangerous in groups. When a board reaches quick consensus, check: is this genuine agreement or social proof cascading through the room?

## Application to Board Decisions

- **Proposal evaluation** — When a compelling pitch comes to the board, apply the Persuasion Audit. Is the proposal genuinely strong, or is it leveraging authority bias (prestigious advisor supports it), social proof (everyone else is doing it), or scarcity (we must act now)?
- **Negotiation** — Understand which principles counterparties are using. Awareness is the primary defense against manipulation.
- **Stakeholder management** — Use ethical influence principles to build board-stakeholder relationships. Genuine reciprocity and demonstrated expertise create durable trust.
- **Groupthink prevention** — Social proof is the primary mechanism of board groupthink. Implement structural safeguards: independent pre-read opinions, mandatory devil's advocate, anonymous first-round voting.
- **Fundraising guidance** — Help founders understand and ethically deploy influence principles in investor conversations.
