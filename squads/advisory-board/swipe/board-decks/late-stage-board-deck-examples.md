# Late-Stage / Pre-IPO Board Deck Examples

## Overview
Late-stage and pre-IPO board decks serve a dual purpose: governing a maturing business and preparing the company for public-market scrutiny. The deck should demonstrate institutional-grade rigor, predictable execution, and clear articulation of the long-term value creation thesis. Governance expectations are at their highest.

## Recommended Structure (25-35 slides)

### Section 1: Executive Summary (2-3 slides)
- CEO letter: strategic narrative positioned for public market context, candid self-assessment
- KPI dashboard: 10-12 key metrics with trend lines over 8+ quarters, traffic light status
- Progress against IPO readiness milestones (audit, S-1 narrative, underwriter selection, roadshow prep)

### Section 2: Financial Performance (5-6 slides)
- GAAP and non-GAAP P&L with variance to plan, prior year, and analyst consensus proxies
- Revenue quality analysis: recurring vs. non-recurring, concentration risk (top 10 customers as % of revenue)
- Cohort economics: LTV/CAC by vintage, net dollar retention by segment
- Free cash flow bridge: operating cash flow, capex, working capital dynamics
- Balance sheet with debt maturity schedule and covenant compliance
- Full-year and next-year guidance framework with scenario bands

### Section 3: Growth and Go-to-Market (4-5 slides)
- Bookings and ARR waterfall: new, expansion, contraction, churn
- Sales productivity: ramp times, quota attainment distribution, pipeline coverage ratios
- Channel mix evolution: direct vs. partner vs. self-serve, with unit economics per channel
- International revenue mix and growth rates by geography
- Customer segmentation: enterprise vs. mid-market vs. SMB, with different KPIs per segment

### Section 4: Product and Technology (3-4 slides)
- Product usage metrics: DAU/MAU, feature adoption curves, engagement depth
- Platform reliability: uptime, incident severity trends, mean time to recovery
- R&D efficiency: investment by category (new product, enhancement, infrastructure, technical debt)
- Competitive technology moat assessment: what is defensible and what is replicable
- Security and compliance posture: SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, industry-specific certifications

### Section 5: People and Organization (3 slides)
- Executive team stability and succession planning status for all C-suite roles
- Organizational scaling metrics: spans, layers, manager-to-IC ratio
- Compensation benchmarking: are we positioned to retain key talent through and post-IPO?
- Public-company readiness: board composition, committee structure, independent director pipeline

### Section 6: Risk, Legal, and Compliance (3 slides)
- Enterprise risk register with quantified exposure estimates
- Regulatory landscape and government affairs strategy
- IP portfolio status and freedom-to-operate analysis
- Pending and potential litigation with materiality assessment
- D&O insurance and public-company liability preparation

### Section 7: Strategic Discussion (3-4 slides)
- Long-range plan (3-5 year): revenue trajectory, margin expansion path, capital allocation framework
- M&A pipeline and integration capacity assessment
- Market positioning for public narrative: what category do we define, and who are our public comps?
- Deep-dive: one strategic issue requiring board deliberation (with pre-read memo)

### Section 8: IPO Readiness (2-3 slides)
- S-1 narrative draft status and key messaging themes
- Financial model audit trail: can we defend every assumption under SEC scrutiny?
- Investor targeting: who are the right long-term shareholders?
- Quiet period, lock-up, and post-IPO governance planning
- Timeline and critical path to listing

### Section 9: Governance and Appendix
- Committee reports with detailed audit committee review
- Related-party transactions disclosure
- Board evaluation results and composition planning
- Detailed financial schedules and backup data

## Key Differences from Growth-Stage Decks
- **Predictability over speed:** Late-stage boards prioritize forecast accuracy and operating leverage
- **Narrative discipline:** Every metric should support the equity story you will tell public investors
- **Risk quantification:** Move from qualitative risk descriptions to quantified exposure estimates
- **Succession depth:** The company must function without any single individual, including the CEO

## Board Application
- Spend 40% of board time on strategy and long-range planning, 35% on risk and governance, 25% on operations
- Require the CFO to present a "public company earnings call simulation" at least twice before filing
- Test the S-1 narrative with the board: does it hold up under adversarial questioning?
- Ensure every board member understands their fiduciary obligations in the public company context
- Begin formal board evaluation process and address any composition gaps before listing
