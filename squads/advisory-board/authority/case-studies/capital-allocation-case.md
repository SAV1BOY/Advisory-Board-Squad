# Case Study: Capital Allocation Mastery

## Title: How Disciplined Capital Allocation Transformed a Mid-Market SaaS Company

## Situation

A B2B SaaS company ("TechCo") reached $35M ARR with strong product-market fit in its core vertical. The company had raised a $50M Series C and faced a critical question: how to deploy that capital for maximum long-term value creation.

**The leadership team proposed three simultaneous investments:**
1. Enter two new vertical markets ($15M estimated cost over 18 months)
2. Build an AI-powered product extension ($8M over 12 months)
3. Acquire a complementary analytics company ($20M purchase price)

Total proposed deployment: $43M of the $50M raise, leaving minimal reserves.

**The board's concern:** Deploying capital across three major initiatives simultaneously would stretch leadership attention, dilute execution quality, and leave no margin for error.

## The Advisory Intervention

### Step 1: Framework Introduction — The Capital Allocation Hierarchy

The advisory board introduced a capital allocation framework that prioritized investments based on three criteria:

**Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) potential:**
- What is the expected return per dollar invested?
- How confident are we in the return estimate?

**Strategic optionality:**
- Does this investment open future doors or close them?
- Is this reversible or irreversible?

**Execution capacity:**
- Does the current team have the capability and bandwidth?
- What is the opportunity cost to the core business?

### Step 2: Rigorous Analysis of Each Initiative

#### Initiative 1: New Vertical Market Entry
- **ROIC estimate:** 2.5x over 3 years (moderate confidence)
- **Strategic optionality:** Opens two new TAM segments; requires dedicated sales and product teams
- **Execution risk:** High — the company had never entered a new vertical; would require hiring vertical-specific expertise
- **Advisory assessment:** Pursue one vertical, not two. Sequence rather than parallelize.

#### Initiative 2: AI Product Extension
- **ROIC estimate:** 4x over 2 years (high confidence — based on customer demand signals)
- **Strategic optionality:** High — creates defensible moat and pricing power in core vertical
- **Execution risk:** Moderate — required hiring 3 ML engineers but leveraged existing product infrastructure
- **Advisory assessment:** Highest priority. Fund fully and accelerate timeline.

#### Initiative 3: Acquisition
- **ROIC estimate:** 1.5x over 3 years (low confidence — integration risk, cultural uncertainty)
- **Strategic optionality:** Moderate — analytics capability could be built internally in 18-24 months
- **Execution risk:** Very high — company had never completed an acquisition; no integration playbook
- **Advisory assessment:** Defer. Build analytics capability organically while developing M&A capabilities for future acquisitions.

### Step 3: Recommended Capital Allocation

| Initiative | Original Ask | Recommended | Rationale |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|-----------|
| AI Product Extension | $8M | $10M (accelerated) | Highest ROIC, strongest confidence, builds moat |
| Single Vertical Entry | $15M (two verticals) | $7M (one vertical) | Sequence to learn before scaling |
| Acquisition | $20M | $0 (deferred) | Build capability organically; develop M&A readiness |
| Strategic Reserve | $0 | $15M | Maintain optionality and survival margin |
| R&D and Core | $0 | $8M | Strengthen core product to support expansion |
| Talent Investment | $0 | $5M | Hire for capability gaps identified above |
| Contingency | $7M | $5M | Reduced due to focused strategy |

**Total: $50M deployed across fewer, higher-conviction bets with meaningful reserves.**

## The Result

### 12-Month Outcomes
- AI product extension launched 2 months ahead of schedule; drove 40% increase in contract value for existing customers
- Single vertical entry achieved $3M ARR in Year 1 (vs. $2M target)
- The deferred acquisition target's performance declined, validating the decision to wait
- Strategic reserve proved critical when a major customer delayed renewal, requiring bridge investment in customer success

### 24-Month Outcomes
- AI extension became the primary competitive differentiator, cited in 70% of new customer wins
- First new vertical reached $8M ARR; second vertical entry initiated with learnings from the first
- The company built internal analytics capabilities at 30% of the acquisition cost
- With the reserve intact and business performing, the company raised a Series D at 3x the Series C valuation

## Principles Demonstrated

### 1. Concentration Beats Diversification
Deploying capital across fewer, higher-conviction bets produced better returns than spreading across three simultaneous initiatives. The advisory board's core insight was that execution capacity, not capital, was the binding constraint.

### 2. Sequencing Creates Learning
Entering one vertical before the second allowed TechCo to develop a vertical expansion playbook. The second vertical entry was faster and cheaper because of the first.

### 3. Reserves Are Not Idle Capital
The $15M strategic reserve was not "money sitting around." It was optionality — the ability to respond to threats, seize opportunities, and survive unexpected setbacks without emergency fundraising.

### 4. Build Before Buy
The deferred acquisition forced TechCo to build analytics capabilities internally. This was slower but produced deeper organizational learning and avoided integration risk.

### 5. The Best Capital Allocation Decision Is Often "Not Yet"
Deferring the acquisition was not saying "no" — it was saying "not yet, and here is what we need to learn first." This preserved the option without committing capital prematurely.

## Advisory Takeaways

- Always assess execution capacity alongside capital availability — money without bandwidth is waste
- Maintain reserves of at least 20-30% of raised capital for optionality and survival
- Sequence investments to generate learning that improves subsequent bets
- The highest-ROIC investment with the highest confidence level should be funded first and funded fully
- Acquisition should be the last tool considered, not the first, for companies without M&A experience
