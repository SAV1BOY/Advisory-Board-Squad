# Edtech Industry Reference

## Business Model Characteristics
- **Revenue models:** B2C subscriptions, B2B SaaS (to schools/universities/corporates), marketplace (course platforms), certification fees
- **Willingness to pay:** Consumer edtech has low willingness to pay. Corporate training and professional certification have higher.
- **Engagement challenge:** Education requires sustained effort. Completion rates for online courses average 5-15%.
- **Outcome measurement:** Increasingly, buyers want evidence of outcomes (skill acquisition, career advancement, ROI).

## Key Segments
- **K-12:** Government-funded, long sales cycles, relationship-driven. Massive TAM but slow to monetize.
- **Higher education:** Disruption through online degrees, bootcamps, micro-credentials. Regulatory complexity.
- **Corporate training / L&D:** Highest willingness to pay. B2B SaaS model. Competing with internal training and traditional providers.
- **Consumer learning:** Language (Duolingo), skills (MasterClass, Skillshare), test prep. Engagement is the key challenge.
- **Creator / platform:** Enabling instructors to create and sell courses (Teachable, Thinkific, Hotmart). Marketplace dynamics.

## Board Considerations
- Evaluate engagement metrics (DAU/MAU, completion rates, time spent) alongside revenue. Revenue without engagement is churn risk.
- For B2C, assess whether the product creates genuine habit (Duolingo model) or relies on initial motivation (high churn).
- For B2B, evaluate whether the buyer (HR/L&D) is satisfied or whether learner engagement is low despite enterprise contracts.
- AI is transforming edtech: personalized learning, AI tutors, automated assessment. Companies not integrating AI risk disruption.
