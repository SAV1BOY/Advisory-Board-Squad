# 11 — Succession Planning Cycle

## Overview

A proactive process to identify, develop, and prepare successors for critical leadership roles — ensuring continuity when transitions occur, whether planned or unplanned. This workflow covers both emergency succession (bus factor) and deliberate development pipelines.

## Trigger

- Annual planning cycle (standing item, typically Q1).
- Executive departure announcement.
- Board effectiveness review identifies succession gaps.
- CEO or Chair signals a planned transition within 24 months.
- Company reaches a growth inflection that demands different leadership capabilities.

## Agents

| Agent | Role |
|---|---|
| board-chair (Board Chair) | Sponsors the process, leads CEO succession planning |
| culture-steward (Brene Brown) | Assesses cultural fit of succession candidates |
| strategic-advisor (Peter Thiel) | Maps future capability requirements against strategy |
| governance-clerk (Simon Sinek) | Maintains the succession register and timeline |
| capital-allocator (Charlie Munger) | Challenges readiness assessments and identifies blind spots |

## Phases

### Phase 1 — Role Criticality Audit (Days 1-10)

1. Identify all critical roles: positions where a sudden vacancy would materially impact the company's operations, strategy, or culture.
2. For each critical role, assess:
   - Bus factor: how many people can perform this role today? (1 = critical risk)
   - Transition complexity: how long would it take to replace this person from outside? (Months)
   - Strategic importance: how central is this role to the company's next 3-year strategy?
   - Knowledge concentration: what institutional knowledge exists only in this person's head?
3. Score each role on a criticality matrix: likelihood of vacancy × impact of vacancy.
4. Prioritize the top 5-7 roles for active succession planning.
5. board-chair reviews and approves the prioritized role list.

### Phase 2 — Candidate Identification & Development (Days 10-30)

1. For each prioritized role, identify potential successors:
   - Internal candidates: current leaders 1-2 levels below, high-potential individuals, cross-functional candidates.
   - External pipeline: industry contacts, search firm relationships, advisory network.
2. Assess each internal candidate against the role's scorecard (see workflow 05 for scorecard methodology):
   - Ready now: could step in within 30 days.
   - Ready in 12 months: needs specific development.
   - Ready in 24+ months: high potential but significant gaps.
3. culture-steward evaluates cultural alignment of each candidate.
4. capital-allocator asks: "Are we only considering the obvious candidates? Who are we overlooking?"
5. For candidates in the "ready in 12 months" category, design a development plan:
   - Stretch assignments that build the missing capabilities.
   - Mentoring or coaching relationships.
   - Exposure to board-level discussions (observer roles, presentation opportunities).
   - External development (executive education, industry conferences, peer networks).

### Phase 3 — Emergency Succession Protocol (Days 20-25)

1. For each critical role, document the emergency succession plan:
   - Interim successor: who takes over within 24 hours if the incumbent is suddenly unavailable?
   - Interim authority: what decisions can the interim make? What requires board approval?
   - Communication plan: who is notified, in what order, with what message?
   - Duration: how long can the interim arrangement last before a permanent solution is needed?
2. Store emergency succession plans in a secure, accessible location (board-chair and governance-clerk have access).
3. Test the emergency plan annually: conduct a tabletop exercise simulating a sudden vacancy.

### Phase 4 — Timeline & Transition Planning (Days 25-40)

1. For planned transitions (retirements, role changes, sabbaticals):
   - Define the transition timeline: announcement, search, overlap period, handoff, departure.
   - Minimum overlap period: 30 days for VP roles, 60 days for C-suite, 90 days for CEO.
   - Knowledge transfer plan: what must be documented, transferred, or taught?
   - Stakeholder communication plan: when and how to announce the transition.
2. For CEO succession specifically:
   - board-chair leads the process with input from the full board.
   - External search firm engaged (even if an internal candidate is likely, validate externally).
   - Board conducts finalist interviews directly.
   - Transition timeline includes a "shadow" period where the successor works alongside the outgoing CEO.

### Phase 5 — Handoff & Monitoring (Ongoing)

1. When a transition occurs, execute the transition plan step by step.
2. board-chair conducts 30-day, 60-day, and 90-day check-ins with the new leader.
3. Monitor the transition against the scorecard for the role.
4. At 6 months, board-chair conducts a formal transition review:
   - Is the new leader meeting expectations?
   - What support do they need?
   - Has the team stabilized?
5. Update the succession register: remove completed transitions, add new critical roles, refresh candidate assessments.
6. Archive transition documentation in `archive/successions/`.

## Quality Gates

| Gate | Criteria | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| G1 — Criticality audit complete | Top 5-7 roles identified and scored | board-chair (Board Chair) |
| G2 — Candidates identified | Each prioritized role has ≥2 potential successors | governance-clerk (Simon Sinek) |
| G3 — Emergency plans documented | Every critical role has an emergency succession plan | governance-clerk (Simon Sinek) |
| G4 — Development plans active | "Ready in 12 months" candidates have active development plans | board-chair (Board Chair) |
| G5 — Annual tabletop exercise | Emergency plan tested via simulation | board-chair (Board Chair) |

## Outputs

- Role criticality matrix.
- Succession register: role, candidates, readiness level, development plans.
- Emergency succession plans for each critical role.
- Development plans for high-potential candidates.
- Transition plans for planned departures.
- Transition review reports.

## Metrics

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Critical roles with ≥2 identified successors | 100 % |
| Critical roles with emergency plans | 100 % |
| "Ready now" coverage for critical roles | ≥60 % of roles have at least one "ready now" candidate |
| Development plan activation | 100 % of "ready in 12 months" candidates have active plans |
| Tabletop exercise completion | Annual, no exceptions |
| Transition success rate | ≥80 % of transitions rated "successful" at 6-month review |

## Common Failures

| Failure Mode | Symptom | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Succession as paperwork | Register exists but is never used or updated | Annual refresh is a board-level agenda item |
| Clone bias | Succession candidates look exactly like the incumbent | capital-allocator asks: "What different capabilities does the future require?" |
| Development lip service | Plans exist but stretch assignments never materialize | board-chair reviews development plan progress semi-annually |
| Emergency plan untested | Plans exist on paper but no one has practiced | Annual tabletop exercise is mandatory |
| CEO succession avoidance | Board does not discuss CEO succession because it feels awkward | board-chair raises it proactively; it is a fiduciary responsibility |
| External neglect | Only internal candidates considered | Maintain external pipeline through search firm relationships and advisory network |
