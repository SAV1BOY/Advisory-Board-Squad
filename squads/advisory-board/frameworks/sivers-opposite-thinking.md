# Sivers Opposite Thinking

## Purpose

Derek Sivers's Opposite Thinking framework proposes that for most conventional wisdom, the opposite is equally valid. When everyone goes one way, enormous opportunity lies in going the other way. This is not contrarianism for its own sake — it is a systematic method for generating non-obvious strategies by inverting the default. In a world where most people follow the same advice, differentiation comes from doing the opposite of what everyone else does.

## When to Use

- When brainstorming strategy and all ideas feel conventional
- When an industry has strong consensus that hasn't been challenged
- When looking for contrarian positioning opportunities
- When conventional approaches are producing diminishing returns
- When you need to break out of groupthink

## Core Model

### The Opposite Test
For any conventional wisdom, state the opposite and test whether it might be equally (or more) valid:
- "Raise as much money as possible" → "Raise as little money as possible"
- "Move fast and break things" → "Move deliberately and build things that last"
- "The customer is always right" → "The customer often doesn't know what they want"
- "Grow your team quickly" → "Stay small as long as possible"
- "Network aggressively" → "Create great work and let it attract people"

### Why Opposites Can Both Be True
Most advice is context-dependent. When the context changes, the opposite advice becomes correct. "Move fast" is right when the market is emerging. "Move carefully" is right when trust is the moat. Both are true — in different contexts.

### The Sivers Insight
"Whatever scares you, go do it. Whatever seems like too much trouble, do it. Whatever makes you wonder 'Can I do that?' — do it." The things that feel wrong or scary often lead to the most differentiated outcomes.

## Steps

1. **Identify the conventional wisdom in your domain.** What does "everyone" do?
2. **State the opposite of each convention.** Don't judge — just generate.
3. **Evaluate each opposite.** Is there a scenario where the opposite is actually better?
4. **Test the most promising opposites.** Small experiments, not full commitments.
5. **Adopt the opposites that work.** They become your differentiation.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- When all advisors agree, try stating the opposite and evaluating it. Unanimous agreement is often a sign of groupthink, not correctness.
- Help founders consider opposite approaches to their biggest challenges. The opposite strategy is often under-explored.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Contrarianism without evaluation.** Being different is not the goal; being different AND right is.
2. **Ignoring context.** The opposite may be right in a different context. Make sure you're in the right context.
3. **Applying to everything.** Not every convention is wrong. Use this as a generator, not a rule.
