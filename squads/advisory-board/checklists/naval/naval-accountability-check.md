# Naval Accountability Check Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures that the organization has clear, visible, and enforceable accountability for decisions and commitments. Naval emphasizes that accountability under your own name creates both risk and reward, and that society rewards those who take accountability. This checklist verifies that accountability is real (not ceremonial), personal (not diffused), and consequential (with genuine rewards for success and consequences for failure).

## When to Use
- When assigning ownership of major initiatives and decisions.
- When accountability seems diffuse and no one owns outcomes.
- When evaluating whether leadership structure enables clear accountability.
- During post-mortems to assess whether accountability was clear before the outcome.
- When designing organizational structure and decision-making authority.

## Gate Criteria

### Clarity of Accountability
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Every major initiative has a single named person who is accountable for the outcome.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The accountable person can describe their scope of authority and the decisions they own.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The difference between accountability (owns the outcome) and responsibility (does the work) is understood.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Accountability assignments are documented and visible to all stakeholders.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: No critical outcome exists without a named accountable owner.

### Accountability Under Name
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Results (positive and negative) are attributed to named individuals in reviews and reports.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders publicly own the outcomes of their decisions and initiatives.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Track records of accountability and delivery are maintained for key leaders.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Accountability is visible enough that reputation is genuinely at stake.

### Consequence and Reward
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Successful accountable owners are recognized and rewarded proportionally.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Failed accountability results in genuine consequences (role change, compensation impact), not just a conversation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Consequences distinguish between poor judgment (addressable) and bad luck (not punished).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization avoids punishing accountability itself (owning a failure is better than hiding it).

### Systemic Accountability
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board holds the CEO accountable through regular, structured performance evaluation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The CEO holds the leadership team accountable through consistent review and feedback.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Cross-functional accountability mechanisms exist so teams do not just manage up.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Accountability cascades: company goals translate to team goals to individual goals.

## Red Flags
- Accountability is assigned to committees rather than individuals.
- Failure never results in meaningful consequences for the accountable person.
- Success is claimed by many but failure is orphaned.
- The most senior person avoids putting their name on decisions.
- Accountability is assigned after the fact based on the outcome, not before.
- People in accountable roles lack the authority to make the decisions their accountability requires.

## Escalation Triggers
- A major initiative fails and no one is accountable or there is confusion about who was accountable.
- An accountable owner is set up to fail: given responsibility without authority or resources.
- A pattern of accountability avoidance emerges among senior leaders.
- The board cannot identify who is accountable for critical company outcomes.
- Employees report that accountability is inequitable: applied to some but not others.
