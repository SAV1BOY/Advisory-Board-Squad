# Board Evaluation Frameworks

## Annual Board Self-Assessment

### Individual Member Evaluation
Each board member rates themselves and their peers on:

1. **Preparation** (1-5) — Consistently reads materials, arrives prepared, asks informed questions
2. **Participation** (1-5) — Actively contributes to discussions, attends meetings, engages between meetings
3. **Strategic value** (1-5) — Brings unique insights that improve decision quality
4. **Independence** (1-5) — Willing to disagree, challenge management, express unpopular views
5. **Relationships** (1-5) — Constructive with fellow board members, productive relationship with management
6. **Domain expertise** (1-5) — Contributes specialized knowledge that the board would otherwise lack
7. **Time commitment** (1-5) — Available for committees, ad hoc conversations, and special situations
8. **Judgment** (1-5) — Demonstrates sound judgment under uncertainty

### Board-Level Evaluation
The board collectively assesses:

1. **Meeting effectiveness** — Are meetings well-structured, productive, and decision-oriented?
2. **Information quality** — Is the board receiving the right information, in the right format, at the right time?
3. **Strategic engagement** — Is the board genuinely shaping strategy, or merely ratifying management's proposals?
4. **Risk oversight** — Is the board identifying and addressing material risks proactively?
5. **CEO relationship** — Is the relationship with the CEO one of productive tension (not rubber-stamping or adversarial)?
6. **Board dynamics** — Is there trust, healthy conflict, commitment, accountability, and results focus?
7. **Composition** — Does the board have the right skills, diversity, and experience for the company's current needs?

### Chair/Lead Director Evaluation
1. **Agenda setting** — Focuses board time on the highest-priority issues
2. **Meeting management** — Ensures all voices are heard, discussions stay productive
3. **CEO coaching** — Provides effective feedback and support to the CEO
4. **Board cohesion** — Manages interpersonal dynamics constructively
5. **External representation** — Represents the board effectively to shareholders and stakeholders

## CEO Performance Evaluation

### Quantitative Assessment
- Financial performance vs. plan (revenue, profitability, cash flow)
- Operational metrics vs. targets
- Strategic milestones achieved
- Relative performance vs. peer companies

### Qualitative Assessment
- Strategy quality and execution
- Leadership team development
- Culture stewardship
- External stakeholder management
- Communication quality and transparency
- Personal development and growth

### Evaluation Process
1. Board agrees on evaluation criteria at the beginning of the year
2. CEO submits self-assessment mid-year and year-end
3. Board members submit independent evaluations
4. Board chair synthesizes and leads executive session discussion
5. Board chair delivers feedback to CEO with specific examples
6. Compensation committee links evaluation to compensation

## Committee Effectiveness Review

Each committee evaluates annually:
1. **Charter compliance** — Are we fulfilling our charter responsibilities?
2. **Meeting quality** — Are meetings productive and well-organized?
3. **Expertise** — Do committee members have the required expertise?
4. **Communication** — Is the committee effectively reporting to the full board?
5. **Value added** — Are committee recommendations improving board decisions?

## Action Planning

After each evaluation cycle:
1. Identify top 3 strengths to maintain
2. Identify top 3 areas for improvement
3. Assign specific action items with owners and deadlines
4. Review progress at the next evaluation cycle
5. Adjust board composition, meeting structure, or processes as needed
