# Corporate Responsibility — Pragmatic Guide

## Overview
Corporate responsibility exists on a spectrum from legal compliance to aspirational leadership. This guide takes a pragmatic approach: what should companies actually do, given real constraints on resources, attention, and competitive pressure? The answer lies between doing nothing and trying to solve every social problem.

## The Responsibility Stack

### Level 1: Legal Compliance (Non-Negotiable)
- Follow all applicable laws and regulations
- Pay taxes, maintain safety standards, respect labor laws
- Maintain honest accounting and transparent reporting
- **Board role:** Ensure compliance infrastructure exists and is funded

### Level 2: Ethical Operations (Expected)
- Treat employees fairly (beyond legal minimums)
- Honor commitments to customers, suppliers, and partners
- Avoid deceptive practices in marketing and sales
- Maintain data privacy and security
- **Board role:** Set ethical standards and hold management accountable

### Level 3: Stakeholder Consideration (Good Practice)
- Consider impact on all stakeholders, not just shareholders
- Invest in employee development and wellbeing
- Build sustainable supply chains
- Engage with community where you operate
- **Board role:** Include stakeholder impact in decision-making criteria

### Level 4: Industry Leadership (Aspirational)
- Set standards that raise the bar for the industry
- Use business model and influence to address systemic problems
- Transparent reporting on progress and shortcomings
- Advocate for policy changes that serve broader interests
- **Board role:** Support without overextending; ensure financial sustainability

## Common Responsibility Challenges

### "We Can't Afford It"
- Often true for Level 4, sometimes true for Level 3
- Usually not true for Level 2 — ethical operations are table stakes
- **Response:** Start with Level 2, build toward Level 3 as resources allow, aspire to Level 4 selectively

### "Our Competitors Don't Do This"
- Competitors' failures don't excuse yours
- However, unilateral action that creates significant competitive disadvantage is also irresponsible (to employees and shareholders)
- **Response:** Collaborate with competitors on industry-wide standards. Some problems require collective action.

### "This Isn't Our Core Business"
- You don't have to solve every problem, but you must address the problems your business creates
- **Response:** Focus responsibility efforts on areas where (1) your business has direct impact and (2) your capabilities can make a unique contribution

## Board Application
- Establish a clear responsibility policy that specifies the company's commitment level for each area
- Use the Responsibility Stack to prioritize: ensure Levels 1 and 2 before investing in Levels 3 and 4
- Require honest reporting on responsibility initiatives, including failures and limitations
- Evaluate responsibility as part of CEO performance review
