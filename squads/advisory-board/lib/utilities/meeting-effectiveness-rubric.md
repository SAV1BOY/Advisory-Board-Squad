# Meeting Effectiveness Rubric

## Purpose

A scoring rubric for evaluating the quality and productivity of board-level meetings. Meetings are the most expensive activity in an organization when measured by the hourly rate of participants multiplied by the number of attendees. This rubric ensures that investment produces returns in the form of decisions made, alignment achieved, and accountability created.

## How to Use

Complete this rubric in the final 5 minutes of every recurring board or leadership meeting. The scribe or facilitator scores each dimension based on the meeting just concluded. Monthly and quarterly aggregation reveals patterns and drives improvement. Feed results into `data/metrics/board-effectiveness-score.md`.

## Scale/Criteria

Score each dimension on a 1-5 scale:

### Dimension 1: Preparation (Weight: 20%)
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | All participants reviewed pre-reads. Discussion started at the analysis level, not the information level. Pre-reads were distributed 48+ hours in advance. |
| 4 | Most participants were prepared. Minimal time spent on information transfer. Pre-reads were timely. |
| 3 | Some participants were clearly unprepared. Moderate time spent bringing people up to speed. |
| 2 | Majority of participants had not reviewed materials. Meeting began with extensive information transfer. |
| 1 | No pre-reads were distributed, or they were sent too late. Meeting was entirely information transfer. |

### Dimension 2: Agenda Discipline (Weight: 15%)
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | Agenda was distributed in advance. Time boxes were respected. Every item had a clear type tag ([INFO]/[DISCUSS]/[DECIDE]) and desired outcome. Items finished on time or were explicitly deferred. |
| 4 | Agenda was distributed. Most time boxes were respected. Minor scope creep on one item. |
| 3 | Agenda existed but was loosely followed. One or more items ran significantly over time. Some items were skipped. |
| 2 | Agenda was informal or created at the start of the meeting. Discussion wandered. Time allocation was reactive. |
| 1 | No agenda. Meeting was a free-form discussion. Critical items were missed. |

### Dimension 3: Decision Throughput (Weight: 25%)
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | All [DECIDE] items on the agenda were resolved with clear, recorded decisions. Decision rationale and dissent were captured. No decisions were deferred without an explicit reason and rescheduled date. |
| 4 | Most [DECIDE] items were resolved. One item was deferred with a clear reschedule. Decisions were recorded. |
| 3 | Some [DECIDE] items were resolved but others were deferred or left ambiguous. Recording was incomplete. |
| 2 | Few decisions were made despite being on the agenda. Discussion dominated without resolution. |
| 1 | No decisions were made. The meeting produced discussion but no commitments. |

### Dimension 4: Participation Quality (Weight: 15%)
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | All voices contributed meaningfully. Dissent was expressed and welcomed. The facilitator actively drew out quieter participants. No single person dominated. |
| 4 | Most participants contributed. One or two were notably quiet but were given opportunity. Dissent was possible. |
| 3 | Three or fewer people dominated the discussion. Others were present but passive. Dissent was tolerated but not solicited. |
| 2 | The meeting was effectively a monologue with occasional questions. Some participants were disengaged (multitasking). |
| 1 | One person spoke for the majority of the meeting. Others were present in body only. No genuine exchange of ideas. |

### Dimension 5: Action Clarity (Weight: 15%)
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | Every discussion produced action items with single owners, specific deadlines, and clear success criteria. Actions were read back before meeting close. |
| 4 | Action items were captured with owners and deadlines. Minor gaps in specificity. |
| 3 | Some action items were captured but lacked owners or specific deadlines. "We should..." language without commitment. |
| 2 | Few action items were defined. Meeting ended with vague next steps. |
| 1 | No action items were captured. Meeting ended without clarity on what happens next. |

### Dimension 6: Time Respect (Weight: 10%)
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | Meeting started on time, ended on time or early. All participants were present at the start. Time was used efficiently with no unnecessary tangents. |
| 4 | Meeting started within 2 minutes of scheduled time and ended on time. Minor tangent quickly corrected. |
| 3 | Meeting started 5+ minutes late or ran 10+ minutes over. One significant tangent. |
| 2 | Meeting started significantly late or ran well over time. Multiple tangents. Participants left before the meeting ended. |
| 1 | Meeting ran more than 25% over scheduled time. Started late. Time was routinely wasted. |

## Scoring Guide

### Calculating the Overall Score
**Overall = (Preparation x 0.20) + (Agenda x 0.15) + (Decisions x 0.25) + (Participation x 0.15) + (Actions x 0.15) + (Time x 0.10)**

### Interpretation
| Score Range | Rating | Action |
|-------------|--------|--------|
| 4.5 - 5.0 | Exceptional | Maintain current practices. Share what works with other meetings. |
| 3.5 - 4.4 | Effective | Solid meetings. Target the weakest dimension for improvement. |
| 2.5 - 3.4 | Mediocre | Meetings are functional but wasteful. Structural changes needed — focus on the two weakest dimensions. |
| 1.5 - 2.4 | Poor | Meetings are destroying more value than they create. Redesign the meeting format from scratch. |
| 1.0 - 1.4 | Failing | Cancel the meeting. Replace with async updates until the meeting can be redesigned. |

### Monthly Review Process
1. Average all meeting scores from the month.
2. Identify the consistently lowest-scoring dimension.
3. Implement one specific improvement targeting that dimension.
4. Review the following month to measure improvement.
5. Track the trend line — effective meeting cultures improve 0.5-1.0 points per quarter when actively managed.

### Quick Post-Meeting Template
