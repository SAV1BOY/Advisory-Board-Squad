# Brene Brown Shame Triggers Checklist

## Purpose
Identifies and mitigates shame triggers in organizational culture. Brene Brown's research reveals that shame is a powerful but destructive emotion in workplaces: it drives people to hide mistakes, avoid risks, and disengage. Unlike guilt ("I did something bad"), shame tells people "I am bad." This checklist helps the organization recognize when its practices, language, and norms create shame rather than accountability.

## When to Use
- When diagnosing why people are hiding mistakes or avoiding accountability.
- When feedback processes feel punitive rather than constructive.
- When reviewing management practices for their emotional impact.
- When organizational trauma (layoffs, public failures, leadership changes) may have created shame.
- During culture assessments that probe beyond surface-level engagement.

## Gate Criteria

### Shame Awareness
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders understand the difference between shame (I am bad) and guilt (I did a bad thing).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization's language about failure focuses on behavior and decisions, not on person identity.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Common shame triggers in the workplace are identified: public humiliation, perfectionism demands, comparison.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The emotional impact of management practices is assessed, not just their operational intent.

### Cultural Practices
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Mistakes are treated as learning opportunities, not evidence of personal inadequacy.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Performance feedback is private and specific, not public and generalizing.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Success is celebrated without implicitly shaming those who did not achieve the same results.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Vulnerability (asking for help, admitting not knowing) is modeled by leaders.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Perfectionism is not the operating standard: good enough is recognized as appropriate in many contexts.

### Systemic Shame Detection
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization monitors for shame-driven behaviors: hiding, withdrawing, blaming, aggression.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Processes that inadvertently create shame (forced rankings, public scoreboards, blame-focused post-mortems) are reviewed and adjusted.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The language used in company communications is checked for shame-inducing patterns.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Exit interviews specifically probe for experiences of shame or humiliation.

### Resilience Building
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Employees are supported in building shame resilience: recognizing triggers and responding constructively.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders create conditions where people can recover from shame without lasting stigma.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization practices empathy when people are struggling, rather than pressure.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Stories of overcoming failure are shared as cultural narratives, normalizing imperfection.

## Red Flags
- People hide mistakes rather than reporting them early.
- The response to failure is to find someone to blame rather than something to fix.
- High performers burn out trying to maintain a facade of perfection.
- People do not ask questions in meetings because they fear looking incompetent.
- Language like "that was stupid" or "how could you" is used by managers.
- Internal competition creates a culture where someone's win requires someone's visible loss.

## Escalation Triggers
- A pattern of hidden mistakes leads to a material operational failure.
- Employee mental health data indicates widespread stress related to workplace culture.
- A leader's behavior is credibly reported as humiliating or shaming.
- Engagement surveys reveal that a significant number of employees do not feel safe being imperfect.
- The organization's culture of perfectionism is identified as a barrier to innovation or risk-taking.
