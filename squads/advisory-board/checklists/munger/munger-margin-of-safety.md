# Munger Margin of Safety Checklist

## Purpose
Applies the principle of margin of safety (borrowed from engineering and value investing) to strategic and operational decisions. A margin of safety means building enough buffer into plans, forecasts, and commitments that the outcome remains acceptable even when things go wrong. This checklist ensures the organization does not plan for the optimistic case and leave itself vulnerable to normal variation and predictable setbacks.

## When to Use
- When evaluating financial projections, budgets, and forecasts.
- Before making commitments to investors, customers, or partners.
- When assessing the robustness of operational plans.
- During capital allocation and investment decisions.
- When planning timelines for critical deliverables.

## Gate Criteria

### Financial Margin of Safety
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Revenue forecasts include a bear case that is genuinely pessimistic, not just base minus 5%.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Cash reserves or credit facilities exceed minimum requirements by at least 20%.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Unit economics remain viable under bear-case assumptions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Debt covenants have at least 25% headroom under current and projected performance.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The business can survive the loss of its largest customer or revenue source.

### Operational Margin of Safety
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Timelines include buffer for the most uncertain phases.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Team capacity plans allow for normal levels of attrition, illness, and competing priorities.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Technology infrastructure is designed for at least 2x current peak load.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Single points of failure in critical processes have been identified and redundancy added.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Supply chain dependencies have backup options for critical components.

### Strategic Margin of Safety
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The strategy does not require every assumption to be correct to succeed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The strategy works even if the timing is off by 6-12 months.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The company has optionality: the ability to change direction without catastrophic cost.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Key partnerships and customer relationships have contracts with reasonable terms and termination provisions.

### Commitment Margin of Safety
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Promises to investors, customers, and partners have built-in achievability buffers.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board-approved targets are stretch but achievable, not aspirational fantasies.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Public commitments (product launches, partnerships, financial targets) have been stress-tested.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team distinguishes between internal stretch targets and external commitments.

## Red Flags
- Plans assume best-case execution with no buffer for setbacks.
- The company is one missed quarter away from a covenant breach.
- Timelines are calculated assuming 100% productivity with zero interruptions.
- The company has made public commitments that require perfect execution to achieve.
- Cash reserves are at the minimum level with no contingency.
- The strategy requires multiple independent things to go right simultaneously.

## Escalation Triggers
- Actual performance erodes the margin of safety below acceptable thresholds.
- A commitment is made to external parties without adequate margin of safety.
- The organization discovers that its margin of safety was illusory (based on incorrect assumptions).
- A near-miss event reveals that the margin of safety was thinner than believed.
- The margin of safety has been deliberately reduced to meet short-term targets.
