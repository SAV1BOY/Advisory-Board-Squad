# Board Composition and Diversity

## The Skill Matrix Approach

### Core Competencies to Map
Every board needs coverage across these domains:
1. **Financial expertise** — Accounting, audit, capital markets, M&A
2. **Industry knowledge** — Deep understanding of the company's sector
3. **Technology** — Technical literacy relevant to the company's product
4. **Operations** — Scaling, supply chain, manufacturing, service delivery
5. **Sales and marketing** — Go-to-market, brand, customer acquisition
6. **Human capital** — Talent strategy, compensation, organizational design
7. **Legal and regulatory** — Governance, compliance, IP, regulatory landscape
8. **Strategy** — Strategic planning, competitive analysis, long-term vision
9. **International** — Global markets, cross-cultural management, geopolitics
10. **ESG** — Environmental, social, and governance expertise

### How to Use the Matrix
- Rate each board member as Expert (3), Competent (2), or Aware (1) for each domain
- Identify domains with no Expert coverage — these are critical gaps
- Prioritize gap-filling in the next board appointment
- Review annually as company needs evolve

## Diversity Dimensions

### Why Diversity Matters for Boards
- **Cognitive diversity** reduces groupthink and increases decision quality
- **Demographic diversity** provides broader stakeholder representation and market insight
- **Experiential diversity** brings different pattern recognition capabilities

### Dimensions to Consider
- **Gender** — Research consistently shows mixed-gender boards outperform all-male boards
- **Ethnicity and race** — Broader perspectives, better representation of diverse customer bases
- **Age** — Mix of experienced perspective and fresh viewpoint; avoid all-same-generation boards
- **Professional background** — Operators, investors, academics, entrepreneurs, public sector
- **Geography** — Especially important for companies with international operations or ambitions
- **Neurodiversity** — Different thinking styles (analytical, creative, intuitive, systematic)
- **Socioeconomic background** — Avoids the blind spots of a purely privileged perspective

### Common Pitfalls
- **Token appointments** — Adding one diverse member without creating genuine inclusion
- **Comfort bias** — Defaulting to "people like us" because they're easy to work with
- **Overweighting networks** — Recruiting only from personal networks perpetuates homogeneity
- **Conflating diversity with lowering standards** — Diverse candidates are not less qualified; the pipeline is smaller, requiring more active sourcing

## Board Refreshment

### Healthy Turnover
- Average board tenure should be 5-8 years per member
- Staggered terms prevent both entrenchment and mass turnover
- Mandatory retirement age is outdated; contribution assessment is better
- Annual contribution assessment: would you reappoint this member if the seat were open?

### Exit Conversations
- Have honest conversations with underperforming members before their term expires
- Offer face-saving exit paths (emeritus status, advisory role)
- Never let a seat go to someone just because they've always been there

## Board Size by Stage
| Stage | Recommended Size | Composition |
|-------|-----------------|-------------|
| Pre-seed/Seed | 3 | Founder, investor, independent |
| Series A | 5 | 2 founders/mgmt, 2 investors, 1 independent |
| Series B-C | 5-7 | 1-2 mgmt, 2-3 investors, 2-3 independents |
| Late stage | 7-9 | 1-2 mgmt, 2-3 investors, 4-5 independents |
| Public | 9-11 | 1-2 mgmt, 8-9 independents (majority independent) |
