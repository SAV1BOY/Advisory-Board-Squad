# Sivers Simplify Cutlist Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the organization regularly simplifies by cutting what is not essential. Sivers advocates for radical simplification: removing features, processes, meetings, and commitments that do not directly serve the core mission. Complexity is the enemy of execution, and it accumulates silently unless actively pruned. This checklist provides a framework for identifying and eliminating organizational bloat.

## When to Use
- Quarterly during operational reviews to identify removal candidates.
- When execution velocity is declining despite stable headcount.
- When new employees report confusion about which processes to follow.
- After growth phases that may have added complexity faster than value.
- When the organization feels sluggish or bureaucratic.

## Gate Criteria

### Process Audit
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Every recurring process has been reviewed in the last 12 months for continued necessity.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Processes that exist because of past problems that no longer occur are identified for removal.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The cost of each major process (time, tools, people) is estimated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Processes that can be simplified (fewer steps, fewer approvals) are identified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Duplicate processes across teams are consolidated.

### Meeting Audit
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Every recurring meeting has been reviewed for continued necessity and value.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Meetings with no clear outcome or that produce no decisions are candidates for elimination.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Meeting durations are reviewed: most meetings could be shorter.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Attendee lists are reviewed: only essential participants are included.

### Feature and Product Audit
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Products or features with low usage and high maintenance cost are identified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Sunsetting candidates are evaluated for removal, not just neglect.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The total cost of maintaining legacy products and features is quantified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization says no to feature requests that do not serve the core value proposition.

### Communication and Reporting Audit
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Reports and dashboards are reviewed: reports no one reads are eliminated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Communication channels are assessed: redundant channels are consolidated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Email lists and distribution groups are pruned.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The total number of tools and platforms is reviewed for consolidation opportunities.

## Red Flags
- The organization has never removed a process, meeting, or tool, only added them.
- New hire onboarding takes longer than 30 days because of process complexity.
- Teams spend more time on internal processes than on customer-facing work.
- Multiple tools exist for the same function with no plan to consolidate.
- Reports are generated but no one can describe who reads them or what decisions they inform.
- The response to every problem is to add a new process rather than fix the existing one.

## Escalation Triggers
- Execution velocity has declined by more than 20% without corresponding increase in complexity of work.
- Employee satisfaction surveys cite process overhead and bureaucracy as top concerns.
- Critical work is delayed because resources are consumed by low-value process compliance.
- The organization cannot onboard new tools or processes because capacity is consumed by existing ones.
- A simplification initiative is proposed but meets resistance from process owners who are invested in complexity.
