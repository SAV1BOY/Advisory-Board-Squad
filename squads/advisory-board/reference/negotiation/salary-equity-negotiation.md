# Salary and Equity Negotiation

## Overview
Executive compensation negotiation is one of the board's most consequential and delicate responsibilities. This file provides frameworks for structuring, evaluating, and negotiating compensation packages.

## Compensation Components

### Base Salary
- Fixed cash compensation for the role
- **Benchmarking:** Use market data from compensation surveys (Radford, Carta, Levels.fyi) for comparable roles at comparable stage/size companies
- **Negotiation range:** Typically 10-20% flexibility around the market median

### Annual Bonus / Variable Cash
- Performance-based cash compensation, typically 20-50% of base for executives
- **Structure:** Tied to company metrics (revenue, profitability) and individual objectives
- **Board governance:** Approve bonus criteria at the beginning of the year, evaluate objectively at year end

### Equity (Options and RSUs)
- **Stock options:** Right to buy shares at a fixed price (strike price). Value comes from appreciation above the strike.
- **RSUs (Restricted Stock Units):** Shares granted that vest over time. Value is the full share price.
- **Vesting:** Standard is 4-year vesting with 1-year cliff. Some companies use 3-year or back-loaded schedules.
- **Refresh grants:** Annual equity grants to prevent the "golden handcuffs wearing off" as initial grants vest

### Severance and Change of Control
- **Severance:** Cash and benefits continuation if terminated without cause. Typically 3-12 months.
- **Change of control:** Acceleration of equity vesting if the company is acquired. Can be single-trigger (accelerates on acquisition) or double-trigger (requires both acquisition AND termination).
- **Board governance:** Double-trigger is preferred. Single-trigger can create perverse incentives.

## Negotiation Principles

### For the Board (Hiring Executive)
- Know the market rate before the conversation begins
- Lead with the total compensation philosophy, not individual numbers
- Be transparent about the company's compensation philosophy and constraints
- Understand the candidate's priorities: some value cash, others equity, others flexibility
- Use the total package, not individual components, as the negotiation unit

### For the Board (Advising Founders)
- Founders at seed/Series A should take below-market salary to preserve cash and signal commitment
- Founder equity should vest (not all upfront) to protect all shareholders
- As the company scales, founder compensation should increase toward market to prevent burnout and resentment
- Founder compensation is a board decision, not a founder decision

## Equity Allocation Guidelines

| Role | Seed Stage | Series A | Series B+ |
|------|-----------|----------|-----------|
| CEO (non-founder) | 5-10% | 3-5% | 1-3% |
| CTO/CPO | 2-5% | 1-3% | 0.5-1.5% |
| VP-level | 0.5-2% | 0.25-1% | 0.1-0.5% |
| Director | 0.1-0.5% | 0.05-0.25% | 0.02-0.1% |
| Senior IC | 0.05-0.2% | 0.02-0.1% | 0.01-0.05% |

*Percentages are of fully-diluted shares. Actual grants vary by company, market, and individual.*

## Board Application
- Establish a compensation philosophy document that guides all compensation decisions
- Use market data, not gut feel, for compensation benchmarking
- Review executive compensation annually against market and performance
- Ensure equity grants are properly documented and approved by the board
- Model dilution impact of equity grants over multiple years
