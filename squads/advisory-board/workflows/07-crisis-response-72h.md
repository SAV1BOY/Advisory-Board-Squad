# 07 — Crisis Response 72-Hour Protocol

## Overview

A rapid-response protocol for material crises: security breaches, leadership scandals, regulatory actions, product failures, PR disasters, or financial shocks. The first 72 hours determine whether a crisis is contained or becomes existential. Every hour matters.

## Trigger

- Security breach or data loss affecting customers.
- Regulatory investigation or enforcement action.
- Executive misconduct allegation.
- Product failure causing customer harm.
- Major media or social media escalation.
- Financial shock (fraud discovery, covenant breach, customer loss >20 % of revenue).
- Any event the Board-Chair classifies as a material crisis.

## Agents

| Agent | Role |
|---|---|
| Board-Chair | Activates the protocol, leads board-level decision-making |
| Crisis-Commander | Operational lead for the first 72 hours (usually CEO unless CEO is the crisis) |
| Risk-Analyst | Assesses blast radius, legal exposure, financial impact |
| Governance-Ops | Communication logistics, stakeholder tracking, documentation |
| Devil's-Advocate | Challenges containment assumptions, identifies hidden exposures |

## Phases

### Phase 0 — Activation (Hour 0-1)

1. Whoever discovers the crisis notifies Board-Chair and CEO immediately. No waiting for "more information."
2. Board-Chair makes the activation decision within 30 minutes. Criteria: Is this a material threat to the company's operations, reputation, or legal standing?
3. If activated, Board-Chair designates the Crisis-Commander (CEO by default; alternate if CEO is compromised).
4. Crisis-Commander assembles the crisis team: relevant functional leads, legal counsel, communications lead.
5. Governance-Ops opens the crisis log: timestamped record of every decision, communication, and discovery from this point forward.
6. Board-Chair notifies all board members that the crisis protocol is active. Brief facts only. No speculation.

### Phase 1 — Containment (Hours 1-12)

1. Crisis-Commander's first priority: stop the bleeding. What actions can be taken right now to prevent the crisis from getting worse?
   - If security breach: isolate affected systems, preserve evidence, engage incident response.
   - If regulatory: engage legal counsel, preserve documents, issue a litigation hold.
   - If executive misconduct: place individual on leave, secure communications, engage outside counsel.
   - If product failure: issue safety notice, halt affected operations, begin root cause investigation.
2. Risk-Analyst produces a rapid assessment (2-page max, delivered within 6 hours):
   - What happened (known facts only, clearly separated from speculation).
   - Blast radius: who is affected (customers, employees, partners, regulators)?
   - Legal exposure: potential liability, regulatory consequences.
   - Financial impact: immediate and projected.
   - What we don't know yet (explicit gaps).
3. Devil's-Advocate asks: "What is the worst-case scenario we are not considering?"
4. Crisis-Commander briefs the Board-Chair at Hour 6 and Hour 12.

### Phase 2 — Communication (Hours 6-24)

1. Determine who must be notified and by when:
   - Legal and regulatory obligations (breach notification laws, SEC disclosure, etc.).
   - Contractual obligations to customers and partners.
   - Employee communication.
   - Media and public communication.
2. Draft holding statements for each audience. Principles:
   - Acknowledge what is known. Do not speculate.
   - State what you are doing about it.
   - Commit to follow-up timing.
   - Express genuine concern for those affected.
   - Do not minimize, blame, or deflect.
3. Board-Chair reviews all external communications before release.
4. Legal counsel reviews for regulatory compliance and liability exposure.
5. Release communications in priority order: affected parties first, then regulators, then public.
6. Governance-Ops tracks all communications: who was told what, when, by whom.

### Phase 3 — Action & Stabilization (Hours 12-48)

1. Crisis-Commander executes the containment plan. Hourly status updates to the crisis log.
2. Risk-Analyst updates the rapid assessment as new facts emerge. Clearly flag what has changed.
3. Board-Chair convenes an emergency board session (virtual, 60 minutes):
   - Crisis-Commander presents: facts, containment status, communication status, open questions.
   - Board discussion: are we doing enough? What are we missing?
   - Decisions: additional resources, external advisors, legal strategy, communication escalation.
4. If the crisis involves potential legal liability, engage outside counsel and establish privilege protocols.
5. Begin the root cause investigation. Separate the investigation from the response — different people, different timeline.
6. Crisis-Commander briefs the Board-Chair every 12 hours. Board-Chair briefs the full board every 24 hours.

### Phase 4 — Resolution & Retro (Hours 48-72 and beyond)

1. At Hour 48, Crisis-Commander assesses: is the crisis contained? Is it escalating? Is it stable?
2. If contained: transition from crisis mode to recovery mode. Define the recovery plan with milestones.
3. If still active: extend the protocol. Re-assess at 24-hour intervals.
4. At Hour 72 (or when contained), Board-Chair schedules a formal retrospective:
   - Timeline reconstruction: what happened, when, what was done.
   - Decision review: which decisions were right? Which would we change?
   - Communication assessment: was communication timely, accurate, and appropriate?
   - Gap identification: what processes, systems, or capabilities failed?
   - Recommendations: specific changes to prevent recurrence or improve response.
5. Retrospective findings feed into the risk register (workflow 16) and post-mortem workflow (19).
6. Archive the complete crisis package: log, assessments, communications, decisions, retrospective.

## Quality Gates

| Gate | Criteria | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| G0 — Activation | Crisis protocol activated within 30 minutes of discovery | Board-Chair |
| G1 — Rapid assessment | 2-page assessment delivered within 6 hours | Risk-Analyst |
| G2 — Communications reviewed | All external communications approved by Board-Chair and legal | Board-Chair |
| G3 — Board briefed | Emergency board session within 48 hours | Board-Chair |
| G4 — Crisis log complete | Timestamped log of every decision, communication, and discovery | Governance-Ops |
| G5 — Retrospective done | Formal retrospective completed within 2 weeks of containment | Board-Chair |

## Outputs

- Crisis log (timestamped, comprehensive).
- Rapid assessment (updated as facts evolve).
- Stakeholder communication log.
- Emergency board session minutes.
- Root cause investigation report (separate timeline).
- Retrospective with recommendations.
- Risk register updates.

## Timeline

| Milestone | Hour |
|---|---|
| Activation decision | Hour 0-1 |
| Crisis team assembled | Hour 1-2 |
| Rapid assessment v1 | Hour 6 |
| First external communication | Hour 12-24 |
| Emergency board session | Hour 24-48 |
| Containment assessment | Hour 48 |
| Retrospective scheduled | Hour 72 |
| Retrospective complete | Week 2-3 post-containment |

## Metrics

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Time to activation | ≤30 minutes from discovery |
| Time to rapid assessment | ≤6 hours |
| Time to first external communication | ≤24 hours (or per legal requirement, whichever is sooner) |
| Board briefing frequency | Every 24 hours during active crisis |
| Crisis log completeness | Every decision and communication timestamped |
| Retrospective completion | Within 2 weeks of containment |

## Common Failures

| Failure Mode | Symptom | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Delayed activation | "Let's wait for more information" while the crisis spreads | Board-Chair has unilateral activation authority; default to activate |
| Information hoarding | Key facts trapped in one person's head | Crisis log is mandatory; Governance-Ops enforces documentation |
| Communication paralysis | Legal says "say nothing" while stakeholders lose trust | Holding statements acknowledge the situation without admitting liability; silence is never the right strategy |
| Hero syndrome | One person tries to handle everything | Crisis-Commander delegates explicitly; Board-Chair monitors for burnout |
| Premature all-clear | Declaring the crisis over before root cause is found | Containment ≠ resolution; root cause investigation runs on a separate track |
| Missing the retro | Everyone is relieved and wants to move on | Board-Chair schedules the retrospective before the crisis ends; it is mandatory |
