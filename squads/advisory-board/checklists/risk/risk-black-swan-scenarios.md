# Risk Black Swan Scenarios Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the organization considers and prepares for low-probability, high-impact events that fall outside normal risk management. Black swans cannot be predicted, but organizations can build resilience by considering extreme scenarios, maintaining buffers, and developing response capabilities for the unexpected.

## When to Use
- Annually during strategic risk reviews.
- When the external environment is unusually uncertain.
- When building the organization's crisis response capabilities.
- During scenario planning exercises.

## Gate Criteria
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: At least five extreme scenarios have been identified across different risk categories.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Scenarios include both external (pandemic, market crash, regulatory upheaval) and internal (fraud, key person event, technology failure) events.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: For each scenario, the impact on the business is assessed (revenue, operations, reputation, survival).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: General resilience measures are in place: cash reserves, insurance, diversification, redundancy.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization has a crisis response framework that can adapt to unforeseen events.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Stress tests are conducted to evaluate the organization's ability to survive extreme scenarios.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization avoids fragility: it does not make bets that assume extreme events will not happen.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Post-event recovery plans exist for the most impactful scenarios.

## Red Flags
- Black swan scenarios have never been discussed by the leadership team.
- The organization's risk management considers only normal-range outcomes.
- Cash reserves and insurance are calibrated for normal operations, not stress scenarios.
- The organization has no crisis response capability.
- The company has made irreversible commitments that assume stable conditions.

## Escalation Triggers
- An event occurs that was not on any scenario list, revealing a gap in imagination.
- The organization discovers it is more fragile than believed (closer to failure under stress).
- Multiple risk factors are moving adversely simultaneously, suggesting a potential cascade.
- External experts warn about emerging risks the organization has not considered.
