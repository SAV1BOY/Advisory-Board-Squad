# Naval Compounding Plan Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the organization is building assets, capabilities, and relationships that compound over time rather than pursuing one-time gains. Naval emphasizes that wealth is created through compounding: each year's effort builds on the last. This checklist verifies that the organization's strategy, investments, and daily operations are oriented toward compounding rather than linear growth.

## When to Use
- During annual and multi-year strategic planning.
- When evaluating where to invest time, capital, and attention.
- When assessing whether current activities build lasting value or are consumable.
- When comparing strategic options with different compounding profiles.

## Gate Criteria

### Asset Building
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization is building durable assets (technology, brand, data, relationships, knowledge) that increase in value over time.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The balance between exploiting current assets and building new ones is intentional.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Assets are assessed for their compounding potential, not just their current value.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization distinguishes between consumable activities (one-time value) and compounding activities (growing value).

### Relationship Compounding
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer relationships deepen over time through increasing value delivery.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Partnership relationships accumulate shared knowledge and mutual benefit.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Employee tenure creates compounding institutional knowledge, not just increasing cost.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Reputation compounds through consistent delivery and ethical behavior.

### Knowledge Compounding
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Organizational learning is captured in systems, not just in individuals' heads.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each project and initiative generates reusable knowledge or capabilities.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization's data asset grows and becomes more valuable over time.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Innovation builds on prior work rather than starting from scratch each time.

### Compounding Protection
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Actions that interrupt compounding (unnecessary pivots, talent exodus, reputation damage) are guarded against.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Short-term pressures do not cause the sacrifice of long-term compounding assets.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization resists the temptation to harvest compounding assets prematurely.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Compounding metrics (LTV trends, retention curves, knowledge asset growth) are tracked.

## Red Flags
- Every quarter starts from zero with no carry-over from previous work.
- Customer relationships are transactional with no deepening engagement.
- Employee turnover is so high that institutional knowledge cannot accumulate.
- The organization frequently pivots, resetting its compounding clock.
- Short-term revenue tactics (heavy discounting, one-time deals) dominate the sales strategy.
- Technology is rebuilt rather than maintained and extended.

## Escalation Triggers
- Key compounding assets (brand, data, technology) are deteriorating.
- The organization's competitive advantage is eroding because compounding has stalled.
- Short-term decisions repeatedly sacrifice long-term compounding potential.
- Customer or employee retention declines, indicating the compounding relationship is weakening.
- The organization cannot point to any asset that is more valuable today than it was a year ago.
