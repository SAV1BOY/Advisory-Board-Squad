# Task: Conflict Resolution

## Objective
Resolve significant interpersonal or inter-functional conflicts at the executive level that are impacting organizational performance. Board involvement when conflicts cannot be resolved at the management level.

## Trigger
- CEO escalates an executive conflict that is affecting organizational performance
- Board member observes dysfunction in executive team interactions
- Multiple team members report cross-functional breakdown traced to executive conflict
- Conflict between CEO and a board member

## Agents Involved
- **Lead:** Board Chair for CEO-involved conflicts, CEO for executive team conflicts
- **Support:** CHRO for process facilitation, external mediator if needed
- **Advisory:** Board members for perspective and governance

## Pre-Requisites
- [ ] Conflict identified and documented with specific observable impacts
- [ ] Both parties have been heard individually
- [ ] Impact on the organization is assessed
- [ ] Decision made on whether to resolve internally or engage external help

## Steps

1. **Step 1:** Assess the conflict scope and impact by documenting specific organizational consequences

2. **Step 2:** Conduct individual conversations with each party to understand perspectives and interests

3. **Step 3:** Identify root causes distinguishing between personal style differences, structural misalignment, resource competition, and values conflicts

4. **Step 4:** Develop resolution options ranging from facilitated dialogue to role restructuring to separation

5. **Step 5:** Facilitate resolution conversation with clear ground rules and desired outcomes

6. **Step 6:** Document agreements and commitments with specific behavioral expectations

7. **Step 7:** Establish monitoring plan to ensure resolution holds and organizational healing occurs

8. **Step 8:** Conflict assessment document; Resolution agreement; Behavioral commitments; Monitoring plan

## Frameworks to Apply
- **Structured Assessment:** Use consistent frameworks and rubrics to reduce bias and increase rigor
- **Data-Driven People Decisions:** Ground all people decisions in observable evidence, not gut feeling
- **Stakeholder Triangulation:** Gather input from multiple perspectives (manager, peers, reports, cross-functional) for balanced assessment

## Checklists

### Quality Gate
- [ ] Assessment is grounded in specific, observable evidence
- [ ] Multiple perspectives gathered (not single-source input)
- [ ] Outcomes and recommendations are specific and actionable
- [ ] Confidentiality maintained throughout the process
- [ ] Legal and compliance requirements met
- [ ] Timeline and owners assigned for all actions

## Output
- Structural issues feed into org-design-review
- Performance issues feed into performance-review-exec
- Cultural concerns feed into culture-audit
- Unresolvable conflicts may trigger succession-planning
