# Munger Checklist for Rational Thinking

## Purpose

Charlie Munger advocates using checklists to systematically avoid the most common thinking errors that afflict even intelligent, experienced decision-makers. Pilots use checklists not because they are incompetent but because the consequences of missing a step are catastrophic. Similarly, this checklist ensures that critical thinking steps are not skipped under time pressure, emotional stress, or overconfidence. The checklist is not a substitute for judgment — it is a scaffold that ensures judgment is applied comprehensively.

## When to Use

- Before making any significant, irreversible decision
- When reviewing someone else's analysis or recommendation
- During board meetings when evaluating strategic proposals
- When your gut says "yes" too quickly or "no" too reflexively
- As a training tool for developing analytical rigor in junior team members
- When the stakes are high enough that a systematic error would be costly

## Core Model

### The Munger Rational Thinking Checklist

**1. Invert, always invert.**
- Have I asked "How could this fail?" not just "How does this succeed?"
- What would I have to believe for this to be wrong?

**2. Check the incentives.**
- Who benefits from this recommendation? How do their incentives shape their analysis?
- What are my own incentives? Am I being objective?

**3. Consider the base rate.**
- What is the historical rate of success for this type of initiative?
- Am I adjusting too far from the base rate based on optimistic assumptions?

**4. Apply multiple mental models.**
- Have I looked at this through at least three different disciplinary lenses?
- Where do the models agree? Where do they disagree?

**5. Seek disconfirming evidence.**
- Have I actively looked for evidence that contradicts my hypothesis?
- What would the smartest person who disagrees with me say?

**6. Check for cognitive biases.**
- Am I anchored on an initial number or frame?
- Am I influenced by recent or vivid examples (availability bias)?
- Am I seeking information that confirms what I already believe?
- Am I avoiding this decision because of loss aversion?

**7. Consider second and third-order effects.**
- What happens next? And after that?
- Who else is affected, and how will they respond?

**8. Assess the opportunity cost.**
- What is the best alternative use of these resources?
- Is this the BEST option, not just a GOOD option?

**9. Verify my circle of competence.**
- Am I Inside my circle on this topic?
- If at the Edge or Outside, have I consulted someone who is Inside?

**10. Test for reversibility.**
- Is this a one-way or two-way door?
- Is the level of analysis proportional to the reversibility?

## Steps

1. **Print or bookmark this checklist.** Make it physically or digitally accessible during every decision-making session.

2. **Before making a decision, run through each item.** Spend 2-3 minutes per item. Total time: 20-30 minutes for a thorough pass.

3. **For each item, write a one-sentence response.** Writing forces specificity. "Yes, I checked" is not sufficient. "I checked the base rate and found that 70% of similar expansions fail within 2 years" is sufficient.

4. **Flag any items where the answer is concerning.** If the base rate is unfavorable, or the incentives are misaligned, or you're outside your competence — these are not automatic deal-breakers, but they require explicit mitigation.

5. **Share the completed checklist with a trusted peer.** A second set of eyes catches what you missed. The checklist is most powerful as a shared tool, not a solo exercise.

6. **After the decision, archive the checklist.** Compare the completed checklist to the eventual outcome. Over time, you'll learn which checklist items are most predictive for your decision domain.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Run this checklist on every major recommendation before presenting it. It takes 30 minutes and prevents the most embarrassing analytical errors.
- Use the checklist collaboratively during board discussions. Assign different checklist items to different board members for parallel review.
- When a founder presents a proposal that hasn't been through a rigorous thinking process, share this checklist as a constructive tool, not a criticism.

### For Founders
- Keep the checklist on your desk. Before signing any contract, approving any major hire, or committing to any strategy, scan it.
- Train your leadership team to use the checklist. Decision quality compounds across the organization.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Treating it as a formality.** Rushing through the checklist without genuine reflection defeats the purpose. Each item should prompt genuine analysis.

2. **Skipping items that feel irrelevant.** Items that feel irrelevant often reveal the biggest blind spots. Complete the full checklist.

3. **Using it only for big decisions.** While the full checklist is for major decisions, abbreviated versions (top 5 items) should be applied to medium-stakes decisions too.

4. **Solo use only.** The checklist is most powerful when shared. A second person catches biases you cannot see in yourself.

5. **Not updating based on experience.** Over time, add items that are specific to your domain or your personal thinking weaknesses.
