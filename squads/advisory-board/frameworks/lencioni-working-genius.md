# Lencioni Working Genius

## Purpose

Patrick Lencioni's Working Genius model identifies six fundamental types of work required for any initiative to succeed — and maps individuals to the types they are naturally gifted at (Working Genius), competent at (Working Competency), and frustrated by (Working Frustration). When people work in their genius zones, they are energized and productive. When forced to work in frustration zones, they burn out. This framework helps leaders assign work, build teams, and structure processes so that every type of work is covered by someone who is energized by it.

## When to Use

- When team members are burning out despite reasonable workloads (they may be in their frustration zone)
- When building teams and need complementary skill profiles
- When a project is stalling and you can't figure out why (a genius type may be missing)
- When restructuring roles to optimize energy and effectiveness
- When coaching individuals on career development and role design

## Core Model

### The Six Types of Working Genius (in order of workflow)

**W — Wonder:** The genius of pondering. Naturally curious about the way things are and why. Asks "why?" and "what if?" Essential for spotting opportunities and problems.

**I — Invention:** The genius of creating solutions. Naturally drawn to generating novel ideas, solutions, and approaches. Essential for innovation.

**D — Discernment:** The genius of instinct and judgment. Naturally able to evaluate ideas, curate options, and provide feedback. Essential for quality.

**G — Galvanizing:** The genius of rallying people. Naturally able to inspire and mobilize others around an idea or initiative. Essential for activation.

**E — Enablement:** The genius of support. Naturally willing to provide whatever help is needed to bring an initiative to fruition. Essential for execution.

**T — Tenacity:** The genius of finishing. Naturally driven to ensure results are achieved, standards are met, and projects are completed. Essential for delivery.

### The Work Flow
Every successful initiative requires all six in sequence: Wonder → Invention → Discernment → Galvanizing → Enablement → Tenacity. If any type is missing or covered by someone in their frustration zone, the initiative suffers.

### Genius, Competency, Frustration
- **Genius (2 types):** Energizes you. You are naturally gifted and lose track of time doing it.
- **Competency (2 types):** You can do it adequately but it neither energizes nor drains you.
- **Frustration (2 types):** Drains you. You can do it but it leaves you exhausted and dissatisfied.

## Steps

1. **Assess each team member's genius profile.** Take the Working Genius assessment (or have honest conversations about what energizes vs. drains each person).
2. **Map the team's coverage.** Are all six types covered by someone with that type as a genius? Where are the gaps?
3. **Redesign roles.** Where possible, shift responsibilities so people spend more time in their genius zones.
4. **Fill gaps through hiring.** If a genius type is missing from the team, prioritize it in the next hire.
5. **Structure projects to include all six.** Every project plan should identify who covers each genius type.
6. **Give people permission to opt out of frustration work.** When someone is in their frustration zone, find someone whose genius it is instead.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Help founding teams identify their collective genius profile. Many founding teams are heavy on Invention and Wonder but light on Tenacity and Enablement.
- When a company is great at generating ideas but poor at executing them, the issue may be a missing genius type, not a lack of discipline.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Forcing people into frustration zones.** This is the primary cause of disengagement and burnout.
2. **Hiring clones.** Teams of people with the same genius types leave critical work uncovered.
3. **Treating it as fixed skill.** Genius types are stable over time; this is not something that changes with training.
4. **Ignoring the workflow sequence.** Skipping Wonder and jumping to Invention produces solutions to the wrong problems.
