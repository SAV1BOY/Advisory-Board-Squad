# The Five Dysfunctions of a Team — Patrick Lencioni

## Summary

Presented as a leadership fable, Lencioni identifies five interrelated dysfunctions that undermine team performance, arranged in a pyramid where each builds on the one below. The foundation is absence of trust (unwillingness to be vulnerable), which leads to fear of conflict, which causes lack of commitment, which enables avoidance of accountability, which results in inattention to results. The model is deceptively simple but ruthlessly accurate. Every dysfunctional team Lencioni has observed fails at one or more of these levels, and the dysfunction always cascades upward.

## Key Principles

- **Trust is the foundation** — Not predictive trust ("I trust you'll do X") but vulnerability-based trust ("I can show weakness and you won't exploit it"). Without this, everything else is theater.
- **Conflict is necessary** — Teams that avoid conflict produce artificial harmony. Healthy conflict is passionate, unfiltered debate about ideas—not personal attacks.
- **Commitment requires clarity and buy-in** — Teams that don't debate don't commit. Silence is not agreement. Consensus is not required, but everyone must be heard before deciding.
- **Accountability is peer-to-peer** — The most effective accountability comes from teammates, not the boss. Reluctance to call out peers on performance or behavior is a sign of dysfunction.
- **Results must be collective** — When individuals prioritize their own status, ego, or department over team results, the team fails. Shared goals must override individual incentives.

## Frameworks Extracted

1. **The Five Dysfunctions Pyramid** — (bottom to top) Absence of Trust → Fear of Conflict → Lack of Commitment → Avoidance of Accountability → Inattention to Results. Diagnose from the bottom up; fix from the bottom up.
2. **The Personal Histories Exercise** — Team members share backgrounds, formative experiences, and challenges. This low-risk vulnerability exercise accelerates trust-building.
3. **The Commitment Clarity Check** — At the end of every meeting, each member states in their own words what was decided and what they will do. Misalignment surfaces immediately.
4. **The Team Effectiveness Exercise** — Each member identifies the single most important contribution each other member makes and the one behavior they should improve. Forces peer accountability.
5. **The Scoreboard** — Create a visible, shared metric that the entire team owns. Individual metrics still exist but are subordinate to the team scoreboard.

## Application to Board Decisions

- **Board team health** — Apply the Five Dysfunctions directly to the board. Most boards suffer from fear of conflict (they defer to the CEO or lead director) and avoidance of accountability (they don't challenge each other).
- **Executive team assessment** — Use the pyramid to diagnose the leadership team. If the C-suite can't debate honestly, strategic commitment will be shallow and execution will drift.
- **Meeting design** — Structure board meetings to require real debate on at least one controversial topic. Artificial harmony in board meetings is a red flag, not a sign of alignment.
- **Accountability mechanisms** — Boards should hold each other accountable for preparation, attendance, and follow-through. Peer accountability on the board signals the standard for the entire organization.
- **Results focus** — Define 2-3 board-level results that all members own collectively. If every board member only cares about their own committee, the board is dysfunctional at the top of the pyramid.
