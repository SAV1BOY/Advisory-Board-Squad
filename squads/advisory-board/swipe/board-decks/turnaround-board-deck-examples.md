# Turnaround Board Deck Examples

## Overview
Turnaround board decks serve companies in distress or significant underperformance. The deck must confront reality without sugarcoating, present a credible recovery plan, and establish the cadence of accountability required to execute under pressure. Boards receiving turnaround decks should expect higher meeting frequency (monthly or biweekly) and more granular operational data.

## Recommended Structure (15-20 slides)

### Section 1: Situation Assessment (3-4 slides)
- **Candid state of the business:** What is broken and why. No euphemisms. "Revenue declined 30% YoY" not "we face headwinds."
- **Root cause analysis:** Distinguish between symptoms and causes. Was it market, execution, product, leadership, or capital structure?
- **Cash position and runway:** Current cash, weekly burn rate, weeks of runway under current trajectory
- **Stakeholder impact map:** Who is affected (employees, customers, investors, creditors) and how

### Section 2: Financial Triage (3-4 slides)
- **13-week cash flow forecast:** Weekly granularity with assumptions clearly stated
- **Cost structure analysis:** Fixed vs. variable, essential vs. discretionary, contractual obligations
- **Revenue at risk:** Customers likely to churn, contracts expiring, pipeline reliability assessment
- **Debt and obligations:** Maturity schedule, covenant status, renegotiation options
- **Breakeven analysis:** What revenue level sustains the business at reduced cost structure?

### Section 3: Turnaround Plan (4-5 slides)
- **Phase 1 — Stabilize (0-90 days):** Stop the bleeding. Cash conservation measures, headcount actions, contract renegotiations, customer retention campaign
- **Phase 2 — Restructure (90-180 days):** Reorganize for the go-forward business. Exit unprofitable segments, renegotiate vendor contracts, right-size the team
- **Phase 3 — Rebuild (180-365 days):** Invest selectively in growth. Relaunch product, re-engage customers, rebuild pipeline
- **Key assumptions:** What must be true for each phase to succeed?
- **Resource requirements:** Additional capital needed, if any, and sources (bridge round, debt facility, asset sales)

### Section 4: Leadership and Organization (2-3 slides)
- **Leadership assessment:** Who stays, who goes, what roles need to be filled
- **Turnaround team:** Dedicated resources assigned to execution, with clear accountability
- **Communication plan:** How employees, customers, and investors will be informed and engaged
- **Morale and retention:** Plan to retain critical talent through the turnaround period

### Section 5: Milestones and Accountability (2-3 slides)
- **30/60/90 day milestones:** Specific, measurable, binary (achieved or not)
- **Weekly reporting cadence:** Key metrics the board will receive between meetings
- **Decision rights:** What the CEO can decide unilaterally vs. what requires board approval during the turnaround
- **Trigger points:** If milestone X is missed by Y date, what is the escalation path?

### Section 6: Scenarios and Contingencies (1-2 slides)
- **Base case:** Turnaround plan works, company stabilizes at reduced scale
- **Downside case:** Plan partially works, additional capital or restructuring required
- **Wind-down case:** Plan fails, what does an orderly wind-down or sale process look like?
- **Each scenario includes:** Timeline, capital requirements, stakeholder impact, board actions required

## Turnaround Deck Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails |
|-------------|-------------|
| "Hockey stick" recovery projections | Destroys credibility; boards have seen this before |
| Blaming external factors exclusively | Signals leadership is not taking accountability |
| Cutting R&D to zero | Saves cash short-term, kills the future |
| Hiding bad news in appendix slides | Board members notice. It destroys trust irreparably. |
| Presenting without a cash flow forecast | Cash is the only metric that matters in a turnaround |
| No contingency plan | Signals the team has not considered failure scenarios |

## Board Application
- Increase meeting frequency to monthly or biweekly until stability is achieved
- Require a 13-week cash flow forecast updated weekly and distributed to all board members
- Establish clear decision rights: define the dollar threshold above which CEO needs board approval
- Appoint a board-level turnaround committee with operating experience
- Insist on written milestones with binary pass/fail criteria — avoid subjective progress reports
- Consider whether the current CEO is the right leader for the turnaround (different skill set than growth)
- Document all decisions meticulously — turnaround situations create legal exposure for directors
