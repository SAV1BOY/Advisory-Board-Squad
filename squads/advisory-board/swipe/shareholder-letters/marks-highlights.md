# Howard Marks Oaktree Memos Highlights

## Overview

Howard Marks' investor memos are widely regarded as masterclasses in risk thinking, market psychology, and contrarian investing. Published since 1990, they offer a framework for decision-making under uncertainty that extends far beyond financial markets.

## Core Philosophical Pillars

### Second-Level Thinking
- First-level thinking: "This is a good company, let's buy the stock"
- Second-level thinking: "This is a good company, but everyone thinks it's great, it's overpriced, let's sell"
- The key question is never "is this good?" but "is this better or worse than the market expects?"

### The Pendulum of Market Psychology
- Markets swing between euphoria and panic, rarely resting at equilibrium
- The advisor's job is recognizing where the pendulum currently sits
- "We never know where we're going, but we ought to know where we are"

### Risk Is Not Volatility
- Risk is the probability of permanent capital loss, not price fluctuation
- The riskiest moment is when everyone believes risk has been eliminated
- "Risk means more things can happen than will happen"

## Essential Memos

### "The Most Important Thing" (Distilled)
- Identifies 20 "most important things" in investing, all interconnected
- Emphasizes that there is no single formula — success requires holding multiple truths simultaneously
- Superior results come from superior insight, not superior information

### "Dare to Be Great" (2014)
- Conventional behavior yields conventional results
- Unconventional behavior requires accepting periods of looking wrong
- The courage to be contrarian must be paired with the judgment to be right

### "There They Go Again... Again" (2017)
- Pattern recognition in market excess and bubble formation
- Checklist of warning signs: "too much money chasing too few deals"
- Historical parallels that repeat with different surface details but identical psychology

### "Coming Into Focus" (2020)
- Real-time memo during COVID crash demonstrating how to think through unprecedented events
- Acknowledged uncertainty while still providing actionable frameworks
- "You can't predict, but you can prepare"

## Marks' Decision-Making Framework

1. **Assess the current environment** — Where are we in the cycle?
2. **Identify the consensus** — What does everyone believe?
3. **Examine the consensus for flaws** — Where might the crowd be wrong?
4. **Evaluate asymmetry** — Is the upside/downside skewed in our favor?
5. **Accept uncertainty** — Make probabilistic bets, not predictions
6. **Maintain discipline** — Process over outcomes in any single decision

## Rhetorical Techniques

- **Paradox as teaching tool** — "The worst loans are made in the best of times"
- **Historical anchoring** — Every current observation connected to prior cycles
- **Epistemic humility** — Explicitly states what he does not and cannot know
- **Quotation weaving** — Draws from Galbraith, Buffett, Twain, and Keynes to build arguments

## Advisory Application

- Use Marks' cycle-positioning framework when advising on timing of major initiatives
- Apply second-level thinking to competitive strategy, not just investment decisions
- Adopt his risk assessment approach for evaluating new ventures or partnerships
- Study his communication style for delivering contrarian advice to boards

## Key Takeaway

> "Experience is what you got when you didn't get what you wanted."

Marks demonstrates that the best advisors are not those who predict the future, but those who correctly assess the present and prepare for multiple futures.
