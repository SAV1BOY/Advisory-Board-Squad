# Hoffman Speed vs Certainty Checklist

## Purpose
Helps the organization calibrate the appropriate balance between speed of action and certainty of outcome. Hoffman argues that in many situations, especially in fast-moving markets, the cost of delay exceeds the cost of being wrong. This checklist ensures the organization is not applying the same decision speed to all situations and that it moves fast when speed matters and slow when irreversibility demands caution.

## When to Use
- When deciding how much analysis to conduct before making a decision.
- When a team is debating whether to wait for more data or act on current information.
- When there is organizational tension between those who want to move fast and those who want more certainty.
- During strategic discussions about market timing and competitive dynamics.

## Gate Criteria

### Speed Assessment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The cost of delay is quantified or estimated (lost market opportunity, competitive advantage erosion).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The cost of being wrong is quantified or estimated (capital lost, reputation damage, time wasted).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The decision is classified: does speed or accuracy matter more in this context.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The minimum viable level of certainty needed to act is defined (not 100% for reversible decisions).

### Speed Enablers
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decision-making authority is placed with the people closest to the information.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization has a mechanism for rapid-fire decisions that bypass lengthy approval chains.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Small-scale tests and experiments are used to reduce uncertainty quickly.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team distinguishes between decisions that need consensus and those that need a single owner.

### Certainty Safeguards
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: For irreversible decisions, adequate time for analysis is protected despite speed pressure.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team has established minimum standards that cannot be waived for speed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Speed does not compromise ethical standards or legal compliance.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: After fast decisions, monitoring and rapid course correction mechanisms are in place.

### Organizational Calibration
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization's default speed bias is appropriate for its market (fast markets demand speed bias, regulated markets demand accuracy bias).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Speed versus certainty tradeoffs are discussed explicitly, not assumed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Teams are not punished for fast decisions that turned out wrong if the decision process was sound.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization learns from cases where it was too slow and too fast.

## Red Flags
- Every decision goes through the same slow approval process regardless of stakes.
- The organization moves fast on everything, including irreversible decisions with large downside.
- Analysis paralysis is the default: the team always needs "one more data point."
- Speed is valued for its own sake, not connected to competitive or strategic logic.
- The organization cannot distinguish between urgency and importance.
- Failed fast decisions are punished, causing everyone to slow down.

## Escalation Triggers
- A competitor captures a market position because the organization moved too slowly.
- A fast decision causes material harm that additional analysis would have prevented.
- Decision-making speed is inconsistent: some teams move fast and others are paralyzed.
- The organization's decision speed is significantly slower than industry norms.
- Key opportunities have been missed due to slow decision processes in the last 12 months.
