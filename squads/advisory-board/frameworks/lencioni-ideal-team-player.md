# Lencioni Ideal Team Player

## Purpose

Patrick Lencioni's Ideal Team Player model identifies the three essential virtues that make someone an effective team member: Humble, Hungry, and Smart (people-smart). When all three are present, you have an ideal team player. When any one is missing, you have a predictable dysfunction. This framework provides a hiring, coaching, and team development tool for building high-performing teams.

## When to Use

- When hiring and need a values-based assessment beyond skills
- When coaching team members who have potential but are causing friction
- When diagnosing why a team member isn't working out
- When building team norms and expectations
- When designing interview processes that assess cultural fit

## Core Model

### The Three Virtues

**Humble:** Lack of excessive ego. Quick to point out others' contributions. Slow to seek attention. Willing to do unglamorous work. More concerned with team success than personal status.

**Hungry:** Strong work ethic and drive. Always looking for more to do, more to learn, more to contribute. Self-motivated. Does not need to be managed to perform.

**Smart (People-Smart):** Not intellectual intelligence but interpersonal awareness. Understands how their words and actions affect others. Reads rooms. Navigates group dynamics effectively.

### Missing Virtue Archetypes

| Missing | Archetype | Problem |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Humble | The Bulldozer (hungry + smart) | Achieves results but at the expense of others |
| Hungry | The Charmer (humble + smart) | Likeable but doesn't contribute enough |
| Smart | The Accidental Mess-Maker (humble + hungry) | Good intentions but creates interpersonal damage |
| Humble + Hungry | The Politician | Manipulative, self-serving, skilled at politics |
| Humble + Smart | The Pawn | Does minimal work, nice but ineffective |
| Hungry + Smart | The Lovable Slacker | Gets by on charm without real contribution |

## Steps

1. **Assess your team.** Rate each member on Humble, Hungry, Smart (1-5 each).
2. **Identify missing virtues.** Where are the gaps? Which archetype patterns do you see?
3. **Coach for the gap.** Missing virtues can sometimes be developed through feedback, coaching, and practice.
4. **Hire for all three.** Design interview questions that assess each virtue. Never hire someone who is strong on two but clearly lacks the third.
5. **Make the three virtues explicit.** Share them with the team as the expected standard.
6. **Address non-fits.** If coaching doesn't develop the missing virtue within a reasonable timeframe, make the hard decision.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Use this model to evaluate founding teams. A team of bulldozers (hungry + smart, no humility) will implode. A team of charmers (humble + smart, no hunger) will stagnate.
- Help founders identify their own missing virtue and compensate for it through hiring or self-development.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Overvaluing hunger at the expense of humble.** Driven people who aren't humble create toxic cultures.
2. **Assuming smart means IQ.** This is EQ — emotional and interpersonal intelligence.
3. **Hiring for two out of three.** The missing virtue always causes problems. Don't compromise.
4. **Not coaching.** Some missing virtues can be developed with direct feedback and practice.
