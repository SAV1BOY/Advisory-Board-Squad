# Board Effectiveness Review Framework

## Purpose

This framework provides a structured process for conducting annual board effectiveness reviews — a practice that most boards neglect but that dramatically improves governance quality. The review evaluates how well the board is functioning as a team, whether it is adding strategic value, and where improvements are needed. Boards that review themselves perform better than those that don't.

## When to Use

- Annually as a standard governance practice
- When board meetings feel unproductive
- When there is a disconnect between the board and management
- After significant changes in board composition
- When preparing for a major governance milestone (IPO, acquisition)

## Core Model

### Review Dimensions
1. **Board composition:** Do we have the right mix of skills, experience, and perspectives?
2. **Meeting effectiveness:** Are meetings well-structured, efficient, and productive?
3. **Strategic contribution:** Is the board adding genuine strategic value?
4. **Oversight quality:** Is the board fulfilling its fiduciary and governance duties?
5. **Board-management relationship:** Is the relationship with the CEO/founder healthy and productive?
6. **Individual contribution:** Is each member contributing at an appropriate level?
7. **Information flow:** Does the board receive the right information at the right time?

### Review Process
1. **Self-assessment survey:** Each board member completes a confidential survey
2. **CEO/management input:** The CEO provides feedback on board performance
3. **One-on-one interviews:** The board chair conducts brief interviews with each member
4. **Board discussion:** Results are discussed openly at a dedicated board session
5. **Action plan:** Specific improvements are identified and assigned

## Steps

1. **Design the survey** covering all seven dimensions (use standard board effectiveness questionnaires as a starting point).
2. **Distribute confidentially.** Anonymity enables honesty.
3. **Analyze results.** Identify strengths, weaknesses, and discrepancies between members' views.
4. **Present findings** at a dedicated board session (not squeezed into a regular meeting).
5. **Develop an action plan** with 2-3 specific improvements.
6. **Follow up at the next meeting** on action plan progress.
7. **Repeat annually.** Effectiveness reviews should be a standard calendar item.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Not doing it at all.** The most common failure.
2. **Superficial review.** Checking the box without genuine reflection.
3. **No action from findings.** A review that produces no change is a waste of time.
4. **Avoiding individual assessment.** Board members who underperform need direct feedback.
