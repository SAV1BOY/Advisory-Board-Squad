# Pivot or Persevere Decision Memo

## Template Guidelines
**Word Limit:** 1,500 words maximum (excluding headers and tables)
**Reading Time:** Under 10 minutes
**Use When:** The company faces a fundamental question about whether to continue on the current strategic path, make a significant directional change (pivot), or wind down a failing initiative.

### Options Analysis (300-400 words)

#### Option A: Persevere — Double Down on Current Strategy
- **Thesis:** The strategy is correct but execution or timing is off. More time and resources will yield the expected results.
- **What Changes:** [Specific operational changes — new hires, process improvements, increased investment]
- **Investment Required:** $[amount] over [timeframe]
- **Success Criteria:** [Specific metrics that must be hit by specific dates to validate]
- **Kill Criteria:** If [metric] does not reach [threshold] by [date], we will revisit.
- **Cognitive Bias Check:** Are we falling prey to sunk cost fallacy? What would a new CEO with no history do?

#### Option B: Pivot — Change Strategic Direction
- **New Thesis:** [State the new strategic hypothesis clearly]
- **What Changes:** [Market, customer segment, product, business model, or go-to-market change]
- **What We Preserve:** [Assets, knowledge, relationships, technology that carry forward]
- **What We Abandon:** [What we stop doing — be explicit]
- **Investment Required:** $[amount] over [timeframe]
- **Transition Cost:** $[amount] — cost of switching (people, technology, contracts, brand)
- **New Success Criteria:** [Metrics and timeline for validating the pivot]
- **Evidence for the Pivot:** [Customer data, market signals, competitive intelligence supporting the new direction]

#### Option C: Reduce and Explore — Minimize Burn, Seek Signal
- **Thesis:** We do not have enough evidence to commit in either direction. Reduce investment to minimum viable, run targeted experiments.
- **Reduced Scope:** [What we keep running at minimum viable level]
- **Experiments:** [2-3 specific experiments with timelines and decision criteria]
- **Investment Required:** $[amount] over [timeframe]
- **Decision Point:** By [date], experiments will provide enough signal to choose persevere or pivot.

#### Option D: Wind Down
- **When This Is Right:** The market opportunity is insufficient, the team cannot execute, or the capital is better deployed elsewhere entirely.
- **Wind-Down Cost:** $[amount] (contracts, severance, obligations)
- **Timeline:** [Weeks/months]
- **Redeployment Plan:** [Where the team, technology, and capital go]

### Cognitive Bias Audit (150 words)

Before making this decision, the board should explicitly consider:

1. **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** We have invested $[amount] and [N] months. This investment is gone regardless of the decision. Does the path forward justify the marginal investment, ignoring sunk costs?

2. **Confirmation Bias:** Has the team selectively presented evidence that supports their preferred option? Request the strongest counter-argument to the recommendation.

3. **Anchoring:** Is the original thesis anchoring our expectations? If we were starting fresh today with everything we now know, would we pursue this?

4. **Social Proof:** Are we persevering because competitors are in this space, or pivoting because others have pivoted? Ground the decision in our own data.

5. **Loss Aversion:** Is the fear of losing what we have built preventing us from seeing a better path?

---

### Recommendation (150-200 words)

State your recommendation and address:
1. **The Decision:** Which option and why.
2. **The Hardest Truth:** What is the most uncomfortable fact that supports this recommendation?
3. **Reversal Conditions:** Under what conditions would you reverse this decision?
4. **Timeline to Validation:** By when will we know if this decision was right?
5. **Pre-Mortem:** If this decision fails in 12 months, the most likely cause is [X]. We mitigate by [Y].

## Required Sections Checklist
- [ ] Honest assessment presents both positive and negative signals with equal rigor
- [ ] Metrics vs. original targets are included with trends
- [ ] At least 4 options analyzed (persevere, pivot, reduce, wind down)
- [ ] Cognitive bias audit completed honestly
- [ ] Recommendation addresses the hardest truth and reversal conditions
- [ ] Implementation plan exists for the recommended option
- [ ] Kill criteria defined for whichever path is chosen

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
1. **Pivot theater:** Calling a minor adjustment a "pivot" to appear decisive. A real pivot fundamentally changes the thesis — be honest about what you are actually proposing.
2. **Persevere by default:** Inertia is not a strategy. Continuing the current path should require the same burden of proof as changing direction.
3. **False urgency:** Not every pivot discussion requires an immediate decision. If reducing and exploring is the honest answer, say so.
4. **Ignoring team impact:** Pivots affect people's careers and motivation. The implementation plan must address the human element.
5. **No kill criteria on perseverance:** If you choose to persevere, set a hard date and hard metrics. Without them, you will have this same discussion in 6 months with less runway.
