# Anything You Want — Derek Sivers

## Summary

Sivers tells the story of building CD Baby from a hobby project into a $100M business, then giving it all away. The book is radically short and radically honest. Its thesis: business should serve your life, not the reverse. Sivers challenges conventional startup wisdom—don't take funding, don't plan for scale, don't optimize for exit. Instead, build something that makes you and your customers happy, stay small by choice, and make decisions based on what kind of life you want to live. It is an antidote to the "growth at all costs" mentality and a reminder that many of the best businesses are built from a place of service and joy.

## Key Principles

- **Business is a utopia you design** — You get to make all the rules. Why copy someone else's rules? Design the company around the life you want.
- **If it's not a hit, switch** — Don't persist with ideas that aren't resonating. If something is working, do more of it. If it's not, change course immediately.
- **Make decisions based on what you'd do if you were already rich** — This strips away desperation and reveals what you actually care about.
- **The real point of doing anything is to be happy** — Revenue, growth, and scale are means, not ends. If the means make you miserable, they're the wrong means.
- **Don't plan for scale you don't want** — Many businesses are destroyed by growth they pursued out of obligation rather than desire.
- **Delegate or die, but delegate thoughtfully** — When you become the bottleneck, either teach others to make decisions or accept staying small.
- **You don't need anyone's permission** — Not investors, not partners, not the market. Start now with what you have.

## Frameworks Extracted

1. **The Hell Yeah or No Filter** — (from Sivers' related work) If a new opportunity doesn't make you say "HELL YEAH!", say no. This prevents calendar bloat and strategic diffusion.
2. **The Revolution Test** — Before making any business decision, ask: would I do this if I never planned to sell? If the answer is no, you're building for an exit, not for value.
3. **The Happy Customer Test** — The only metric that ultimately matters: are your customers genuinely happy? If yes, everything else (revenue, referrals, retention) follows. If no, nothing else matters.
4. **The Delegation Ladder** — (1) Do it yourself, (2) Show someone how, (3) Write down the process, (4) Let them decide and tell you, (5) Let them decide without telling you. Move up the ladder as fast as you can stomach.

## Application to Board Decisions

- **Founder alignment** — Before advising growth strategies, understand what kind of business the founder actually wants. Not every founder wants a billion-dollar company, and that's valid.
- **Exit pressure** — Boards often push toward exits prematurely. Sivers' framework asks: is this exit serving the founder and the customers, or just the investors?
- **Simplicity as strategy** — Challenge the assumption that complexity is a sign of sophistication. Many of the most profitable businesses are intentionally simple.
- **Customer obsession** — Apply the Happy Customer Test as a board-level metric. If customer satisfaction is declining while revenue is growing, the business is borrowing from the future.
- **Decision speed** — Sivers' "hit or switch" mentality applies to board governance. If a strategy isn't working after a defined period, don't persist out of sunk cost bias—switch.
