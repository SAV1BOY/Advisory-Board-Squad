# Capital Allocation Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures that every material capital allocation decision maximizes long-term value creation per dollar deployed. Capital allocation is the single most consequential activity of leadership. This checklist prevents capital from being allocated by inertia, politics, or the loudest voice in the room rather than by rigorous analysis of return on invested capital.

## When to Use
- Before approving annual budgets or material budget reallocation.
- When evaluating M&A opportunities, major capex, or new ventures.
- When deciding between organic growth investment, share buybacks, dividends, or debt repayment.
- During quarterly reviews of capital deployment versus plan.
- When fundraising proceeds need allocation to specific initiatives.

## Gate Criteria

### Return Analysis
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Expected return on invested capital (ROIC) is calculated for each major allocation with explicit assumptions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: ROIC is compared against the company's weighted average cost of capital (WACC) or hurdle rate.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Base, bull, and bear scenarios are modeled with probability weightings.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Payback period is calculated under each scenario.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Opportunity cost is quantified: what is the next-best use of this capital?

### Strategic Alignment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each allocation maps to a specific strategic priority approved by the board.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Allocations reinforce the company's moat rather than scatter resources across unrelated bets.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The balance between invest-for-growth and protect-the-core is explicit and intentional.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Capital reserved for optionality and opportunistic moves is defined (not zero, not excessive).

### Risk and Downside Protection
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Maximum downside exposure is quantified for each allocation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Concentration risk is assessed: no single allocation exceeds the defined threshold without board approval.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Staged funding with go/no-go gates is used for high-uncertainty investments.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The impact on cash runway under stress scenarios is modeled.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Covenant compliance and debt capacity impacts are evaluated.

### Governance and Accountability
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each allocation has a named owner accountable for delivering the projected return.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Performance metrics and review dates are defined before capital is deployed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Historical allocation accuracy is reviewed: how did previous allocations perform versus projections.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A mechanism exists to reallocate capital mid-cycle if an investment underperforms.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board approval thresholds are followed based on materiality levels.

## Red Flags
- Capital is allocated proportionally to last year's budget rather than to current strategic priorities.
- No investment has ever been killed mid-cycle despite underperformance.
- The CEO or CFO cannot articulate the ROIC hurdle rate.
- More than 30% of capital is allocated to initiatives without measurable return expectations.
- Capital allocation discussions focus on how much each department gets rather than expected returns.
- The company is sitting on excess cash with no articulated plan.

## Escalation Triggers
- Actual returns consistently fall below projected returns by more than 25% across multiple investments.
- Cash runway drops below 12 months without a funded plan to extend.
- A single investment consumes more than the board-approved threshold without reauthorization.
- Capital is deployed to an initiative that was not part of the approved plan without proper authorization.
- Debt covenants are at risk of being breached due to capital allocation decisions.
