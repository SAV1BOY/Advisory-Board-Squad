# Fundraising Market Conditions

## Key Findings

1. **Funding volumes have recovered selectively.** Total venture funding in 2025 reached $345B globally, up 18% from 2024's trough, but still 31% below the 2021 peak. Recovery is concentrated in AI infrastructure, climate tech, and defense tech — most other sectors remain in a disciplined funding environment.
2. **Valuation multiples have bifurcated.** Top-decile companies (>150% YoY revenue growth, >70% gross margins) command 20-40x ARR multiples. Median companies trade at 6-10x, and bottom-quartile companies struggle to raise at any valuation premium. The gap between "hot" and "normal" has never been wider.
3. **Time between rounds is extending.** Median time from Series A to Series B has increased from 18 months (2021) to 28 months (2025). Series B to C has extended from 20 to 32 months. Boards must plan for longer runway requirements between rounds.
4. **Investor diligence depth has permanently increased.** Data room expectations now include 18-24 months of cohort-level unit economics, customer-level retention data, and bottoms-up financial models. The era of raising on vision and TAM slides is over for all but the most exceptional founders.
5. **Secondary markets are becoming mainstream.** 42% of Series C+ rounds now include a secondary component allowing early employees and investors to achieve partial liquidity. Boards should proactively plan secondary strategies to retain key talent.
6. **Bridge rounds carry stigma but are common.** 29% of companies that raised in 2025 did a bridge or extension round before their priced round. These are often disguised as "inside rounds" but carry signaling risk with new investors.

## Implications for Board

- **Runway management.** With inter-round timelines extending, the board should ensure the company maintains at least 18 months of runway at all times. The decision to begin fundraising should be triggered at 12 months of remaining runway, not 6.
- **Fundraising readiness.** The board should verify that management maintains a perpetually updated data room with cohort metrics, retention curves, and bottoms-up models. Scrambling to assemble diligence materials is a red flag to investors.
- **Valuation expectations.** The board should calibrate valuation expectations against current multiples, not the last round. If the company is not in the top decile of growth metrics, plan for a flat or modest up-round and model the dilution accordingly.
- **Secondary strategy.** For companies at Series B or later, the board should discuss secondary liquidity proactively. Losing a key engineer because they cannot afford to stay is a preventable failure.
- **Bridge round governance.** If a bridge round is needed, the board should ensure it is structured to minimize signaling risk — convertible notes with reasonable caps, insider participation requirements, and clear milestones to the next priced round.
