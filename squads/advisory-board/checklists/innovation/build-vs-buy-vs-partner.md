# Build vs. Buy vs. Partner Decision Checklist

## Purpose
Provides a structured evaluation framework for decisions about whether to build a capability internally, acquire it through M&A, or access it through a partnership. Prevents the common failure modes of defaulting to "build" (NIH syndrome), overpaying for acquisitions, or entering partnerships without clear value exchange.

## Pre-Decision Gates

### Strategic Clarity
- [ ] The capability needed is clearly defined (not vague "we need AI" or "we need international presence")
- [ ] The capability's role in the company's competitive advantage is articulated: is it core differentiator, important enabler, or table stakes?
- [ ] The time constraint is explicit: when must this capability be operational?
- [ ] The decision has a defined owner and a deadline for resolution

### Information Quality
- [ ] Internal build cost and timeline have been estimated by engineering/operations (not just leadership)
- [ ] At least 3 potential acquisition targets have been identified and preliminarily assessed
- [ ] At least 2 potential partnership structures have been explored
- [ ] The total cost of each option includes ongoing maintenance, not just initial investment

## Build Evaluation

- [ ] The company has relevant technical expertise in-house or can hire it within the required timeline
- [ ] The estimated build time fits within the strategic window of opportunity
- [ ] The build cost (fully loaded: people, infrastructure, opportunity cost) has been compared to buy and partner costs
- [ ] The company is willing to maintain this capability long-term (building creates permanent obligations)
- [ ] The capability will be a source of competitive differentiation, not a commodity
- [ ] The build does not require the company to develop expertise far outside its core competency

## Buy (Acquire) Evaluation

- [ ] At least one acquisition target exists that delivers the needed capability at a reasonable valuation
- [ ] The target's technology and/or team can be integrated within 6-12 months
- [ ] Cultural compatibility has been assessed (not just technology fit)
- [ ] Key employees at the target have been identified and retention plans are outlined
- [ ] Integration costs (people, systems, process, culture) have been estimated and added to acquisition price
- [ ] The acquisition does not introduce unacceptable regulatory, legal, or customer risk
- [ ] A post-acquisition integration plan exists with clear milestones and an accountable owner

## Partner Evaluation

- [ ] A potential partner exists with the required capability and willingness to collaborate
- [ ] The partnership value exchange is clear: what does each party contribute and receive?
- [ ] The partnership does not create an unacceptable dependency (partner controls a critical path)
- [ ] Exit terms are defined: what happens if the partnership ends?
- [ ] IP ownership and data rights are clearly delineated
- [ ] The partnership does not educate a potential future competitor
- [ ] The partner's financial stability and strategic direction are aligned with a multi-year collaboration

## Comparative Scoring

| Dimension | Build | Buy | Partner |
|-----------|-------|-----|---------|
| Time to capability | Score 1-5 | Score 1-5 | Score 1-5 |
| Total cost (3-year) | Score 1-5 | Score 1-5 | Score 1-5 |
| Strategic control | Score 1-5 | Score 1-5 | Score 1-5 |
| Execution risk | Score 1-5 | Score 1-5 | Score 1-5 |
| Reversibility | Score 1-5 | Score 1-5 | Score 1-5 |
| Competitive moat created | Score 1-5 | Score 1-5 | Score 1-5 |

- [ ] All six dimensions have been scored for each option
- [ ] Scores have been reviewed by at least two stakeholders to reduce individual bias
- [ ] The option with the highest weighted score aligns with the qualitative strategic assessment

## Pass/Fail Criteria

**Pass:** All pre-decision gates are satisfied. At least two of the three options (build, buy, partner) have been seriously evaluated. The recommended option has a clear rationale that addresses time, cost, risk, and strategic fit. An integration or execution plan exists.

**Fail (any one triggers):**
- Only one option was considered (defaulting to build without evaluating alternatives)
- Total cost comparison does not include ongoing maintenance and opportunity costs
- The decision is driven primarily by a single executive's preference without structured analysis
- No integration or execution plan accompanies the recommendation
- Key risks of the chosen option are not identified with mitigation plans

## Board Application
- Require build/buy/partner analysis for any capability investment above a defined threshold
- Challenge "build" defaults: ask "Why is buying or partnering inferior?" and require specific answers
- For acquisitions, insist on integration plans before approving the deal — the deal is only as good as the integration
- For partnerships, review exit terms and dependency risk before approval
- Revisit the decision 12 months after execution: did the chosen approach deliver the expected outcome?
