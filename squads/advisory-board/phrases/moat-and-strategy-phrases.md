# Moat & Strategy Phrases

Phrases for evaluating competitive advantages, challenging strategic positioning, and testing whether a company has a real moat or a temporary head start.

## Testing the Moat

1. **"What do we do that is genuinely hard to copy? Not hard to think of — hard to replicate."**
   Use to distinguish between ideas (easy to copy) and execution advantages (hard to copy). Most "moats" are just ideas.

2. **"If a well-funded competitor decided to build exactly what we have, how long would it take them?"**
   Use to quantify the durability of the advantage. If the answer is "6 months," it's not a moat — it's a head start.

3. **"Our moat is [X]. How do we know that's true? What evidence would disprove it?"**
   Use to test whether the claimed moat is real or assumed. Many companies claim moats that their customers and competitors would not recognize.

4. **"Does our advantage get stronger as we grow, or does it stay the same?"**
   Use to identify network effects, data advantages, and economies of scale. A real moat compounds; a temporary advantage decays.

5. **"What would a customer say our competitive advantage is? Not what we say — what they say."**
   Use to ground the moat assessment in external reality. Internal narratives about advantages often diverge from customer perception.

## Challenging Strategy

6. **"If this is our strategy, what are we saying no to? If the answer is nothing, it's not a strategy."**
   Use to test strategic specificity. A strategy that accommodates everything directs nothing.

7. **"We say our strategy is [X]. But our resource allocation says [Y]. Which one is the real strategy?"**
   Use to expose the gap between stated strategy and actual behavior. Follow the money and the people.

8. **"Are we competing to be the best, or are we competing to be unique? Those require very different strategies."**
   Use to challenge the competitive frame. Being the best in a race is exhausting. Being the only option for a specific need is powerful.

9. **"What trade-offs does this strategy require that our competitors are unwilling to make?"**
   Use to test for genuine strategic differentiation. A strategy that makes no painful trade-offs can be copied by anyone.

10. **"Is this strategy winning, or is the market growing fast enough to mask the absence of a strategy?"**
    Use during growth phases to distinguish between strategic success and market tailwinds.

## Evaluating Competitive Position

11. **"Who is our real competitor? Not the one we pitch against — the one we lose to."**
    Use to identify the actual competitive threat, which may not be the obvious one.

12. **"What does our competitor know that we don't? What do we know that they don't?"**
    Use to assess information asymmetry. The side with better information about customer needs usually wins.

13. **"If our competitor's CEO were in this room, what would they be most worried about from us? And what would they be least worried about?"**
    Use to calibrate competitive threat assessment from the opponent's perspective.

14. **"Are we building barriers to entry for competitors, or are we just running faster?"**
    Use to distinguish between durable positioning and speed advantage. Speed advantages are temporary.

15. **"What is the minimum viable moat for this business? What would a bare-bones defensible position look like?"**
    Use to define the floor. If we can't identify a minimum moat, the business may be fundamentally undefendable.

## Long-Term Strategic Thinking

16. **"What are we building today that will matter in 10 years? What are we building that won't?"**
    Use to separate lasting assets from temporary advantages. Invest in the lasting ones.

17. **"If the market shifts in [direction], does our strategy get stronger or weaker?"**
    Use to stress-test strategy against plausible futures. Robust strategies work across multiple scenarios.

18. **"What is the one decision we could make today that would be the hardest for a competitor to reverse-engineer?"**
    Use to identify the highest-leverage strategic move. The hardest decisions to copy are usually the most valuable.

19. **"Are we playing a finite game (win this quarter) or an infinite game (stay in the game indefinitely)?"**
    Use to calibrate strategic time horizon. Finite game moves can undermine infinite game positioning.

20. **"What would we do differently if we assumed this market will exist for 50 years?"**
    Use to shift thinking from short-term optimization to long-term value creation.

## Strategic Clarity

21. **"Can you explain our strategy in one sentence that a smart outsider would understand?"**
    Use to test strategic clarity. If it takes a paragraph, the strategy isn't clear enough.

22. **"What are the three to five things that must be true for this strategy to work? How confident are we in each?"**
    Use to identify the critical assumptions underlying the strategy and assess their robustness.

23. **"If we execute this strategy perfectly, what does the company look like in three years?"**
    Use to create a concrete vision that the strategy is driving toward. Abstract strategies produce abstract results.
