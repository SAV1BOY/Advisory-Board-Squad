# Task: Trust Repair Plan

## Objective
Restore organizational trust after a breach event such as layoffs handled poorly, leadership misconduct, broken promises, or major operational failures that damaged the psychological contract with employees.

## Trigger
- Trust breach identified through engagement data, exit interviews, or direct feedback
- After poorly handled layoffs or restructuring
- After discovery of leadership misconduct
- When employee confidence in leadership drops below critical threshold
- After a public failure that affects employee morale

## Agents Involved
- **Lead:** CEO with CHRO support
- **Support:** Communications lead, legal counsel, affected team leaders
- **Advisory:** Board Chair for governance-level trust breaches

## Pre-Requisites
- [ ] Trust breach clearly identified and acknowledged internally
- [ ] Root cause understood not just symptoms
- [ ] Leadership is genuinely committed to repair not just optics
- [ ] Resources available for sustained effort

## Steps

1. **Step 1:** Acknowledge the breach explicitly and specifically in a leadership communication that does not minimize or deflect

2. **Step 2:** Conduct listening sessions across the organization to understand the full scope of trust damage and what repair looks like from employees' perspective

3. **Step 3:** Identify the specific trust dimensions that were damaged such as competence trust, integrity trust, benevolence trust, or communication trust

4. **Step 4:** Develop a repair plan with concrete commitments, measurable actions, and a realistic timeline acknowledging that trust rebuilds slowly

5. **Step 5:** Implement immediate visible actions that demonstrate commitment to change not just words

6. **Step 6:** Establish transparent progress reporting sharing both successes and ongoing challenges honestly

7. **Step 7:** Monitor trust recovery through pulse surveys at 30, 60, 90, and 180 day intervals

8. **Step 8:** Report to board on trust trajectory and any emerging risks to recovery

## Frameworks to Apply
- **Competing Values Framework:** Assess culture across clan, adhocracy, market, and hierarchy dimensions
- **Edgar Schein's Three Levels:** Analyze culture at the artifact, espoused values, and underlying assumptions levels
- **Behavioral Indicators:** Ground all cultural assessment in observable behaviors, not aspirational statements

## Checklists

### Quality Gate
- [ ] Assessment combines quantitative data and qualitative insights
- [ ] Multiple organizational levels and functions represented in data collection
- [ ] Findings distinguish between symptoms and root causes
- [ ] Recommendations are specific, actionable, and resourced
- [ ] Success metrics defined with measurement plan
- [ ] Timeline is realistic (culture change takes quarters, not weeks)
- [ ] Leadership commitment secured before proceeding

## Output
- Trust Repair Plan document
- Communication plan
- Pulse survey cadence
- Progress reports
- 180-day assessment

## Handoffs
- Recovery progress feeds into culture-health-tracker
- Leadership accountability feeds into performance-review-exec
- Systemic issues feed into values-integrity-check
- Ongoing trust monitoring feeds into culture-audit

## Metrics
| Metric | Target | Measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Assessment completion | Within agreed timeline | Calendar tracking |
| Employee participation | >70% for surveys, representative sample for qualitative | Participation rates |
| Recommendation adoption | >80% of recommendations actioned | Action tracking |
| Culture score improvement | Measurable improvement within 6-12 months | Pulse surveys |
| Board confidence in culture trajectory | >4/5 | Board survey |
