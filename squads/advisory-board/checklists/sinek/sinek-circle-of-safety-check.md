# Sinek Circle of Safety Check Checklist

## Purpose
Evaluates whether leadership has created a Circle of Safety: an environment where people feel safe from internal threats and can focus their energy on external challenges. Sinek argues that when leaders create safety, teams cooperate naturally. When leaders create internal competition and fear, teams turn on each other. This checklist assesses the operational reality of psychological safety, not just the stated intention.

## When to Use
- During organizational health and culture assessments.
- When internal conflict is consuming energy that should be directed externally.
- When turnover or disengagement suggests people do not feel safe.
- When new leadership is establishing team norms.
- After organizational trauma (layoffs, crises, leadership changes).

## Gate Criteria

### Safety Indicators
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Employees report that they can take risks without fear of punishment for honest failure.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: People raise problems and bad news early because they trust the response will be constructive.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Internal competition is healthy (pushing each other to improve) not toxic (sabotaging each other).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: People focus their competitive energy on external challenges, not internal politics.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Layoffs and organizational changes are handled with dignity and transparency.

### Leadership Behavior
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders absorb uncertainty and pressure rather than passing it down the hierarchy.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders protect their teams from unfair blame or excessive demands.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders are accessible and approachable, not isolated behind layers.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders share hardship (salary cuts, reduced perks) before imposing them on others.

### Systemic Safety
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Performance management distinguishes between skill gaps (coachable) and values violations (not tolerable).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Reporting mechanisms for safety concerns exist and are used without retaliation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization does not use fear, shame, or intimidation as management tools.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Policies protect individuals who speak up about problems or wrongdoing.

### Trust Outcomes
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Collaboration happens naturally without requiring mandates or incentives.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: People help colleagues outside their direct team because they want to, not because they are measured on it.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Innovation emerges because people feel safe to propose unconventional ideas.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Attrition is low and exit interviews do not cite fear or lack of safety.

## Red Flags
- People spend more time protecting themselves than doing their best work.
- Information is hoarded because sharing it feels risky.
- Managers use threats (implicit or explicit) to drive performance.
- Internal politics determine outcomes more than merit.
- People regularly look for new jobs as a safety precaution.
- After a mistake, the first question is "who is to blame" rather than "what can we learn."

## Escalation Triggers
- A credible allegation of retaliation against someone who raised a concern.
- Team attrition exceeds 25% in a 12-month period and exit interviews cite safety concerns.
- An employee survey reveals a significant drop in psychological safety scores.
- An incident of bullying, harassment, or intimidation by leadership is reported.
- The organization's safety failures become public through media, social media, or legal action.
