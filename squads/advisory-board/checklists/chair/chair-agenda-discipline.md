# Chair Agenda Discipline Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the chair maintains discipline in agenda design and meeting management so that board time produces maximum governance value. Agenda discipline means every minute of board time is intentionally allocated to the highest-value use, discussions stay on track, and outcomes are clear. This checklist is the chair's operational tool for running effective meetings.

## When to Use
- When designing the agenda for each board meeting.
- During the meeting to manage time and flow.
- After each meeting to assess agenda effectiveness.
- When board members express frustration about meeting productivity.

## Gate Criteria

### Agenda Design
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The agenda is designed around decisions and discussions, not presentations.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each item has a clear outcome designation: decision, discussion, or information.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Time allocations are realistic and total to the meeting duration.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The most important items are placed in the first half of the meeting.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Consent agenda is used for routine items to preserve time for strategic matters.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The agenda is finalized and distributed at least one week before the meeting.

### Meeting Management
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The chair starts and ends the meeting on time.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The chair redirects tangential discussions to the parking lot or future agenda.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The chair ensures time is allocated according to the agenda, adjusting real-time if needed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The chair summarizes decisions and action items before moving to the next topic.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The chair ensures all voices are heard, not just the loudest.

### Post-Meeting Assessment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The chair reviews whether agenda outcomes were achieved.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board member feedback on meeting effectiveness is collected.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Items that were not addressed are carried forward to the next meeting with priority assessment.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The chair reflects on facilitation quality and identifies improvements.

### Strategic Calendar
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: An annual calendar maps major topics to specific meetings throughout the year.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The calendar ensures all governance responsibilities are covered (strategy, financial review, risk, talent, compliance).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The calendar is flexible enough to accommodate emerging issues.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Standing items are reviewed annually for continued relevance.

## Red Flags
- Presentations consume more than 30% of meeting time.
- Meetings regularly run 30 or more minutes over the scheduled time.
- Agenda items are added at the last minute without adequate preparation.
- The same items appear on the agenda meeting after meeting without resolution.
- The chair does not intervene when discussions go off track.
- Board members multitask during meetings, indicating disengagement.

## Escalation Triggers
- Board members express that meetings are not a productive use of their time.
- Critical decisions are deferred repeatedly because the agenda is consumed by less important items.
- The chair cannot maintain meeting discipline, and the board culture normalizes overruns.
- Material governance topics (risk, compliance, succession) are consistently squeezed or omitted.
- Board members stop preparing because they believe the agenda will not be followed.
