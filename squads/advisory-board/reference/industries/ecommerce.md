# E-commerce Industry Reference

## Business Model Characteristics
- **Revenue model:** Product sales (1P), marketplace fees (3P), subscriptions, advertising, fulfillment services
- **Margins:** Highly variable. Fashion: 50-65% gross. Electronics: 15-25% gross. Grocery: 20-35% gross.
- **Key metric:** Unit economics per order: AOV, COGS, shipping cost, return rate, customer acquisition cost
- **Capital intensity:** Inventory, warehousing, logistics. Capital requirements increase with scale.

## Key Metrics
- **AOV (Average Order Value):** Revenue per transaction
- **Repeat purchase rate:** % of customers who buy again within 12 months. > 40% is strong.
- **Return rate:** % of orders returned. Fashion: 20-30%. Electronics: 5-10%.
- **Contribution margin per order:** Revenue - COGS - Shipping - Returns - Payment processing
- **Customer acquisition cost vs. first-order margin:** If CAC > first-order margin, you need repeat purchases to break even

## Competitive Dynamics
- **Amazon is the center of gravity.** Any e-commerce strategy must have a clear answer to "why not just sell on Amazon?"
- **Brand is the moat** for DTC companies. Without brand, you're competing on price against Amazon.
- **Logistics is the battlefield.** Customer expectations for speed and free shipping keep rising.
- **Mobile-first is mandatory.** 70%+ of e-commerce traffic is mobile in most markets.

## Board Considerations
- Focus on unit economics per order, not just revenue growth. Growing revenue while losing money on every order is fatal.
- Evaluate the brand's strength: can you charge a premium, or are you a commodity competing on price?
- Monitor inventory management: overstocking ties up capital; understocking loses sales.
- Assess logistics strategy: own fulfillment vs. 3PL. Each has tradeoffs on cost, speed, and control.
