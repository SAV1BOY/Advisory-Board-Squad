# Calibration: Dissent and Commit

## The Concept

Dissent-and-commit is a decision-making protocol that separates two distinct questions:

1. **Do you agree with the decision?** (You may or may not.)
2. **Will you support the decision once it's made?** (You must.)

This protocol makes it safe to disagree by guaranteeing that disagreement will be heard and recorded. It also prevents disagreement from becoming sabotage by requiring explicit commitment to the outcome.

## When to Apply

Dissent-and-commit applies when:

- The board has debated a decision thoroughly.
- At least one advisor disagrees with the majority position.
- The decision needs to be made now (no more information will resolve the disagreement).
- The disagreement is about judgment, not facts. (If the disagreement is about facts, get the facts.)

It does **not** apply when:

- The decision has ethical or legal implications that the dissenter cannot in conscience support.
- The process was flawed (inadequate information, insufficient discussion, excluded voices).
- The dissenter believes the decision is catastrophically wrong and irreversible.

In these cases, the dissenter should escalate, not commit.

## The Protocol

### Step 1: Ensure the dissent was genuinely heard

The Chair confirms the dissent by restating it: "Let me make sure I have your position right: you disagree because [specific reason]. Is that accurate?"

The dissenter confirms or corrects.

### Step 2: The group engages with the dissent

The majority must engage with the dissent's strongest arguments, not just acknowledge them. If the dissent raises a genuinely new point, the decision may change. If it raises a known concern, the majority explains why they weigh it differently.

### Step 3: Record the dissent formally

The Decision-Tracker records:
- Who dissented.
- The specific reasoning for the dissent.
- The specific concerns about the proposed decision.
- The conditions under which the dissent should trigger a decision review.

### Step 4: Ask for the commitment

The Chair asks explicitly: "[Name], the decision is [X]. Your dissent is recorded. Will you commit to supporting this decision as we move forward?"

### Step 5: The dissenter responds

Acceptable responses:
- "I disagree, and I commit. I'll support this fully."
- "I disagree, and I commit, with one condition: if [specific trigger] occurs, I want us to revisit."
- "I cannot commit. Here's why." (This triggers an escalation — see below.)

### Step 6: Honor the commitment

The dissenter:
- Supports the decision publicly.
- Does not undermine it in side conversations.
- Executes their part of the decision with the same energy as if they had agreed.
- Does not say "I told you so" if the outcome is bad. (The formal record speaks for itself.)

The majority:
- Respects the dissenter for having the courage to disagree.
- Monitors the dissent's trigger conditions.
- If the trigger conditions arise, revisits the decision — and acknowledges the dissenter's foresight.

## Calibrating the Commitment

### Full commitment
The dissenter agrees to treat the decision as if it were their own. They do not hedge, delay, or half-implement.

**Language:** "I disagree with the direction, but I'm fully committed to making it work. Here's how I'll support it."

### Conditional commitment
The dissenter commits but sets a specific, measurable trigger for review.

**Language:** "I'll commit fully, but I want to flag: if [metric] drops below [threshold] by [date], I want us to revisit. Can we agree to that?"

### Cannot commit
Rare but important. The dissenter believes the decision is so wrong — ethically, strategically, or legally — that they cannot support it in good conscience.

**Language:** "I respect the process, but I cannot commit to this decision. My concern is [specific concern]. I need to escalate this to [appropriate body/person]."

**Chair's response:** Take this seriously. Reconvene the discussion if warranted. If the decision stands, the dissenter may need to recuse themselves from the implementation — or, in extreme cases, resign from the board.

## When the Dissenter Was Right

This is the most important moment in the dissent-and-commit cycle. How the board handles vindicated dissent determines the future quality of dissent.

### Do
- Acknowledge it publicly: "[Name] raised this concern when we made the decision. The concern has materialized."
- Update the decision framework: "What did the dissenter see that the majority missed?"
- Thank the dissenter: "This is exactly why we value dissent on this board."
- Learn from the pattern: "Are there other decisions where we may be making the same error?"

### Don't
- Brush past it: "Well, everyone makes mistakes."
- Re-attribute the insight: "We all had some concerns."
- Punish the majority: The majority made a reasonable decision with the information available.

## When the Dissenter Was Wrong

Also important. How the board handles non-vindicated dissent determines whether people will dissent again.

### Do
- Note it factually: "The concern didn't materialize. The decision is working as intended."
- Affirm the value of dissent: "The dissent was still valuable because it forced us to [examine X / prepare for Y / think more carefully about Z]."
- Treat the dissenter the same as always. No penalty for being wrong.

### Don't
- Mock or marginalize: "See, you were worried for nothing."
- Use it as evidence against future dissent: "Last time you disagreed, you were wrong."
- Keep score. Dissent is not a betting market.

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | What Happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Forced commitment | "Everyone agree? Great, moving on." — no space for genuine dissent | Chair explicitly asks: "Does anyone disagree?" and waits |
| Pocket veto | Dissenter commits verbally but undermines privately | Chair monitors execution; address privately if detected |
| Dissent fatigue | Same person dissents every time, stops being taken seriously | Private conversation: is the dissent pattern-based or habitual? |
| No follow-through on triggers | Conditional commitment trigger hits but nobody revisits | Governance-Ops tracks trigger conditions and alerts the Chair |
| Missing the record | Dissent expressed but not formally documented | Decision-Tracker records every dissent before moving to the next item |
