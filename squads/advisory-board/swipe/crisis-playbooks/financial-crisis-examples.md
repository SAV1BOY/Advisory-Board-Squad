# Financial Crisis Examples — Playbook

## Overview
Financial crises—cash shortfalls, failed fundraises, revenue collapses, fraud discovery—threaten the company's survival. The board's role shifts from advisory to active governance. These playbooks provide response patterns.

## Crisis Type 1: Cash Shortfall / Runway Crisis

### Response Pattern (30-Day Sprint)
**Week 1: Assess**
- Precise cash position and monthly burn rate
- Committed vs. discretionary spend analysis
- Revenue-at-risk assessment (what revenue might not materialize?)
- All available sources of emergency capital (credit lines, investor bridges, asset sales)

**Week 2: Cut**
- Reduce discretionary spend immediately
- Freeze all hiring
- Renegotiate vendor contracts (payment terms, volume reductions)
- Identify potential headcount reductions if needed

**Week 3: Fund**
- Approach existing investors for bridge financing
- Explore revenue-based financing, venture debt, or credit facilities
- Consider strategic partnerships with financial components
- Evaluate asset sales (non-core products, IP, subsidiaries)

**Week 4: Restructure**
- Implement organizational changes
- Communicate honestly with employees (partial transparency is worse than silence)
- Update the board on 13-week cash flow forecast (weekly updates from here)
- Establish weekly cash management meetings

### Communication Principles
- **To employees:** Honest about the situation without creating panic. "We're making difficult changes to ensure the company's long-term success."
- **To customers:** Reassure continuity of service. Don't let customers learn about your financial difficulties from the press.
- **To investors:** Full transparency. They'd rather know now and help than be surprised later.

## Crisis Type 2: Revenue Collapse

### Response Pattern
1. **Diagnose:** Is this market-wide or company-specific? Temporary or structural?
2. **Scenario plan:** Model scenarios for recovery timeline (3 months, 6 months, never)
3. **Match cost structure to new revenue reality:** Don't wait for recovery to cut costs. Cut to the worst-case scenario and recover from there.
4. **Protect the core:** Identify the smallest viable business that can survive and protect it at all costs
5. **Communicate:** Board receives weekly updates during revenue collapse

## Crisis Type 3: Fraud Discovery

### Response Pattern
1. **Preserve evidence:** Do not destroy, alter, or allow access to relevant records
2. **Engage external counsel immediately:** Attorney-client privilege protects the investigation
3. **Board committee:** Form a special committee of independent directors to oversee the investigation
4. **Regulatory notification:** If required by law, notify regulators within the required timeframe
5. **Personnel decisions:** Based on investigation findings, not assumptions
6. **Financial restatement:** If financial statements are affected, work with auditors on restatement
7. **Public disclosure:** If material, disclose to shareholders and the market

## Board Application
- Every board should have a crisis response protocol approved before a crisis occurs
- In financial crises, the board should shift to weekly or biweekly meetings
- The board's primary obligation is to the company's stakeholders, not to any individual (including the CEO)
- Document all board actions during a crisis meticulously—these become the legal record
- Post-crisis, evaluate what early warning signs were missed and strengthen monitoring
