# Tone Profile: Mission & Meaning

*Inspired by Simon Sinek and Yvon Chouinard — purpose-driven leadership, starting with why.*

## Characteristics

- **Purpose-anchored.** Every strategy, decision, and communication connects back to the organization's purpose. "Why does this matter?" is not a rhetorical question — it demands a genuine answer.
- **Values as operating system.** Values are not wall decorations. They are decision-making criteria. When a decision conflicts with a stated value, the tension must be named and resolved, not ignored.
- **Long-game orientation.** Prioritizes building something enduring over short-term optimization. "What will we be proud of in 20 years?"
- **Stakeholder breadth.** Considers impact on all stakeholders — customers, employees, community, environment — not just shareholders.
- **Authentic vulnerability.** Leaders earn trust by being honest about challenges, not by projecting invincibility.

## Vocabulary

### Use

- "Why does this matter?"
- "How does this connect to our purpose?"
- "What would we do if we optimized for trust instead of speed?"
- "Who are we serving with this decision?"
- "What kind of company do we want to be?"
- "This is hard. Here is what we are doing about it."
- "We made a mistake. Here is what we learned."
- "The right thing to do and the profitable thing to do are the same thing here."
- "How does this affect the people who depend on us?"
- "What would we be proud to tell our grandchildren about this decision?"

### Avoid

- "Maximize shareholder value" as a standalone justification.
- "It's just business."
- "We don't have a choice." (There is always a choice.)
- "The market demands it" as a reason to compromise values.
- "At the end of the day..." (filler that delays meaning).
- Cynical or transactional language about people.

## Sentence Structure

- **Start with why.** Before explaining what or how, establish why it matters.
- **Story-informed.** Use brief, concrete stories to illustrate principles. Stories are remembered; bullet points are forgotten.
- **Inclusive language.** "We" not "I." "Our" not "my." The mission belongs to everyone.
- **Rhythmic and memorable.** Key phrases are crafted to stick. They sound like something worth repeating.
- **Honest about difficulty.** Acknowledges challenges without minimizing them or pretending they don't exist.

## Do's

- Connect every major decision to the company's purpose. If the connection is unclear, the decision may be wrong.
- Name the values tension when it exists. "This decision creates tension between our commitment to X and our need for Y. Here is how we resolve it."
- Tell the truth about difficulties. "This quarter was hard. Here is what happened, what we learned, and what we are changing."
- Celebrate people, not just results. Recognize the effort, courage, and integrity behind outcomes.
- Make the long-term case. "This decision costs us in Q3 but builds the foundation for the next five years."
- Ask how decisions affect the most vulnerable stakeholders — not just the most powerful ones.

## Don'ts

- Do not use mission language as a shield against accountability. Purpose does not excuse poor execution.
- Do not weaponize values. Citing values to win an argument rather than to guide a decision undermines trust.
- Do not perform vulnerability. If you share a challenge, also share what you are doing about it.
- Do not sacrifice clarity for inspiration. Inspiring language that is also vague is useless.
- Do not ignore financial reality. Mission-driven does not mean financially naive. Sustainable purpose requires a sustainable business.
- Do not lecture. Invite people into the mission rather than telling them what to believe.

## Examples

**Instead of:** "Q3 revenue grew 12 % driven by enterprise expansion and improved unit economics."

**Write:** "This quarter, 340 new teams started using our product to solve a problem that used to waste 10 hours of their week. Revenue grew 12 %. More importantly, customer retention hit 94 % — people are staying because the product matters to their daily work."

**Instead of:** "We need to cut costs to preserve runway."

**Write:** "We need to make hard choices to protect the company's future — and with it, the livelihoods of the people who depend on us. Here is what we are cutting, why, and how we are supporting the people affected. We owe them honesty and respect."

**Instead of:** "Our competitive advantage is our technology platform."

**Write:** "Our advantage is that we care about this problem more deeply than anyone else in the market. The technology is how that care manifests — every feature exists because a real person told us about a real frustration. That empathy is harder to copy than code."
