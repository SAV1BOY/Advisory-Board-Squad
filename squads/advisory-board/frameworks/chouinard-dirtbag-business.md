# Chouinard Dirtbag Business Framework

## Purpose

Yvon Chouinard's "dirtbag" business philosophy embraces simplicity, frugality, and anti-corporate culture as strategic advantages. Born from climbing culture where resourcefulness trumped resources, this framework challenges the assumption that growth, scale, and corporate sophistication are always desirable. Sometimes the most powerful business strategy is staying small, staying weird, and staying true to your roots.

## When to Use

- When a company is growing for growth's sake rather than mission
- When corporate culture is replacing the founder's authentic culture
- When overhead and bureaucracy are consuming resources that should go to product and mission
- When evaluating whether to raise capital or bootstrap
- When a company's soul is being lost in the pursuit of scale

## Core Model

### Dirtbag Business Principles
1. **Simplicity over sophistication.** Keep the business as simple as possible. Complexity is a cost.
2. **Frugality as a value.** Low overhead enables mission-focused spending and survival during downturns.
3. **Authenticity over polish.** Customers trust real over polished. Imperfection is human.
4. **Experience over credentials.** Hire people who have done the thing, not people who studied the thing.
5. **Culture preservation.** Protect the founding culture as the company grows. Culture is the product.
6. **Work to live, don't live to work.** The best work comes from people with full lives, not from grinding.

### The Anti-Growth Thesis
Not every company should grow as fast as possible. Growth brings:
- Complexity and coordination costs
- Dilution of culture and mission
- Pressure from investors for returns
- Risk of becoming what you set out to disrupt

Sometimes the right strategy is to stay small, profitable, and mission-focused.

### When Dirtbag Business Applies
- Mission-driven companies where growth might compromise mission
- Bootstrapped companies that don't need venture capital
- Lifestyle businesses where founder quality of life matters
- Companies in markets where authenticity IS the competitive advantage

## Steps

1. **Ask: Why are we growing?** Is growth serving the mission or just serving metrics?
2. **Audit overhead.** Where is money going that doesn't directly serve product quality or mission?
3. **Protect culture deliberately.** What founding values are at risk as the company scales?
4. **Consider staying small.** Model the business at current scale with optimized operations. Is it viable and fulfilling?
5. **Hire for culture, train for skill.** Skills can be taught. Values alignment cannot.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Not every company should be venture-scaled. Help founders evaluate whether venture growth is genuinely right for their mission and market.
- When a company is losing its soul, the dirtbag framework provides a mirror: what would the founder's climbing-buddy version say about how the company is operating?

## Common Mistakes

1. **Romanticizing smallness.** Small is not inherently virtuous. Some missions require scale.
2. **Using dirtbag culture to justify poor management.** Simplicity and frugality are not excuses for chaos.
3. **Rejecting all growth.** The question is not "should we grow" but "should we grow in a way that preserves what matters."

