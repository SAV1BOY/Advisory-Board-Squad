# Munger Circle of Competence Check Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the organization operates primarily within its circle of competence and explicitly manages decisions at or beyond its boundaries. Knowing what you do not know is more valuable than what you know. This checklist prevents the failure mode of organizations extending into domains where they lack the expertise to evaluate opportunities, risks, or execution quality.

## When to Use
- Before entering a new market, launching a new product category, or making an acquisition.
- When evaluating strategic advice from outside the team's domain expertise.
- When the organization is making a decision in a domain where it has no track record.
- During annual strategic planning to assess the alignment between strategy and competence.

## Gate Criteria

### Competence Mapping
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization's core competencies are documented and regularly updated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The boundaries of competence are explicitly defined: what we are good at and what we are not.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Individual leaders' circles of competence are understood and respected.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team can distinguish between what they know, what they think they know, and what they do not know.

### Boundary Decisions
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decisions outside the circle of competence are identified as such before proceeding.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: External experts are engaged for decisions outside the circle, with their track record verified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The cost of the decision being wrong is assessed more conservatively outside the circle.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The decision to extend the circle is made deliberately with a learning plan, not by accident.

### Expansion Discipline
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Plans to expand into new competence areas include realistic timelines for developing expertise.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Initial investments outside the circle are sized as learning bets, not full commitments.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Mentors, advisors, or hires with relevant competence are engaged before significant commitment.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization distinguishes between adjacent expansions (lower risk) and distant expansions (higher risk).

### Honesty and Humility
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders model intellectual humility by openly acknowledging the limits of their expertise.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization does not confuse success in one domain with competence in another.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Past failures from operating outside the circle are documented and referenced.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team resists the temptation to over-extend when overconfident from recent successes.

## Red Flags
- The company enters a market based on one leader's enthusiasm with no relevant experience.
- The organization assumes transferable skills cover domain-specific knowledge gaps.
- Advisors for a new domain were selected based on availability rather than deep expertise.
- The investment thesis for a new area does not mention the competence gap or how it will be closed.
- The team dismisses domain-specific complexity as "not that different from what we already do."
- No one on the team has failed in the new domain and can share those lessons.

## Escalation Triggers
- A material investment outside the circle of competence is underperforming and the team cannot diagnose why.
- The organization extends into a regulated domain without adequate compliance expertise.
- A key hire for a competence expansion leaves and the knowledge gap is exposed.
- Losses from out-of-circle activities exceed the learning budget allocated.
- External experts unanimously advise against a course of action that the organization is pursuing.
