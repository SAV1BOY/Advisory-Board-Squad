# Hoffman Ten Commandments of Platform

## Purpose

Reid Hoffman's platform framework identifies the principles that make platforms succeed or fail. Platforms are distinct from products: products deliver value directly, while platforms enable third parties to create value. This framework provides the governing rules for building, scaling, and managing platform businesses effectively.

## When to Use

- When evaluating whether to build a product or a platform
- When designing platform governance and ecosystem rules
- When managing the balance between openness and control
- When building marketplace or ecosystem businesses
- When platform growth is stalling and needs strategic diagnosis

## Core Model

### The Ten Commandments

1. **Start with a product, become a platform.** No platform starts as a platform. Build a valuable product first, then open it up.
2. **Eat your own cooking.** Use your platform yourself. Build on your own APIs.
3. **Design for the developer/partner first.** The platform's primary customer is the third party building on it.
4. **Build trust through consistency.** Rules must be stable, predictable, and fairly enforced.
5. **Take a smaller piece of a larger pie.** Generous economics build the ecosystem. Greed kills it.
6. **Create more value than you capture.** Net positive value for all participants is non-negotiable.
7. **Solve the chicken-and-egg problem.** Subsidize one side initially to attract the other.
8. **Maintain quality through curation.** Open platforms attract noise. Curate without over-controlling.
9. **Invest in partner success.** Tools, documentation, support, co-marketing. Partner success IS platform success.
10. **Evolve governance with scale.** Rules that work at 100 participants break at 10,000. Update proactively.

## Steps

1. **Validate the product first.** Build a valuable standalone product with engaged users.
2. **Identify the platform opportunity.** What value can third parties create that you cannot alone?
3. **Design APIs and interfaces.** Simple, powerful, well-documented entry points for partners.
4. **Launch with curated partners.** Start with 5-10 high-quality partners to validate the model.
5. **Define governance rules.** Platform rules, dispute resolution, violation consequences.
6. **Scale deliberately.** Open gradually, maintaining quality at each stage.
7. **Monitor platform health.** Partner count, partner revenue, user satisfaction, take rate, violation rate.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Most companies should be products, not platforms. Platforms require massive scale to work.
- Focus on partner economics. If partners aren't profitable, the platform fails.
- Watch for take-rate creep — extracting too much value over time destroys ecosystems.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Starting as a platform.** Build product value first, then open up.
2. **Taking too much value.** A 30% take rate may work at Apple scale; it kills nascent platforms.
3. **Ignoring partner economics.** Partner profitability is the leading indicator of platform health.
4. **Rules without enforcement or enforcement without rules.** Both destroy trust.
