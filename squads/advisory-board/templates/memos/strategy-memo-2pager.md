# Strategy Memo — 2-Pager

## Template Guidelines
**Word Limit:** 1,500 words maximum (excluding headers and tables)
**Reading Time:** Under 10 minutes
**Use When:** A strategic decision with multiple dimensions, longer time horizons, or significant resource implications that cannot be adequately captured in a 1-page decision memo.

### Executive Summary (75-100 words)
In one paragraph, state: (1) what decision is needed, (2) what you recommend, and (3) why it matters now. A reader who only reads this paragraph should understand the core of the memo.

**Good Example:** "We recommend entering the UK market in Q3 through a partnership with [Partner], investing $800K over 12 months, targeting $2M ARR by month 18. The UK represents our largest untapped market with 3 existing inbound enterprise prospects. Delaying past Q3 risks losing the partnership exclusivity window and ceding the market to [Competitor], who announced UK expansion last month."

**Bad Example:** "This memo discusses our international expansion strategy and recommends we consider expanding to new markets." (No decision, no specifics, no urgency.)

---

### Context and Problem Statement (200-250 words)
Set the stage with facts. Answer these questions:
- What is the current situation?
- What has changed that makes this decision necessary?
- What is the cost of not deciding?
- What prior decisions or strategies are relevant?

Include a brief timeline of how we arrived at this point. Reference decision-log IDs for prior related decisions.

---

### Strategic Analysis (300-400 words)

#### Market Opportunity
Quantify the opportunity with bottom-up analysis. Include TAM/SAM/SOM where relevant. Cite sources.

#### Competitive Dynamics
Who else is pursuing this opportunity? What is their position, and what is our right to win?

#### Strategic Fit
How does this align with our stated strategy? Reference specific strategic priorities. If this represents a departure from current strategy, acknowledge it explicitly.

#### Moat Implications
Does this decision strengthen or weaken our competitive moat? Consider network effects, switching costs, economies of scale, brand, and proprietary technology.

### Recommendation (150-200 words)
State your recommendation clearly. Address:
1. **What:** The specific action you recommend.
2. **Why:** The 2-3 most compelling reasons.
3. **How:** High-level execution approach and phasing.
4. **Guardrails:** Conditions under which you would change course.
5. **Pre-Mortem:** "If this fails in 18 months, the most likely reason is [X]. We mitigate this by [Y]."

## Required Sections Checklist
- [ ] Executive summary is self-contained and under 100 words
- [ ] Context establishes urgency and references prior decisions
- [ ] Strategic analysis covers market, competition, fit, and moat
- [ ] At least 3 options with quantified costs, returns, and risks
- [ ] Weighted comparison matrix completed
- [ ] Recommendation includes pre-mortem and reversal conditions
- [ ] Implementation roadmap includes phase gates and kill criteria
- [ ] All data points are sourced with confidence levels
- [ ] Total word count under 1,500

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
1. **Boiling the ocean:** If the memo needs more than 1,500 words, the scope is too broad. Split into multiple decisions or use a dedicated brief format.
2. **Analysis without synthesis:** Presenting data without drawing conclusions forces the reader to do your thinking.
3. **Anchoring bias:** Presenting your preferred option first and most favorably. Present options as neutrally as possible; save advocacy for the Recommendation section.
4. **Ignoring second-order effects:** Consider how this decision interacts with other strategic initiatives, team capacity, and market positioning.
5. **No kill criteria:** Every strategic initiative needs predefined conditions for pulling the plug. Without them, sunk-cost bias takes over.
