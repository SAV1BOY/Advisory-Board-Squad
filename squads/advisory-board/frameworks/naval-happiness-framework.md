# Naval Happiness Framework

## Purpose

Naval Ravikant's Happiness Framework reframes happiness from an external achievement to an internal skill that can be developed deliberately. For advisory board members, this framework is relevant because unhappy founders make poor decisions, burned-out teams underperform, and organizations that ignore well-being eventually collapse. Sustainable high performance requires sustainable well-being. This framework provides the principles for building happiness as a foundational capability.

## When to Use

- When coaching founders showing signs of burnout or chronic stress
- When organizational culture is optimized for output but not for sustainability
- When decision-making is being distorted by anxiety, fear, or exhaustion
- During personal development planning for leaders
- When evaluating whether a company's pace is sustainable or self-destructive

## Core Model

### Naval's Core Principles on Happiness

**1. Happiness is the absence of desire.** Every time you desire something you don't have, you suffer. Happiness is being present with what is, not chasing what isn't.

**2. Happiness is a skill, not a circumstance.** It can be trained through meditation, mindfulness, and deliberate practice. It is not the result of achieving goals.

**3. A calm mind makes better decisions.** Anxiety and urgency produce reactive, short-term decisions. Calm produces reflective, long-term decisions. Happiness is a strategic advantage.

**4. The three big happinesses:** Health (physical well-being), wealth (financial freedom from anxiety), and relationships (meaningful connection). But none of these guarantees happiness — they remove obstacles to it.

**5. Desire is a contract with yourself to be unhappy until you get what you want.** Choose desires carefully. Every desire you add is a source of suffering until fulfilled.

### The Happiness Audit
- **Physical state:** Sleep, exercise, nutrition, health
- **Mental state:** Anxiety level, rumination, presence, calm
- **Social state:** Quality of close relationships, belonging, connection
- **Financial state:** Not wealth, but freedom from financial anxiety
- **Purpose state:** Meaningful work, contribution, alignment of actions with values

## Steps

1. **Audit your current state** across the five dimensions above.
2. **Identify the biggest deficit.** Which dimension, if improved, would have the greatest impact?
3. **Reduce unnecessary desires.** List what you are currently striving for. For each, ask: "Will achieving this actually make me happier?" Remove desires that won't.
4. **Build daily practices.** Meditation, exercise, journaling, gratitude — these are not luxuries but foundational capabilities.
5. **Design your environment for happiness.** People, places, and routines that support calm and presence.
6. **Monitor and adjust.** Weekly check-in on the five dimensions.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- A founder's personal well-being is a leading indicator of company health. If the founder is burning out, the company will follow.
- Normalize conversations about well-being in board settings. "How are you, really?" is a legitimate board question.
- Model sustainable behavior yourself. Advisors who glorify 80-hour weeks while advising "work-life balance" are not credible.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Treating happiness as a reward for achievement.** "I'll be happy when..." is the formula for perpetual unhappiness.
2. **Ignoring physical health.** Sleep deprivation, poor nutrition, and no exercise destroy both happiness and decision quality.
3. **Confusing busyness with meaning.** Activity is not purpose. Many busy people are profoundly unhappy.
4. **Isolation.** Founders often isolate under stress. Connection is a happiness requirement, not a luxury.
