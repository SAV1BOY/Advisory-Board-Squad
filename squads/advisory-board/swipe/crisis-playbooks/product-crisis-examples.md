# Product Crisis Examples — Playbook

## Overview
Product crises—security breaches, data loss, critical bugs, safety issues—require rapid, coordinated response. The difference between a manageable incident and a catastrophic crisis is usually the speed and quality of the response in the first 24-48 hours.

## Response Framework

### Phase 1: Triage (0-4 hours)
1. **Assess scope:** How many users are affected? What's the severity?
2. **Contain:** Stop the bleeding. Take systems offline if necessary.
3. **Assemble the team:** CEO, CTO, Legal, Communications, Customer Success
4. **Classify severity:** Critical (safety/security), Major (data loss/extended outage), Minor (degraded experience)
5. **Initial communication:** Internal notification to all employees. External holding statement if media is aware.

### Phase 2: Response (4-24 hours)
1. **Root cause investigation:** What happened and why? (Preliminary—full root cause may take longer)
2. **Fix implementation:** Deploy the fix or workaround
3. **Customer communication:** Direct notification to affected users. Be specific about what happened, what we're doing, and what they should do.
4. **Stakeholder briefing:** Board notification, investor notification if material
5. **Media response:** If media is covering it, prepared statement with specifics

### Phase 3: Recovery (24-72 hours)
1. **Verification:** Confirm the fix is working. Monitor for recurrence.
2. **Customer follow-up:** What are we doing to make affected customers whole? (Credits, refunds, enhanced service)
3. **Root cause report:** Detailed internal document on what happened, why, and what changes prevent recurrence
4. **Process improvements:** Implement changes to monitoring, testing, deployment, or architecture

### Phase 4: Post-Mortem (1-2 weeks)
1. **Blameless post-mortem:** Focus on systems and processes, not individuals
2. **Lessons learned documentation:** Distributed to the entire engineering organization
3. **Board review:** Present the incident, response, and structural improvements to the board
4. **External transparency:** Publish a public post-mortem if appropriate (builds trust)

## Communication Principles
- **Speed over perfection:** A quick, honest acknowledgment beats a delayed, polished response
- **Empathy first:** "We understand this impacts your business and we take that seriously"
- **Specificity:** "X data was exposed for Y users between dates A and B" beats "a security incident occurred"
- **Accountability:** "This was our failure and here's what we're doing to fix it" beats "unauthorized third party..."
- **Updates:** Commit to regular updates (every 4-8 hours during active crisis) and deliver them

## Board Application
- The board should be notified within 4 hours of any Critical-severity product crisis
- Board should receive a briefing within 24 hours with facts, response, and preliminary assessment
- The board's role during a crisis is to provide support and counsel, not to micromanage the response
- Post-crisis, the board should review systemic improvements and ensure they're implemented
