# Org Health Memo Examples

## Overview

Organizational health memos bring the same rigor to people, culture, and team dynamics that financial memos bring to capital allocation. Most boards review financials monthly and culture annually (if at all). This file provides templates for regular org health reporting that boards should expect.

## Quarterly Org Health Memo

### Structure
1. **Executive Summary** — 3-5 sentences on the state of organizational health
2. **Team Metrics Dashboard**
   - Headcount: current, plan, variance
   - Attrition: voluntary, involuntary, regrettable
   - Time-to-hire: average by role type
   - Engagement score: trend over 4 quarters
   - eNPS: employee Net Promoter Score
3. **Leadership Team Assessment**
   - Performance rating for each direct report of CEO (Green/Yellow/Red)
   - New hires in last quarter and early signal on fit
   - Leadership gaps: roles needed but not yet filled
4. **Culture Health Indicators**
   - Glassdoor/Blind sentiment trend
   - Internal survey highlights (top 3 strengths, top 3 concerns)
   - Culture incidents: any values violations or concerning patterns
5. **Organizational Risk Register**
   - Key-person risk: who is irreplaceable and what's the backup plan?
   - Burnout risk: which teams are overloaded?
   - Succession risk: what happens if the CEO or any C-suite member departs?
6. **Recommendations** — 2-3 specific actions for board consideration

### Example Section: Leadership Team Assessment

| Executive | Role | Rating | Commentary |
|-----------|------|--------|------------|
| Sarah Chen | CTO | Green | Shipped v3 on time, team morale high, strong technical vision |
| Marcus Jones | VP Sales | Yellow | Q2 target missed by 12%, pipeline building but conversion lagging |
| Priya Sharma | CFO | Green | Clean audit, improved reporting, proactive cash management |
| David Kim | VP Product | Red | Third quarter of missed roadmap commitments, team attrition 25% |

**Action Items:**
- David Kim: 30-day performance improvement plan. If no improvement, transition to new VP Product by end of Q3.
- Marcus Jones: Weekly pipeline review with CEO for 60 days. Evaluate whether issue is people, process, or market.

## Culture Crisis Memo

### Structure
1. **What happened** — Factual account of the incident
2. **Impact** — On people, brand, operations, legal exposure
3. **Root cause analysis** — Is this isolated or systemic?
4. **Immediate response** — What has already been done
5. **Structural changes** — What will prevent recurrence
6. **Communication plan** — Internal and external messaging

## Succession Planning Memo

### Structure
1. **Critical roles** — Which roles are mission-critical?
2. **Readiness assessment** — For each critical role: emergency successor, 12-month successor, 24-month successor
3. **Development plans** — What is being done to prepare successors?
4. **External pipeline** — Relationships maintained with potential external candidates
5. **Board role** — What the board needs to do to support succession readiness

## Board Application

- Require quarterly org health memos alongside financial reports
- Treat a "Red" leadership rating as seriously as a revenue miss
- Ensure succession plans exist for CEO and every C-suite role
- Use engagement trends as a leading indicator of future performance
