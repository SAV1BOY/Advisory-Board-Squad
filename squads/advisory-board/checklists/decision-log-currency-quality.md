# Decision Log Currency Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the decision log is maintained as a living document that provides an authoritative, searchable record of all significant decisions, their context, rationale, and outcomes. A stale or incomplete decision log leads to repeated debates, inconsistent action, and loss of institutional memory. Currency means the log is current, complete, and actively used.

## When to Use
- Monthly to verify the decision log is up to date.
- Before any board meeting to ensure decisions from the last meeting are logged.
- When onboarding new board members or executives who need decision context.
- When a decision is questioned or revisited to reference the original rationale.
- During annual governance audits.

## Gate Criteria

### Completeness
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Every decision from every board meeting is logged within 48 hours.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each entry includes: date, decision text, decision-maker, rationale summary, and affected parties.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The log includes decisions made between meetings (email votes, delegated authority decisions).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decisions that were deferred or tabled are logged with the reason and expected revisit date.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Material decisions made by the CEO under delegated authority are logged.

### Searchability and Access
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The log is searchable by date, topic, decision-maker, and status.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Related decisions are cross-referenced for easy navigation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The log is accessible to all board members and authorized management.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A summary view or dashboard shows recent decisions and pending follow-ups.

### Follow-Up Tracking
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each decision entry tracks implementation status (pending, in progress, complete, abandoned).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Outcomes are recorded when a decision is fully implemented.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decisions that did not produce expected results are flagged for review.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Superseded decisions are marked with a reference to the replacing decision.

### Quality and Integrity
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decision entries are written in neutral language that captures the decision accurately.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Dissenting views are recorded as part of the rationale section.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The log has an owner responsible for maintenance and currency.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: An audit trail exists showing when entries were created or modified.

## Red Flags
- The most recent entry in the decision log is more than 30 days old despite active decision-making.
- Decisions are recorded without rationale, making future interpretation impossible.
- The log is maintained by someone who does not attend meetings and relies on secondhand accounts.
- Multiple versions of the decision log exist with no authoritative source.
- No one references the decision log during meetings or planning sessions.
- Key decisions are missing because they were made informally and never documented.

## Escalation Triggers
- A material decision is discovered that was never logged.
- A decision is reversed without acknowledging the original decision or the change in rationale.
- The decision log contradicts the meeting minutes.
- A legal or regulatory inquiry requires decision records and the log is inadequate.
- Two or more leadership team members have conflicting understandings of what was decided.
