# Hiring Questions

Phrases for evaluating executive candidates — testing judgment, self-awareness, values alignment, and track record. Use these in structured interviews, reference checks, and hiring debriefs.

## Testing Judgment

1. **"Tell me about a decision you made with incomplete information that turned out to be wrong. What did you learn?"**
   Use to assess how candidates handle uncertainty and failure. Great executives learn from bad outcomes; mediocre ones blame circumstances.

2. **"What is the most important strategic decision you influenced in your last role? Walk me through the options you considered."**
   Use to test strategic thinking depth. Look for structured reasoning, consideration of alternatives, and honest assessment of trade-offs.

3. **"Describe a time you changed your mind on something important. What new information or argument persuaded you?"**
   Use to assess intellectual flexibility. Executives who never change their minds are either not learning or not honest.

4. **"What is the hardest trade-off you have made in your career? What did you give up, and why?"**
   Use to assess values in action. Trade-offs reveal what someone truly prioritizes when they cannot have everything.

5. **"Tell me about a time you said no to a popular idea. What was the idea, and why did you push back?"**
   Use to assess independence of thought and courage. Following consensus is easy; disagreeing constructively is rare and valuable.

## Testing Self-Awareness

6. **"What are you not good at? Give me a specific example from the last year."**
   Use to assess self-awareness and honesty. Candidates who cannot name a genuine weakness are either unaware or performing.

7. **"If I called your last three direct reports and asked them what it's like to work for you, what would they say?"**
   Use to surface the candidate's perception of their leadership impact. Follow up with actual reference calls to validate.

8. **"What feedback have you received more than once that you have not fully addressed?"**
   Use to assess growth trajectory. Persistent feedback that goes unaddressed is a pattern, not an anomaly.

9. **"What kind of company or team brings out your best work? What kind brings out your worst?"**
   Use to assess fit. Self-aware candidates can articulate their operating conditions. This predicts success or failure in your specific environment.

10. **"What is the biggest misconception people have about you?"**
    Use to understand how the candidate perceives the gap between their intent and their impact. The answer reveals both self-awareness and communication style.

## Testing Values Alignment

11. **"Tell me about a time you witnessed something unethical at work. What did you do?"**
    Use to assess moral courage. The answer reveals whether the candidate acts on values or merely holds them.

12. **"How do you handle a direct report who delivers great results but treats people poorly?"**
    Use to test the candidate's stance on the performance-values trade-off. This is one of the hardest leadership challenges. There is no right universal answer, but the reasoning matters.

13. **"What would you do if a board member asked you to do something you believed was wrong for the company?"**
    Use to assess how the candidate navigates authority and integrity. Look for directness, not compliance.

14. **"Describe the best team culture you have experienced. What made it work? What was your role in creating it?"**
    Use to understand what the candidate values in a team environment and whether they take ownership of culture.

15. **"What would cause you to leave this role in the first year?"**
    Use to surface expectations, deal-breakers, and risk factors early. Honest answers prevent mismatches.

## Testing Capability

16. **"If you started this role on Monday, what would you do in the first 30 days? Be specific."**
    Use to assess preparation, judgment about sequencing, and humility about the learning curve. Great answers start with listening and asking questions, not with imposing a plan.

17. **"What is the most complex organizational problem you have solved? How did you diagnose it?"**
    Use to assess systems thinking. Look for someone who identifies root causes, not just symptoms.

18. **"Tell me about a time you built something from zero — a team, a product, a process. What was your approach?"**
    Use to assess builder instinct. Building from zero requires a different skill set than optimizing something that exists.

19. **"How do you make decisions when your team disagrees and you need to move forward?"**
    Use to assess decision-making style under pressure. Look for a process that values input but doesn't require consensus.

20. **"What is your approach to performance management? How do you handle someone who is underperforming?"**
    Use to assess management rigor. Vague answers suggest the candidate avoids difficult conversations.

## Reference Check Phrases

21. **"On a scale of 1-10, how would you rate [candidate]? ... What would it take to make them a 10?"**
    Use to surface the gap between good and great. The "what would it take" follow-up reveals specific development areas.

22. **"Would you hire this person again? In what role?"**
    Use as the strongest signal in reference checks. A "no" or hesitation is significant.

23. **"What should I know about this person that they probably won't tell me themselves?"**
    Use to surface blind spots. References often share critical context with this open-ended prompt.

24. **"How did this person handle their worst day at your company?"**
    Use to assess resilience and composure under pressure. Leadership is most visible when things go wrong.
