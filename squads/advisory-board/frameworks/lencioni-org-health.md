# Lencioni Organizational Health

## Purpose

Patrick Lencioni argues that organizational health — the alignment of strategy, culture, management, and communication — is the single greatest competitive advantage available to any company. Smart organizations have strong strategy and expertise. Healthy organizations have minimal politics, high morale, low turnover, and high productivity. Most leaders focus on being smart; the advantage goes to those who also get healthy. This framework provides the diagnostic and action plan for building organizational health.

## When to Use

- When an organization has good strategy but poor execution
- When politics and silos are consuming energy that should go to customers and products
- When turnover is high despite competitive compensation
- When the organization "feels" dysfunctional but the cause is hard to pinpoint
- During organizational assessments for board review

## Core Model

### The Four Disciplines of Organizational Health

**1. Build a Cohesive Leadership Team:** The leadership team must trust each other, engage in productive conflict, commit to decisions, hold each other accountable, and focus on collective results (see Five Dysfunctions).

**2. Create Clarity:** The leadership team must be aligned on six critical questions (see Six Questions framework): Why do we exist? How do we behave? What do we do? How will we succeed? What is most important right now? Who does what?

**3. Over-Communicate Clarity:** Once clarity exists, it must be communicated repeatedly through every channel until employees can recite it. Leaders drastically underestimate the repetition required.

**4. Reinforce Clarity Through Human Systems:** Hiring, onboarding, performance management, compensation, and promotion must all reinforce the clarity. Misaligned systems undermine even the clearest communication.

## Steps

1. **Assess current health** using employee surveys, leadership team assessment, and cultural observation.
2. **Build leadership team cohesion** using the Five Dysfunctions model.
3. **Answer the Six Questions** as a leadership team until there is genuine alignment (not just agreement).
4. **Create a communication rhythm** that repeats the Six Question answers at every opportunity.
5. **Audit human systems** (hiring, reviews, promotions) for alignment with the Six Questions.
6. **Repeat the cycle** quarterly. Organizational health requires ongoing maintenance.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Organizational health is a leading indicator of performance. Healthy organizations with mediocre strategy outperform unhealthy organizations with brilliant strategy.
- When a portfolio company is underperforming, assess health before reassessing strategy. The strategy may be fine; the organization may be sick.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Prioritizing smart over healthy.** Strategy, technology, and talent acquisition get 90% of attention. Health gets 10%. This ratio should be closer to 50/50.
2. **Delegating health to HR.** Organizational health is a leadership team responsibility, not an HR program.
3. **Under-communicating clarity.** If you are not tired of repeating the message, you have not said it enough.
4. **Confusing perks with health.** Healthy organizations may or may not have great perks. The defining feature is alignment, trust, and minimal politics.
