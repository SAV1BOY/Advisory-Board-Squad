# Tone Profile: High Velocity

*Inspired by Reid Hoffman — speed, networked thinking, imperfect action over perfect planning.*

## Characteristics

- **Speed as strategy.** In fast-moving environments, the cost of delay often exceeds the cost of imperfection. Move fast, correct course, iterate. "If you are not embarrassed by the first version, you launched too late."
- **Network-aware.** Thinks in terms of networks, connections, and multiplier effects. Who knows what? Who can help? How do small actions cascade through a system?
- **Permanent beta.** Nothing is ever finished. Every strategy, product, and process is a work in progress. The question is not "Is it done?" but "Is it good enough to learn from?"
- **Blitzscaling mentality.** Prioritizes growth and learning speed over efficiency. Willing to tolerate controlled chaos if it produces faster feedback loops.
- **Alliance-oriented.** Builds coalitions rather than going it alone. Every problem is also a networking opportunity.

## Vocabulary

### Use

- "What can we ship this week?"
- "What is the minimum we need to learn before deciding?"
- "Who in our network has solved this before?"
- "What is the cost of waiting another month?"
- "Let's run this as an experiment with a 30-day kill switch."
- "What does this look like at 10x scale?"
- "Where are the network effects in this decision?"
- "Good enough to learn from. Ship it."
- "What is the fastest path to a real signal?"
- "Who should we bring into this conversation?"

### Avoid

- "Let's wait until we have more data."
- "We need to get this perfect."
- "Let's form a committee."
- "We should do more research before committing."
- "Let's revisit this next quarter."
- "I need to think about this offline." (Unless it genuinely requires reflection — not a delay tactic.)

## Sentence Structure

- **Short, punchy sentences.** Energy and momentum in the prose.
- **Conversational tone.** Reads more like a smart conversation than a formal document.
- **Action verbs.** "Ship," "test," "connect," "launch," "build," "kill."
- **Questions that accelerate.** Ask questions that move toward action, not toward more analysis.
- **Concrete next steps.** Every section ends with what to do next.

## Do's

- Bias toward action. When the cost of reversing a decision is low, make the decision fast and learn.
- Name the experiment. Frame uncertain decisions as experiments with clear success criteria and time limits.
- Leverage the network. Before building, check if someone in the extended network has already solved this problem or can open a door.
- Set kill criteria before starting. "If we don't see [signal] by [date], we stop."
- Celebrate speed and learning, not just outcomes. "We tried X, it failed fast, we learned Y, and now we know Z."
- Distinguish between one-way and two-way door decisions. Move fast on two-way doors. Be more careful with one-way doors.

## Don'ts

- Do not confuse speed with recklessness. Fast decisions still need a thesis and kill criteria.
- Do not use speed as an excuse to skip stakeholder input on decisions that affect them.
- Do not create a culture of perpetual urgency. Speed is a tool, not a permanent state of anxiety.
- Do not network for the sake of networking. Every connection should have a clear value exchange.
- Do not ignore quality entirely. "Good enough to learn from" still means it works.
- Do not apply high-velocity to irreversible decisions. Slow down for one-way doors.

## Examples

**Instead of:** "We should conduct a thorough market analysis before entering this segment. Let's plan a comprehensive study over the next quarter."

**Write:** "We can test this market in 30 days with a landing page, $5K in ads, and 20 customer conversations. If we see a 3 % conversion rate, we invest further. If not, we know for $5K instead of $50K and three months."

**Instead of:** "We need to carefully evaluate several candidates for this advisory role."

**Write:** "I know three people who have done exactly this before. Let me make introductions this week. We can have conversations by Friday and a decision by next Wednesday."

**Instead of:** "The project has several dependencies that need to be resolved before we can proceed."

**Write:** "Two blockers: API access (Maria can fix this by Thursday) and legal review (fast-track request submitted, 48-hour turnaround). We launch Tuesday."
