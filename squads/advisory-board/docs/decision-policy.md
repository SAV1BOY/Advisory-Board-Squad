# Decision Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines how the Advisory Board makes, records, reviews, and learns from decisions. It ensures consistency, transparency, and continuous improvement in decision quality.

## Decision Types

### Type 1: Irreversible, High-Impact
Decisions that are difficult or impossible to reverse and have material impact on the company. Examples: M&A, C-suite hiring, market entry/exit, major capital commitments.

**Process:** Full board deliberation. Pre-read required. Devil's-Advocate counter-case required. Formal vote. Decision recorded with full rationale and dissent.

### Type 2: Reversible, High-Impact
Decisions with material impact that can be reversed or adjusted. Examples: strategic initiative launch, partnership terms, organizational restructuring.

**Process:** Board discussion with pre-read. Recommendation from Strategy-Analyst. Devil's-Advocate review. Chair calls the decision. Recorded with rationale.

### Type 3: Low-Impact, Operational
Decisions that the executive team can make without board involvement. Examples: tactical execution choices, internal process changes, team-level resource allocation.

**Process:** Delegated to the CEO/executive team. Board is informed if the decision has strategic implications.

## Decision-Making Framework

### Step 1: Frame the decision
- State the question in one sentence.
- Classify: Type 1, 2, or 3.
- Identify: who is the decision-maker? Who is an advisor? Who is informed?

### Step 2: Generate options
- Every decision requires at least three options (not just "yes/no/defer").
- Each option is evaluated against explicit criteria.

### Step 3: Evaluate
- For Type 1 decisions: scenario analysis (base/upside/downside), expected value calculation, risk assessment.
- For Type 2 decisions: criteria-based evaluation with pros/cons analysis.
- Devil's-Advocate presents the strongest counter-case for the leading option.

### Step 4: Decide
- Board-Chair calls for the decision.
- If consensus: confirm and record.
- If dissent: record the dissent, apply disagree-and-commit protocol.
- If not ready: identify the specific missing information and defer with a date.

### Step 5: Record
Every decision is logged by Decision-Tracker:

```
Decision ID: [YYYY-MM-DD-NNN]
Date: [YYYY-MM-DD]
Type: [1/2/3]
Question: [One-sentence framing]
Options considered: [List]
Decision: [What was decided]
Rationale: [Why this option was chosen]
Dissent: [Who disagreed and why]
Owner: [Who is responsible for execution]
Review date: [When to evaluate the outcome]
Kill criteria: [If applicable, conditions that trigger reversal]
```

### Step 6: Review
- Quarterly: lightweight review of decisions from the past 90 days.
- Annually: deep decision quality review (workflow 12).

## Thresholds

| Category | Threshold | Board Role |
|---|---|---|
| Capital commitment | >10 % of annual budget | Board approval required |
| M&A | >5 % of company value | Board approval required |
| Executive hiring | C-suite | Board participates in interviews, approves hire |
| Fundraising | Any external round | Board reviews and approves terms |
| Partnership | Exclusive or >12 months | Board reviews and approves |
| Strategic pivot | Change to core strategy | Board discussion and approval |

Thresholds are reviewed annually and updated by the Board-Chair.

## Delegation

Below threshold, decisions are fully delegated to the CEO and executive team. Delegation means:
- The board trusts the executive team to make good decisions.
- The executive team informs the board of material below-threshold decisions in the next board meeting.
- The board does not second-guess delegated decisions retroactively.

## Decision Quality Standards

A good decision, regardless of outcome, has:
- A clear question.
- Multiple options considered.
- Explicit evaluation criteria.
- Evidence-based analysis.
- Dissent invited and recorded.
- A specific owner and review date.
- Kill criteria for reversible decisions.

A bad decision process, even with a good outcome, should be flagged and improved.

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Description | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Decision by drift | No explicit decision; the default becomes the choice | Chair calls the question explicitly |
| Decision by exhaustion | Group agrees because they're tired of discussing | Chair calls a break or defers rather than forcing a fatigued decision |
| Decision without dissent | Unanimous agreement with no challenge | Devil's-Advocate is mandated to present a counter-case for Type 1 decisions |
| Decision without record | Decision made but not logged | Decision-Tracker reads back every decision before moving on |
| Decision without owner | Everyone assumes someone else will execute | Every decision has one named owner |
| Decision without review | Decisions are never revisited | Every decision has a review date |
