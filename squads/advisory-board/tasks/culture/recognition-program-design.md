# Task: Recognition Program Design

## Objective
Design and implement an employee recognition program that reinforces company values, drives desired behaviors, increases engagement, and creates visible cultural rituals. The program should be authentic, scalable, and resistant to becoming performative or political.

## Trigger
- Company reaches scale where informal recognition no longer reaches everyone (typically 30+ employees)
- Engagement survey reveals recognition as a gap area
- Culture audit identifies weak reinforcement of stated values
- Leadership team observes that high performers feel undervalued
- Post-acquisition integration requires unified cultural rituals
- Annual culture evolution planning cycle

## Agents Involved
- **Lead:** CHRO or People Lead
- **Support:** CEO (executive sponsorship and modeling), functional leaders (program champions), finance (budget)
- **Advisory:** Board members with experience in culture-driven organizations

## Pre-Requisites
- [ ] Company values clearly defined and documented
- [ ] Engagement survey data identifying recognition gaps
- [ ] Budget parameters approved by finance
- [ ] Benchmarking data on recognition programs at comparable companies
- [ ] Input gathered from employees across levels and functions
- [ ] Current informal recognition practices inventoried

## Steps

1. **Define program objectives and principles (Week 1).** Establish what the program must achieve: values reinforcement, peer-to-peer connection, retention, performance motivation. Set guiding principles: authenticity over formality, behavior-specific over generic praise, timely over accumulated, inclusive across all roles and levels.

2. **Map recognition categories (Week 1-2).** Design categories that align with company values and strategic priorities. Examples: values-in-action awards (one per core value), peer nominations, milestone celebrations (tenure, project completion), customer impact recognition, innovation spotlights. Avoid generic "employee of the month" models that create competition rather than culture.

3. **Design recognition mechanisms (Week 2-3).** Build multiple channels that suit different recognition needs:
   - **Real-time:** Slack/Teams channel for instant peer-to-peer shoutouts tied to specific values
   - **Weekly:** Team meeting recognition moments with specific behavior callouts
   - **Monthly:** All-hands spotlight featuring stories of values in action
   - **Quarterly:** Peer-nominated awards with meaningful (not expensive) rewards
   - **Annual:** Significant recognition events tied to company milestones

4. **Define reward structure (Week 3).** Determine the mix of intrinsic and extrinsic rewards. Research consistently shows that specific, public acknowledgment of contribution matters more than monetary awards. Design a tiered approach: public acknowledgment (most frequent), small tokens or experiences (quarterly), meaningful rewards (annual). Set budget per employee per year.

5. **Build nomination and selection process (Week 3-4).** Create transparent criteria for each recognition category. For peer-nominated awards, design a simple nomination form that requires a specific story demonstrating the value in action. Define selection committees that rotate to prevent cliques. Ensure geographic and functional representation.

6. **Develop communication and launch plan (Week 4).** Introduce the program with leadership modeling — CEO and executives publicly recognize team members using the new system before asking anyone else to participate. Create program guides, FAQs, and training for managers on effective recognition practices.

7. **Implement and iterate (Week 5+).** Launch with a pilot group or single mechanism, gather feedback, and iterate before full rollout. Monitor adoption metrics weekly during launch. Address low adoption immediately — it usually signals that the program feels inauthentic or burdensome.

8. **Measure and report (Ongoing).** Track participation rates, sentiment data, correlation with engagement scores, and retention metrics. Report to leadership quarterly and board annually as part of culture health reporting.

## Frameworks to Apply
- **Culture as Operating System** — Recognition is a cultural feedback loop; design it to reinforce the OS
- **Incentives Design** — Ensure recognition incentives align with desired behaviors, not gaming
- **Lencioni Organizational Health** — Recognition contributes to clarity and reinforcement of values
- **Brene Clear Is Kind** — Specific, honest recognition is more valuable than generic praise

## Checklists

### Quality Gate
- [ ] Every recognition category maps directly to a stated company value or strategic behavior
- [ ] Program includes peer-to-peer mechanisms, not only top-down recognition
- [ ] Criteria are transparent and consistently applied
- [ ] Budget approved and sustainable at current and projected headcount
- [ ] Inclusivity reviewed: all roles, levels, geographies, and work arrangements can participate
- [ ] Anti-gaming measures in place (rotating committees, story-based nominations)
- [ ] Manager training on effective recognition practices scheduled
- [ ] Measurement plan defined with baseline metrics captured before launch

## Output
- Recognition program design document with categories, mechanisms, and budget
- Nomination forms and selection criteria
- Communication and launch plan
- Manager training materials on recognition best practices
- Measurement dashboard with baseline metrics

## Handoffs
- Program metrics feed into culture-health-report and culture-audit
- Budget feeds into budget-review
- Manager training connects to leadership-coaching
- Engagement impact feeds into team-health-check
- Values reinforcement data supports values-integrity-check

## Metrics
| Metric | Target | Measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Participation rate | >70% of employees give or receive recognition quarterly | Platform/survey data |
| Recognition distribution | No team or level excluded for >1 quarter | Distribution analysis |
| Engagement score lift | Measurable improvement in recognition-related survey items within 6 months | Pulse surveys |
| Manager adoption | >90% of managers actively participate | Usage tracking |
| Voluntary turnover impact | Recognized employees show lower turnover | Retention cohort analysis |
| Values awareness | >85% of employees can name core values | Survey data |
