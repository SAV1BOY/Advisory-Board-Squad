# Brene Brown Courageous Conversations Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the organization is equipped to have difficult but necessary conversations with courage and skill. Brene Brown's research shows that avoiding hard conversations is more damaging than having them imperfectly. This checklist provides a framework for engaging in conversations about performance, conflict, feedback, and uncomfortable truths with clarity, empathy, and accountability.

## When to Use
- When a difficult conversation needs to happen and is being avoided.
- When providing critical feedback on performance, behavior, or decisions.
- When addressing conflict between team members or leaders.
- When surfacing uncomfortable truths that the organization needs to face.
- During training and coaching for leaders on conversation skills.

## Gate Criteria

### Preparation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The purpose of the conversation is clear: what needs to change and why.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Facts are separated from assumptions and stories before the conversation begins.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The desired outcome is defined: what does success look like after this conversation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The speaker's own emotional state is assessed: can they be clear, kind, and direct.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The setting is appropriate: private, unhurried, and without power imbalances.

### Delivery
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The conversation leads with observed behavior, not character judgment.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The impact of the behavior is described in specific, concrete terms.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The speaker owns their perspective ("I observed" / "I feel") rather than making universal claims.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The other person is given genuine space to respond, explain, and be heard.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The conversation maintains respect for the person even when addressing the behavior.

### Resolution
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A specific, measurable agreement about what changes is reached.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Support needed for the change is identified and offered.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A follow-up date is set to review progress.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Both parties leave the conversation with clarity about expectations and next steps.

### Organizational Capacity
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders are trained in having courageous conversations, not just expected to figure it out.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Courageous conversations are normalized as a positive leadership behavior, not a negative one.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization tracks whether difficult conversations are happening or being avoided.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders model courageous conversations by having them publicly when appropriate.

## Red Flags
- Known performance or behavior issues persist for months without direct conversation.
- Feedback is delivered through third parties rather than directly.
- Difficult conversations happen only during annual reviews, not in real time.
- Leaders describe their team as "high-performing" but avoid addressing visible issues.
- The organizational culture labels direct feedback as "aggressive" or "unkind."
- Conversations about problems focus on who to blame rather than what to fix.

## Escalation Triggers
- A leader repeatedly avoids a necessary conversation despite coaching and support.
- An unaddressed issue escalates to a formal complaint, legal action, or public incident.
- Team performance suffers because feedback is not flowing.
- Multiple employees cite lack of direct communication as a frustration in engagement surveys.
- A trust breach occurs because something was discussed behind someone's back instead of with them.
