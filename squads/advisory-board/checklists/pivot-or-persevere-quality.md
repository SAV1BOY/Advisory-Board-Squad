# Pivot or Persevere Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Provides a structured framework for making the critical decision of whether to change strategic direction (pivot) or maintain course with adjustments (persevere). This is one of the most consequential decisions a company can face. This checklist prevents both premature abandonment of strategies that need more time and stubborn persistence with strategies that are failing.

## When to Use
- When key metrics are consistently below expectations for two or more quarters.
- When a significant market or competitive shift occurs that challenges current strategy.
- When the board or leadership team is divided on strategic direction.
- At predetermined strategic review milestones.
- When fundraising or growth metrics suggest the current approach may not work.

## Gate Criteria

### Evidence Assessment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Current performance data is compiled across all key metrics (financial, product, customer).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Performance is assessed against the original hypotheses and milestones, not moving targets.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The analysis separates execution failure from strategy failure.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer feedback and behavior data (not just financial data) is included.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Sufficient time has passed to evaluate the current strategy fairly (not reacting to noise).

### Honest Diagnosis
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The sunk cost fallacy is explicitly guarded against: prior investment is not a reason to continue.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Confirmation bias is checked: the team has sought disconfirming evidence, not just supporting data.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team can articulate what has been learned that changes the outlook.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: External perspectives (advisors, customers, industry experts) have been sought.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The root cause of underperformance is identified and the team agrees on the diagnosis.

### Pivot Evaluation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: If pivoting, the new direction is based on validated learning, not just a new hypothesis.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The pivot preserves the assets, knowledge, and relationships that are still valuable.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The cost and risk of the pivot are quantified (financial, talent, customer, time).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team has the capabilities and resources to execute the new direction.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The new strategy is evaluated against the same rigor as the original (not held to a lower standard because of urgency).

### Persevere Evaluation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: If persevering, specific changes to execution or approach are identified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: New milestones and a timeline for the next evaluation are set.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Additional resources or capability needs are identified and funded.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The conditions that would trigger reconsideration are defined in advance.

### Decision Process
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The decision-making process includes diverse perspectives (board, management, frontline).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The decision is documented with rationale for future reference.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Communication plan for the decision covers all stakeholders.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The decision is made with a clear timeline; deliberation does not become paralysis.

## Red Flags
- The team is pivoting because they are bored with the current strategy, not because evidence demands it.
- The team is persevering because of sunk cost emotional attachment, not because evidence supports it.
- No learning has been extracted from the current strategy's performance.
- The pivot is to a completely unrelated market with no knowledge transfer from current experience.
- The decision is being made by one person without board or leadership team input.
- The same pivot decision has been debated for months without resolution.

## Escalation Triggers
- Cash runway is insufficient to complete either the pivot or the perseverance plan.
- Key team members are leaving because they have lost confidence in the direction.
- Customers or partners are defecting at an accelerating rate.
- The board and management team are fundamentally divided on the direction.
- External advisors unanimously recommend a different direction from what the team is pursuing.
