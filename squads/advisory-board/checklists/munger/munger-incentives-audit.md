# Munger Incentives Audit Checklist

## Purpose
Applies Charlie Munger's foundational insight that incentives drive behavior more reliably than any other factor. "Show me the incentive and I will show you the outcome." This audit goes beyond compensation to examine all incentive structures: financial, social, career, psychological, and structural. It identifies where incentives are misaligned with desired outcomes and where perverse incentives exist that rational actors will inevitably exploit.

## When to Use
- When diagnosing unexpected or undesirable behavior in the organization.
- When designing compensation, bonus, or equity programs.
- Before approving any initiative that changes how people are measured or rewarded.
- When evaluating why a well-designed strategy is producing poor results.
- Annually as part of organizational health review.

## Gate Criteria

### Financial Incentive Mapping
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Every key role's compensation structure (base, bonus, equity, commission) is mapped to company objectives.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Variable compensation metrics cannot be gamed without creating genuine value.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Short-term incentives (quarterly bonuses) do not undermine long-term value creation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Clawback provisions exist for performance that is later revealed to be unsustainable or fraudulent.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The incentive structure does not reward revenue without regard to quality, margin, or sustainability.

### Non-Financial Incentive Mapping
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Career advancement criteria are explicit and aligned with desired behavior.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Social incentives (recognition, status, access) reward collaboration, not just individual performance.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Information-sharing is incentivized; information-hoarding is penalized.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The incentive to report problems early is stronger than the incentive to hide them.

### Perverse Incentive Detection
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A red-team exercise has identified the top three ways a rational actor would game the current incentive system.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The incentive system does not penalize appropriate risk-taking or experimentation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The system does not create incentives to sandbag targets, pull revenue forward, or defer expenses.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Cross-functional cooperation is not undermined by team-specific incentives.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The system does not create an incentive to prioritize easily measured activities over important but hard-to-measure ones.

### Stakeholder Incentive Alignment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer incentives are aligned: the company benefits when customers succeed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Partner and vendor incentive structures are understood and tested for alignment.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board member and advisor incentive structures are assessed for conflicts of interest.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Investor incentives (time horizon, return expectations) are compatible with the company's strategy.

## Red Flags
- People are hitting their individual targets while the company underperforms.
- Top sellers are churning customers after closing deals due to commission structure.
- Engineers optimize for shipping features rather than customer outcomes because that is what is measured.
- Managers hoard headcount because their status is tied to team size.
- No one raises concerns about strategy because disagreement is career-limiting.
- The sales team regularly discounts heavily at quarter-end to hit quotas.

## Escalation Triggers
- Evidence of systematic gaming of incentive metrics that harms customers or the company.
- An executive's incentive structure creates a conflict of interest with their fiduciary duty.
- Whistleblower reports suggest incentives are driving unethical behavior.
- Incentive-driven behavior causes a customer, partner, or regulatory incident.
- The incentive audit reveals that the system has not been reviewed in over two years despite strategic changes.
