# Moat and Competition Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Evaluates whether the organization has honestly assessed its competitive advantages and the durability of those advantages. A moat is not a feature list; it is a structural barrier that makes it difficult for competitors to replicate the company's value proposition. This checklist prevents wishful thinking about competitive position from corrupting strategic decisions.

## When to Use
- During annual strategic planning when reassessing competitive position.
- Before entering a new market or launching a new product line.
- When a competitor makes a move that could erode existing advantages.
- When evaluating M&A targets for strategic fit.
- During investor or board presentations that claim competitive advantage.

## Gate Criteria

### Moat Identification
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The company's competitive advantages are enumerated and categorized (network effects, switching costs, cost advantages, brand, regulatory, data, scale).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each claimed advantage is supported by quantitative evidence, not assertion.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The analysis distinguishes between advantages that are strengthening versus eroding over time.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The analysis honestly assesses moat width (how much advantage) and moat trend (growing or shrinking).

### Competitive Landscape
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Direct competitors are identified with market share estimates.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Indirect competitors and substitute products are identified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Adjacent players who could enter the market are assessed with probability and timeline.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Competitor capabilities, funding, and strategic intent are analyzed based on observable evidence.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The analysis avoids dismissing competitors based on current size rather than trajectory.

### Durability Assessment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A scenario is described in which the moat is breached, with estimated timeline and triggering events.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Technology shifts that could commoditize the advantage are identified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Regulatory changes that could eliminate or create barriers are assessed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The cost for a well-funded competitor to replicate the advantage is estimated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer lock-in is assessed honestly, including whether it relies on friction versus genuine value.

### Strategic Response Readiness
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Investments to strengthen the moat are identified with budget and timeline.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Early warning indicators of moat erosion are defined and monitored.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Contingency strategies exist for the top two moat-erosion scenarios.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The company is building new moats, not just defending existing ones.

## Red Flags
- The only claimed advantage is "our team" or "our culture" with no structural analysis.
- Competitors are dismissed as irrelevant or inferior without evidence.
- The moat analysis has not been updated in over 12 months.
- The company confuses first-mover advantage (usually temporary) with a durable moat.
- Customer retention is attributed to product quality when it actually depends on switching costs or inertia.
- No investment is allocated to moat maintenance or expansion.

## Escalation Triggers
- A competitor replicates a claimed core advantage within a shorter timeline than anticipated.
- Customer churn increases in a segment where the moat was assumed to be strong.
- A technology shift renders a core advantage irrelevant (e.g., AI commoditizing proprietary data).
- An investor or analyst publicly challenges the moat thesis and the company lacks a credible rebuttal.
- Market share declines for two consecutive quarters in a core segment.
