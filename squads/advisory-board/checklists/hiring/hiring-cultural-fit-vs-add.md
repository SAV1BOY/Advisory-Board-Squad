# Hiring Cultural Fit vs Add Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the organization hires for cultural contribution (what new perspectives and strengths does this person add) not just cultural fit (how similar is this person to us). Cultural fit without cultural add leads to homogeneity, groupthink, and stagnation. This checklist prevents using "culture" as a proxy for conformity.

## When to Use
- During the design of interview processes.
- When evaluating candidates for cultural alignment.
- When the organization notices increasing homogeneity.
- When defining the cultural criteria for a role.

## Gate Criteria
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The definition of culture for hiring purposes is based on values and behaviors, not personality or style.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Interview questions assess values alignment (non-negotiable) separately from style alignment (flexible).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each hire is evaluated for what new perspective, experience, or strength they bring to the team.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The hiring panel includes diverse perspectives to prevent bias.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: "Culture fit" is not used to reject candidates who are different but aligned on values.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Interviewers are trained to distinguish between discomfort with difference and genuine values misalignment.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team actively seeks candidates who challenge existing assumptions and bring new viewpoints.

## Red Flags
- "I could see having a beer with them" is the primary culture assessment.
- The team looks, thinks, and backgrounds are increasingly homogeneous.
- Candidates who push back or ask challenging questions are rejected for "not fitting in."
- Culture assessment is not structured: each interviewer applies a different standard.

## Escalation Triggers
- Hiring data reveals patterns that suggest demographic bias in cultural assessments.
- Innovation or creativity declines as the team becomes more homogeneous.
- Candidates from underrepresented backgrounds consistently receive lower culture scores.
- The team cannot point to a recent hire who brought a genuinely different perspective.
