# Lencioni Five Dysfunctions of a Team

## Purpose

Patrick Lencioni's Five Dysfunctions model identifies the five interconnected failures that prevent teams from functioning effectively. The dysfunctions form a pyramid where each builds on the one below — you cannot fix higher-level dysfunctions without addressing lower-level ones first. This is the most practical team-diagnostic framework available and should be in every board advisor's toolkit.

## When to Use

- When a leadership team is underperforming despite individual talent
- When meetings are unproductive, conflict-avoidant, or full of politics
- When team members are not following through on commitments
- When results are suffering and accountability is absent
- When building a new team and establishing healthy norms from the start

## Core Model

### The Five Dysfunctions (Bottom to Top)

**Dysfunction 1: Absence of Trust** (Foundation)
- Team members are unwilling to be vulnerable — they hide mistakes, don't ask for help, and don't admit weaknesses
- Without vulnerability-based trust, everything above is impossible
- Fix: Structured vulnerability exercises, personal history sharing, behavioral profile discussions

**Dysfunction 2: Fear of Conflict**
- Without trust, people avoid productive ideological debate
- Artificial harmony replaces honest discussion; real opinions go underground
- Fix: Mining for conflict (the leader actively draws out disagreements), real-time permission to debate

**Dysfunction 3: Lack of Commitment**
- Without productive conflict, people don't buy in to decisions because their views weren't heard
- Ambiguity prevails; people say yes but don't mean it
- Fix: Commitment clarity (at end of every meeting: "What did we decide? Is everyone committed?"), deadlines, cascading communication

**Dysfunction 4: Avoidance of Accountability**
- Without commitment, people don't hold each other accountable because the expectations were never clear
- Low standards are tolerated; confrontation is avoided
- Fix: Public commitments, peer accountability, regular progress reviews

**Dysfunction 5: Inattention to Results** (Top)
- Without accountability, people prioritize individual status, ego, or department goals over team results
- The team exists in name only; individuals optimize for themselves
- Fix: Public scoreboard of team results, team-based incentives, collective ownership of outcomes

### The Key Insight
You must work bottom-up. Trying to fix accountability (Dysfunction 4) without first building trust (Dysfunction 1) and healthy conflict (Dysfunction 2) always fails.

## Steps

1. **Assess the team** using the Five Dysfunctions questionnaire (available in Lencioni's book).
2. **Identify the lowest dysfunction** that is present. Start there — don't skip levels.
3. **Build trust first.** This takes time and requires the leader to go first in showing vulnerability.
4. **Normalize conflict.** Explicitly give the team permission to debate. Model productive disagreement.
5. **Drive commitment.** End every meeting with clear decisions and commitment confirmation.
6. **Enable accountability.** Make commitments public. Create peer-to-peer accountability norms.
7. **Focus on results.** Define team-level success metrics. Celebrate team wins over individual wins.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- The board itself is a team and is subject to the same five dysfunctions. Assess the board's health using this model.
- When advising founders on team problems, start with the Five Dysfunctions diagnostic. Most "performance" problems are actually trust or conflict problems.
- The founder must model the behavior at each level. Trust starts with the founder being vulnerable. Conflict starts with the founder welcoming disagreement.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Starting at the top.** "We need more accountability!" without building the trust and conflict capacity underneath. It never works.
2. **Confusing artificial harmony with trust.** Teams that never disagree are not trusting — they are hiding.
3. **Treating this as a one-time exercise.** The five dysfunctions require ongoing attention, not a single offsite.
4. **Blaming individuals.** The dysfunctions are systemic, not individual. Fix the system.
