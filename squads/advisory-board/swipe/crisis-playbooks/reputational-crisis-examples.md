# Reputational Crisis Examples — Playbook

## Overview
Reputational crises—PR disasters, executive misconduct, customer outrage, social media firestorms—damage the intangible asset that's hardest to rebuild. These playbooks provide response patterns for the most common reputational crisis types.

## Crisis Type 1: Executive Misconduct

### Response Pattern
1. **Immediate investigation:** Engage external investigators for credibility. Do not investigate yourself.
2. **Suspend, don't fire (initially):** Allow the investigation to proceed. "We take these allegations seriously and have placed [name] on administrative leave pending investigation."
3. **Decision:** Based on investigation findings, terminate or reinstate with findings-based communication.
4. **Cultural response:** If systemic issues are revealed, announce structural changes (training, reporting, accountability).
5. **Board communication:** The board leads this response. The CEO may be the subject of the investigation.

### Communication Template
"We were made aware of [nature of allegation] on [date]. We immediately engaged [independent investigation firm] to conduct a thorough investigation. [Person] has been placed on administrative leave pending the outcome. We are committed to maintaining a workplace that [values statement]. We will share findings when the investigation is complete."

## Crisis Type 2: Customer Data Breach

### Response Pattern
1. **Contain and assess:** Stop the breach, determine scope
2. **Legal and regulatory:** Notify legal counsel immediately. Determine notification obligations (GDPR 72-hour rule, state breach notification laws, LGPD requirements)
3. **Customer notification:** Direct, specific communication to affected customers
4. **Public statement:** If the breach is material, proactive public disclosure
5. **Remediation:** Credit monitoring, password resets, security improvements

## Crisis Type 3: Social Media Firestorm

### Response Pattern
1. **Listen before responding:** Understand what's driving the outrage. Is it justified?
2. **Don't escalate:** Defensive or dismissive responses amplify the crisis.
3. **If the criticism is valid:** Acknowledge it quickly, specifically, and with a commitment to action.
4. **If the criticism is based on misinformation:** Provide clear, factual correction without attacking the critics.
5. **Know when to go silent:** Some firestorms burn out on their own. Responding can feed the fire.

### The 3A Framework
- **Acknowledge:** "We hear you. This matters."
- **Apologize (if warranted):** "We got this wrong and we're sorry."
- **Act:** "Here's what we're doing about it."

## Board Application
- Every company should have a crisis communication playbook approved by the board BEFORE a crisis occurs
- The board should designate a crisis spokesperson (usually the CEO, but may be the board chair for executive misconduct)
- In reputational crises, the board's instinct to "protect the company" must be balanced against the need for transparency
- Post-crisis, conduct a thorough review of the response and update the playbook
