# Naval Leverage Stack

## Purpose

Naval Ravikant's Leverage Stack framework identifies the four forms of leverage available in the modern economy and provides a strategic approach to stacking them for maximum output per unit of input. The core insight: labor and capital are old-world leverage requiring permission. Code and media are new-world leverage requiring no permission. The wealthiest and most impactful individuals and companies stack multiple forms of leverage simultaneously.

## When to Use

- When designing a business model and evaluating its scalability
- When a founder is working hard but output is proportional to hours worked (no leverage)
- When deciding between business models with different leverage profiles
- When evaluating what type of company to build or invest in
- When advising on resource allocation between leverage types
- When assessing why some companies scale effortlessly while others grind

## Core Model

### The Four Forms of Leverage

**1. Labor (People)**
- Other people working for you
- Oldest form of leverage
- Requires permission: people must agree to work for you
- Linear scaling: 2x people ≈ 2x output (minus coordination costs)
- Limitations: management overhead, hiring difficulty, communication loss

**2. Capital (Money)**
- Money working for you through investment, lending, or deployment
- Traditional leverage of banks, investors, and financiers
- Requires permission: someone must give you capital
- Can be non-linear: a great capital allocator creates exponential returns
- Limitations: requires track record, relationships, and trust to access

**3. Code (Software/Technology)**
- Software that replicates decisions and processes infinitely at zero marginal cost
- New-world leverage: permissionless (anyone can write code)
- Exponential scaling: code serves 1 user or 1 billion users at similar cost
- The most powerful form of leverage in the modern economy
- Limitations: requires technical skill or the ability to hire/partner with those who have it

**4. Media (Content/Distribution)**
- Content that reaches audiences while you sleep — books, podcasts, videos, tweets, newsletters
- New-world leverage: permissionless (anyone can publish)
- Exponential scaling: content consumed by 1 person or 1 million at similar cost
- Compounds over time: a great blog post generates value for years
- Limitations: requires unique insight, communication skill, and consistency

### The Leverage Stack
The most powerful position is stacking multiple leverage types:
- **Code + Media**: A software company with strong content marketing (Basecamp)
- **Code + Capital**: A software company with investor capital for growth (most VC-backed startups)
- **Code + Media + Capital**: The maximum modern leverage stack (platform companies)
- **Labor + Capital**: The traditional corporation (diminishing advantage)

### Permission vs. Permissionless Leverage
- Labor and capital require someone's permission (employees must accept your offer, investors must fund you)
- Code and media require no permission (you can start tonight)
- This is why Naval advises starting with permissionless leverage

## Steps

1. **Audit your current leverage.** For each of the four types, assess: How much of this leverage do I currently employ? What is the multiplier it provides?

2. **Identify leverage gaps.** Which forms of leverage is your business NOT using? These represent scaling opportunities.

3. **Prioritize permissionless leverage.** If you don't have code or media leverage, start there. They require no permission and compound over time.

4. **Stack deliberately.** Don't try to add all four simultaneously. Add one new form of leverage every 6-12 months, building on the previous ones.

5. **Measure leverage ratio.** Track output per unit of input over time. If you're doubling input and only doubling output, you're not leveraged. If you're doubling output without doubling input, leverage is working.

6. **Invest in leverage-building skills.** Coding, writing, speaking, investing — these are the meta-skills that unlock leverage. Invest in learning them or hiring people who have them.

7. **Design the business model around leverage.** The highest-leverage business model is: productized code + media distribution + capital efficiency + minimal labor.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Evaluate portfolio companies' leverage profiles. A company relying solely on labor leverage will always struggle to scale. Push toward code and media leverage.
- Help founders understand that "working harder" (labor) is the least effective path to scaling. "Working smarter through leverage" is the path.
- The best advisory contributions often increase a company's media leverage (introductions, co-authored content, endorsements).

### For Founders
- Your personal leverage stack matters. A founder who codes and writes has more personal leverage than one who only manages people.
- Build code leverage early. Every manual process you automate is leverage you keep forever.
- Build media leverage consistently. A weekly newsletter, podcast, or blog compounds into a massive distribution advantage over time.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Defaulting to labor leverage.** Hiring more people to solve problems that code could solve. This is the most common scaling mistake.
2. **Ignoring media leverage.** Most technical founders undervalue content and distribution. A great product with no distribution loses to a good product with great distribution.
3. **Seeking capital before building other leverage.** Capital amplifies existing leverage. Without code or media leverage, capital just buys more labor.
4. **Not compounding.** Leverage compounds over time. Starting a blog and quitting after 3 months captures zero compound value.
