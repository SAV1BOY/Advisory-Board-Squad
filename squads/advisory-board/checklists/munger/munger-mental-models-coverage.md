# Munger Mental Models Coverage Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures that important decisions are analyzed through multiple mental models from different disciplines, not just the default framework of the decision-maker's expertise. Munger's lattice of mental models requires drawing from psychology, economics, biology, physics, mathematics, and other fields to avoid the "man with a hammer" problem where every problem looks like a nail. This checklist verifies that cognitive diversity in analysis compensates for individual blind spots.

## When to Use
- Before any major strategic or investment decision.
- When a decision seems obvious and no one is raising concerns.
- During post-mortems to assess whether relevant mental models were considered.
- When building the analytical capacity of the leadership or advisory team.

## Gate Criteria

### Model Application
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The decision has been analyzed through at least five mental models from different disciplines.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Psychological models are applied: incentives, social proof, confirmation bias, loss aversion, anchoring.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Economic models are applied: supply/demand, opportunity cost, marginal analysis, comparative advantage.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Systems models are applied: feedback loops, emergence, tipping points, nonlinear effects.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Biological models are applied: evolution, adaptation, competition, niche selection.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Mathematical models are applied: probability, compounding, regression to the mean, scale effects.

### Conflict and Convergence
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Conclusions from different models are compared for agreement and disagreement.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Where models conflict, the analysis explains why and which model is more applicable.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Where models converge, the conclusion is strengthened with higher confidence.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The analysis identifies which model, if wrong, would most change the conclusion.

### Blind Spot Check
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The analysis team includes people with diverse disciplinary backgrounds.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The dominant mental model of the decision-maker is identified and alternative models are specifically sought.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Historical failures caused by reliance on a single model are referenced as cautionary examples.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Someone has been assigned the role of applying counterintuitive or contrarian models.

### Documentation and Learning
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The mental models applied to each major decision are documented in the decision record.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Post-decision review assesses which models were most predictive.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization's library of useful mental models is maintained and expanded.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Training or discussion of mental models occurs at least quarterly.

## Red Flags
- Every problem is analyzed with the same framework regardless of context.
- The decision team lacks anyone from a discipline outside the company's core domain.
- Analysis uses only financial models for decisions with significant human or organizational dimensions.
- No one asks "what does this look like from the perspective of psychology, biology, or systems theory?"
- The most important mental model for the decision was never considered.
- The team confuses having many opinions with having multiple models.

## Escalation Triggers
- A major decision failure is traced to a mental model that should have been applied but was not considered.
- The advisory team's expertise is concentrated in fewer than three disciplines.
- A decision is being made using only financial analysis when it has significant cultural or organizational implications.
- Post-mortems consistently reveal the same category of mental model was overlooked.
- The organization demonstrates no growth in analytical framework diversity over 12 months.
