# 06 — Org Health Turnaround

## Overview

A structured intervention when organizational health metrics indicate systemic dysfunction: engagement collapse, leadership misalignment, execution breakdown, or cultural erosion. This workflow moves from diagnosis through alignment to measurable recovery within 90 days.

## Trigger

- Employee engagement scores drop below threshold (e.g., <60 % favorable).
- Executive team alignment score falls below 70 %.
- Voluntary attrition spikes above 2x industry baseline.
- Board receives whistleblower report or pattern of escalations.
- Culture audit (workflow 13) reveals critical findings.

## Agents

| Agent | Role |
|---|---|
| board-chair (Board Chair) | Sponsors the turnaround, holds CEO accountable |
| culture-steward (Brene Brown) | Leads diagnosis, designs interventions |
| strategic-advisor (Peter Thiel) | Quantifies the business impact of org health issues |
| capital-allocator (Charlie Munger) | Challenges proposed fixes for depth and realism |
| growth-navigator (Naval Ravikant) | Monitors intervention milestones and leading indicators |

## Phases

### Phase 1 — Diagnosis (Days 1-14)

1. culture-steward conducts a rapid assessment:
   - Review existing data: engagement surveys, exit interviews, Glassdoor themes, internal support tickets.
   - Conduct 10-15 confidential 1:1 interviews across levels and functions. Standard questions: "What is broken? What would you fix first? Why do people leave?"
   - Analyze org structure for span-of-control issues, single points of failure, and unclear accountability.
2. strategic-advisor quantifies impact: revenue at risk from attrition, productivity loss estimates, customer satisfaction correlation.
3. Synthesize findings into a diagnostic report:
   - Root causes (not symptoms) — use "5 whys" to get beneath surface complaints.
   - Severity rating: critical (existential), serious (impacting performance), moderate (needs attention).
   - Interconnections: which issues amplify each other?
4. board-chair reviews the diagnostic. If critical-severity issues are found, accelerate the timeline.

### Phase 2 — Alignment (Days 14-28)

1. board-chair convenes an alignment session with the executive team:
   - Present the diagnostic (no sugarcoating).
   - Each executive responds: agree, disagree, or add context.
   - Identify the 3-5 root causes the team will address (not 15 surface symptoms).
2. For each root cause, design an intervention:
   - What changes: structure, process, behavior, or personnel?
   - Who owns it?
   - What does success look like at 30, 60, and 90 days?
   - What resources are required?
3. capital-allocator stress-tests each intervention: "Will this actually fix the root cause, or just address a symptom?"
4. CEO presents the turnaround plan to the board for review and approval.
5. Board approves the plan, timeline, and resource allocation.

### Phase 3 — Execution (Days 28-90)

1. CEO communicates the turnaround plan to the organization. Transparency about what was found and what is changing.
2. Each intervention owner begins execution. Week-by-week milestones.
3. culture-steward runs a "pulse check" at Day 45: short survey to 20 % sample of the org.
4. growth-navigator publishes a bi-weekly turnaround dashboard:
   - Milestone status (green/yellow/red).
   - Leading indicators: voluntary attrition trend, internal mobility, engagement pulse.
   - Lagging indicators: productivity metrics, customer satisfaction, revenue per employee.
5. board-chair conducts a mid-point review at Day 45:
   - What is working?
   - What needs adjustment?
   - Are any interventions failing? Pivot or double down?
6. Interventions that are failing get 2 weeks to course-correct. If no improvement, escalate to the board.

### Phase 4 — Measurement & Sustainability (Days 75-120)

1. culture-steward runs a full engagement survey at Day 90 (or uses the next scheduled survey cycle).
2. strategic-advisor compares pre-turnaround and post-turnaround metrics:
   - Engagement scores by team and function.
   - Attrition rates.
   - Productivity and business performance indicators.
3. Board reviews the 90-day results:
   - Which root causes are resolved?
   - Which need continued attention?
   - What systemic changes should become permanent?
4. Embed successful interventions into standard operating procedures.
5. Schedule a 6-month follow-up to confirm sustainability.
6. Archive the turnaround package in `archive/org-health/`.

## Quality Gates

| Gate | Criteria | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| G1 — Diagnostic complete | Root causes identified, severity rated, impact quantified | culture-steward (Brene Brown) |
| G2 — Executive alignment | Exec team agrees on root causes and interventions | board-chair (Board Chair) |
| G3 — Plan approved | Board reviews and approves turnaround plan | board-chair (Board Chair) |
| G4 — Mid-point review | Day 45 pulse check and dashboard reviewed | board-chair (Board Chair) |
| G5 — 90-day measurement | Full survey results compared to baseline | culture-steward (Brene Brown) |

## Outputs

- Organizational health diagnostic report.
- Executive alignment session notes.
- Turnaround plan with interventions, owners, milestones.
- Bi-weekly turnaround dashboard.
- Day 45 pulse check results.
- Day 90 full measurement report.
- Permanent process changes documentation.

## Metrics

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic interview completion | ≥12 interviews across ≥3 levels |
| Root causes identified | 3-5 (not 15 symptoms) |
| Executive alignment score | ≥80 % agreement on root causes |
| Intervention milestone completion | ≥75 % on track at Day 45 |
| Engagement improvement | ≥10 percentage points at Day 90 |
| Voluntary attrition reduction | ≥30 % decrease from peak |

## Common Failures

| Failure Mode | Symptom | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Symptom-chasing | Plan addresses complaints, not root causes | culture-steward uses "5 whys"; capital-allocator challenges depth |
| Executive denial | Leaders dismiss the diagnostic | board-chair presents data directly; makes it safe to accept uncomfortable truths |
| Plan without teeth | Nice words, no structural changes | Every intervention must specify what changes, who owns it, and measurable milestones |
| Communication vacuum | Org knows something is wrong but hears nothing | CEO communicates the plan within 48 hours of board approval |
| Measurement theater | Surveys run but results are not acted on | Board reviews results directly; specific follow-up actions required |
| Premature victory | Declare success at Day 30 and move on | 6-month sustainability check is mandatory |
