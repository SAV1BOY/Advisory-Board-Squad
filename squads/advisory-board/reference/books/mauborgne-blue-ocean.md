# Blue Ocean Strategy — W. Chan Kim & Renee Mauborgne

## Summary

Kim and Mauborgne argue that the best strategic moves don't beat competitors—they make competitors irrelevant by creating uncontested market space (blue oceans). Red oceans are crowded, competitive markets where companies fight for shrinking profits. Blue oceans are new market spaces created by simultaneously pursuing differentiation AND low cost. The book provides analytical tools and frameworks for systematically identifying and creating blue ocean opportunities, drawing from a study of 150+ strategic moves across 30+ industries over 100 years.

## Key Principles

- **Value innovation** — The simultaneous pursuit of differentiation and low cost. Not choosing between them but achieving both by redefining what "value" means in the industry.
- **Make the competition irrelevant** — Instead of benchmarking against competitors, reconstruct market boundaries to serve unmet needs.
- **Non-customers matter more than customers** — The biggest growth opportunity lies in the three tiers of non-customers who currently don't buy from your industry at all.
- **The Four Actions Framework** — Which factors should be eliminated, reduced, raised, and created relative to the industry standard?
- **Strategy Canvas** — A visual tool that maps the competitive factors in an industry and shows how your offering differs.

## Frameworks Extracted

1. **The Four Actions Framework** — Eliminate (factors the industry competes on that no longer matter), Reduce (factors overserved relative to what customers actually need), Raise (factors underserved that should be elevated), Create (factors the industry has never offered).
2. **The Strategy Canvas** — X-axis: key competitive factors. Y-axis: level of offering. Plot your company and competitors. A blue ocean strategy creates a divergent value curve.
3. **The Three Tiers of Non-Customers** — Tier 1: soon-to-be non-customers (on the edge of your market). Tier 2: refusing non-customers (consciously chose against your market). Tier 3: unexplored non-customers (in distant markets, never considered as potential customers).
4. **The Six Paths Framework** — Look across: (1) alternative industries, (2) strategic groups within industries, (3) buyer groups, (4) complementary products, (5) functional-emotional appeal, (6) time trends.

## Quotable Lines

- "The only way to beat the competition is to stop trying to beat the competition."
- "Value innovation is the cornerstone of blue ocean strategy."
- "Companies often take their industry's conditions as given and set strategy accordingly. Value innovators don't."
- "Instead of focusing on beating the competition, focus on making the competition irrelevant."

## Application to Board Decisions

- **Strategic differentiation** — When the company is stuck in a red ocean, use the Four Actions Framework at the board level to reimagine the value proposition.
- **Growth strategy** — Target non-customers as a growth strategy. Most companies focus on stealing share from competitors when the bigger opportunity is converting non-consumers.
- **Product portfolio review** — Use the Strategy Canvas to visualize competitive positioning and identify opportunities for divergent value curves.
- **M&A screening** — Evaluate acquisitions for their blue ocean potential, not just their red ocean market share.
