# Channel Guide: Board Email

## When to Use

Board email is the primary written communication channel between the Chair and board members, and between Governance-Ops and the board. Use it for:

- Pre-read distribution and meeting logistics.
- Decision follow-ups and action item reminders.
- Urgent notifications between meetings.
- Sharing materials, reports, and updates.
- Requesting async input or votes.

Do **not** use board email for:
- Sensitive personnel matters (use encrypted channel or phone).
- Lengthy analysis (attach a document; keep the email short).
- Informal relationship-building (use 1:1 calls or messages).

## Format

### Subject Line

The subject line is the single most important line. Make it actionable and scannable.

**Pattern:** `[Type] — Topic — Action needed by [date]`

**Types:**
- `[PRE-READ]` — Materials for an upcoming meeting.
- `[DECISION]` — Decision recorded or async decision requested.
- `[ACTION]` — Action item or follow-up.
- `[FYI]` — Information only, no action needed.
- `[URGENT]` — Time-sensitive, requires response within 24 hours.
- `[LOGISTICS]` — Meeting scheduling, calendar changes.

**Examples:**
- `[PRE-READ] Q3 Strategy Review — Please read by March 15`
- `[DECISION] Capital allocation for Project Atlas — Approved with conditions`
- `[URGENT] CEO transition — Board call at 4pm today`
- `[FYI] Monthly risk register update — February 2026`

### Email Body Structure

```
1. BOTTOM LINE (first sentence)
   What does the reader need to know or do?

2. CONTEXT (2-3 sentences)
   Why are they receiving this? What's the background?

3. DETAILS (if needed)
   Bullet points for specifics. Links to documents.

4. ASK (final sentence)
   What specific action is needed? By when?

5. SIGN-OFF
   Name, role
```

### Length

- **Maximum 15 sentences.** If you need more, write a memo and attach it.
- **Ideal: 5-8 sentences.** The email should be readable in under 60 seconds.
- **Attachments for depth.** The email is the summary; the attachment is the detail.

## Tone

- **Professional but not stiff.** Board members are colleagues, not strangers. Write naturally.
- **Respectful of time.** Every sentence earns its place. No filler.
- **Clear about action.** If you need something, say exactly what you need and by when.
- **Confident.** Avoid hedging language ("I was wondering if maybe..."). State what you need.

## Examples

### Pre-read distribution

```
Subject: [PRE-READ] Q1 Board Meeting — Materials for March 25

Board members,

Attached are the pre-read materials for our March 25 quarterly meeting.

Package includes:
- Agenda (2 pages)
- Financial dashboard (3 pages)
- Strategy update memo (2 pages)
- Risk register summary (1 page)

Please review all materials before the meeting. If you have questions
or comments, send them to me by March 22 so we can incorporate them
into the discussion.

Focus areas for this meeting: the capital allocation decision on
Project Atlas and the Q1 risk register update.

[Name]
Governance-Ops
```

### Decision communication

```
Subject: [DECISION] Project Atlas — Approved with conditions

Board members,

The board approved the Project Atlas investment at today's meeting.

Decision: Fund Phase 1 ($1.2M over 6 months).
Conditions: Monthly milestone reviews; Phase 2 funding contingent
on hitting Q2 milestones.
Dissent: [Name] dissented on timeline (preferred 9 months).
Review date: September 15, 2026.
Owner: [Name], VP Product.

Full meeting minutes will be distributed by tomorrow EOD.

[Name]
Board-Chair
```

### Urgent notification

```
Subject: [URGENT] Security incident — Board call at 4pm ET today

Board members,

We discovered a data security incident this morning. Crisis protocol
is active.

What happened: Unauthorized access to [system]. Containment is underway.
No customer data confirmed compromised at this time.

Action needed: Emergency board call today at 4pm ET. Dial-in details
below. [CEO] and [CISO] will brief.

I will send a rapid assessment document by 3pm for your review.

[Dial-in details]

[Name]
Board-Chair
```

## Rules

1. **One email, one topic.** Do not bundle unrelated items.
2. **Reply-all with care.** Use reply-all only when the full board needs to see your response.
3. **Acknowledge receipt of urgent items.** A simple "Received, will attend" is sufficient.
4. **Do not use email for debate.** If a topic needs discussion, schedule a call. Email threads with 8 replies are a sign that a conversation is needed.
5. **Archive, don't delete.** Board emails are governance records. Retain them.
6. **Confidentiality.** Board emails contain sensitive information. Do not forward outside the board without Chair approval.
