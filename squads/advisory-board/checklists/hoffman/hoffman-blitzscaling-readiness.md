# Hoffman Blitzscaling Readiness Checklist

## Purpose
Evaluates whether the organization is ready for blitzscaling: the strategy of prioritizing speed over efficiency in the face of uncertainty, specifically to achieve first-scaler advantage in a winner-take-most market. Blitzscaling is not appropriate for every situation; it requires specific market conditions and organizational capabilities. This checklist prevents both premature blitzscaling (spending before conditions warrant it) and missed blitzscaling opportunities (being too cautious when the market demands speed).

## When to Use
- When considering aggressive growth investment that prioritizes speed over efficiency.
- When a competitor is blitzscaling and the team must decide whether to match.
- When the market shows winner-take-most dynamics.
- During fundraising discussions about growth-stage capital deployment.

## Gate Criteria

### Market Conditions
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The market has winner-take-most or winner-take-all dynamics (network effects, high switching costs).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: First-scaler advantage is real: the first to reach scale will lock out competitors.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The market is large enough to justify the investment required for blitzscaling.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The competitive landscape confirms that speed is the primary differentiator right now.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer acquisition at scale is feasible: the distribution channels can handle rapid growth.

### Product Readiness
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Product-market fit is confirmed with core metrics (retention, engagement, willingness to pay).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The product can scale technically without catastrophic degradation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The core value proposition is strong enough that fast growth will not be undermined by quality issues.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Unit economics are negative but have a credible path to positive at scale.

### Organizational Readiness
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The leadership team has experience operating in high-growth environments.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Hiring pipelines can deliver the volume of talent required.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Organizational processes can tolerate the chaos that comes with rapid scaling.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team understands that blitzscaling deliberately accepts inefficiency in exchange for speed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization can handle the cultural strain of rapid headcount growth.

### Capital and Risk
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Capital runway is sufficient for the blitzscaling timeline (minimum 18 months at burn rate).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Follow-on funding is likely based on milestones the blitzscaling plan will hit.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The cost of failure is modeled and is survivable.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Exit ramps exist: the company can transition to sustainable growth if blitzscaling is not working.

## Red Flags
- Blitzscaling is proposed for a market without winner-take-most dynamics.
- Product-market fit is assumed, not validated, before committing to blitzscaling.
- The capital required for blitzscaling exceeds what is available or likely to be raised.
- The leadership team has no experience with hypergrowth environments.
- Blitzscaling is used as justification for avoiding financial discipline rather than as a deliberate strategy.
- The organization is blitzscaling because a competitor is, without independent analysis.

## Escalation Triggers
- Cash burn exceeds plan by more than 30% without proportional growth acceleration.
- Key growth metrics plateau despite continued investment.
- The market evolves away from winner-take-most dynamics, undermining the blitzscaling thesis.
- Organizational quality (customer satisfaction, employee engagement) deteriorates below acceptable thresholds.
- The fundraising environment changes and follow-on capital becomes uncertain.
