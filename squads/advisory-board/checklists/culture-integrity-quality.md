# Culture Integrity Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Verifies that the organization's lived culture matches its declared culture. Culture integrity means the stated values are not wall posters but operational realities that influence hiring, firing, promotion, and daily behavior. This checklist detects the gap between what the organization says it values and what it actually rewards.

## When to Use
- During quarterly or semi-annual culture health assessments.
- When employee survey results suggest a gap between stated and experienced values.
- Before and after significant organizational changes that may stress culture.
- When onboarding new executives who will shape culture.
- When external events (media scrutiny, regulatory inquiries) test cultural claims.

## Gate Criteria

### Values-Behavior Alignment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each stated value can be illustrated with three specific recent examples of behavior that was rewarded.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each stated value can be illustrated with at least one example of behavior that was corrected for violating it.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Employees at all levels can describe the values without consulting a document.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Performance reviews explicitly assess behavior against stated values, not just outcomes.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Promotion decisions include values alignment as a gating criterion.

### Hiring and Separation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Interview processes include structured assessment of values alignment.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The company has separated from high performers who violated cultural values (values are not overridden by results).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: New hire onboarding explicitly teaches values through stories and examples, not just slides.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Exit interviews probe for cultural factors in departure decisions.

### Leadership Modeling
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Senior leaders are held to the same cultural standards as individual contributors.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders model vulnerability, learning, and accountability consistent with stated values.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: When leaders violate values, it is addressed visibly enough to maintain credibility.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leadership communication consistently references and reinforces values in context.

### Measurement and Feedback
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Culture is measured through employee surveys with specific behavioral questions, not vague sentiment scores.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Survey results are shared transparently with the organization, including uncomfortable findings.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Action plans address the top three culture gaps with named owners and deadlines.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: An anonymous channel exists for reporting cultural violations without retaliation.

## Red Flags
- The company touts values but cannot name anyone who was fired for violating them.
- Senior leaders are exempt from cultural expectations that apply to everyone else.
- Employee survey comments reveal widespread cynicism about stated values.
- Culture is discussed only during onboarding and never again.
- "Culture fit" is used as a proxy for demographic similarity rather than values alignment.
- Values were last updated more than five years ago despite significant company evolution.

## Escalation Triggers
- A credible allegation surfaces that a senior leader systematically violates stated values.
- Employee engagement surveys show a widening gap between stated and experienced culture.
- External media or social media surfaces cultural violations that damage reputation.
- A high-profile departure cites cultural hypocrisy as the reason for leaving.
- Legal action is filed alleging a culture of harassment, discrimination, or retaliation.
