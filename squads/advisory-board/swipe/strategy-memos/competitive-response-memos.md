# Competitive Response Memos — Swipe File

## Overview
When a competitor makes a significant move—new product, price cut, acquisition, market entry—the board must help the leadership team respond thoughtfully rather than reactively. These swipe examples illustrate the range of competitive responses and the reasoning behind each.

## The Competitive Response Spectrum

### Response 1: Ignore (Do Nothing)
- **When appropriate:** The competitor's move doesn't affect your core market, their execution is weak, or responding would distract from your priorities.
- **Example:** A competitor launches a product for a different segment. Your customers are unaffected. Responding would divert resources from your roadmap.
- **The Sinek filter:** Does this response serve our Just Cause, or does it serve our ego?

### Response 2: Accelerate (Do What You're Already Doing, Faster)
- **When appropriate:** The competitor's move validates your strategy but increases urgency.
- **Example:** A competitor raises a large round to enter your market. You need to accelerate product development and market capture before they scale.
- **Key question:** Do we have the resources and team to accelerate without breaking quality?

### Response 3: Differentiate (Pivot Away from the Competitor)
- **When appropriate:** Direct competition will be costly and the competitor has advantages in a head-to-head fight.
- **Example:** A well-funded competitor launches a similar product at a lower price. Instead of competing on price, you move upmarket and add enterprise features they can't match.
- **The Thiel filter:** How do we create a monopoly in a space they don't occupy?

### Response 4: Counter-Attack (Compete Directly)
- **When appropriate:** The competitor is attacking your core market and you have the resources and capability to defend.
- **Example:** A competitor launches a directly competing product. You respond with a feature blitz, pricing adjustment, and aggressive marketing campaign.
- **Warning:** This is the most expensive response and should be the last resort, not the first instinct.

### Response 5: Partner or Acquire (Remove the Threat)
- **When appropriate:** The competitor has something you need, or acquiring them is cheaper than competing.
- **Example:** A small competitor launches an innovative product that threatens your market position. Acquiring them brings the technology in-house and removes the competitive threat.

## Competitive Response Memo Template

1. **What did the competitor do?** Factual description.
2. **How does this affect us?** Revenue impact, customer risk, market perception, talent risk.
3. **What is the timeline?** How quickly will the impact be felt? How urgently must we respond?
4. **Response options:** At least 3 options with tradeoffs for each.
5. **Recommended response:** Which option and why.
6. **Resource requirements:** What does the response cost in money, people, and attention?
7. **Anti-envy check (Munger):** Are we responding because this is right for us, or because a competitor did something and we feel we must react?

## Board Application
- The board's primary role is to prevent reactive, ego-driven responses
- Apply the anti-envy check to every competitive response proposal
- Require at least three response options before making a decision
- Monitor the cost of competitive response: if it's consuming more resources than the threat justifies, recalibrate
