# Platform Business Model Economics

## Key Findings

1. **Platform economics differ fundamentally from linear businesses.** Platforms create value by facilitating exchanges between two or more participant groups rather than producing goods or services directly. The marginal cost of an additional transaction approaches zero, creating winner-take-most dynamics in many categories. Boards must evaluate platform businesses using different metrics than traditional product companies.
2. **Monetization timing is a strategic decision, not a financial one.** Platforms that monetize too early choke network growth; platforms that monetize too late train users to expect free services. The optimal monetization window opens when the platform has achieved liquidity — the point where supply and demand reliably find each other without platform intervention.
3. **Multi-homing costs determine competitive defensibility.** When users can easily participate on multiple competing platforms simultaneously (low multi-homing costs), no single platform achieves dominance. Platforms that increase multi-homing costs through data lock-in, reputation portability barriers, or workflow integration build stronger competitive positions.
4. **Subsidy structures shape market outcomes.** Most successful platforms subsidize the harder-to-acquire side (the "money side" pays, the "subsidy side" participates free or at reduced cost). Incorrect subsidy allocation is a primary cause of platform failure.
5. **Regulatory risk is accelerating.** Antitrust scrutiny of platform businesses has intensified globally. The EU Digital Markets Act, US platform competition proposals, and similar frameworks in India and Japan are creating compliance costs that favor established platforms over new entrants.

## Implications for Board

- **Unit economics assessment.** Platform boards should track contribution margin per transaction, not just overall revenue growth. A platform growing revenue but declining in per-transaction economics is subsidizing growth unsustainably.
- **Liquidity metrics over vanity metrics.** Track match rate, time-to-match, and repeat transaction rate rather than total registered users. A platform with 100K registered users and 5% monthly activity is weaker than one with 20K users and 60% activity.
- **Take rate optimization.** The board should review take rate annually against competitive benchmarks. Take rates above market norms invite disintermediation; rates below market leave value on the table.
- **Regulatory preparedness.** Platforms operating across jurisdictions should present a regulatory risk matrix quarterly. The board should ensure the company has legal counsel experienced in platform-specific regulation.
- **Platform leakage monitoring.** Track the percentage of transactions that bypass the platform after initial discovery. Leakage above 15% indicates a value proposition or pricing problem.
