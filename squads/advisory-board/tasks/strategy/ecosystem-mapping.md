# Task: Map the Competitive Ecosystem and Adjacencies

## Objective

Create a comprehensive map of the competitive ecosystem including direct competitors, indirect competitors, potential entrants, adjacent markets, and the value chain participants who influence competitive dynamics.

## Context

Most companies define their competitive landscape too narrowly. They track direct competitors but miss the adjacent players, platform shifts, and value chain movements that represent the greatest strategic threats and opportunities.

## Deliverables

### 1. Core Competitive Map
- Identify all direct competitors (same product, same customer, same use case)
- Categorize by tier: existential threats, meaningful competitors, niche players
- For each: document revenue estimate, funding, growth trajectory, key differentiators
- Map customer overlap and switching patterns

### 2. Adjacent Market Analysis
- Identify 3-5 adjacent markets the company could plausibly enter
- For each adjacent market: size, growth rate, current leaders, entry barriers
- Assess which adjacencies share capabilities, customers, or distribution with the core business
- Rank by strategic attractiveness (market size x fit x defensibility)

### 3. Value Chain Map
- Document every participant in the value chain from supplier to end customer
- Identify which value chain positions are consolidating or fragmenting
- Assess vertical integration threats (suppliers moving downstream, customers building in-house)
- Flag dependencies where a single vendor or partner holds disproportionate power

### 4. Potential Entrant Assessment
- List companies with the capability, distribution, or incentive to enter this market
- Include Big Tech platforms, well-funded startups, and international players
- For each: what would trigger their entry? What would their unfair advantage be?
- Estimate probability and timeline for each potential entry

### 5. Ecosystem Dynamics Report
- Identify secular trends reshaping the ecosystem (technology shifts, regulatory changes, customer behavior evolution)
- Map alliance and partnership patterns among competitors
- Identify potential acquisition targets that would strengthen competitive position
- Flag ecosystem risks: platform dependency, standards shifts, regulatory disruption

## Process

### Step 1: Internal Knowledge Capture (Week 1)
- Interview sales team on competitive encounters and win/loss patterns
- Interview product team on feature comparison and roadmap awareness
- Interview executives on strategic threats and opportunities they are tracking
- Collect all existing competitive intelligence and market research

### Step 2: External Research (Weeks 2-3)
- Analyze competitor public filings, press releases, job postings, and patent filings
- Review industry analyst reports and market sizing data
- Monitor competitor product launches, pricing changes, and partnership announcements
- Conduct customer interviews about alternatives they considered

### Step 3: Synthesis and Mapping (Week 4)
- Create visual ecosystem map showing relationships and competitive dynamics
- Draft adjacency attractiveness matrix
- Develop potential entrant scenario analyses
- Present findings to leadership with strategic implications

## Output Format

The final ecosystem map should be a living document updated quarterly, containing:
- Visual competitive landscape diagram
- Adjacency opportunity matrix (scored and ranked)
- Value chain power analysis
- Potential entrant watchlist with trigger events
- Strategic implications and recommended responses

## Success Criteria

- Leadership can articulate the competitive ecosystem beyond direct competitors
- At least one previously unrecognized threat or opportunity is identified
- The map informs strategic planning, M&A screening, and partnership priorities
- Quarterly update process is established and owned by a specific team member

## Time Estimate

Initial mapping: 4 weeks of dedicated effort
Quarterly updates: 1 week per quarter
