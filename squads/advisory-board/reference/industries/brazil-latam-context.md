# Brazil and Latin America Context

## Market Characteristics

### Brazil
- **5th largest country by population, 12th largest economy.** Massive domestic market but complex to navigate.
- **Digital adoption:** 150M+ internet users. Mobile-first market. PIX (instant payment) adoption faster than any comparable system globally.
- **Regulatory environment:** Complex tax system (multiple overlapping federal, state, and municipal taxes). Labor laws are protective and costly for employers. Data protection law (LGPD) modeled on GDPR.
- **Startup ecosystem:** São Paulo is the hub. Growing rapidly but still earlier than US/EU ecosystems. Fewer late-stage investors domestically.
- **Currency risk:** BRL volatility affects international operations and fundraising. Dollar-denominated revenues are a strategic advantage.

### Broader Latin America
- **650M+ population across the region.** Young demographics, growing middle class, increasing digital access.
- **Mexico:** Second-largest economy in the region. Strong manufacturing base. Proximity to US creates unique trade dynamics.
- **Colombia:** Growing tech ecosystem. Favorable time zone for US clients. Improving regulatory environment.
- **Argentina:** Strong technical talent but extreme macroeconomic instability. Currency controls complicate business.
- **Chile:** Most stable economy in the region. Smaller market but gateway to Pacific trade.

## Doing Business in Brazil — Key Considerations

### Tax Complexity
- Simples Nacional, Lucro Presumido, Lucro Real: different tax regimes with different rules and rates
- ICMS (state sales tax) varies by state and product. Interstate transactions have complex rules.
- Payroll taxes can add 70-100% on top of base salary
- **Board role:** Ensure competent tax advisory from day one. Tax errors compound rapidly.

### Labor Law
- CLT (Consolidação das Leis do Trabalho) governs employment. Highly protective of employees.
- Termination costs include FGTS (employment guarantee fund), notice period, and potential labor court claims
- PJ (contractor) arrangements common in tech but carry significant reclassification risk
- **Board role:** Understand labor cost structure. Budget for termination costs in any restructuring scenario.

### Payment and Financial Infrastructure
- PIX has transformed payments: instant, free, 24/7. Adoption above 70% of adults.
- Boleto bancário remains important for non-banked population and B2B transactions
- Credit card installments (parcelamento) are culturally embedded; pricing must account for this

### Cultural Business Norms
- Relationships matter enormously. Business is personal before it's transactional.
- Decision-making can be slower than in US/EU. Patience and relationship investment are required.
- "Jeitinho brasileiro" — creative problem-solving around bureaucratic obstacles. Can be an asset or a risk depending on context.

## Fundraising in LATAM

### Local vs. International Investors
- Local investors (Kaszek, Monashees, Valor Capital) understand the market but rounds are typically smaller
- International investors (SoftBank, a16z, Tiger) bring larger rounds but may have less patience for LATAM market dynamics
- Dual-entity structures (Delaware holding company + Brazilian operating company) are standard for VC-backed companies

### Valuation Context
- LATAM valuations typically 30-50% discount to comparable US companies
- However, market size in key segments (fintech, e-commerce, edtech) is enormous and under-penetrated
- Currency effects can significantly impact returns for international investors

## Board Considerations
- Ensure the board includes at least one member with deep LATAM operational experience
- Tax and labor compliance must be board-level concerns, not just operational details
- Currency strategy (hedging, dollar-denomination, multi-currency pricing) should be discussed at board level
- Understand the regulatory trajectory: LATAM regulations are evolving rapidly, creating both risk and opportunity
