# Media & Content Industry Reference

## Business Model Characteristics
- **Revenue models:** Advertising, subscription, transaction (PPV/purchase), licensing, sponsorship, events
- **Content economics:** High fixed cost to produce, near-zero marginal cost to distribute. Scale creates leverage.
- **Attention economy:** Competing with every other form of content and entertainment for finite audience attention.
- **Creator economy:** Shift from institutional content production to individual creator-led content.

## Key Metrics
- **Subscribers / audience size:** Scale of reach
- **Engagement:** Time spent, return frequency, interaction rate
- **ARPU (Average Revenue Per User):** Revenue per subscriber or per audience member
- **Churn:** For subscription models, monthly/annual subscriber loss
- **LTV/CAC:** Cost to acquire a subscriber vs. expected revenue over their subscription lifetime
- **Content ROI:** Revenue generated per dollar of content investment

## Competitive Dynamics
- **Platform power:** Distribution platforms (YouTube, Spotify, Netflix, TikTok) hold enormous leverage over content creators
- **Unbundling and rebundling:** Traditional media bundles (cable, newspapers) have been unbundled; now rebundling into new forms (streaming bundles)
- **AI disruption:** AI-generated content is rapidly approaching human quality for many content types, potentially commoditizing production

## Board Considerations
- Evaluate whether the company owns the audience relationship (email list, app, community) or depends on platforms (social media algorithms)
- For subscription businesses, monitor churn obsessively. In content, the product must continuously earn the subscriber's continued attention.
- Assess content production efficiency: are hit rates improving? Is the company learning what resonates, or producing blindly?
- AI strategy is urgent: how will AI change content production costs, audience expectations, and competitive dynamics?
