# 18 — M&A Evaluation Flow

## Overview

End-to-end process for evaluating a potential acquisition — from initial screening through financial and operational due diligence, term negotiation, board decision, and post-close integration planning. Designed for bolt-on acquisitions and transformative deals alike.

## Trigger

- Inbound acquisition opportunity presented by a banker, advisor, or direct approach.
- Strategy refresh (workflow 02) identifies M&A as a growth lever.
- Competitive move (competitor makes an acquisition) prompts defensive evaluation.
- Target company approaches with a sale process.

## Agents

| Agent | Role |
|---|---|
| board-chair (Board Chair) | Sponsors the evaluation, leads board decision process |
| strategic-advisor (Peter Thiel) | Builds the strategic and financial case |
| risk-sentinel (Ray Dalio) | Conducts risk assessment and downside modeling |
| capital-allocator (Charlie Munger) | Challenges the acquisition thesis at every phase |
| governance-clerk (Simon Sinek) | Manages the process, NDA flow, data room, timeline |

## Phases

### Phase 1 — Initial Screen (Days 1-7)

1. strategic-advisor conducts a rapid strategic screen:
   - Strategic rationale: why would we acquire this? (Capability, market access, talent, technology, revenue, defensive)
   - Fit assessment: does this align with our stated strategy?
   - Size check: is this affordable? What would the approximate valuation range be?
   - Integration complexity: how hard would it be to integrate this business?
   - Red flags: any immediate disqualifiers (regulatory, reputational, cultural)?
2. Score on a go/no-go framework: strategic fit, financial feasibility, integration complexity, risk level.
3. board-chair reviews the screen and decides: proceed to evaluation, decline, or request more information.
4. If proceeding, governance-clerk initiates NDA and requests initial information from the target.

### Phase 2 — Strategic & Financial Evaluation (Days 7-30)

1. strategic-advisor builds the acquisition case:
   - Strategic value: what do we get that we cannot build or buy elsewhere?
   - Revenue synergies: cross-sell, market expansion, pricing power (quantified, with probability weights).
   - Cost synergies: redundancy elimination, scale benefits, procurement leverage (quantified, with timeline).
   - Standalone value: what is the target worth without synergies?
   - Synergy-adjusted value: standalone + synergies - integration costs.
2. Financial model:
   - Three scenarios: base, upside, downside.
   - Accretion/dilution analysis for key metrics.
   - Financing options: cash, debt, equity, or combination.
   - Return on invested capital vs. hurdle rate.
3. capital-allocator challenges:
   - "Are synergies realistic or aspirational?"
   - "What is the base rate for acquisitions of this type succeeding?"
   - "What would we do with the same capital if we didn't acquire?"

### Phase 3 — Due Diligence (Days 20-50)

1. governance-clerk sets up the due diligence process: workstreams, owners, checklists, timeline.
2. Due diligence workstreams:
   - Financial: audit quality, revenue recognition, working capital, liabilities, tax.
   - Legal: contracts, IP, litigation, regulatory compliance.
   - Operational: technology stack, processes, key vendor relationships, customer concentration.
   - People: talent assessment, key person risk, cultural compatibility, compensation structures.
   - Commercial: customer relationships, market position, competitive dynamics, growth trajectory.
3. Each workstream produces a findings report: confirmed information, risks identified, open questions.
4. risk-sentinel synthesizes across workstreams:
   - Material findings: what changes the valuation or the strategic thesis?
   - Deal-breakers: anything that should stop the process?
   - Risk matrix: likelihood × impact for each identified risk.
5. strategic-advisor updates the financial model with due diligence findings.

### Phase 4 — Term Negotiation & Decision (Days 40-60)

1. strategic-advisor recommends a valuation range based on the updated model.
2. board-chair advises on negotiation strategy: opening offer, walk-away price, key terms.
3. Key terms to negotiate:
   - Purchase price and structure (cash, equity, earnout).
   - Representations and warranties.
   - Indemnification provisions.
   - Key employee retention provisions.
   - Closing conditions and regulatory approvals.
   - Integration commitments (brand, location, team).
4. risk-sentinel reviews final terms for downside exposure.
5. capital-allocator presents the final counter-case: "Here is why we should not do this deal."
6. Board decision session (60-90 minutes):
   - strategic-advisor presents the case: strategic rationale, financial model, due diligence findings.
   - risk-sentinel presents risks and mitigations.
   - capital-allocator presents the counter-case.
   - Board discussion.
   - Decision: approve, reject, or approve with conditions.
7. Record the decision with detailed rationale, conditions, and dissenting views.

### Phase 5 — Close & Integration Planning (Days 55-90)

1. If approved, legal counsel drives definitive agreements and closing process.
2. governance-clerk tracks closing conditions, regulatory approvals, and timeline.
3. strategic-advisor drafts the integration plan (begins during diligence, finalizes at close):
   - Day 1 plan: what happens on the first day after close? Communication, leadership, immediate decisions.
   - 30-day plan: organizational structure, reporting lines, key process integration.
   - 90-day plan: system integration, cultural integration, synergy capture.
   - 12-month plan: full integration milestones and synergy realization targets.
4. Board reviews the integration plan before close.
5. Post-close: growth-navigator monitors integration against the plan (monthly board updates for the first year).
6. 12-month acquisition retrospective: did we achieve the strategic rationale? Were synergies realized? What did we learn?

## Quality Gates

| Gate | Criteria | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| G1 — Screen complete | Strategic fit, financial feasibility, red flags assessed | strategic-advisor (Peter Thiel) |
| G2 — Financial model built | Three scenarios, synergy analysis, financing options | strategic-advisor (Peter Thiel) |
| G3 — Due diligence complete | All workstreams report findings, risk matrix produced | governance-clerk (Simon Sinek) |
| G4 — Board decision | Approve/reject/conditional with rationale and dissent recorded | board-chair (Board Chair) |
| G5 — Integration plan approved | Day 1, 30-day, 90-day, 12-month plans reviewed by board | strategic-advisor (Peter Thiel) |

## Outputs

- Initial screening scorecard.
- Strategic and financial acquisition case.
- Due diligence findings by workstream.
- Risk matrix and synthesis.
- Negotiation parameters and term sheet.
- Board decision memo.
- Integration plan (Day 1 through 12 months).
- 12-month acquisition retrospective.

## Metrics

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Screen turnaround | ≤7 days from opportunity to go/no-go |
| Due diligence thoroughness | All 5 workstreams completed with findings reports |
| Model accuracy | Actual performance within ±25 % of base case at 12 months |
| Synergy realization | ≥70 % of projected synergies captured within 18 months |
| Integration milestone completion | ≥80 % of milestones met on schedule |
| Acquisition success rate | ≥70 % of acquisitions rated "strategic objectives achieved" at 12-month review |

## Common Failures

| Failure Mode | Symptom | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Winner's curse | Overpaying because of deal excitement | capital-allocator presents the counter-case; board-chair sets walk-away price before bidding |
| Synergy fantasy | Projected synergies never materialize | Probability-weight synergies; track realization monthly |
| Integration neglect | Deal closes but integration never really happens | Integration plan is a gate; board reviews monthly for 12 months |
| Cultural collision | Acquired team leaves within 12 months | People due diligence includes cultural assessment; retention plans for key talent |
| Due diligence speed-run | Pressure to close fast leads to superficial diligence | governance-clerk enforces the checklist; board-chair can slow the process |
| Distraction cost | The acquisition process consumes executive bandwidth | Time-box executive involvement; delegate where possible |
