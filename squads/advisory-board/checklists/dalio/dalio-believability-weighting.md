# Dalio Believability Weighting Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures that decisions appropriately weight input based on the demonstrated competence and track record of contributors, not their seniority, volume, or assertiveness. Dalio's concept of believability-weighted decision-making means that not all opinions are equal: the opinion of someone who has repeatedly navigated similar situations successfully should carry more weight. This checklist prevents both the tyranny of authority (highest-paid person's opinion wins) and the tyranny of democracy (all votes weighted equally regardless of expertise).

## When to Use
- When making decisions involving input from multiple people with varying expertise.
- When resolving disagreements about strategy, product, or organizational issues.
- When evaluating the reliability of forecasts or recommendations from different sources.
- When designing decision-making processes for committees or advisory boards.

## Gate Criteria

### Believability Assessment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: For each key decision input, the contributor's relevant track record is identified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Track record is assessed on outcomes in analogous situations, not credentials or tenure.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Contributors are transparent about the limits of their expertise and experience.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The assessment distinguishes between domain expertise and general intelligence.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Multiple dimensions of believability are considered: technical knowledge, judgment, pattern recognition.

### Decision Process Integration
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The decision process explicitly acknowledges differences in believability among contributors.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Higher-believability contributors have proportionally more influence, not just equal votes.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The weighting methodology is transparent, not hidden or applied post-hoc.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Outlier views from high-believability individuals receive serious examination even if they are minority positions.

### Safeguards Against Abuse
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Believability is domain-specific; high believability in one area does not grant authority in others.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: New information can override prior track record when the evidence is compelling.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The system allows for emerging expertise; new team members can build believability through demonstrated results.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The process guards against groupthink among high-believability individuals.

### Calibration and Learning
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decision outcomes are tracked against the predictions of contributors to calibrate future believability.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Contributors who are consistently wrong have their weighting adjusted.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization learns from cases where low-believability contributors were right and high-believability contributors were wrong.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Believability assessments are revisited as the company's strategic context changes.

## Red Flags
- The CEO's opinion always wins regardless of the domain or the expertise of others in the room.
- No one tracks whether advisors and decision-makers have good prediction track records.
- Expertise is assessed by credentials and years of experience rather than demonstrated outcomes.
- A culture of false egalitarianism gives equal weight to all opinions despite vast differences in relevant experience.
- Believability weighting is used as a tool to silence dissent by labeling dissenters as low-believability.
- The same individuals dominate every decision regardless of domain relevance.

## Escalation Triggers
- A decision is dominated by a single voice whose track record in the relevant domain is poor.
- Post-mortem reveals that a high-believability contributor's warnings were ignored without reason.
- The believability weighting system is perceived as unfair or opaque by team members.
- A pattern emerges of consistently overweighting seniority over domain expertise.
- New team members with relevant expertise are systematically excluded from key decisions.
