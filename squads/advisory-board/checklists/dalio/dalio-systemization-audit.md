# Dalio Systemization Audit Checklist

## Purpose
Evaluates whether the organization has systematized its key processes and decision-making so they are repeatable, improvable, and not dependent on heroic individual effort. Dalio views an organization as a machine that should produce predictable outcomes. Systemization means converting ad-hoc practices into documented, testable, and refinable processes. This checklist identifies where the organization relies on tribal knowledge rather than systems.

## When to Use
- During annual operational reviews.
- When a key employee leaves and their knowledge is at risk of being lost.
- When scaling requires processes that worked informally to work formally.
- When the same type of error occurs repeatedly despite prior corrections.
- When evaluating organizational readiness for growth.

## Gate Criteria

### Process Documentation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Critical processes are documented with steps, decision criteria, and responsible roles.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Documentation is current (reviewed within the last 12 months).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Documentation is accessible to all relevant team members.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Process documentation includes the purpose and expected outcome, not just steps.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Exception handling is documented: what to do when the standard process does not apply.

### Automation and Tooling
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Repetitive manual tasks have been evaluated for automation opportunities.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Tools and systems support the documented processes rather than requiring workarounds.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Data collection for process monitoring is automated where feasible.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Alerts and notifications exist for process deviations or failures.

### Measurement and Feedback
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Key processes have defined metrics for effectiveness and efficiency.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Process performance is reviewed on a regular cadence.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Feedback from process participants is collected and used for improvement.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Process improvements are tracked with before/after comparisons.

### Dependency Reduction
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: No critical process depends on a single individual's knowledge or availability.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Cross-training plans exist for all critical roles.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Institutional knowledge is captured in systems, not just in people's heads.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: New team members can execute core processes within their first 30 days using documentation.

## Red Flags
- The same process produces wildly different outcomes depending on who executes it.
- A key employee's vacation or absence creates visible operational disruption.
- Processes exist on paper but are not followed in practice.
- There is no documentation for processes that the company would not survive losing.
- "That's how we've always done it" is the only rationale for current process design.
- Process improvement suggestions are ignored or deprioritized indefinitely.

## Escalation Triggers
- A key person departure causes a material operational failure.
- A process failure causes customer harm or regulatory violation.
- An audit reveals that documented processes do not reflect actual practice.
- New hires consistently report being unable to learn critical processes from documentation.
- The organization cannot scale a successful pilot because the process is not systematized.
