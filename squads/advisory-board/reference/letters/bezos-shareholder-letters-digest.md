# Bezos Shareholder Letters Digest

## Overview

Jeff Bezos's annual letters to Amazon shareholders (1997-2020) chart the evolution of Amazon from online bookseller to global infrastructure company. The letters are a masterclass in long-term thinking, customer obsession, high-velocity decision-making, and operational invention. This digest synthesizes the most decision-relevant themes for board-level application.

## Core Philosophy: Day 1 Thinking

- **Day 1 vs. Day 2** — Day 1 is a mentality of urgency, experimentation, and customer obsession. Day 2 is stasis, irrelevance, painful decline, and death. The goal is to always be Day 1.
- **Day 2 symptoms** — Process becomes proxy for results, decisions slow to consensus, market research replaces customer intuition, managing to metrics replaces serving customers.
- **The antidote** — True customer obsession (not competitor obsession), resist proxies, embrace external trends eagerly, and maintain high-velocity decision-making.

## Customer Obsession

- **Start with the customer and work backward** — Not from capabilities, not from technology, not from competitors. The customer is the beginning of everything.
- **Customers are divinely discontent** — They always want better, even when they can't articulate what better looks like. Anticipate their desires before they express them.
- **Inputs over outputs** — Focus on controllable inputs (selection, price, convenience, speed) and the outputs (revenue, profit, market share) will follow.

## Decision-Making Framework

- **Type 1 vs. Type 2 decisions** — Type 1: irreversible, high-consequence (one-way doors). Type 2: reversible, lower-consequence (two-way doors). Most decisions are Type 2 and should be made quickly by individuals or small groups.
- **Disagree and commit** — When a decision must be made and there's no consensus, a leader can disagree with the direction but commit fully to its execution. This prevents slow consensus-seeking.
- **70% information rule** — If you wait for 90% of the information, you're too slow. Make decisions at 70% and course-correct quickly.
- **High-velocity decisions** — Speed matters more than perfection for reversible decisions. Being wrong at high speed is better than being right slowly.

## Operational Invention

- **Working backward (PR/FAQ)** — Start with the press release for the finished product, then work backward to build it. This ensures customer value is defined before a line of code is written.
- **Two-pizza teams** — Teams small enough to be fed by two pizzas. Small autonomous teams move faster, innovate more, and have clearer accountability.
- **Missionaries, not mercenaries** — Teams should be driven by mission, not metrics. Missionary teams build better products and sustain effort through difficulties.
- **Failure and invention are inseparable** — To invent, you must experiment. To experiment, you must tolerate failure. Scaling the size of your experiments increases both failure rate and the magnitude of successes.

## Long-Term Orientation

- **It's always Day 1** — This phrase anchored every letter from 1997 forward.
- **Optimize for free cash flow, not earnings** — Earnings can be manipulated; free cash flow is real. Per-share free cash flow is the true measure of value creation.
- **The long-term market weights investments correctly** — Short-term investors punish investments in the future. Long-term investors reward them. Choose your shareholders.
- **High standards are contagious** — Set them in one area and they spread to others. Lower them in one area and that spreads too.

## Board Application

- Apply the Type 1/Type 2 framework to every board decision to calibrate appropriate speed and rigor
- Use "disagree and commit" to prevent board gridlock
- Monitor for Day 2 symptoms in the organization
- Apply the PR/FAQ method for evaluating new product or market entry proposals
- Evaluate whether the company optimizes for inputs (controllable) or outputs (lagging)
