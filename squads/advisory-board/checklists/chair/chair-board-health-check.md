# Chair Board Health Check Checklist

## Purpose
Provides the chair with a diagnostic tool for assessing the overall health and effectiveness of the advisory board as a functioning body. Board health is more than the sum of individual member quality; it includes dynamics, culture, information flow, and the quality of the board-management relationship. This checklist surfaces systemic issues that reduce board effectiveness.

## When to Use
- Annually as a formal board self-assessment.
- When the chair senses declining board engagement or effectiveness.
- After significant board composition changes.
- When the board-management relationship shows strain.
- When governance failures or near-misses occur.

## Gate Criteria

### Engagement and Participation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board meeting attendance exceeds 80% for all members over the trailing 12 months.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: All board members participate actively in discussions, not just one or two dominant voices.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board members come prepared, having read and annotated pre-read materials.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board members make themselves available between meetings when needed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board members contribute beyond meetings (introductions, mentoring, expert input).

### Dynamics and Culture
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board has a culture of constructive challenge, not deference or antagonism.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Trust levels between board members are high enough for candid conversation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: New board members are effectively onboarded and integrated into the group dynamics.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The chair manages dynamics effectively, preventing domination or exclusion.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Executive sessions (without management) occur regularly and are productive.

### Board-Management Relationship
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Management provides complete and timely information to the board.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board provides constructive challenge without micromanaging.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The CEO uses the board as a genuine resource, not just a compliance requirement.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Difficult conversations between the board and management happen directly, not through intermediaries.

### Composition and Capability
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board's skills matrix covers the company's current strategic needs.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board diversity (expertise, perspective, background) is assessed and improvement plans exist.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board member tenure is appropriate: long enough for continuity, short enough for fresh perspective.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Succession planning for board seats is active, not reactive.

### Continuous Improvement
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board conducts an annual self-assessment and acts on findings.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board effectiveness feedback from management is solicited and considered.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board benchmarks its practices against governance best practices.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The chair seeks coaching or peer feedback on their facilitation effectiveness.

## Red Flags
- Board meetings feel like a performance for the board rather than a working session.
- One or two members dominate while others disengage.
- The CEO dreads board meetings rather than valuing them.
- Board members have not visited the company's operations or spoken with customers.
- The annual self-assessment is perfunctory or does not result in changes.
- Board members are overboarded and cannot give adequate attention.

## Escalation Triggers
- A board member's attendance drops below 60% without leave of absence.
- The board-management relationship deteriorates to the point of mutual distrust.
- A governance failure occurs that the board should have caught.
- The annual self-assessment reveals significant dissatisfaction without corrective action.
- A board member's behavior (domination, disengagement, confidentiality breach) is harming board effectiveness.
