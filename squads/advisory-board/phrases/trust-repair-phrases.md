# Trust Repair Phrases

Phrases for rebuilding trust after it has been damaged — between the board and management, between team members, with external stakeholders, or with the organization at large.

## Acknowledging the Break

1. **"Trust was damaged when [specific event]. I want to name that directly so we can address it."**
   Use to break the silence around a trust breach. You cannot repair what you will not name.

2. **"I understand why you lost trust. In your position, I would feel the same way."**
   Use to validate the other party's experience. Validation is not agreement — it is acknowledgment.

3. **"We said [X] and then did [Y]. That inconsistency is on us, and it's fair for you to question our reliability."**
   Use to own the gap between promise and behavior. Specificity demonstrates accountability.

4. **"I realize that my actions affected your trust in [this board / this company / this relationship]. I want to repair that, and I want to do it through actions, not just words."**
   Use to signal genuine intent. The commitment to action over words is critical — broken trust is not repaired by speeches.

5. **"I'm not going to ask you to trust me. I'm going to earn it back through consistent behavior over time."**
   Use when trust damage is severe. Asking for trust is presumptuous. Earning it is the only path.

## Taking Responsibility

6. **"Here is what I did wrong, specifically: [list]. Here is what I should have done instead: [list]."**
   Use to demonstrate genuine accountability. Vague apologies ("mistakes were made") do not rebuild trust.

7. **"I have no excuse. The context was [context], but the choice was mine, and it was the wrong one."**
   Use when context explains but does not excuse. Context without accountability is rationalization.

8. **"I owe you an apology. Not because someone told me to apologize, but because I genuinely regret [specific action]."**
   Use to make the apology credible. Coerced apologies damage trust further.

9. **"I broke a commitment. That's a violation of how I want to operate, and I take it seriously."**
   Use to demonstrate that the break matters to you, not just to the affected party.

10. **"You deserved better from me. Here is what I'm going to do differently."**
    Use to combine acknowledgment with commitment to change. The forward-looking commitment matters as much as the backward-looking acknowledgment.

## Rebuilding Through Action

11. **"I'm going to over-communicate with you for the next [period] to rebuild predictability. You'll hear from me [cadence]."**
    Use to create a structured trust-rebuilding cadence. Increased transparency and frequency demonstrate commitment.

12. **"I want to make small commitments and deliver on all of them before I make big commitments again."**
    Use to reset the trust bar at a manageable level. Small, reliable deliveries rebuild trust faster than grand promises.

13. **"Here is what I committed to last week. Here is exactly what I delivered. Any gaps, I'll explain."**
    Use as a recurring trust-rebuilding check-in. Transparent follow-through is the currency of trust repair.

14. **"I'm going to give you full visibility into [process/decision/information]. Ask me anything, and I'll be transparent."**
    Use to open up information that was previously restricted or opaque. Transparency is the fastest trust-building tool.

15. **"If I fall short again, I want you to call me on it immediately. Don't store it up."**
    Use to invite real-time accountability. The willingness to be held accountable is itself a trust signal.

## Rebuilding Team Trust

16. **"This team has been through a difficult period. I want to acknowledge that before we talk about moving forward."**
    Use to create space for acknowledging team damage. Teams cannot move forward if the past is unprocessed.

17. **"I've heard that people don't feel safe [speaking up / disagreeing / sharing bad news]. That's my failure as a leader, and I'm going to change it."**
    Use when leadership behavior has eroded psychological safety. Owning the problem is the first step.

18. **"I'm going to stop [specific behavior that damaged trust] starting today. If you see me doing it, tell me."**
    Use to make the behavior change observable and accountable. Public commitment creates social accountability.

19. **"What would it take for you to trust this team again? I'm asking because I genuinely want to know, not because I expect an easy answer."**
    Use to invite honest dialogue about trust repair. The answer may be uncomfortable, but it's essential.

## Stakeholder Trust Repair

20. **"We let you down. Here is our remediation plan: [specific actions, owners, timelines]."**
    Use with external stakeholders after a failure. Plans rebuild trust; promises do not.

21. **"We are implementing [specific change] to ensure this cannot happen again. You can verify this by [specific mechanism]."**
    Use to offer verifiable proof, not just assurance. Trust is rebuilt by evidence, not by claims.

22. **"We would like to earn back your business. We're willing to [specific concession] as a demonstration of our commitment."**
    Use when trust damage has a commercial dimension. Tangible gestures signal genuine intent.

## Long-Term Trust Maintenance

23. **"Trust is built in drops and lost in buckets. Let's be deliberate about the drops."**
    Use as a cultural reminder. Trust maintenance is an active practice, not a passive state.

24. **"The best time to invest in trust is when things are going well, not when they've broken."**
    Use to motivate proactive trust-building behaviors. Trust reserves are what carry relationships through crises.

25. **"How is our trust account with [stakeholder]? Are we depositing or withdrawing?"**
    Use as a metaphor in strategic discussions. Every interaction either builds or erodes trust.
