# Sinek Communication One-Liner Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures that important organizational messages can be distilled into clear, compelling one-liners that people can remember and repeat. Sinek demonstrates that great leaders communicate complex ideas simply. If a message cannot be reduced to its essence, it is not clear enough. This checklist verifies that strategic narratives, value propositions, and organizational messages pass the one-liner test.

## When to Use
- When crafting key messages for the board, investors, customers, or employees.
- When launching strategic initiatives that need organizational alignment.
- When evaluating whether the company's positioning is crisp enough to be memorable.
- During presentation and communication reviews.
- When employees cannot consistently articulate what the company does or why it matters.

## Gate Criteria

### Clarity
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The core message can be stated in one sentence that a non-expert would understand.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The one-liner avoids jargon, buzzwords, and internal language.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The message starts with Why (purpose), not What (product).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The message is specific enough to distinguish the company from any competitor.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A 12-year-old could understand the core idea (complexity test).

### Memorability
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The message is short enough to remember after hearing it once.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The message uses concrete language, not abstract concepts.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Key phrases are tested for stickiness: do people repeat them naturally.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The message resonates emotionally, not just intellectually.

### Consistency
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The same core message is used across all audiences with appropriate depth adjustments.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: All leaders can deliver the core message consistently.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Marketing, sales, and product descriptions all trace back to the same one-liner.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The message has remained stable long enough for it to sink in (not changed every quarter).

### Action Connection
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The message implies action: it is clear what the company does based on the Why.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The message helps employees make daily decisions aligned with the narrative.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customers can understand the value they receive from the one-liner alone.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The message creates alignment without requiring a 50-page strategy deck.

## Red Flags
- It takes five minutes to explain what the company does.
- The elevator pitch differs depending on who delivers it.
- The company description relies on industry jargon that outsiders do not understand.
- The message is about the product's features rather than the customer's transformation.
- The message has been workshopped so many times it is a compromise that inspires no one.
- Employees cannot articulate what the company does at a dinner party.

## Escalation Triggers
- Customers express confusion about what the company offers or why it matters.
- Investors or analysts struggle to position the company in their frameworks.
- Internal alignment suffers because teams interpret the company's mission differently.
- Recruiting is hampered because candidates do not have a clear picture of the company's purpose.
- Media coverage consistently mischaracterizes the company despite PR efforts.
