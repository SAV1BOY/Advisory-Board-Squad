# Investment Memo Examples

## Overview

Investment memos—whether for venture capital, M&A, or internal capital allocation—should force rigorous thinking about risk, return, and the assumptions underlying both. This file provides templates and examples for the most common investment memo types relevant to advisory boards.

## Venture/Growth Investment Memo

### Structure
1. **Company Overview** (3-5 sentences) — What does the company do, for whom, and why now?
2. **Team Assessment** — Founder backgrounds, specific knowledge, track record, team completeness
3. **Market** — TAM/SAM/SOM with methodology, growth drivers, market timing
4. **Product** — What it does, competitive differentiation, product-market fit evidence
5. **Business Model** — Revenue model, unit economics, margins, scalability
6. **Traction** — Key metrics and their trajectory (MRR, growth rate, retention, engagement)
7. **Competition** — Competitive landscape, moats, vulnerability to disruption
8. **Deal Terms** — Valuation, structure, investor rights, use of proceeds
9. **Key Risks** — The 3-5 things most likely to cause failure
10. **Investment Thesis** — Why this investment will generate returns despite the risks

### Example: Series A Investment Memo (Abbreviated)

**Company:** DataPipe — AI-powered data pipeline automation for mid-market companies.

**Team:** CEO (ex-Snowflake engineering lead, 12 years data infrastructure), CTO (PhD distributed systems, 3 patents), VP Sales (ex-Segment, $0-$8M ARR build). Gap: no CFO yet.

**Market:** Data infrastructure market $42B by 2027 (Gartner). Mid-market segment underserved — enterprise tools too complex and expensive, SMB tools too simple. Target: 50K companies, $30K ACV = $1.5B serviceable market.

**Traction:** $1.8M ARR, 15% MoM growth, 130% net revenue retention, 42 customers (6 > $50K ACV). NDR above SaaS median.

**Unit Economics:** CAC $18K, LTV $84K, LTV/CAC 4.7x, Payback 8 months. Gross margin 82%.

**Key Risks:** (1) Enterprise vendors move downmarket, (2) Open-source alternative gains traction, (3) Key person risk on CTO, (4) Sales cycle elongation in downturn, (5) Data privacy regulation changes.

**Investment Thesis:** Mid-market data infrastructure is an underserved, high-growth segment with demonstrated willingness to pay. The team has rare domain expertise and has achieved capital-efficient growth with strong unit economics. Risk-adjusted return potential: 8-12x at current entry price.

## M&A Evaluation Memo

### Structure
1. **Strategic Rationale** — Why this acquisition, why now
2. **Target Overview** — Business, financials, team, technology
3. **Synergies** — Revenue synergies (with skepticism), cost synergies (more reliable), strategic synergies
4. **Valuation** — Multiple approaches: DCF, comparable transactions, comparable companies
5. **Integration Plan** — 90-day plan for Day 1, structural integration, cultural integration
6. **Deal-Breakers** — Conditions that would cause us to walk away
7. **Alternative Uses of Capital** — What else could we do with this money?

## Internal Capital Allocation Memo

### Structure
1. **Opportunity** — What are we investing in and why?
2. **Expected Return** — IRR, payback period, NPV
3. **Assumptions** — Listed explicitly with sensitivity analysis
4. **Alternatives** — Other uses of this capital (including returning it to shareholders)
5. **Reversibility** — Can we unwind this if it doesn't work?
6. **Decision** — Go, no-go, or further diligence

## Board Application

- Every material investment should have a written memo reviewed at least 48 hours before the board vote
- Require explicit risk sections; memos that only present upside are advocacy documents, not analysis
- Keep a library of past investment memos to audit prediction accuracy over time
- Use the memo format to slow down decisions that deserve deliberation
