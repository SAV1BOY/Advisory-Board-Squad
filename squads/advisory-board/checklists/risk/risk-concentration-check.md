# Risk Concentration Check Checklist

## Purpose
Identifies and manages concentration risks: situations where the organization is dangerously dependent on a single customer, vendor, employee, technology, channel, or market. Concentration is the quiet killer of organizations; everything looks fine until the concentrated dependency fails.

## When to Use
- Quarterly during risk reviews.
- When evaluating new customer or vendor relationships.
- When key person risk is identified.
- When assessing the resilience of the business model.

## Gate Criteria
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Revenue concentration is mapped: no single customer accounts for more than 20% of revenue without explicit risk acceptance.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Vendor concentration is mapped: no single vendor provides a critical service without a backup option.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Key person risk is identified: roles where one departure would cause major disruption.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Technology concentration is assessed: single points of failure in critical systems.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Channel concentration is evaluated: dependency on a single sales or distribution channel.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Geographic concentration is assessed: exposure to a single market or jurisdiction.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: For each concentration risk, a mitigation or diversification plan exists.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Concentration levels are reported to the board at least quarterly.

## Red Flags
- The top three customers account for more than 50% of revenue.
- A single vendor powers the entire technology infrastructure with no failover.
- One employee holds all critical knowledge for a core business function.
- All growth comes from a single channel controlled by a third party.

## Escalation Triggers
- A concentrated dependency shows signs of instability (customer dissatisfaction, vendor financial trouble, key employee disengagement).
- Concentration exceeds the board-approved threshold.
- A competitor targets the organization's concentrated dependency (poaching the key customer or employee).
- A concentration risk materializes, causing material disruption.
