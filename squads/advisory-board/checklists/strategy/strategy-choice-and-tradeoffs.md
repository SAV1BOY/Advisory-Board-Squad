# Strategy Choice and Tradeoffs Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures every strategy articulates explicit choices and tradeoffs. Strategy without tradeoffs is not strategy; it is wishful thinking. This checklist forces clarity about what the organization will and will not do, what it is optimizing for, and what it is willing to sacrifice.

## When to Use
- During strategic planning and strategy reviews.
- When evaluating whether a strategic plan is genuinely strategic or just a list of goals.
- When the organization seems to be trying to do everything at once.

## Gate Criteria

### Choice Clarity
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The strategy states where the organization will compete (segments, geographies, channels).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The strategy states where the organization will NOT compete.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: How-to-win choices are explicit: cost leadership, differentiation, niche focus, or platform.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The choices are mutually reinforcing, not contradictory.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The choices are different from the top three competitors.

### Tradeoff Transparency
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: At least three material tradeoffs are identified and the chosen side is justified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Opportunity costs of the strategy are quantified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Resources allocated to chosen areas are explicitly NOT available for unchosen areas.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization has said no to at least one attractive opportunity because of strategic focus.

### Assumption Testing
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Critical assumptions underlying the strategy are listed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each assumption is tagged by confidence level and validation method.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Kill criteria exist: what would invalidate the strategy.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Assumptions are reviewed quarterly against new evidence.

### Communication and Alignment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: All leaders can articulate the strategy's key choices consistently.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Teams can explain why their priorities exist and what was deprioritized.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Resource allocation reflects stated strategic choices.

## Red Flags
- The strategy reads as a comprehensive list of everything the organization could do.
- No one can identify what the organization chose NOT to pursue.
- Every department has its own strategy that is not derived from the company strategy.
- The word "and" appears more than "or" in strategic statements.

## Escalation Triggers
- Resource allocation contradicts stated strategic choices.
- The organization pursues contradictory strategies simultaneously.
- Strategic assumptions are invalidated but the strategy is not updated.
