# Crisis Communications Framework

## Purpose

This framework provides a structured protocol for communicating during organizational crises — data breaches, product failures, PR incidents, leadership departures, financial difficulties, or regulatory actions. The first 24 hours of crisis communication determine whether the organization emerges with trust intact or permanently damaged. Speed, honesty, and empathy are the three non-negotiable elements.

## When to Use

- Immediately upon identifying any event that could generate significant negative attention
- When a product failure affects customers
- When a data breach or security incident occurs
- When a key leader departs unexpectedly
- When negative press coverage threatens the brand
- When regulatory or legal action is taken against the company

## Core Model

### The Crisis Communication Protocol

**Hour 0-1: Assess and Assemble**
- Assess the situation: What happened? Who is affected? What is the ongoing risk?
- Assemble the crisis team: CEO, communications, legal, relevant function head
- Determine who needs to know first: affected parties, employees, board, media

**Hour 1-4: First Response**
- Communicate to affected parties FIRST (customers, employees)
- Acknowledge the situation. Do not deny, deflect, or minimize.
- State what you know, what you don't know, and when you'll provide an update
- Template: "We are aware of [situation]. We are [actions being taken]. We will update by [time]."

**Hour 4-24: Full Communication**
- Detailed communication to all stakeholders
- What happened (facts, not speculation)
- What the impact is (honest assessment)
- What you are doing about it (specific actions)
- How you will prevent recurrence (systemic fix)
- How affected parties can get help (concrete support)

**Day 2+: Follow Through**
- Daily updates until the situation is resolved
- Deliver on every commitment made in initial communications
- Conduct a postmortem after the crisis is resolved
- Share the postmortem findings publicly (appropriate level of detail)

### The Three Non-Negotiables
1. **Speed:** Silence during a crisis is interpreted as guilt or incompetence. Communicate early, even if incomplete.
2. **Honesty:** Never lie, minimize, or deflect. Every dishonesty that surfaces later compounds the damage exponentially.
3. **Empathy:** Lead with concern for affected parties, not concern for the company's reputation.

## Steps

1. **Pre-crisis: Build the protocol now.** Don't wait for a crisis to design the communication plan.
2. **Designate a crisis team and spokesperson.**
3. **Create templates** for common crisis types (data breach, product failure, PR incident).
4. **When crisis hits: Execute the hour-by-hour protocol.**
5. **Document everything** in real-time for the postmortem.
6. **After crisis: Conduct postmortem and publish learnings.**

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Help companies build crisis communication protocols BEFORE they need them.
- During a crisis, the board's role is to support the CEO's communication, not to take over.
- Push for speed and honesty. Lawyers will want to say nothing; communications should push for transparency within legal boundaries.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Silence.** The most damaging response. Nature abhors a vacuum — if you don't tell the story, someone else will.
2. **Minimizing.** "This only affected a small number of users" when the number is actually large. Always discovered, always devastating.
3. **Blame-shifting.** "Our vendor caused this" may be true but still reads as avoidance of responsibility.
4. **Legal-first communication.** Letting lawyers write the statement produces language that protects the company but alienates stakeholders.
5. **No follow-through.** Making promises during the crisis and not delivering.
