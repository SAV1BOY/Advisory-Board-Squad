# Sinek Why Clarity Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the organization has a clear, authentic, and consistently communicated "Why": the purpose, cause, or belief that inspires the organization beyond making money. Simon Sinek's Golden Circle framework states that people do not buy what you do; they buy why you do it. This checklist verifies that the Why is genuine, understood, and operational, not just a marketing tagline.

## When to Use
- When founding or refounding the organization's purpose statement.
- When the organization feels directionless or employees lack motivation.
- When hiring to assess whether candidates connect with the Why.
- When making strategic decisions to verify alignment with core purpose.
- During annual culture and brand reviews.

## Gate Criteria

### Why Articulation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization's Why is stated in a single sentence that focuses on impact and belief, not products or services.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The Why is authentic: it reflects what the founders or leaders genuinely believe.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The Why is distinct from the What (products) and the How (processes).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The Why remains constant even as products, markets, and strategies change.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The Why can be tested: decisions that conflict with the Why are identifiable.

### Organizational Understanding
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Employees at all levels can articulate the Why in their own words.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: New hires are taught the Why as a core part of onboarding.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The Why is referenced in decision-making, not just in marketing materials.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders use the Why as a filter for strategic choices.

### Consistency and Authenticity
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The Why is consistent across all communications: internal, external, investor, customer.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Company actions and investments are consistent with the stated Why.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customers and partners can perceive the Why through their experience with the company.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: When the company faces a difficult choice, the Why provides genuine guidance.

### Why-Driven Impact
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The Why attracts and retains talent who share the belief.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The Why differentiates the company in the market beyond product features.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The Why creates emotional loyalty among customers that transcends rational comparison.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The Why motivates resilience during difficult periods.

## Red Flags
- The Why reads like a marketing tagline with no operational substance.
- Employees cannot distinguish the company's Why from any competitor's mission statement.
- The Why was crafted by an external agency without deep involvement from leadership.
- Products and decisions frequently contradict the stated Why without acknowledgment.
- The Why has changed three times in five years, suggesting it is not authentic.
- No one references the Why in daily work or decision-making.

## Escalation Triggers
- A major strategic decision directly contradicts the stated Why.
- Employee surveys reveal widespread disconnect between the Why and day-to-day experience.
- Customer perception of the company contradicts the stated Why.
- The organization cannot retain mission-driven talent because the Why feels hollow.
- A competitor articulates a clearer, more authentic Why and attracts the organization's talent and customers.
