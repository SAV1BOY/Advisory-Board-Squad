# SaaS Industry Benchmarks

## Key Findings

1. **The "Rule of 40" remains the gold standard for SaaS health, but expectations have shifted.** Revenue growth rate + profit margin should exceed 40%. In the current market, investors weight profitability more heavily — a company growing 25% with 20% margins is valued more favorably than one growing 45% with 0% margins.
2. **Net revenue retention (NRR) is the single strongest predictor of long-term enterprise value.** Top-quartile SaaS companies maintain NRR above 120%, meaning they grow revenue from existing customers by 20%+ annually even with zero new customer acquisition.
3. **Gross margins separate SaaS from services.** True SaaS companies maintain 70-85% gross margins. Companies below 65% are typically misclassified — they have significant services, infrastructure, or support cost components that require different valuation frameworks.
4. **Payback period has become more important than CAC ratio.** Investors now focus on months to recover CAC (target: <18 months for SMB, <24 months for mid-market, <30 months for enterprise) rather than LTV/CAC ratios which are easily manipulated.
5. **Logo churn masks revenue churn.** A company can have 15% annual logo churn but only 5% revenue churn if small customers churn and large customers expand. Both metrics must be tracked separately.

## Implications for Board

- **Benchmark against stage, not aspiration.** A Series A company should not be measured against Series C benchmarks. The board should use stage-appropriate targets and focus on trajectory (rate of improvement) rather than absolute position.
- **NRR as strategic indicator.** If NRR is below 100%, the company has a leaky bucket problem that no amount of new customer acquisition can fix. This should be a board-level priority before increasing sales spend.
- **Gross margin scrutiny.** If gross margins are trending down, investigate whether professional services, hosting costs, or customer support costs are scaling faster than revenue. This signals architectural or operational problems.
- **Burn multiple as funding readiness signal.** A burn multiple above 2x (burning $2 for every $1 of new ARR) signals inefficient growth that will make fundraising difficult. The board should monitor this monthly.
- **Churn decomposition.** Require management to present churn segmented by customer size, cohort, and reason. Aggregate churn numbers hide the actionable insight.
