# Language Guide: Meeting Facilitation

## Purpose

This guide provides the language patterns for facilitating Advisory Board meetings — opening, managing discussion, handling conflict, driving decisions, and closing. Effective facilitation uses deliberate language to keep meetings focused, inclusive, and productive.

## Opening a Meeting

### Setting context
- "We have 90 minutes and three decisions to make. Let me walk through the agenda so we can manage our time."
- "The pre-reads covered the background. I'll assume everyone has read them. If you haven't, say so now — no shame, but we need to know."
- "Before we start, any conflicts of interest to declare on today's topics?"

### Establishing norms
- "A reminder of how we work: we want genuine disagreement in this room. If you disagree, say so. That's why you're here."
- "I'll be watching the clock and may cut discussions short. That's not rudeness — it's respect for everyone's time."
- "Let's aim for decisions, not discussions. If a topic isn't ready for a decision, we'll say so explicitly and schedule it properly."

## Managing Discussion

### Inviting participation
- "I haven't heard from [name] yet. [Name], what's your read on this?"
- "Let's do a quick round-robin. 60 seconds each. Where do you come out on this question?"
- "I'm hearing agreement from several voices. Before we converge, does anyone see this differently?"
- "Who has the strongest objection to what's been proposed? I want to hear it."

### Redirecting
- "That's an important point, and it connects to the next agenda item. Let's hold it for 20 minutes."
- "We're drifting into implementation. The board's question is whether to approve the direction. Implementation belongs with the exec team."
- "We've been on this topic for 15 minutes. I need to check: are we making progress, or are we circling?"
- "Let me restate the question we're trying to answer: [restate]. Is that still the right question?"

### Deepening
- "You said [X]. Can you give me a specific example?"
- "What's the strongest argument against your own position?"
- "If we assume you're right about [premise], what else would have to be true?"
- "Let's steelman the opposing view for a moment. Who can make the best case for option B?"

### Managing energy
- "I can feel the energy dropping. Let's take a 5-minute break and come back sharp."
- "We just made a big decision. Let me acknowledge that before we rush to the next item."
- "This topic has more heat than light right now. Let's step back and name what's actually at stake."

## Driving Decisions

### Framing the decision
- "Here is the decision before us: [restate in one sentence]. The options are A, B, or defer. Not 'discuss more.'"
- "This is a [reversible / partially reversible / irreversible] decision. That affects how much deliberation it warrants."
- "The pre-read recommended option B. Let's hear the case against it before we converge."

### Testing for consensus
- "I'm going to attempt a summary of where I think we are. Correct me if I'm wrong."
- "It sounds like we're converging on [X]. Before I call it, does anyone feel strongly that we're making a mistake?"
- "Let's use a quick fist-of-five. Five means strong support. One means strong opposition. Show me."
- "I'm hearing two camps. Let me name them: Camp A believes [X], Camp B believes [Y]. Is that accurate?"

### Calling the decision
- "Based on this discussion, we are deciding to [decision]. The rationale is [rationale]. [Name] is the owner. We review in [timeframe]. Any objections?"
- "I hear dissent from [name]. Let me record that explicitly. [Name], is your dissent strong enough that you want us to reconsider, or are you willing to disagree and commit?"
- "We don't have consensus. I'm making the call: we go with [X]. Here's why. We will review at [date]."
- "We're not ready to decide. The missing information is [X]. [Name], please bring that to the next meeting. We defer."

### Recording
- "Let me read back the decision for the record: [decision], [rationale], [owner], [review date], [dissent]. Is that accurate?"
- "Decision-Tracker, confirm you have this."

## Handling Difficult Moments

### When someone dominates
- "Thank you, [name]. I want to make sure we hear other perspectives. [Other name], what's your take?"
- "[Name], you've made your point clearly. Let me invite a response before you continue."
- "I'm going to impose a 2-minute time limit per speaker so we can hear from everyone."

### When the room goes silent
- "Silence usually means one of two things: agreement or discomfort. Which is it?"
- "I'll interpret silence as agreement unless someone speaks up in the next 30 seconds."
- "Let me ask a different way: what are you most worried about with this proposal?"

### When conflict escalates
- "I notice the temperature rising. Let's pause and clarify: are we disagreeing about the facts, or about the interpretation?"
- "Both of you are making valid points. Let me see if I can find the underlying tension."
- "Let's separate the personal from the professional. The question on the table is [restate the question]."
- "I'm going to call a 5-minute break. When we come back, I want each of you to state the other person's position in a way they would endorse."

### When the discussion stalls
- "We've been going back and forth for 15 minutes without progress. Let me propose a path forward."
- "What specific piece of information, if we had it, would resolve this disagreement?"
- "Let's try inversion: instead of debating which option is best, what would make each option fail?"

## Closing a Meeting

### Summarizing
- "Let me run through what we decided today: [list each decision with owner and review date]."
- "We covered three topics. On topic one, we decided [X]. On topic two, we deferred pending [Y]. On topic three, we asked [name] to bring a revised proposal."

### Action items
- "Here are the action items. I'll read each one. If you're the owner, confirm you accept it."
- "Every action item has a deadline. If the deadline is unrealistic, say so now — not next meeting."

### Looking forward
- "The next meeting is [date]. Pre-reads are due [date]. [Name] and [name] are the pre-read owners."
- "If anything changes between now and the next meeting that the board should know about, notify me directly."

### Closing
- "Thank you for your time and candor today. This board works because you show up prepared and willing to disagree."
- "Meeting adjourned. Minutes will be in your inbox within 24 hours."
