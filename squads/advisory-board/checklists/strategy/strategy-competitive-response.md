# Strategy Competitive Response Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the organization has a disciplined approach to evaluating and responding to competitive moves rather than reacting emotionally or ignoring them. Not every competitive action warrants a response, and the wrong response can be more damaging than no response. This checklist provides a framework for calibrated competitive engagement.

## When to Use
- When a competitor makes a significant move (product launch, pricing change, acquisition, market entry).
- During quarterly competitive landscape reviews.
- When planning market entries that will provoke competitive responses.
- When competitive anxiety is driving internal decisions.

## Gate Criteria

### Assessment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The competitive move is analyzed factually, without emotional overreaction or dismissal.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The impact on the company's customers, revenue, and strategic position is estimated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The competitor's likely strategic intent behind the move is assessed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The move's sustainability is evaluated: is it a one-time action or a permanent shift.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer response to the competitive move is measured, not assumed.

### Response Evaluation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Multiple response options are evaluated, including no response.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each response option is assessed for alignment with the company's own strategy.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The cost and distraction of responding are weighed against the cost of not responding.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The second-order effects of each response (competitor's counter-response) are anticipated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The response strengthens the company's position rather than just matching the competitor.

### Discipline
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The company does not abandon its strategy to chase a competitor's move.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Competitive intelligence is gathered from multiple sources, not just one.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The decision to respond or not respond is documented with rationale.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The timeline for response is calibrated: fast enough to matter, slow enough to be thoughtful.

## Red Flags
- Every competitor move triggers an immediate, reactive response.
- Competitive intelligence is based on assumptions, not data.
- The company's strategy is primarily defined by what competitors are doing.
- Internal discussions about competitors are emotional rather than analytical.
- No mechanism exists for monitoring competitive landscape changes.

## Escalation Triggers
- A competitive move causes material customer attrition within 90 days.
- The company's strategic position fundamentally changes due to a competitor's action.
- The organization cannot agree on a response, causing internal paralysis.
- A series of competitive moves suggests a coordinated attack on the company's position.
