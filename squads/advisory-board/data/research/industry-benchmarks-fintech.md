# Fintech Industry Benchmarks

## Key Findings

1. **Regulatory compliance cost is the hidden margin killer.** Fintech companies spend 8-15% of revenue on compliance at scale, compared to 3-5% for SaaS companies. This structural cost differential means fintech companies need higher gross margins or higher revenue per customer to achieve comparable profitability.
2. **Embedded finance is outgrowing standalone fintech.** Non-financial companies embedding financial services (payments, lending, insurance) are capturing 22% of new fintech revenue. Standalone fintech companies face increasing pressure from platforms that add financial features to existing user relationships.
3. **Customer acquisition through trust is a fintech-specific moat.** Switching costs in financial services are higher than most tech categories because consumers must trust a company with their money. Once trust is established, fintech companies see 65-80% 3-year retention rates — significantly higher than SaaS or e-commerce.
4. **Unit economics mature slowly.** Fintech companies typically require 18-36 months for customer-level unit economics to turn positive due to high onboarding costs, fraud prevention investment, and regulatory compliance overhead. Boards must accept longer payback periods while ensuring the trajectory is positive.
5. **Revenue diversification from payments to financial services is the growth playbook.** Companies that start with payments (low margin, high volume) and expand into lending, insurance, or wealth management (higher margin) achieve the strongest long-term unit economics.

## Implications for Board

- **Compliance as strategic investment.** The board should not treat compliance cost as pure overhead. Companies that over-invest in compliance early build regulatory moats that protect against both competitors and enforcement risk. Require a compliance roadmap alongside the product roadmap.
- **Fraud and risk monitoring.** Fraud loss rates should be a standing board agenda item. A 10 basis point increase in fraud rate can eliminate margins entirely in payments. Require quarterly fraud trend reporting.
- **Regulatory relationship management.** The board should maintain a regulatory relationship map — which regulators oversee the company, what is the relationship quality, and what regulatory changes are on the horizon. At least one board member should have deep regulatory expertise.
- **Revenue diversification timeline.** If the company is mono-product (e.g., payments only), the board should review the revenue diversification strategy annually. Single-product fintech companies face margin compression as the category matures.
- **Trust metrics.** Unlike SaaS, fintech companies should track trust-specific metrics: customer-reported trust scores, social media sentiment, and complaint rates. A trust crisis in fintech can cause a bank-run-like churn event.
