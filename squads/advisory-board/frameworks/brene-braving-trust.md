# Brene Brown BRAVING Trust

## Purpose

BRAVING is Brene Brown's operationalized model of trust, breaking down the abstract concept into seven specific, observable, and actionable behaviors. Trust is not built in grand gestures but in small, everyday moments. This framework provides a concrete vocabulary for discussing trust — building it, diagnosing its breakdown, and repairing it. Each letter of BRAVING represents a specific trust behavior that can be assessed, discussed, and improved.

## When to Use

- When trust has been damaged and needs to be rebuilt
- When diagnosing why a relationship (professional or personal) feels "off"
- When building new team relationships and setting norms
- When evaluating trustworthiness of potential partners, hires, or advisors
- When giving feedback about trust-eroding behaviors
- As a regular self-assessment tool for leaders

## Core Model

### The BRAVING Inventory

**B — Boundaries:** You respect my boundaries. When you're not sure what's OK, you ask. You are willing to say no.

**R — Reliability:** You do what you say you'll do. You are aware of your competencies and limitations and don't overpromise.

**A — Accountability:** You own your mistakes, apologize, and make amends. You don't blame or deflect.

**V — Vault:** You don't share information or experiences that are not yours to share. I can trust that what I share stays between us.

**I — Integrity:** You choose courage over comfort. You practice your values rather than just professing them. You choose what's right over what's fun, fast, or easy.

**N — Non-judgment:** I can tell you how I feel and ask for what I need without being judged. You can do the same with me.

**G — Generosity:** You extend the most generous interpretation of my intentions, words, and actions.

## Steps

1. **Self-assess on each dimension.** Rate yourself 1-5 on each BRAVING element. Be honest — low scores are data, not failure.
2. **Assess the relationship.** For a specific relationship where trust is an issue, rate the other person on each dimension.
3. **Identify the gap.** Which specific dimension is the weakest? That is where trust-building effort should be focused.
4. **Have the conversation.** Use the BRAVING vocabulary to discuss trust specifically: "I need us to work on Vault — I don't feel confident that what I share stays between us."
5. **Define behavioral commitments.** For each weak dimension, define specific behavioral changes both parties commit to.
6. **Review regularly.** Trust is maintained through ongoing attention, not one-time fixes.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Use BRAVING to build trust with founders. If a founder doesn't trust their board, the board is ineffective regardless of the quality of its advice.
- When trust breaks down in the founder-board relationship, use BRAVING to diagnose the specific dimension that failed.
- Self-assess: Are you reliable (doing what you promised)? Do you maintain the vault (confidentiality)? Are you generous in interpretation?

## Common Mistakes

1. **Treating trust as binary.** Trust is a spectrum across seven dimensions. You can be trusted on some dimensions but not others.
2. **Focusing on the wrong dimension.** If the problem is Vault (confidentiality breach), improving Reliability won't help.
3. **Expecting trust without earning it.** Trust is earned through repeated demonstration, not declared.
4. **Not self-assessing.** Most people assess others' trustworthiness without examining their own.

- **Sinek Circle of Safety** — Trust is what the Circle of Safety is made of
- **Dalio Radical Transparency Protocol** — Transparency builds the Vault and Accountability dimensions
- **Principal-Agent Framework** — Trust reduces agency costs
