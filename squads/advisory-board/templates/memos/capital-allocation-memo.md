# Capital Allocation Decision Memo

## Template Guidelines
**Word Limit:** 1,200 words maximum (excluding headers and tables)
**Reading Time:** Under 8 minutes
**Use When:** Any capital deployment decision exceeding the management-delegated authority threshold, including investments, major expenditures, debt, or equity transactions.

### Executive Summary (75-100 words)
State the capital request, recommended action, and strategic rationale in one paragraph. Include the dollar amount, expected return, and timeline.

**Good Example:** "We request board approval to invest $1.2M over 9 months to build and launch an automated onboarding platform. Based on current customer acquisition rates, this investment will reduce onboarding cost by 60% ($480K/year savings), improve time-to-value from 45 to 12 days, and pay back in 30 months. The primary risk is engineering execution — we mitigate this by using a proven vendor framework and phased delivery."

**Bad Example:** "We need money to build a new onboarding system because the current one is bad."

---

### Investment Thesis (200-250 words)
Articulate why this capital should be deployed here rather than elsewhere. Address:
1. **The Problem:** What business problem does this capital solve? Quantify the cost of the problem.
2. **The Opportunity:** What value does this capital create? Quantify the expected return.
3. **Why Now:** What makes this the right time to invest? Reference market conditions, competitive dynamics, or internal readiness.
4. **Why This Approach:** What alternatives were evaluated and why is this the best use of capital?

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
1. **Revenue-only analysis:** Always include cost savings and strategic value, but also always quantify opportunity cost of deploying capital here vs. elsewhere.
2. **Hockey-stick projections:** If Year 3 revenue is 10x Year 1 without a clear mechanism, the model lacks credibility.
3. **Ignoring cash timing:** NPV and IRR matter, but so does the cash flow profile. A positive-NPV investment can kill a startup if the cash outflows happen too early.
4. **Contingency theater:** A 5% contingency on a novel project is not a contingency — it is optimism. Use 15-25% for projects with significant unknowns.
5. **Sunk cost framing:** Never justify additional investment by referencing what has already been spent. Evaluate the marginal investment against the marginal return.
