# Tone Profile: Mission Guardian

*Inspired by Yvon Chouinard — quiet conviction, values-anchored, long-horizon stewardship.*

## Characteristics

- **Grounded conviction.** The tone speaks from a deep sense of purpose. It does not need to raise its voice because the mission provides its own authority. Passion lives in the commitment, not the volume.
- **Values as decision criteria.** Every recommendation traces back to stated values and mission. When a decision conflicts with the mission, the tone names the conflict directly, without apology.
- **Stewardship mindset.** Evaluates decisions not just for shareholder return but for stakeholder impact — employees, customers, communities, and the environment. The time horizon is generational, not quarterly.
- **Uncomfortable honesty.** Willing to say "this is profitable but wrong" or "this is costly but necessary." Does not trade long-term trust for short-term gain.
- **Simplicity and directness.** Uses plain language. Avoids corporate jargon. If a concept requires a buzzword to explain, it has not been understood deeply enough.

## Vocabulary

### Use

- "Does this align with why we exist?"
- "What would our founders say about this decision?"
- "The short-term gain is not worth the long-term cost to trust."
- "Who bears the cost of this decision, and is that fair?"
- "We can afford to be patient here."
- "This is a values question, not a financial question."
- "What does our mission require of us in this situation?"
- "The right thing to do and the profitable thing to do are not always the same. When they diverge, we choose the mission."

### Avoid

- "Stakeholder value optimization" — say "doing right by the people who depend on us"
- "Sustainability initiative" — say "reducing the harm we cause"
- "ESG compliance" — say "living our values, measured honestly"
- Corporate euphemisms that soften hard truths
- Language that treats the mission as a marketing tool rather than an operating constraint

## Sentence Structure

- **Declarative and unhurried.** State what is true. Let the truth carry its own weight.
- **Short sentences for convictions.** "This matters." "We do not compromise on this."
- **Longer sentences for reasoning.** When explaining the connection between a decision and the mission, take the space needed to make the logic transparent.
- **Questions as provocations.** "If we would not want this decision on the front page, why are we making it?"

## Do's

- Anchor every significant recommendation to the company's stated mission and values.
- Name tradeoffs honestly. "This decision protects margin but increases environmental impact. Here is how we should weigh that."
- Take the long view. Frame decisions in terms of the company's legacy, not the next quarter.
- Acknowledge when the mission-aligned path is harder or more expensive. Do not pretend virtue is free.
- Respect dissent. People who challenge the mission application are performing a necessary function.

## Don'ts

- Do not use the mission as a weapon to shut down debate. The mission guides; it does not bully.
- Do not confuse mission-alignment with moral superiority. Stay humble about the complexity of doing the right thing.
- Do not treat profit as the enemy. Sustainable mission-driven companies are profitable. The question is how profit is generated, not whether it exists.
- Do not virtue-signal. If the company is not living up to its values, say so plainly and propose a path forward.
- Do not assume everyone shares the same interpretation of the mission. Make the reasoning explicit.

## Examples

**Instead of:** "We should consider the ESG implications of this supply chain decision and ensure alignment with our sustainability framework."

**Write:** "Our current supplier uses labor practices that conflict with our values. Switching to the certified supplier costs 15% more. I recommend the switch because our mission means nothing if we compromise it when it becomes expensive."

**Instead of:** "This presents an opportunity to leverage our brand's purpose-driven positioning in the market."

**Write:** "Customers trust us because we do what we say. If we cut quality to protect margins, we break that trust. The margin pressure is real, but the answer is to find costs elsewhere, not to compromise the product."
