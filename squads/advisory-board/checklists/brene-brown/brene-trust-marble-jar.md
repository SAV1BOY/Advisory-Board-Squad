# Brene Brown Trust Marble Jar Checklist

## Purpose
Applies Brene Brown's marble jar metaphor for trust: trust is built in small moments of connection and reliability, not in grand gestures. Every small act of keeping a commitment, listening attentively, or choosing vulnerability adds a marble to the jar. Every broken promise, dismissive behavior, or inconsistency removes marbles. This checklist tracks the micro-behaviors that accumulate into trust or distrust over time.

## When to Use
- When assessing the health of a specific relationship (leader-team, peer-peer, company-customer).
- When trust has eroded and the cause needs to be diagnosed.
- When onboarding new leaders to establish trust-building expectations.
- During team retrospectives to surface trust dynamics.
- As a framework for 360-degree feedback conversations.

## Gate Criteria

### Trust-Building Behaviors (Adding Marbles)
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders follow through on small commitments consistently (returning calls, keeping meeting times, delivering promised information).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: People remember and reference previous conversations, showing they listen and value input.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Confidences are kept: information shared in trust is not disclosed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: People check in during difficult times without being asked.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Credit is shared generously and blame is accepted personally.

### Trust-Eroding Behaviors (Removing Marbles)
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization monitors for patterns of broken small commitments.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Gossiping about colleagues is identified and addressed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders who take credit for others' work are held accountable.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Inconsistency between what is said in meetings and what happens after is tracked.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Dismissive behaviors (eye-rolling, interrupting, ignoring input) are addressed.

### Relationship Health
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Key relationships in the organization are assessed for trust level on a regular basis.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Low-trust relationships are identified and intervention plans exist.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Trust repair mechanisms are known and used when marbles are removed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization invests in relationship-building time (not just task-focused interaction).

### Systemic Trust Conditions
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Organizational processes support trust-building (transparency, consistency, follow-through).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Systems do not inadvertently undermine trust (surveillance, micromanagement, information hoarding).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Trust is recognized as a measurable organizational asset, not a soft concept.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Trust-building is part of leadership development and performance evaluation.

## Red Flags
- Leaders make many promises but follow through on few.
- People say one thing in meetings and another in private conversations.
- Small courtesies (thank you, acknowledgment, checking in) have disappeared from the culture.
- Trust is demanded but not earned through consistent behavior.
- The organization focuses on big trust-building events (offsites, retreats) while neglecting daily micro-behaviors.
- There is a visible gap between stated intentions and actual follow-through.

## Escalation Triggers
- A key relationship (CEO-board, leader-team, company-customer) shows signs of trust collapse.
- Trust erosion is causing visible operational dysfunction (information withholding, lack of collaboration).
- A leader's trust deficit is identified but no corrective action is taken for 60 days.
- Employee surveys reveal declining trust metrics across multiple teams.
- A trust breach becomes public and damages the organization's reputation.
