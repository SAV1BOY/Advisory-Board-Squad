# Fintech Industry Reference

## Business Model Characteristics
- **Revenue models:** Transaction fees, interchange, subscription, lending margins, float income, data monetization
- **Regulatory reality:** Financial services are heavily regulated. Compliance is not optional and is expensive.
- **Trust requirement:** Money is personal. Trust barriers are higher than in most software categories.
- **Unit economics:** Often thin margins per transaction but high volume potential. Scale is essential.

## Key Segments
- **Payments:** Processing, POS, cross-border, B2B payments. Margins thin (0.1-0.5%) but massive volume.
- **Lending:** Consumer, SMB, embedded lending. Revenue from interest spread. Risk management is core competency.
- **Banking-as-a-Service:** Providing banking infrastructure to non-banks. Regulatory complexity is the moat.
- **Wealth management / investing:** Robo-advisors, fractional investing, alternative assets. AUM-based revenue.
- **Insurance (Insurtech):** Distribution, underwriting, claims automation. Long regulatory approval cycles.
- **Crypto / digital assets:** Exchanges, wallets, DeFi infrastructure. Extreme regulatory uncertainty.

## Regulatory Landscape
- Know Your Customer (KYC) and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) are non-negotiable
- Licensing requirements vary by jurisdiction and product type
- Banking charter vs. money transmitter license vs. broker-dealer registration
- Data privacy (GDPR, LGPD, CCPA) applies to all financial data

## Board Considerations
- Regulatory compliance is a board-level responsibility. Ensure compliance is adequately funded and staffed.
- Evaluate whether the fintech has its own licenses or depends on banking partners. Partnership dependency is a strategic risk.
- Monitor credit risk carefully if the company does any lending. Defaults in a downturn can be existential.
- Assess whether the regulatory moat is widening (protecting the business) or the regulatory burden is growing (threatening the business).
