# Munger Inversion Check Checklist

## Purpose
Applies Munger's principle of inversion: instead of only asking "how do we succeed?" systematically ask "how would we fail?" and then avoid those failure modes. Inversion reveals risks and failure paths that forward-thinking often misses because people are naturally biased toward optimistic planning. This checklist ensures that pre-mortem and inversion thinking are integrated into every significant decision.

## When to Use
- Before committing to any major strategic initiative or investment.
- During the planning phase of new products, partnerships, or market entries.
- When a plan seems flawless and no risks have been identified.
- As a complement to standard business case analysis.
- During board review of management proposals.

## Gate Criteria

### Inversion Exercise
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A pre-mortem has been conducted: the team imagined the initiative failed and worked backward to identify causes.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: At least five distinct failure modes have been identified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Failure modes are specific and actionable, not generic ("poor execution").
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The most embarrassing, painful, or costly failure scenario has been explicitly described.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: External factors that could cause failure (market shifts, competitor actions, regulatory changes) are included.

### Avoidance Strategies
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each identified failure mode has a specific avoidance or mitigation strategy.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The avoidance strategies are incorporated into the execution plan, not listed separately.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Early warning indicators for each failure mode are defined and monitored.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Kill criteria are established: what evidence would cause us to stop the initiative before the full investment is made.

### Behavioral and Cognitive Failure Modes
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The analysis includes cognitive biases that could lead to failure: overconfidence, sunk cost, confirmation bias.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Organizational dynamics that could cause failure are assessed: turf wars, information silos, misaligned incentives.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team has asked: "What would a smart, well-funded competitor do to defeat this initiative?"
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The team has asked: "What would we do if this initiative were someone else's proposal we were evaluating?"

### Integration with Forward Planning
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The inversion analysis changes the forward plan (it is not just an exercise).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Resources are allocated to the most critical avoidance strategies.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The decision-maker can articulate what makes them confident despite the identified failure modes.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The inversion findings are included in the decision memo presented to the board.

## Red Flags
- The pre-mortem produces only one or two generic failure modes.
- The team treats inversion as a required exercise but does not change the plan based on findings.
- No one can articulate how the initiative could fail.
- The team immediately dismisses failure scenarios as unlikely without analysis.
- The inversion exercise is conducted by the same team that designed the initiative with no external perspective.
- The team confuses identifying risks with managing risks.

## Escalation Triggers
- An initiative fails in exactly the way the inversion exercise predicted but no mitigation was implemented.
- The team refuses to conduct an inversion exercise citing time pressure.
- Kill criteria are reached but the initiative continues because of sunk cost attachment.
- A pattern emerges of consistently underestimating the same category of failure mode.
- The board receives proposals without inversion analysis for material decisions.
