# Incentive Map

## Purpose

A structured tool for mapping the explicit and implicit incentives that drive behavior among all stakeholders affected by a decision. Most decision failures are not failures of analysis but failures to account for how people's incentives shape their actions. An incentive map surfaces alignment and misalignment before a decision is made, not after it fails.

## How to Use

### Step 1: List All Stakeholders
Identify every person, role, or group whose behavior influences the decision's success. Include internal (executives, teams, individual contributors) and external (investors, customers, partners, regulators) stakeholders.

### Step 2: Map Each Stakeholder's Incentives
For each stakeholder, document:
- **Explicit incentives:** Formal rewards and penalties (compensation, bonuses, equity, KPIs, OKRs, contractual terms).
- **Implicit incentives:** Informal drivers (career advancement, reputation, workload, peer approval, risk aversion, empire building, autonomy preservation).
- **What they optimize for:** The single thing this stakeholder is most motivated to maximize or protect.
- **What they avoid:** The single thing this stakeholder most wants to prevent.

### Step 3: Assess Alignment
For each stakeholder, evaluate: does their incentive structure naturally lead them toward behaviors that support the decision's success?
- **Aligned:** Their incentives directly reward behaviors that make the decision succeed.
- **Neutral:** Their incentives neither help nor hinder.
- **Misaligned:** Their incentives reward behaviors that undermine the decision's success.
- **Conflicted:** Their incentives pull them in multiple directions simultaneously.

### Step 4: Design Incentive Interventions
For misaligned or conflicted stakeholders, define interventions:
- **Realign:** Change the incentive structure (adjust comp, change KPIs, restructure reporting).
- **Compensate:** Add an incentive that counterbalances the misalignment (bonus for cooperation, recognition for desired behavior).
- **Monitor:** Accept the misalignment but watch for problematic behavior.
- **Remove:** In extreme cases, remove the stakeholder from the decision's influence.

### Step 5: Predict Behavioral Response
For each stakeholder under the proposed decision, predict: "Given their incentives, what will this person actually do?" Not what you want them to do, not what they say they will do, but what their incentives will lead them to do.

## Scale/Criteria

### Alignment Rating
| Rating | Description | Action Required |
|--------|-------------|-----------------|
| Strong alignment | Stakeholder's incentives directly support the decision | No action — monitor to maintain |
| Moderate alignment | Generally supportive but with some competing incentives | Awareness only — watch for drift |
| Neutral | Incentives do not affect the decision meaningfully | No action unless the decision needs their active support |
| Moderate misalignment | Key incentives pull against the decision | Intervention needed — realign or compensate |
| Strong misalignment | Stakeholder's core incentives actively undermine the decision | Critical intervention — realign, compensate, or remove from influence |

### Incentive Type Classification
| Type | Examples | Visibility |
|------|---------|------------|
| Financial | Salary, bonus, equity, revenue share | Usually visible |
| Career | Promotion, title, scope expansion, market value | Partially visible |
| Social | Peer recognition, status, belonging | Often invisible |
| Autonomy | Decision authority, independence, schedule control | Often invisible |
| Purpose | Mission alignment, impact, meaning | Variable |
| Security | Job stability, risk avoidance, predictability | Often invisible |
| Power | Headcount, budget, influence, information access | Often invisible |

## Scoring Guide

### Red Flags to Watch For
1. **The person who has to execute has no upside:** If the person doing the work only faces downside risk (failure = blame) with no upside (success = credit goes to leader), expect minimal effort or quiet sabotage.
2. **Competing metrics:** If two teams are measured on metrics that conflict (sales measured on new logos, CS measured on retention), expect finger-pointing and suboptimal behavior at the boundary.
3. **Short-term incentives driving long-term decisions:** If quarterly bonuses drive behavior on a decision with 2-year payoff, expect short-term optimization that undermines the long-term outcome.
4. **Invisible incentives dominating:** When the predicted behavior does not match the stated incentives, look for invisible incentives (career anxiety, political dynamics, personal relationships).
5. **Unanimous agreement from misaligned stakeholders:** If everyone agrees but their incentives would predict disagreement, they are either not being honest or not thinking clearly. Probe deeper.

### Template

```
Decision: [What is being decided]

| Stakeholder | Explicit Incentives | Implicit Incentives | Optimizes For | Alignment | Intervention |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Name/Role] | [Formal rewards] | [Informal drivers] | [Core motivation] | [Rating] | [Action] |
```

### Worked Example

**Decision:** Implement new CRM system across sales and customer success teams.

| Stakeholder | Explicit Incentives | Implicit Incentives | Optimizes For | Alignment | Intervention |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| VP Sales | Quota attainment bonus; team revenue | Reputation as revenue driver; board visibility | New revenue closed this quarter | Misaligned — any disruption to current workflow threatens quota | Adjust: Q1 quota relief during transition; bonus for CRM adoption metrics |
| Sales reps | Commission on deals closed | Minimize admin work; preserve existing relationships and shortcuts | Personal commission this month | Strongly misaligned — new CRM means relearning and temporary slowdown | Compensate: Adoption bonus; do not launch during end-of-quarter push |
| VP Customer Success | Retention rate; NPS score | Wants better data to prove CS value to leadership | Churn reduction | Aligned — better CRM gives better customer visibility | Monitor only — maintain enthusiasm |
| IT Lead | Project delivery on time and budget | Career advancement through successful implementation | On-time delivery | Aligned but conflicted — incentive to under-scope to hit deadline | Realign: Success metric includes adoption rate, not just go-live date |
| CFO | ROI on CRM investment | Reputation for fiscal discipline | Cost control and ROI | Neutral — supports if ROI case is strong, opposes if costs overrun | No action — provide monthly cost tracking |
| CEO | Company growth metrics | Board confidence, fundraising narrative | Revenue growth and operational maturity story | Aligned — wants both the outcome and the narrative | No action — use CEO as champion |

**Key insight from the map:** The people who must change their daily behavior (sales reps) have the strongest misalignment. Implementation plan must address this directly or the CRM will be technically "implemented" but practically unused.
