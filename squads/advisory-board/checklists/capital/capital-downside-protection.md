# Capital Downside Protection Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the organization has adequate protection against downside scenarios that could threaten its financial viability. Downside protection is not about avoiding risk; it is about ensuring that even bad outcomes are survivable. This checklist prevents the failure mode of optimizing for upside while leaving the organization fatally exposed to predictable downside scenarios.

## When to Use
- During annual financial planning and risk reviews.
- Before any material capital commitment.
- When evaluating the risk profile of the investment portfolio.
- When external conditions (market downturn, funding environment, customer concentration) change.

## Gate Criteria

### Downside Identification
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The top five financial downside scenarios are identified and quantified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Revenue loss scenarios (customer churn, market downturn, competitive displacement) are modeled.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Expense increase scenarios (input costs, regulatory compliance, talent market) are modeled.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: External shock scenarios (recession, pandemic, credit freeze) are considered.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The combined probability of any downside scenario occurring within 24 months is assessed.

### Protection Mechanisms
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Cash reserves or credit facilities cover at least three months of full operations under a downside scenario.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Insurance coverage is reviewed annually for adequacy.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Contract terms include protections (caps, floors, termination for cause) where appropriate.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Revenue diversification reduces exposure to any single customer, product, or market.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Cost structure includes sufficient variable costs that can be reduced quickly if needed.

### Response Plans
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Cost-reduction plans are pre-developed for each major downside scenario.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Priority order for cost cuts is defined (discretionary first, then non-critical, then essential).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The time from scenario trigger to cost-reduction implementation is under 30 days.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Contingency plans preserve the organization's ability to recover and grow after the crisis.

### Monitoring and Triggers
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leading indicators for downside scenarios are monitored regularly.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Trigger thresholds for activating response plans are defined.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board is informed when leading indicators suggest elevated downside risk.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Stress tests are conducted at least annually.

## Red Flags
- No cost-reduction plan exists that can be activated within 30 days.
- More than 30% of revenue comes from a single customer with no contingency.
- The company has no credit facility and cash reserves cover less than three months.
- Insurance coverage has not been reviewed in over 18 months.
- The cost structure is primarily fixed with limited ability to reduce spending quickly.

## Escalation Triggers
- A downside scenario begins to materialize and no response plan is ready.
- Leading indicators trigger a response threshold and leadership hesitates to act.
- Cash reserves drop below the minimum threshold without a funded plan to replenish.
- A material customer or revenue source signals intent to leave or reduce.
- Market conditions deteriorate and the organization's exposure exceeds pre-approved limits.
