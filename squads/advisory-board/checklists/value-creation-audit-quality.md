# Value Creation Audit Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Provides a comprehensive framework for assessing whether the company is genuinely creating long-term value for all stakeholders or merely capturing value through unsustainable means. A value creation audit goes beyond financial metrics to examine the durability, breadth, and ethical foundations of value generation. This checklist prevents the common trap of mistaking value transfer (zero-sum gains at others' expense) for value creation (positive-sum growth).

## When to Use
- During annual strategic reviews and board strategy sessions.
- When preparing for significant fundraising rounds that require value narrative.
- When M&A activity (buying or selling) requires valuation justification.
- When activist investors or stakeholders challenge the company's value creation claims.
- During leadership transitions to establish a baseline understanding of value.

## Gate Criteria

### Financial Value Assessment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Revenue growth is analyzed by quality: recurring versus one-time, organic versus acquired.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Unit economics are positive and improving, or there is a credible path to positive unit economics.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Return on invested capital exceeds the cost of capital on a sustained basis.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Free cash flow generation is assessed independently from accounting profits.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Capital efficiency (revenue per dollar invested) trends are tracked.

### Customer Value Assessment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer satisfaction metrics (NPS, CSAT, retention) demonstrate the company delivers value to customers.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer lifetime value is growing, not just from price increases but from deeper engagement.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The company is solving a genuine customer problem, not creating artificial dependency.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer acquisition channels are sustainable and diversified.

### Employee Value Assessment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Employee engagement and satisfaction metrics are healthy and stable or improving.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Total compensation is competitive for the market and role level.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Career development and growth opportunities are available and utilized.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The company invests in employee capabilities that are valuable beyond the current role.

### Ecosystem Value Assessment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Partners, suppliers, and vendors are treated fairly and sustainably.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The company's growth does not depend on extracting disproportionate value from the ecosystem.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Community and environmental impact are assessed and managed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The company contributes to industry development, not just its own competitive position.

### Sustainability and Durability
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Value creation drivers are durable, not dependent on one-time conditions or subsidies.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The company is reinvesting sufficiently to sustain value creation over the next 5-10 years.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Growth does not come at the expense of quality, trust, or long-term capability.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The value creation story is internally consistent across financial, customer, and employee dimensions.

## Red Flags
- Revenue growth is entirely from price increases with no improvement in customer value delivered.
- The company's success depends on exploiting information asymmetry rather than creating genuine value.
- Employee turnover is high and institutional knowledge is being lost faster than it is created.
- The company captures most of the value in its ecosystem, leaving partners and suppliers squeezed.
- Short-term financial metrics are strong but leading indicators of value creation (innovation, talent, customer health) are declining.
- The company has no clear explanation for why it deserves to exist beyond generating returns for shareholders.

## Escalation Triggers
- A credible third party (analyst, journalist, regulator) challenges the company's value creation claims with evidence.
- Customer metrics indicate value destruction: declining NPS, increasing churn, growing complaints.
- Employee attrition reaches levels that suggest the company is not creating sufficient value for talent.
- The company's value creation narrative diverges significantly from observable market performance.
- Ethical concerns surface that suggest value is being captured through questionable means.
