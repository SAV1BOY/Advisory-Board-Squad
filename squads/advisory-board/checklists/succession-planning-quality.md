# Succession Planning Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the organization has credible, actionable succession plans for critical leadership roles. Succession planning is not about naming replacements on a chart; it is about systematically developing internal talent and maintaining external awareness so that no single departure creates a crisis. This checklist prevents the organization from being dangerously dependent on any individual.

## When to Use
- Annually as part of talent review and leadership development cycles.
- When a senior leader announces departure or signals intent to leave.
- When organizational growth creates new leadership roles.
- During board reviews of CEO and executive team continuity.
- When a key-person risk is identified in organizational health assessments.

## Gate Criteria

### Coverage and Currency
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Succession plans exist for the CEO and all direct reports.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each plan identifies at least two internal candidates and at least one external profile.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Plans are reviewed and updated at least annually.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Emergency succession is documented separately from planned succession (who takes over tomorrow if needed).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board-level roles (chair, committee chairs) have succession considerations documented.

### Candidate Readiness
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Internal candidates are assessed against the role requirements with gap analysis.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Development plans exist for each internal candidate with specific milestones and experiences.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Candidates are given stretch assignments and exposure that accelerate readiness.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Readiness is assessed on a timeline: ready now, ready in 12 months, ready in 24 months.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Candidates' own career aspirations are understood and aligned with succession plans.

### Process Rigor
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The succession planning process includes input from the board, CEO, and CHRO.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Assessment criteria are consistent across all succession plans.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: External market awareness is maintained: who are the best leaders in the industry and adjacent sectors.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Diversity and inclusion are considered in the succession pipeline.

### Risk Mitigation
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Single points of failure (roles with no viable successor) are identified and flagged.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Knowledge transfer plans exist for critical institutional knowledge.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Retention strategies are in place for high-potential succession candidates.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization has relationships with executive search firms for emergency needs.

## Red Flags
- The CEO succession plan is "we'll figure it out when the time comes."
- Internal candidates are not being developed; the plan is to hire externally every time.
- Succession plans exist on paper but no development actions are being taken.
- The same candidate is listed as successor for multiple roles simultaneously.
- The board has not discussed CEO succession in the last 12 months.
- Key technical or institutional knowledge resides in one person with no documentation.

## Escalation Triggers
- A critical leader departs and no viable internal or external candidate is identified.
- The top succession candidate for a critical role leaves the company.
- The board learns that succession plans have not been updated for over 18 months.
- An emergency succession scenario occurs and the documented plan proves inadequate.
- Development plans for succession candidates are consistently deprioritized in favor of operational demands.
