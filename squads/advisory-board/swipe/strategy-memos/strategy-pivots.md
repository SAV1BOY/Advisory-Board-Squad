# Strategy Pivots — Swipe File

## Overview
The best pivots preserve what's working (team, technology, customer relationships) while fundamentally changing the strategy (market, product, business model). These examples provide patterns for boards guiding companies through strategic inflection points.

## Classic Pivot Patterns

### Customer Segment Pivot
- **Slack:** Started as an internal tool for a gaming company (Tiny Speck). Game failed. The communication tool was the real product. Pivoted from game developer to enterprise communication.
- **Pattern:** Build for yourself → others want it → pivot to serve them
- **Board signal:** Users who aren't your target are more engaged than your target. Follow the engagement.

### Technology Pivot
- **Shopify:** Started as an online snowboard store. Built custom e-commerce software because existing tools were terrible. Pivoted to selling the software platform.
- **Pattern:** Build infrastructure for your own need → realize the infrastructure IS the product
- **Board signal:** You're spending more time on the tools than the business. The tools may be the business.

### Business Model Pivot
- **Adobe:** Transitioned from perpetual software licenses ($600-2000 one-time) to subscription model ($20-50/month). Short-term revenue declined 35%. Long-term revenue increased 400%.
- **Pattern:** One-time revenue → recurring revenue. Painful transition but fundamentally better economics.
- **Board signal:** Customer lifetime value increases dramatically under subscription even if revenue dips initially.

### Market Pivot
- **YouTube:** Started as a video dating site. Users ignored the dating features and uploaded random videos. Pivoted to general video sharing.
- **Pattern:** Users find a different use case than intended. The market reveals itself.
- **Board signal:** If users are using your product in unexpected ways, study that behavior rather than forcing them back to your original vision.

## The Pivot Decision Framework

1. **What's working?** Identify the assets to preserve (team, technology, customer relationships, brand)
2. **What's not working?** Be specific: is it the product, the market, the business model, or the go-to-market?
3. **What evidence supports the pivot?** Customer behavior, market data, competitive dynamics
4. **What's the new hypothesis?** State it clearly and make it testable
5. **What's the cost of pivoting?** Lost momentum, team confusion, market perception
6. **What's the cost of NOT pivoting?** Continued decline, resource depletion, team demoralization
7. **How quickly can we test the new hypothesis?** Define a 90-day experiment

## Board Application
- The board's role in a pivot is to ask hard questions, not to dictate the new direction
- Ensure the pivot is driven by evidence, not by desperation or boredom
- Support the CEO through the social and emotional difficulty of admitting the current strategy isn't working
- Define success criteria for the pivot with a clear timeline for evaluation
