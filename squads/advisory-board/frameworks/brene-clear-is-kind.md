# Brene Brown Clear Is Kind

## Purpose

"Clear is kind. Unclear is unkind." Brene Brown's principle that direct, honest communication — even when uncomfortable — is an act of respect and care, while vague, indirect, or withheld communication is an act of self-protection that harms others. This framework provides practical tools for delivering clear communication in organizational settings, especially for feedback, expectations, and difficult conversations.

## When to Use

- When feedback needs to be delivered and you're tempted to soften it into meaninglessness
- When expectations are unclear and causing performance problems
- When avoiding a difficult conversation because it will be uncomfortable
- When designing communication norms for teams and organizations
- When "nice" culture is actually "avoid conflict" culture
- When board advisors need to deliver uncomfortable truths to founders

## Core Model

### The Core Principle
Being unclear is not being nice — it is being selfish. When you avoid hard feedback to protect YOUR comfort, you deny the other person the information they need to improve. Clarity is a gift, even when it's uncomfortable.

### The Kindness Spectrum
- **Cruel:** Direct but intentionally hurtful. Goal: to wound.
- **Critical:** Direct but not constructive. Goal: to judge.
- **Clear (Kind):** Direct AND constructive. Goal: to help. This is the target.
- **Nice (Unkind):** Indirect, vague, or withheld. Goal: to avoid discomfort. Actually harmful.

### The Clear Communication Framework
1. **Name the behavior.** Specific, observable, factual. Not interpretation.
2. **Describe the impact.** How the behavior affects you, the team, or the outcome.
3. **State the request.** What you need to change. Be specific.
4. **Express care.** Why you are sharing this — because you care about the person's success.

### Clear vs. Unclear Examples
| Unclear (Unkind) | Clear (Kind) |
|-------------------|-------------|
| "Things are going OK" | "We missed our Q3 target by 15%, and here's what needs to change" |
| "Maybe think about it differently" | "Your approach has a specific flaw: [X]. I suggest [Y] instead" |
| "Everyone's doing great" | "Three team members are exceeding expectations. Two are struggling with [specific areas]" |

## Steps

1. **Prepare the clear message.** Write down what you need to say. Strip out softening language that obscures the point.
2. **Check your intention.** Am I sharing this to help or to hurt? If to help, proceed. If to vent or punish, pause.
3. **Deliver directly.** Use the Name-Impact-Request-Care structure. Don't bury the message in qualifiers.
4. **Stay present.** The other person may react emotionally. Hold space for their response without retreating from your message.
5. **Follow up.** Clear communication is not a one-time event. Check in on progress and provide ongoing support.
6. **Audit your "nice" habits.** Where are you being unclear to avoid discomfort? Those are the places where clarity is most needed.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Board advisors who are "nice" instead of "clear" are failing in their primary responsibility. A founder needs honest assessment, not comfortable platitudes.
- Deliver feedback using the Name-Impact-Request-Care structure. "Your burn rate will exhaust runway in 8 months. You need to cut expenses by 30% or raise immediately. I'm telling you this because I want the company to survive."
- Create a board culture where clear is the norm. Model it yourself. Celebrate it in others.

## Common Mistakes

1. **The compliment sandwich.** Burying feedback between two compliments dilutes the message. Be direct.
2. **Confusing clear with harsh.** Clear communication is delivered with respect and care. Harsh communication is delivered with contempt.
3. **Being clear about others but not yourself.** Apply the same clarity to your own performance and mistakes.
4. **Using "clear is kind" to justify cruelty.** The test: Is this in service of the other person's growth, or is it in service of my frustration?

## Output Format

```markdown
# Clear Communication: [Topic]

## The Clear Message
- **Behavior (specific, observable):** [What happened]
- **Impact:** [How it affected outcomes/people]
- **Request:** [What needs to change]
- **Care:** [Why I'm sharing this]

## Delivery Plan
- When: [Timing]
- Where: [Private, 1:1]
- Follow-up: [Date for check-in]
```

## Related Frameworks

- **Brene Dare to Lead** — Clarity requires the courage of vulnerability
- **Brene BRAVING Trust** — Clear communication builds trust through Accountability and Integrity
- **Dalio Radical Transparency Protocol** — Clarity is transparency made actionable
- **Lencioni Five Dysfunctions** — Fear of conflict (Dysfunction 2) is cured by clear communication
- **Sinek Circle of Safety** — Safety enables clear communication
