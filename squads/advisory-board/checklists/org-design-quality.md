# Org Design Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures that organizational structure decisions are made intentionally to enable strategy execution, clear accountability, and efficient decision-making. Org design is not an HR exercise; it is a strategic tool. This checklist prevents the common failure of designing organizations around people rather than around the work that needs to be done.

## When to Use
- When restructuring teams, departments, or the entire organization.
- After significant leadership changes that necessitate role reassignment.
- When strategy shifts require different capabilities or coordination patterns.
- During annual organizational health reviews.
- When chronic execution problems suggest structural misalignment.

## Gate Criteria

### Strategy-Structure Alignment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The org structure is derived from strategic priorities, not from headcount or existing personnel.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each structural unit maps to a specific value stream or strategic capability.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Coordination mechanisms between units are defined (shared goals, liaison roles, cross-functional teams).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The structure minimizes handoffs for the most critical workflows.

### Accountability and Decision Rights
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Every key decision has a single accountable owner (no shared accountability for critical decisions).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Decision rights are documented and communicated using a RACI or similar framework.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Span of control is appropriate: no manager has more than 8-10 direct reports for complex roles.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Escalation paths are defined and known across the organization.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The number of management layers is justified; each layer adds clear value.

### Talent and Capability
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Critical roles are identified and staffed or have a credible plan to fill.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The structure creates career progression paths that retain top performers.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Single points of failure (roles where one departure causes major disruption) are identified and mitigated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The structure enables skill development and cross-functional learning.

### Communication and Information Flow
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Information flows required for good decision-making are mapped and enabled by the structure.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The structure minimizes information silos between teams that need to collaborate.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Meeting structures and rhythms align with the org design.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Feedback loops from front-line employees to senior leadership are built into the structure.

### Change Management
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The transition plan from current to future state is defined with milestones.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Communication of the change addresses the "why" before the "what."
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Impact on individual roles is communicated directly, not left to interpretation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A 90-day check-in is scheduled to assess whether the new design is working as intended.

## Red Flags
- The org chart is designed around specific individuals rather than strategic needs.
- Accountability for key outcomes is shared across multiple leaders with no single owner.
- The reorganization is the third in 18 months with no post-mortem on why the previous structures failed.
- New layers of management are added without removing old ones.
- Teams that must collaborate daily report to leaders who rarely interact.
- The org design discussion focuses on titles and reporting lines rather than workflows and decisions.

## Escalation Triggers
- Execution failures are traced to structural misalignment that was identified but not addressed.
- Key talent exits cite organizational confusion as a contributing factor.
- Decision-making speed has materially slowed after a reorganization.
- Customer-facing metrics deteriorate within 90 days of a restructuring.
- Cross-functional conflict escalates to the CEO more than three times per quarter.
