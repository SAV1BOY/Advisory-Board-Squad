# Thiel Monopoly Test Checklist

## Purpose
Evaluates whether the organization is building toward a monopoly position or is engaged in commoditized competition. Thiel argues that competition is destructive and that the most successful companies build monopolies through proprietary technology, network effects, economies of scale, and brand. This checklist assesses whether the company's strategy leads to a monopoly-like position or to a crowded market where profits are competed away.

## When to Use
- During strategic planning and positioning discussions.
- When evaluating new market entries or product launches.
- When assessing competitive threats to existing monopoly-like positions.
- During investor narratives to validate competitive advantage claims.
- When growth is slowing and the company needs to assess its competitive position.

## Gate Criteria

### Monopoly Characteristics Assessment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Proprietary technology is identified that provides at least a 10x improvement over alternatives.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Network effects are present and quantified: does each new user make the product more valuable for existing users.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Economies of scale exist: does cost per unit decrease meaningfully with growth.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Brand strength is assessed: would customers pay a premium for this brand versus a generic alternative.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The company dominates a specific niche even if it does not dominate a broad market.

### Market Definition Honesty
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The market is defined accurately, not expanded to make the company look small (competitive camouflage) or shrunk to make it look dominant.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Market share is measured against real competitors, not an artificially broad addressable market.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The company is honest about whether it faces substitutes and alternatives.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The analysis distinguishes between market share and pricing power as indicators of monopoly.

### Monopoly Building Path
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The strategy starts with dominating a small market before expanding to adjacent markets.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The expansion path from initial niche to broader market is mapped with logical sequences.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each expansion step reinforces monopoly characteristics rather than diluting them.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The timeline to monopoly-like economics is realistic and funded.

### Monopoly Sustainability
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The monopoly position is protected by structural barriers, not just execution speed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Investment in moat strengthening is ongoing, not assumed to be complete.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Regulatory risks to the monopoly position are assessed and managed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The company creates enough value for stakeholders to maintain social license for its market position.

## Red Flags
- The company describes a massive TAM but has no specific strategy for a niche within it.
- Pricing power is nonexistent: the company matches competitor prices rather than setting them.
- The company is in a market with more than ten well-funded competitors and no clear differentiation.
- Network effects are claimed but not demonstrated with data.
- The company's "moat" is simply being first to market with no structural advantage.
- Growth strategy is to acquire market share through unsustainable spending.

## Escalation Triggers
- Pricing power erodes as competitors enter the market with similar offerings.
- The company is forced to compete primarily on price, indicating commodity dynamics.
- A single competitor captures a monopoly position in a market the company competes in.
- Network effects begin to reverse as users leave the platform.
- The board realizes the company is competing, not monopolizing, and margin compression accelerates.
