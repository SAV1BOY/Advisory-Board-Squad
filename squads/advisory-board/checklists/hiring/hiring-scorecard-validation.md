# Hiring Scorecard Validation Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures that hiring scorecards are defined before the search begins and are rigorous enough to produce high-quality hiring decisions. A scorecard defines success for the role in measurable terms, ensuring candidates are evaluated against objective criteria rather than gut feeling.

## When to Use
- Before initiating any search for a role.
- When reviewing the hiring process for quality and consistency.
- When hiring outcomes (early performance, retention) suggest scorecard issues.

## Gate Criteria
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A written scorecard exists before the job posting is created.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The scorecard defines outcomes expected in 12 months, not just responsibilities.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Competencies are categorized as must-have versus nice-to-have.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Cultural and behavioral criteria are included alongside technical skills.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The scorecard reflects input from the hiring manager, team, and cross-functional peers.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Each candidate is evaluated against the same scorecard using a consistent rating scale.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The scorecard is calibrated: different interviewers produce similar assessments of the same candidate.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Post-hire performance is compared to scorecard predictions to improve future scorecards.

## Red Flags
- The scorecard is written after candidates are already being evaluated.
- Different interviewers are evaluating different criteria.
- The scorecard is a job description, not an outcome-based assessment tool.
- Gut feeling overrides scorecard assessments in hiring decisions.

## Escalation Triggers
- Early-tenure performance of hires consistently underperforms scorecard predictions.
- The hiring manager overrides the scorecard without documenting the rationale.
- Multiple hires from the same scorecard fail within 12 months.
