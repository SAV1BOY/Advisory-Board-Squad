# Stakeholder Communication Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures that communications to key stakeholders (investors, board members, employees, customers, partners, regulators) are accurate, timely, and calibrated to the audience. Poor stakeholder communication creates distrust, confusion, and misaligned expectations. This checklist prevents both under-communication (leaving stakeholders in the dark) and over-communication (drowning them in noise without signal).

## When to Use
- Before issuing any material communication to investors, the board, or the public.
- During crisis communication planning.
- When preparing board updates, investor letters, or all-hands presentations.
- When a significant strategic change needs to be communicated to multiple audiences.
- During annual review of stakeholder communication effectiveness.

## Gate Criteria

### Audience Calibration
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The target audience is identified with their specific information needs and concerns.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The communication is tailored to the audience's level of context and expertise.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The channel is appropriate for the audience and the message urgency.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The communication anticipates likely questions and addresses them proactively.

### Content Quality
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Key messages are limited to three or fewer per communication.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Facts are verified and sources are available for any claims made.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The communication distinguishes between facts, projections, and opinions.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Bad news is communicated directly, not buried or euphemized.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Next steps and timeline for follow-up are included.

### Consistency and Alignment
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The message is consistent across all stakeholder audiences (no contradictions between what investors and employees hear).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The communication aligns with previous messages; any changes in direction are acknowledged.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: All spokespersons are aligned on key messages and talking points.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Legal and compliance review is completed for regulated communications.

### Timing and Cadence
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The communication is timely: stakeholders learn important news from the company, not from third parties.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A regular communication cadence is maintained (not just when there is good news).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Sensitive communications are timed to minimize unintended consequences.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Internal stakeholders (employees) are informed before or simultaneously with external stakeholders.

### Feedback and Measurement
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Mechanisms exist for stakeholders to ask questions and provide feedback.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Stakeholder perception is measured periodically (surveys, feedback sessions).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Communication effectiveness is reviewed and the approach is refined over time.

## Red Flags
- Employees learn about company news from media before internal communication.
- Investor communications are consistently more optimistic than internal operating assessments.
- The company goes silent during difficult periods and only communicates when things are going well.
- Stakeholders frequently express surprise about decisions or developments they should have known about.
- Communication is one-directional with no mechanism for feedback or questions.
- Different stakeholder groups receive contradictory messages about the same topic.

## Escalation Triggers
- A material misstatement is discovered in a public communication.
- A regulatory body issues a notice about inadequate disclosure.
- A stakeholder group (investors, employees, customers) reports a significant trust deficit.
- Media coverage reveals information that stakeholders should have received directly from the company.
- An employee leaks internal communications because they feel external communication is misleading.
