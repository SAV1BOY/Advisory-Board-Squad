# Naval Leverage Map Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the organization understands and maximizes its leverage: the ability to multiply the impact of each unit of effort. Naval identifies three forms of leverage: labor (people), capital (money), and products with zero marginal cost (code, media, content). This checklist assesses whether the organization is deploying leverage effectively and whether its leaders understand which forms of leverage create the most value at the current stage.

## When to Use
- During strategic planning when allocating resources.
- When evaluating the scalability of business models or initiatives.
- When assessing executive effectiveness and organizational efficiency.
- When deciding between building, buying, and partnering.
- When evaluating whether growth requires proportional resource increase.

## Gate Criteria

### Leverage Identification
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: All three forms of leverage (labor, capital, code/media) are mapped for the business.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The primary leverage mechanism for the current stage is identified and prioritized.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The ratio of output to input is measured for key activities.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Activities with high leverage are distinguished from activities with low leverage.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization knows which activities would benefit most from increased leverage.

### Labor Leverage
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Key leaders multiply the output of their teams rather than doing individual contributor work.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Team structure maximizes each person's highest-leverage contribution.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Low-leverage tasks are delegated, automated, or eliminated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization avoids adding headcount where technology or process can scale instead.

### Capital Leverage
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Capital is deployed to amplify human effort, not just to sustain operations.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The return on each dollar of capital deployed is tracked.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Capital allocation favors high-leverage investments over incremental ones.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization understands the difference between spending capital and investing capital.

### Product/Code/Media Leverage
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Products with zero or near-zero marginal cost are prioritized in the product strategy.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Technology is used to replicate expertise without requiring additional human effort.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Content, brand, and media assets create compounding value over time.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization builds assets that work while people sleep.

### Leverage Optimization
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leaders spend at least 50% of their time on the highest-leverage activities.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization regularly audits time allocation against leverage impact.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Leverage metrics are included in performance reviews and planning.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization is increasing its leverage ratio over time, not decreasing it.

## Red Flags
- Revenue growth requires proportional headcount growth with no improvement in leverage.
- Leaders spend most of their time on low-leverage operational tasks.
- The company builds custom solutions for every customer rather than leveraging technology for scale.
- Capital is spent on maintaining legacy systems rather than investing in leverage-creating assets.
- The organization adds people as the first solution to every capacity problem.
- No one tracks or optimizes the ratio of output to input.

## Escalation Triggers
- Revenue per employee is declining over time.
- The organization cannot serve growing demand without proportionally growing the team.
- Key technology investments fail to deliver the expected leverage improvement.
- Leadership time is consumed by operational crises, leaving no capacity for strategic leverage.
- Competitors achieve similar results with significantly fewer resources.
