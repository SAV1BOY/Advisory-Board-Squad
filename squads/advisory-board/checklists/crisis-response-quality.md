# Crisis Response Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures the organization can respond to crises with speed, accuracy, and coordination. A crisis is any event that threatens the company's operations, reputation, financial position, or stakeholder trust and requires an immediate response outside normal operating procedures. This checklist provides a structured framework to prevent chaos, miscommunication, and delayed action during high-pressure situations.

## When to Use
- Immediately when a crisis is identified or declared.
- During crisis simulation exercises and tabletop drills.
- When developing or updating the crisis response plan.
- Post-crisis to evaluate response effectiveness.

## Gate Criteria

### Initial Response (First 2 Hours)
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A crisis commander is identified and empowered to make decisions without normal approval chains.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The crisis is classified by type and severity using a predefined framework.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A situation assessment is completed: what happened, what is the current impact, what is still unknown.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Key stakeholders (board, legal, comms, affected teams) are notified within the first hour.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Immediate containment actions are identified and initiated.

### Communication
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Internal communication is issued to employees with facts, guidance, and a timeline for updates.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: External communication is drafted and reviewed by legal before release.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A single spokesperson is designated; all external inquiries are routed to this person.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer communication addresses their specific concerns and provides actionable guidance.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Communication acknowledges what is known, what is unknown, and avoids speculation.

### Coordination and Decision-Making
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A crisis team with clear roles (commander, comms, legal, operations, technical) is activated.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A decision log is maintained recording every significant decision, who made it, and the rationale.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Regular situation updates are scheduled (every 2-4 hours minimum during active crisis).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Dependencies on external parties (regulators, vendors, law enforcement) are identified and contacts engaged.

### Containment and Resolution
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Root cause investigation is initiated in parallel with containment efforts.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The blast radius is defined: which customers, systems, data, or operations are affected.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A resolution plan with milestones and estimated timeline is documented.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Resource requirements for resolution are identified and allocated.

### Post-Crisis
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: A post-mortem is scheduled within two weeks of crisis resolution.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The post-mortem identifies systemic failures, not just proximate causes.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Corrective actions from the post-mortem are assigned owners and deadlines.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The crisis response plan is updated based on lessons learned.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Affected stakeholders receive follow-up communication summarizing what happened and what changed.

## Red Flags
- No crisis response plan exists or it has not been reviewed in over 12 months.
- The crisis team has never conducted a simulation exercise.
- Multiple people are issuing conflicting public statements.
- The company's first instinct is to minimize or deny rather than assess and respond.
- Legal considerations paralyze communication, leaving stakeholders in an information vacuum.
- No one is documenting decisions during the crisis.

## Escalation Triggers
- The crisis escalates beyond the crisis commander's authority or capability.
- Regulatory agencies initiate formal inquiries or investigations.
- Media coverage becomes widespread and the narrative is not aligned with facts.
- Customer or employee safety is at risk.
- The financial impact exceeds the materiality threshold for board notification.
- The crisis reveals a systemic vulnerability that could cause recurrence.
