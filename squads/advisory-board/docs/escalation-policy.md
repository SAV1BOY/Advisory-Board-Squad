# Escalation Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines when and how issues are escalated to the Advisory Board. Effective escalation ensures the board addresses critical issues promptly without being overwhelmed by operational matters.

## Escalation Levels

### Level 0 — Routine (No escalation)
Day-to-day operational matters handled by the executive team within delegated authority.

**Board involvement:** None. Informed at the next regular meeting if strategically relevant.

### Level 1 — Advisory (Async escalation)
Issues where board input would improve the decision, but the decision is not blocked pending board response.

**Board involvement:** Board-Chair is notified via email. Response within 5 business days. May be addressed async or held for the next meeting.

**Examples:**
- Emerging competitive threat that may affect strategy.
- Market opportunity outside the current plan.
- Personnel situation that could affect board-level priorities.
- Unusual customer or partner development.

### Level 2 — Decision Required (Urgent escalation)
Issues that require board approval per the charter or decision policy, or where the executive team requests board guidance on a material matter.

**Board involvement:** Board-Chair is notified immediately. Dedicated discussion scheduled within 1 week. May require an ad-hoc session.

**Examples:**
- Capital commitment above threshold.
- M&A opportunity or approach.
- Executive departure or misconduct allegation.
- Term sheet received during a fundraise.
- Material contract negotiation.

### Level 3 — Crisis (Immediate escalation)
Events that pose an immediate threat to the company's operations, reputation, legal standing, or survival.

**Board involvement:** Board-Chair is notified within 1 hour. Crisis protocol activated (workflow 07). Emergency board session within 24-48 hours.

**Examples:**
- Security breach or data loss.
- Regulatory investigation or enforcement.
- Executive misconduct confirmed.
- Product failure causing customer harm.
- Financial fraud or misstatement.
- Media crisis with viral potential.

## Escalation Process

### Who Can Escalate
Any agent or any member of the executive team can escalate directly to the Board-Chair. There is no hierarchy filter — the person closest to the issue escalates directly.

### How to Escalate

**Level 1:**
Email to Board-Chair with subject line: `[ESCALATION L1] — [Topic]`
Include: What is the issue? Why is board input valuable? What is the timeline?

**Level 2:**
Email and phone call to Board-Chair with subject line: `[ESCALATION L2] — [Topic]`
Include: What is the issue? What decision is needed? What are the options? What is the deadline?

**Level 3:**
Phone call to Board-Chair immediately. Follow with email: `[ESCALATION L3 — CRISIS] — [Topic]`
Include: What happened? Who is affected? What has been done so far?

### Board-Chair Response

| Level | Response Time | Action |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | ≤5 business days | Acknowledge receipt; advise async or schedule for next meeting |
| L2 | ≤24 hours | Acknowledge; schedule discussion within 1 week |
| L3 | ≤1 hour | Activate crisis protocol; assemble crisis team |

## Escalation Triggers

### Automatic Triggers (No judgment required — escalate always)
- Any event matching a crisis definition (Level 3 automatic).
- Any capital commitment above threshold (Level 2 automatic).
- Any executive termination or resignation (Level 2 automatic).
- Risk register item moves to "critical" status (Level 2 automatic).

### Judgment-Based Triggers (Escalate when in doubt)
- "If this goes wrong, would the board want to have known about it?" → Escalate.
- "If I wait for the next meeting, will the situation change materially?" → Escalate now.
- "Am I avoiding escalation because it's uncomfortable?" → That's exactly when you should escalate.

## De-Escalation

Once an escalation is resolved:
1. Board-Chair confirms the issue is resolved or reclassified to a lower level.
2. Governance-Ops logs the escalation and resolution in the escalation register.
3. Lessons learned are captured and fed into the relevant workflow (post-mortem, risk register update, etc.).

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Problem | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Under-escalation | "I didn't want to bother the board" — board learns about problems too late | When in doubt, escalate. The board would rather be informed early than surprised later. |
| Over-escalation | Every operational issue goes to the board — meeting time consumed by trivia | Use the level definitions. L0 issues stay with the executive team. |
| Escalation as blame-shifting | Escalating to avoid personal accountability | Escalation is for getting help and input, not for transferring ownership. The escalator stays involved. |
| Slow escalation | Waiting for "more information" on a Level 3 event | Level 3 triggers immediate escalation. More information comes after activation, not before. |
| Escalation without context | "We have a problem" with no details | Every escalation includes: what, who, options, and timeline. |
