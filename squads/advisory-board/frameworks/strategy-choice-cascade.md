# Strategy Choice Cascade

## Purpose

The Strategy Choice Cascade, developed by A.G. Lafley and Roger Martin in "Playing to Win," provides a rigorous five-question framework for making integrated strategic choices. Strategy is not a plan, a vision, or a set of goals — it is a set of choices about where to play and how to win. This cascade forces those choices to be explicit, mutually reinforcing, and testable. It is the single most practical strategy framework for translating ambition into action.

## When to Use

- During annual strategic planning to set or refresh company strategy
- When entering a new market, launching a new product, or making a major pivot
- When the organization lacks strategic clarity (doing too many things, winning at none)
- When new leadership needs to articulate strategic direction
- During board strategy reviews to evaluate whether the company has a real strategy or just a list of priorities
- When translating a high-level vision into operational decisions

## Core Model

### The Five Questions (in order — each constrains the next)

#### 1. What is Our Winning Aspiration?
The purpose of the enterprise — what winning looks like in broad terms. Not a financial target, but a statement of the impact you seek to have.
- What does success look like for our stakeholders?
- What would the world look like if we won?
- What are we fundamentally trying to achieve?
- This is aspirational but bounded — it sets the playing field.

#### 2. Where Will We Play?
The specific choices about where to compete. This is the most important strategic choice because it determines the game you are playing.
- **Geography**: Which markets, regions, countries?
- **Customer segments**: Which customers, defined by need, behavior, or demographics?
- **Channels**: How will we reach customers?
- **Product/service categories**: What will we offer?
- **Value chain stages**: Which activities will we perform ourselves?
- **Critical**: Where to play is also about where NOT to play. Strategy is choice, and choice means saying no.

#### 3. How Will We Win?
The specific competitive advantage you will create in your chosen playing field.
- What is our unique value proposition?
- Why will customers choose us over alternatives?
- What can we do that competitors cannot easily replicate?
- This must be a differentiation or cost leadership choice — not both, and not "we'll be better."
- **Differentiation**: Offering superior value that commands a premium
- **Cost leadership**: Delivering comparable value at sustainably lower cost

#### 4. What Capabilities Must We Have?
The reinforcing set of capabilities required to win where you have chosen to play.
- Which 3-5 capabilities are essential to our How to Win?
- Do we have them today? If not, how do we build or acquire them?
- How do these capabilities reinforce each other?
- Capabilities are activities you perform consistently better than competitors. They are not resources (people, money) — they are the ability to do specific things well.

#### 5. What Management Systems Do We Need?
The systems, structures, and processes that support and measure the capabilities.
- How do we measure progress against our strategy?
- What governance structures support strategic choices?
- How do we ensure the organization stays aligned with the cascade?
- What feedback loops tell us if the strategy is working?

### The Cascade is Integrated
Each level must reinforce the others. If your Where to Play doesn't support your Winning Aspiration, the cascade is broken. If your Capabilities don't enable your How to Win, execution will fail. Integration is what separates strategy from a wish list.

## Steps

1. **Start with the current cascade.** Document the implicit strategy the organization is currently executing. Most organizations have a strategy — it is just implicit and often inconsistent.

2. **Articulate the Winning Aspiration.** In one paragraph, describe what winning means for this organization. Avoid financial metrics at this stage — focus on impact and position.

3. **Generate Where to Play options.** List all possible combinations of geography, segment, channel, product, and value chain choices. Be exhaustive, then narrow ruthlessly.

4. **For each Where to Play option, define How to Win.** What competitive advantage is achievable in that space? If you cannot articulate a clear How to Win, that Where to Play choice is wrong.

5. **Identify required capabilities for each Where/How combination.** What must you be world-class at to execute this strategy?

6. **Assess capability gaps.** Map current capabilities against required capabilities. Large gaps indicate high execution risk.

7. **Choose the cascade.** Select the Where/How/Capabilities combination that is most achievable and most valuable. Document the full cascade.

8. **Design management systems.** Build the scorecards, meetings, and governance structures that operationalize the cascade.

9. **Communicate the cascade.** Every employee should be able to state the strategy in one sentence: "We win by [How to Win] in [Where to Play] because we are uniquely capable of [Key Capabilities]."

10. **Review the cascade quarterly.** Strategy is a hypothesis. Test it against results and adjust.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Use the cascade to evaluate whether a company has a real strategy. If the founder cannot articulate consistent answers to all five questions, they have activities, not strategy.
- The most common strategic failure is trying to play everywhere. Push founders to make sharper Where to Play choices.
- When reviewing strategy, test for integration: Does the How to Win actually work in the chosen Where to Play? Do the capabilities actually enable the How to Win?

### For Founders
- Write the cascade on a single page. If it takes more than a page, it is not yet clear enough to execute.
- Use the cascade to say no. Every opportunity that doesn't fit the Where to Play is a distraction, regardless of how attractive it looks.
- Revisit the cascade before every major resource allocation decision. Does this investment strengthen a capability in the cascade?

## Common Mistakes

1. **Confusing aspiration with strategy.** "We want to be the market leader" is an aspiration. "We will win the mid-market segment through the lowest total cost of ownership enabled by our self-serve platform" is a strategy.

2. **Choosing Where to Play without How to Win.** Entering a market because it is large or growing, without a clear competitive advantage, is a recipe for mediocrity.

3. **Trying to win everywhere.** The most common strategic error. Serving all segments, all geographies, all channels. This is not strategy — it is the absence of strategy.

4. **Generic How to Win.** "We'll have better technology and better people" is not a How to Win. Everyone says this. A real How to Win is specific and falsifiable.

5. **Ignoring capability gaps.** A brilliant strategy with missing capabilities is a fantasy. Honestly assess what you can and cannot do.

6. **Skipping Management Systems.** The cascade without operational systems is a poster, not a strategy. You need metrics, reviews, and feedback loops.

- **Specific advantage:** [One sentence]

|           |                      |     |                   |

| System | Purpose | Owner | Frequency |
|--------|---------|-------|-----------|
|        |         |       |           |

## Cascade Integration Check
- [ ] Winning Aspiration is achievable in the chosen Where to Play
- [ ] How to Win is specific to the chosen Where to Play
- [ ] Capabilities enable the How to Win

- [ ] The cascade can be stated in one sentence

## One-Sentence Strategy
"We win by [How] in [Where] because we are uniquely capable of [Capabilities]."

## Related Frameworks

- **Moat Map** — Moats are the structural expression of "How to Win"
- **Competitive Response Model** — Test the cascade against likely competitive responses
- **Scenario Planning** — Stress-test the cascade against multiple futures
- **Opportunity Cost Framework** — The Where to Play choices define your opportunity costs
- **Flywheel Framework** — A well-designed cascade creates flywheel dynamics
- **Decision Memo Framework** — Major cascade decisions should be formally documented
