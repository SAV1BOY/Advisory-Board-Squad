# Lencioni Six Questions

## Purpose

Patrick Lencioni's Six Questions framework provides the essential clarity that every organization needs. When the leadership team can answer these six questions identically and without hesitation, the organization has clarity. When answers are vague, inconsistent, or debated, the organization has confusion — and confusion drives politics, misalignment, and wasted effort.

## When to Use

- During strategic planning to establish or refresh organizational clarity
- When different leaders give different answers to basic questions about the company's direction
- When onboarding new executives who need to understand the organization's identity
- When employees are confused about priorities or direction
- During board sessions to assess strategic alignment

## Core Model

### The Six Questions

1. **Why do we exist?** Your core purpose beyond making money. (Maps to Sinek's WHY)
2. **How do we behave?** The 2-3 core values that define your culture. Not aspirational — actual.
3. **What do we do?** Your business description in simple terms. What you'd tell your neighbor.
4. **How will we succeed?** Your 3 strategic anchors — the deliberate decisions that differentiate you.
5. **What is most important right now?** Your single overriding priority (the "thematic goal") for the next 3-6 months.
6. **Who must do what?** Clear roles and responsibilities. No overlap, no gaps.

### The Clarity Test
Ask each member of the leadership team to answer each question independently. Compare answers. Discrepancies reveal where clarity is missing. The goal is not word-for-word agreement but substantive alignment.

## Steps

1. **Assemble the leadership team** for a half-day to full-day session.
2. **Answer each question independently first** (written, no discussion).
3. **Compare answers and discuss discrepancies.** The discussions about discrepancies are the most valuable part.
4. **Align on a single set of answers.** Write them down. Make them crisp and memorable.
5. **Create a "playbook"** — a single document with all six answers that every employee can reference.
6. **Communicate relentlessly.** Repeat the six answers at every opportunity: all-hands, team meetings, onboarding, reviews.
7. **Review every 6 months.** Questions 1-4 should be stable. Question 5 changes every 3-6 months. Question 6 updates as the team evolves.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Ask the founder to answer the Six Questions. If they struggle or their answers differ from their leadership team's, there is an organizational clarity problem.
- Question 5 (What is most important right now?) is the most actionable and the most often missing. A company with 7 priorities has zero priorities.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Aspirational values instead of actual values.** Core values should describe how the organization actually behaves, not how it wishes it behaved.
2. **Too many priorities.** Question 5 must have ONE answer. Multiple "top priorities" is a contradiction.
3. **Leadership alignment without organizational communication.** If only the leadership team knows the answers, clarity doesn't exist at scale.
4. **Set it and forget it.** The answers need ongoing reinforcement through communication and systems.
