# Stakeholder Mapping Framework

## Purpose

Stakeholder mapping provides a systematic method for identifying all parties who affect or are affected by an organization's decisions, assessing their influence and interest, and developing engagement strategies for each. Organizations that ignore key stakeholders are blindsided by opposition. Organizations that engage all stakeholders proactively build broader support and avoid costly surprises.

## When to Use

- Before major strategic decisions that affect multiple parties
- When planning product launches, market entries, or organizational changes
- During fundraising to understand the full landscape of influential parties
- When facing opposition or resistance from unexpected quarters
- When building a communications strategy for a major announcement
- During crisis preparation to know who needs to be communicated with

## Core Model

### The Stakeholder Map (Power-Interest Grid)

|  | High Interest | Low Interest |
|--|-------------|-------------|
| **High Power** | **Manage Closely**: These stakeholders can block or enable your success and care deeply | **Keep Satisfied**: Powerful but disengaged — don't surprise them |
| **Low Power** | **Keep Informed**: They care deeply but have limited power — keep them in the loop | **Monitor**: Low priority but track for changes |

### Stakeholder Categories
- **Internal:** Employees, founders, board members, investors
- **External-Direct:** Customers, suppliers, partners, distributors
- **External-Indirect:** Regulators, media, community, competitors, industry analysts
- **Hidden:** Future employees, potential acquirers, non-obvious influencers

### Engagement Strategies by Quadrant
- **Manage Closely:** Regular, proactive communication. Involve in decisions. Build personal relationships.
- **Keep Satisfied:** Periodic updates. No surprises. Address concerns promptly.
- **Keep Informed:** Regular communication through scalable channels (newsletters, all-hands). Listen to their feedback.
- **Monitor:** Track through media and industry channels. Engage only when their position changes.

## Steps

1. **List all stakeholders.** Be exhaustive. Include hidden stakeholders that are easy to forget.
2. **Assess each stakeholder's power and interest** on the 2x2 grid.
3. **Understand each stakeholder's needs and concerns.** What do they want? What are they afraid of?
4. **Design engagement strategies** by quadrant.
5. **Assign relationship owners.** Each "Manage Closely" stakeholder should have a named relationship owner.
6. **Review quarterly.** Stakeholder positions change. Power and interest shift.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Forgetting hidden stakeholders.** Regulators, future employees, and community members are often overlooked until they cause problems.
2. **Treating all stakeholders equally.** The power-interest grid exists because resources are finite. Prioritize.
3. **One-way communication.** Stakeholder engagement is listening, not just broadcasting.
4. **Static mapping.** Stakeholder positions change. Update the map regularly.
