# Pre-Read Standards

## Purpose

Pre-reads are the foundation of productive board meetings. When pre-reads are excellent, the meeting focuses on discussion and decisions. When they are poor, the meeting devolves into presentations and confusion. This document defines the standard for all materials submitted to the board.

## General Standards

### Length
- **Decision memos:** 2 pages maximum. Appendices for supporting data.
- **Information memos:** 1-2 pages.
- **Data packs:** 3-5 pages with visualizations.
- **Strategic briefs:** 3-5 pages.

If a document exceeds the page limit, it is returned for revision.

### Structure
Every pre-read follows the structure in `voice/language-guides/memo-writing-style.md`:
1. Header block (title, author, date, type, read time).
2. Bottom line (first paragraph).
3. Context.
4. Analysis.
5. Risks and mitigations.
6. Ask (what does the board need to do?).

### Writing Quality
- Active voice. No passive constructions.
- Average sentence length: 15-20 words. Maximum: 35 words.
- One idea per paragraph. Maximum 5 sentences per paragraph.
- Data, not adjectives. "Revenue grew 14 %" not "Revenue grew significantly."
- Sources cited for all data points.

### Formatting
- Scannable structure: headers, bullet points, tables.
- Bold the single most important sentence per section.
- Tables for comparisons (options, scenarios, timelines).
- No decorative formatting, colored text, or clip art.

## Submission Process

### Timeline
| Milestone | Timing |
|---|---|
| Pre-read assignment | T-30 (assigned in prior meeting or by Board-Chair) |
| Draft submission to Governance-Ops | T-10 |
| Governance-Ops review and feedback | T-8 |
| Revised version submitted | T-7 |
| Final package distributed to board | T-5 |
| Board questions/comments due | T-2 |

### Quality Check
Governance-Ops reviews every submission against this checklist before distribution:

- [ ] Bottom line in the first paragraph.
- [ ] Ask is specific and actionable.
- [ ] Within page limits.
- [ ] Active voice throughout.
- [ ] All data sourced.
- [ ] No sentence over 35 words.
- [ ] Formatting aids scanning.
- [ ] Risks and mitigations included.
- [ ] For decision memos: at least three options presented.

Non-compliant submissions are returned with specific feedback. No exceptions.

## Pre-Read Types

### Decision Memo
Used when the board needs to make a decision.

**Required elements:**
- The question (one sentence).
- Options (≥3, genuinely distinct).
- Evaluation criteria.
- Analysis of each option against criteria.
- Recommendation with rationale.
- Risks and mitigations for the recommended option.
- Ask: "Approve option [X]" or "Choose between options [A] and [B]."

### Information Memo
Used when the board needs to be informed but no decision is required.

**Required elements:**
- The finding or update (first paragraph).
- Context: why is the board receiving this?
- Key data points.
- Implications: what does this mean for the board's existing decisions or priorities?
- Ask: "No action required" or "Note for future discussion."

### Data Pack
Used to provide quantitative context for board discussions.

**Required elements:**
- Dashboard or data visualization.
- Narrative summary (1 page): what the data shows.
- Variances from plan or prior period, with explanations.
- Trend analysis: what direction is the data moving?

### Strategic Brief
Used for complex strategic topics that require deeper context.

**Required elements:**
- Executive summary (1 page).
- Diagnostic or analysis (2-3 pages).
- Options and/or recommendations.
- Open questions for board discussion.

## Reader Experience

The test for every pre-read: **A busy, intelligent reader should extract the key message in 60 seconds and the full argument in 5 minutes.**

If the pre-read fails this test, it needs revision regardless of content quality.

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Problem | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Buried lead | Key message on page 3 | Bottom line in paragraph 1 |
| Presentation deck as pre-read | Slides without narrative are not pre-reads | Written memos with complete sentences and arguments |
| Data without interpretation | 10 charts with no "so what" | Every data point has a narrative explanation |
| Missing the ask | Board doesn't know what to do | Every memo ends with a specific ask |
| Last-minute submission | Materials arrive at T-1 | Deadline is T-10 for drafts; distribution at T-5 |
| Option-less decision memo | "Should we do X?" (yes/no) | ≥3 options for every decision |
