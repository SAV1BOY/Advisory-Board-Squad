# Board Effectiveness Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Provides a framework for evaluating whether the board is fulfilling its governance role effectively. Board effectiveness is not about individual directors; it is about the collective body's ability to provide strategic guidance, ensure accountability, and protect stakeholder interests. This checklist surfaces structural and behavioral issues that diminish board impact.

## When to Use
- During annual board self-assessment processes.
- When onboarding new board members to set expectations.
- After a significant governance failure or near-miss.
- When the board chair changes and governance practices are being reset.
- When external advisors assess board performance.

## Gate Criteria

### Composition and Capability
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board skills matrix is maintained and maps to current strategic needs.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Gaps in board expertise relative to the company's strategic challenges are identified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board diversity (expertise, background, perspective) is assessed and improvement plans exist.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board member commitment levels (attendance, preparation, engagement) are tracked.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board refreshment pipeline exists for planned succession.

### Meeting Quality
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board meetings allocate more time to discussion and decisions than to presentations.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Pre-read materials are consistently high quality and sent on time.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: All board members participate actively in discussions (not dominated by one or two voices).
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Sensitive topics (CEO performance, executive compensation, succession) receive adequate time.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Executive sessions without management occur at every meeting.

### Strategic Engagement
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board is engaged in strategy setting, not just strategy ratification.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board members bring independent perspectives, not just validation of management proposals.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board proactively identifies strategic risks and opportunities, not only responding to management agenda.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board members maintain current knowledge of the industry, competitive landscape, and market trends.

### Accountability and Governance
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: CEO evaluation is conducted annually with clear criteria and candid feedback.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board committees have clear charters, are staffed appropriately, and report to the full board.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The board holds itself accountable for its own effectiveness, not just management's performance.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Governance policies are reviewed annually and updated as needed.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Information asymmetry between management and the board is actively managed.

### Culture and Dynamics
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Dissent is welcomed and constructive disagreement is a norm.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The chair effectively manages meeting dynamics and ensures balanced participation.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Board members provide support to management between meetings when requested.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Relationships between board members are collegial but not clubby (friendship does not override governance).

## Red Flags
- Board meetings are primarily a management reporting session with little board-initiated discussion.
- The same board member dominates every discussion while others remain silent.
- The board has not changed composition in three or more years despite evolving strategic needs.
- No executive session is held or it is perfunctory.
- Board members lack industry expertise relevant to the company's current challenges.
- The board unanimously approves every management proposal without substantive questioning.

## Escalation Triggers
- A governance failure occurs that could have been prevented by more effective board oversight.
- Board member attendance drops below 75% for any individual over a 12-month period.
- Material information is withheld from the board, indicating a management-board trust failure.
- External stakeholders (investors, regulators) raise concerns about board governance.
- The annual board self-assessment reveals significant dissatisfaction but no corrective action is taken.
