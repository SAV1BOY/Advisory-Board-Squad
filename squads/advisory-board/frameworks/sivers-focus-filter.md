# Sivers Focus Filter

## Purpose

Derek Sivers's Focus Filter is elegantly simple: "If it's not a HELL YES, it's a no." This framework provides a binary decision tool for filtering opportunities, requests, and commitments. The insight: most people say yes to good opportunities, filling their lives with good-but-not-great commitments that crowd out the truly exceptional. By raising the bar to "hell yes," you create space for the things that genuinely matter.

## When to Use

- When overwhelmed with opportunities and unable to prioritize
- When calendar is full but productivity is low
- When a founder is saying yes to every partnership, feature request, and meeting
- When evaluating new initiatives against current commitments
- When building a personal or organizational operating system for decision-making
- When the default answer is "yes" and it should be "no"

## Core Model

### The Binary Filter
Every opportunity gets one of two responses:
- **HELL YES!** (Excitement, energy, alignment with goals, clear value) → Commit fully
- **No.** (Anything less than hell yes) → Decline gracefully

There is no "maybe," no "let me think about it," no "yes, but later." The filter is binary because ambivalence is a signal that the opportunity is not compelling enough to deserve your finite resources.

### Why It Works
- **Protects time.** Time is the one non-renewable resource. Every yes is a no to something else.
- **Protects energy.** Half-hearted commitments drain energy without producing proportional value.
- **Creates clarity.** When you say no to everything that isn't hell yes, your true priorities become obvious.
- **Enables excellence.** Doing three things with full commitment produces more than doing ten things with partial attention.

### The Attention Budget
Assume you have 5 "full attention" slots. Every hell-yes commitment fills one slot. When all slots are full, the next opportunity — no matter how good — is a no until a slot opens.

## Steps

1. **List all current commitments.** Everything on your plate: projects, meetings, relationships, obligations.
2. **Apply the filter.** For each, ask: "If this opportunity came to me today, fresh, with no prior commitment, would I say HELL YES?" Mark each as Hell Yes or No.
3. **Plan to exit the No's.** For commitments you can't immediately exit, create a timeline for graceful closure.
4. **Apply the filter to all new opportunities.** Before saying yes to anything, pause and apply the test.
5. **Review monthly.** Hell-yes commitments can become no's as circumstances change. Re-filter regularly.

## Application Guidelines

### For Board Advisors
- Help founders apply this filter to their product roadmap. Most roadmaps are cluttered with "good idea" features that aren't "hell yes" features.
- Apply it to your own advisory commitments. If an advisory opportunity isn't hell yes, decline. Your effectiveness depends on focus.
- Use the filter in board discussions: "Is this initiative a hell yes? If not, should we be doing it?"

## Common Mistakes

1. **FOMO overriding the filter.** "But what if I miss out?" If it's not hell yes now, it's still no.
2. **Guilt-based yes.** Saying yes because you feel obligated, not because the opportunity excites you.
3. **Confusing novelty with hell yes.** New things feel exciting. True hell yes persists beyond the initial novelty.
4. **Not applying it to existing commitments.** The filter works retroactively too. Audit what you've already said yes to.
