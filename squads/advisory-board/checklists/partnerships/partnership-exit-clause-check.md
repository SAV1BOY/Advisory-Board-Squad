# Partnership Exit Clause Check Checklist

## Purpose
Ensures every partnership agreement includes clear, fair, and actionable exit provisions. Partnerships that are easy to enter but impossible to exit create long-term liability. This checklist verifies that the organization can end partnerships when they no longer serve strategic interests without catastrophic cost or disruption.

## When to Use
- Before signing any partnership or vendor agreement.
- During contract renewals to reassess exit provisions.
- When considering ending a partnership.
- When legal reviews partnership agreements.

## Gate Criteria
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Termination clauses are explicit with defined notice periods.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Termination can be initiated for convenience (not only for cause) with reasonable terms.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Financial costs of termination (penalties, transition costs, stranded investments) are quantified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Data portability provisions ensure the organization can retrieve its data upon exit.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Customer relationship ownership post-exit is clearly defined.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Transition support obligations (knowledge transfer, system migration) are specified.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: IP and work product ownership upon termination is unambiguous.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: Non-compete or exclusivity restrictions post-termination are reasonable and time-bound.
- [ ] **Pass/Fail**: The organization can survive the partnership ending without catastrophic disruption to operations.

## Red Flags
- No exit clause exists or termination requires both parties' consent.
- Exit costs are so high they effectively lock in the partnership.
- IP or data ownership provisions are ambiguous regarding termination.
- The partnership creates customer or operational dependencies that cannot be unwound.

## Escalation Triggers
- The organization wants to exit a partnership but discovers the terms make it impractical.
- A partner threatens to exercise exit provisions as leverage in negotiations.
- Exit costs exceed the originally estimated range.
- Legal review reveals exit clause deficiencies in an existing critical partnership.
