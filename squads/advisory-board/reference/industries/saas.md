# SaaS Industry Reference

## Business Model Characteristics
- **Revenue model:** Recurring subscriptions (monthly/annual). Predictability is the primary investor appeal.
- **Gross margins:** 70-85% (software delivery cost is low relative to revenue)
- **Key metric:** ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue) is the north star. MRR for early-stage.
- **Growth pattern:** Land and expand. Low initial contract, grow within accounts over time.
- **Sales models:** Self-serve (< $500/mo), inside sales ($500-$5K/mo), field sales (> $5K/mo)

## Key Metrics and Benchmarks
- **Net Revenue Retention (NRR):** > 120% (excellent), 100-120% (good), < 100% (problem)
- **Gross margin:** > 75% expected for pure software; < 65% signals services dependency
- **LTV/CAC:** > 3x; CAC payback < 18 months
- **Rule of 40:** Growth rate + profit margin > 40%
- **Burn multiple:** Net burn / net new ARR. < 2x is efficient.
- **Quick ratio:** (New MRR + Expansion MRR) / (Churn MRR + Contraction MRR). > 4x is healthy.

## Competitive Dynamics
- **Winner-take-most in horizontal SaaS** (CRM, HR, Accounting). Switching costs create lock-in.
- **Room for multiple winners in vertical SaaS** (dental, construction, logistics). Specialization is the moat.
- **Platform risk:** Building on top of another platform (Salesforce, Shopify) creates dependency.
- **AI disruption:** AI-native SaaS products may displace incumbent workflow tools by automating the workflow entirely.

## Board Considerations
- Monitor NRR as the single best health indicator. Declining NRR = early warning of product-market fit erosion.
- Evaluate whether growth is efficient (low burn multiple) or bought (high burn multiple)
- For vertical SaaS, assess total addressable market carefully; many verticals are smaller than they appear
- Watch for services revenue disguised as software revenue; it compresses margins and doesn't scale
