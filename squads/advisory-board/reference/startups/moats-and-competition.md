# Moats and Competition

## Moat Types

### Network Effects
- **Direct:** More users make the product more valuable for each user (social networks, messaging)
- **Indirect/Cross-side:** More users on one side attract more on the other (marketplaces, platforms)
- **Data:** More users generate more data, improving the product (recommendations, AI)
- **Strength test:** What happens if a competitor launches with the same product but zero users? If the answer is "nothing, our users stay," the network effect is strong.

### Switching Costs
- **Financial:** Cost of migrating data, retraining staff, or paying termination fees
- **Procedural:** Complexity of changing workflows, integrations, and processes
- **Relational:** Trust and familiarity built with existing vendor's team
- **Strength test:** How many months of effort would it take a customer to switch? > 6 months = strong.

### Economies of Scale
- **Cost advantages** that increase with volume: purchasing, distribution, R&D amortization
- **Demand-side scale:** Brand recognition and trust that come with market share
- **Strength test:** Can a well-funded competitor replicate our cost structure at 10% of our volume? If yes, weak moat.

### Brand
- **Trust premium:** Customers pay more or choose you by default because of brand trust
- **Not just awareness** — awareness without trust is not a moat
- **Strength test:** Would customers pay a 20%+ premium for our product over an identical unbranded alternative?

### Regulatory/Legal
- **Patents, licenses, regulatory approvals** that create barriers to entry
- **Strength test:** How long would it take a competitor to obtain equivalent regulatory status? > 2 years = meaningful.

### Proprietary Technology
- **Must be 10x better** (Thiel's threshold), not just incrementally better
- **Degrades over time** as competitors catch up; must be continuously refreshed
- **Strength test:** Is our technology advantage widening or narrowing?

## Competitive Analysis Framework

### The Five Forces (Porter, Simplified)
1. **Rivalry among competitors** — How intense is direct competition?
2. **Threat of new entrants** — How easy is it to enter this market?
3. **Threat of substitutes** — What else could customers use instead?
4. **Buyer power** — How much leverage do customers have?
5. **Supplier power** — How much leverage do suppliers have?

## Board Application
- Conduct an annual moat assessment using the Moat Monitoring Dashboard
- Challenge management to identify whether moats are widening or narrowing
- Competitive analysis should focus on structural advantages, not feature comparison
- When evaluating growth investments, prioritize those that widen moats
